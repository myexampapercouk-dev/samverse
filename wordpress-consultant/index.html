What a WordPress consultant can help with

Audits: a review of plugins, theme, hosting, speed, security and SEO basics, with problems listed in order of importance. Architecture: planning custom post types, taxonomies, user roles and integrations before a build, so the site does not need restructuring later. Plugin choice: which plugins to keep, replace or remove, and when a small custom plugin is safer than three overlapping ones.

Migrations: planning a move between hosts, or from Wix, Shopify or an old theme, with a redirect plan to protect search traffic; see WordPress migration. Performance: finding what is actually slowing the site, rather than adding another optimisation plugin; see speed optimization.

One-off consultation vs ongoing advisory

A one-off consultation answers a specific question: an audit, a quote review or a platform decision, delivered as a call and a written summary. Ongoing advisory suits teams with their own developers or marketers who want a WordPress specialist to check plans, review code or answer questions each month, under a small retainer.

Many clients start with a one-off audit and then hand the fixes to me or to their own team. There is no obligation to hire me for the work, and the recommendations are written so any competent developer can act on them.

What you get from an audit

A written report in plain language: what was checked, what is fine, what is wrong, and what to do about each issue, ranked as urgent, important or optional. Each recommendation gives a rough idea of the effort involved. Where something needs a developer, the report says what to ask for, so you can get comparable quotes.

Pricing

Consultations are quoted up front based on the size of the site and the question. A focused review of one issue is a small fixed fee; a full audit of a large site or WooCommerce store costs more because it takes longer. Ongoing advisory is a monthly fee for an agreed number of hours. You know the price before anything starts.

Who it is for, and who it is not for

Consulting suits business owners who want an independent view before spending on a redesign, marketing managers responsible for a WordPress site but without a developer, and agencies that want a second pair of eyes on a build or a hosting setup. It is not a fit if you want guaranteed rankings or traffic numbers, which no honest consultant can promise, or if you need a large agency to run a long, multi-team programme. Samverse is one freelance developer, and the advice comes from hands-on WordPress work rather than a sales team.

FAQ

Common questions

What does a WordPress consultant do?

A WordPress consultant reviews your site or plans and recommends what to do: which plugins and hosting to use, how to fix speed or security problems, how to structure a new build and whether a quote is reasonable. The output is advice, and the work can be done by anyone you choose.

Do I have to hire you to do the work afterwards?

No. The recommendations are written so your own team or another developer can act on them. If you would like me to do the work, I quote it separately.

What access do you need for an audit?

Usually a temporary WordPress administrator account and, for hosting and speed checks, access to the hosting panel. Logins should be shared through a password manager or a temporary account, and access can be removed as soon as the review is finished.

Can you review a quote from another agency?

Yes. I can read the proposal, explain what each part means, point out anything missing or unusual, and suggest questions to ask. I do not criticise other developers for the sake of it.

Can you advise on WordPress vs Shopify?

Yes. I build on both, so the advice is based on your products, team and budget rather than a preference for one platform.

Do you offer consultations for clients outside India?

Yes. Consultations run over video call and email with clients in the UK, the US, Australia and elsewhere, at a time that suits your time zone.
