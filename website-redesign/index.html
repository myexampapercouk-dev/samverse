Redesigning a website without losing what works

A redesign is the riskiest thing most businesses do to their website. The new design rarely causes trouble; the losses come from pages quietly removed, URLs changed without redirects, or a staging setting left on at launch. This is how I run a redesign so the pages that already bring traffic and enquiries survive it.

A redesign project, step by step

  1. Free review and a fixed quote. Send your website address and what is not working. If a refresh would do the job, I will say so.
  2. Audit. With access to Search Console and GA4, I record which pages bring visitors, rankings and enquiries today.
  3. Structure and redirect map. A new page list and navigation, with every old URL marked keep, redirect or merge.
  4. Design. The homepage and one key inner page first, shared for your feedback before the rest is built.
  5. Build on staging. Content moved across and improved, then a review round and your sign-off.
  6. Launch and monitoring. The launch-day checklist, then Search Console checked closely over the following weeks.

The audit before any design

Before anyone opens a design tool, I record a baseline:

  • Search Console Performance: the pages and searches that earn clicks over the last twelve months. These pages get the most care.
  • Search Console Links: pages other websites link to, which must keep working or redirect.
  • GA4: landing pages that lead to enquiries, form submissions or WhatsApp clicks, not just visits.
  • A full crawl and the sitemap: every URL, including PDFs and images that get search traffic.
  • Speed and enquiries: PageSpeed results for key templates, and roughly how many enquiries arrive each month.

Search Console reports explained shows where each figure lives.

The URL and redirect map

The redirect map is a spreadsheet with one row per old URL: the new URL, the action, and a note. The rules I follow:

  • Keep URLs that work. The safest URL is one that does not change, even if a new name looks tidier.
  • Redirect to the closest match, never everything to the homepage.
  • Merge thin or overlapping pages into one stronger page, with each old URL redirected to it.
  • Remove pages only deliberately, after checking they have no traffic or links worth keeping.

Restructuring the content

A redesign is the right moment to fix structure, not just looks. The menu is rebuilt around how customers choose, usually one page per service or product family, and pages nobody visits are merged or retired using the map. Content on pages that rank is kept and improved, never trimmed just to make a layout look minimal. On Sahni Power Solutions, a generator rental site I built, the fleet is split into seven capacity bands, each with its own page, because buyers think in capacity first. Structuring your website navigation covers the approach.

Staging and approval

The new site is built on a staging copy that is password-protected and blocked from search engines, while your current site stays live; staging sites explained covers how that works. You review real pages on your own phone, not mock-ups, and send feedback as one consolidated list from one decision-maker, which keeps rounds short; see giving clear website feedback. Nothing goes live until you approve it. In the last few days before launch, changes to the old site are paused or noted so they can be copied across.

The launch-day checklist

  • A full backup of the old site, kept until the new one has settled.
  • The "discourage search engines" setting and any noindex tags from staging removed.
  • Every 301 redirect live, tested against the full list of old URLs.
  • HTTPS on every page, with one preferred version of the domain.
  • Titles, descriptions, headings and structured data present on key pages.
  • Forms, WhatsApp buttons and phone links tested on real phones, and GA4 recording key events.
  • A fresh sitemap submitted in Search Console, and speed checked against the baseline.

The website launch checklist has the full list.

Monitoring rankings after launch

For the first two weeks I check Search Console daily for 404 errors, indexing problems and redirects that were missed, and fix them straight away. After that, top pages and enquiries are compared with the baseline as Google recrawls the site. Small ranking movements for a few weeks are normal. I cannot promise rankings will not move at all; what I can make sure of is that nothing is lost through a missing redirect or a forgotten setting. How long it takes

Most redesigns take 1 to 3 weeks once content is ready. Large sites, a move from another platform, heavy content rewriting and slow feedback stretch it. The audit and redirect map happen before design starts, so they never get rushed at the end.

Who it is for

Businesses whose website looks dated, is hard to use on phones, is slow or cannot be edited without a developer, especially those with traffic and enquiries worth protecting, and companies going through a rebrand. If only a few things are wrong, a refresh is the smaller, safer job.

FAQ

Common questions

Will I lose my Google rankings?

Not if it's done properly. I keep important URLs, set up 301 redirects for changed pages and preserve your SEO settings.

Can you redesign a site that isn't on WordPress?

Yes. I can move your site from Wix, HTML, Joomla or another platform onto WordPress as part of the redesign.

Will my website go offline during the redesign?

No. I build the new design on a staging site and switch over only when you approve it.

Can I keep my existing content?

Yes. I can reuse and improve your existing content, images and pages.

Do I need a full redesign or just a refresh?

If the site works on phones, updates safely and still matches what you sell, a refresh of content, photos, buttons and speed is often enough. If it is not built for phones, runs on an abandoned theme or no longer fits your business, a redesign usually costs less over time than patching it.

Can the redesign be done in phases?

Yes. A common approach is to redesign the homepage, top service pages and contact page first, then the rest in later stages. Each phase still goes through staging, approval and redirect checks before it goes live.

How soon after launch will I know if rankings are affected?

Problems such as missing redirects or pages blocked from Google show up in Search Console within days, which is why it is checked daily for the first two weeks. Normal ranking movement usually settles over a few weeks as Google recrawls the site.

Can I keep updating my current site while the new one is built?

Yes. Your current site stays live and editable during the build. In the last few days before launch, changes are paused or noted so they can be copied to the new site and nothing is lost.
