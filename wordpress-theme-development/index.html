Custom theme vs premium theme vs page builder

A premium theme is the quickest start and suits a tight budget, but it ships with features for thousands of sites, so you load code you never use and your design is limited to its options. A page builder such as Elementor gives visual editing freedom, which is useful, but it adds weight and lets editors break layouts. I also build Elementor sites when visual editing matters most.

A custom theme costs more at the start, but it carries only what your site needs, matches the design exactly and gives editors safe, defined building blocks. It is the better long-term choice when speed, brand consistency or a growing content team matter. If your needs are simple, I will tell you a premium theme is enough.

What is included in custom WordPress development

The theme itself: templates for each page type, headers, footers and navigation, and archive and single templates for posts and custom post types. Content editing: custom Gutenberg blocks or ACF fields built around your design, with block patterns for common sections. Front-end quality: responsive layouts, accessible markup, lazy-loaded images, minimal JavaScript and no render-blocking extras.

Custom development often goes beyond the theme: custom post types for services, team members or locations, integrations with a CRM or booking tool, and WooCommerce templates. For a full site build including content and setup, see WordPress website development.

Block themes and Full Site Editing

WordPress now supports block themes, where templates, headers and footers are built from blocks and edited in the Site Editor. They suit teams who want to adjust layouts themselves. Classic themes with custom blocks or ACF suit sites where the layout should stay fixed and only content changes. I build both, and recommend one after seeing who will edit the site and how often.

Pricing and timelines

Each theme is quoted after reviewing the design, because the price depends on the number of distinct templates, custom blocks and integrations, and how much content needs moving. As a rough guide, a small business theme with a handful of templates takes a few weeks, and larger sites with many custom post types and integrations take longer. The written quote states the exact scope, price and timeline before work starts.

Who it is for, and who it is not for

A custom theme suits businesses with a finished design or a clear brand, sites that have become slow or hard to manage on a premium theme, and agencies that need designs coded faithfully. It is not the best fit for a very small budget, a site needed within days, or teams that want to redesign layouts themselves every week; a well-chosen premium theme or a page builder serves those better.

FAQ

Common questions

How much does a custom WordPress theme cost?

It depends on the number of templates, custom blocks, integrations and content to move. You get a fixed written quote after I review the design or brief, so the price is known before work starts.

Will I be able to edit the site myself?

Yes. Content is edited through custom blocks or ACF fields in the WordPress editor. You can change text, images, posts and pages, and the layout stays consistent with the design.

Do you build block themes or classic themes?

Both. Block themes suit teams that want to change layouts in the Site Editor. Classic themes with custom blocks or ACF suit sites where layouts should stay fixed. The choice is made with you after looking at who will edit the site.

Can you convert my existing site to a custom theme?

Yes. I build the new theme on a staging copy of your site, keep your content and URLs, and switch over once you have approved it, with redirects for any URLs that change.

Do I own the theme code?

Yes. You own all the code written for your project, with no licence fees. It can be handed to another developer later, along with the handover notes.

Can you build from my designer's Figma file?

Yes. That is one of the most common starting points. I review the file first and flag anything that will be hard to edit or slow to load before building.
