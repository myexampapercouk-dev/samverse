Structuring the product catalogue

The basics are to group products by type, then by application. Beyond that, these decisions make a catalogue work:

  • Family pages or single products. When items differ only by size or rating, one product family page with a full specification table usually beats forty near-identical pages that Google may treat as duplicates.
  • Organise by how buyers specify. Sahni Power Solutions, a generator rental company, splits its fleet into seven capacity bands, each with its own page covering typical uses, what affects the rental price and questions specific to that size.
  • Search that understands model numbers, and filters by capacity, material or size once the range is large.
  • Specifications as real text, not images, so buyers can copy them and Google can read them.

RFQ forms that give sales what it needs

A request for quote is not a contact form. The buyer wants a price, so the form should collect enough to prepare one without days of back-and-forth:

  • The product name filled in automatically from the page the buyer was on.
  • Quantity with a unit dropdown, the delivery city or country, and requirement details.
  • Uploads for drawings and specifications in the formats your team opens, such as PDF, DWG or STEP.
  • Contact details last, and the page address captured so sales knows what the buyer was viewing.

On Vansh Group, an electronics OEM, the enquiry form has a service dropdown (product, PCB, part or tool development), so every enquiry arrives tagged with the kind of work needed. RFQ forms for B2B websites covers routing and follow-up.

Capability and infrastructure pages

Large buyers want to know whether you make things yourself. Name your machines and facilities instead of writing "state-of-the-art infrastructure". Vansh Group's site names a tool room, power press machines, an SMT room and injection moulding machines, gives three manufacturing units in Northern India, introduces the leadership team and shows testimonials with each person's name, title and company. Add production capacity, your quality checks and real photos of the shop floor, not stock images. The website trust signals checklist lists what else buyers look for.

Certifications and compliance

Show the certifications you actually hold, such as ISO, BIS or CE where relevant, with the certificate number, scope and validity date, and a PDF copy buyers can verify. Remove any that have lapsed. Product-level compliance belongs on product pages: Sahni Power Solutions refers to CPCB-IV+ emission standards and Karnataka State Pollution Control Board noise limits for its silent units, right where buyers need them.

Export buyers and multilingual sites

  • Clear English with correct technical terms, and international units where buyers expect them.
  • Countries served, minimum order quantities, typical lead times and shipping terms such as FOB or CIF, stated only where accurate.
  • Translated pages for key markets, each language on its own URL with hreflang tags, written or checked by a fluent speaker; see hreflang and multilingual SEO.
  • Hosting or a CDN that keeps the site fast for overseas visitors.

PDFs and brochures that help SEO

Buyers still ask for a catalogue to forward internally. Vansh Group offers its company profile and two product catalogues as PDF downloads. Google can index PDFs, but only when they contain real text, are linked from a crawlable page and are not locked behind a form. Every important product should also have a web page, with the PDF as the printable companion. I upload brochures with descriptive file names, a proper document title, and your phone, WhatsApp and website inside the file, compressed for mobile data. FAQ

Common questions

Can I add and update products myself?

Yes. Products are managed from the WordPress dashboard, so you can add items, change specifications and upload brochures without code.

Do you build catalogues without online payments?

Yes. Most B2B sites use a catalogue with "Request a Quote" instead of a cart, and I can set up either.

Can the website support export enquiries?

Yes. I can structure the site for international buyers and add multilingual support if you need it.

How long does a B2B website take?

Usually 2–3 weeks depending on the number of products and pages, once content is ready.

Should every product have its own page?

Not always. Products that differ only by size or rating usually work best as one product family page with a full specification table. Products with different uses, materials or buyers deserve their own pages, because each one answers a different search.

Can buyers attach drawings to a quote request?

Yes. RFQ forms can accept drawings and specifications in the formats your team uses, such as PDF, DWG or STEP, with size limits and file-type checks, and a secure link in the notification email instead of large attachments.

Can you import our products from a spreadsheet or old catalogue?

Yes. Product data in a spreadsheet can be imported in one go, which is much faster than adding a large range by hand. If your products only exist in a PDF catalogue, the data is first organised into a spreadsheet so it can be checked before import.

Should our brochure be a free download or behind a form?

It depends on what you want from it. A free download can be found on Google and shared freely. Asking for contact details first gives you leads, but Google will not index the file. A common middle ground is to keep the main catalogue free and put a detailed guide behind a short form.
