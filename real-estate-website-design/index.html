Building a real estate website, in detail

Property buyers shortlist projects online long before they visit a site, and they check the details: the RERA registration, the carpet area, the builder's completed projects and how far the site really is from the highway or metro. This is how I plan and build websites for builders, developers and property agents in India: project pages, RERA details, lead capture, ad landing pages and tracking. Real estate website must-haves lists the features in brief.

A real estate website project, step by step

  1. Free review and a fixed quote. Send your current site, the projects you want to feature and whether you run Google or Meta ads.
  2. Structure. We decide how projects are organised, by city, status or type, and whether a launch needs its own microsite; project microsites explains the trade-off.
  3. Content and compliance details. RERA numbers, approved plans, carpet areas, amenities, location details and brochures, all from your team.
  4. Build on staging. A project template, enquiry and site-visit forms, gated brochure downloads and WhatsApp, at a private address.
  5. Tracking and testing. Test enquiries for every project, conversion tracking for ads, and each page checked on phones over mobile data.
  6. Launch and walkthrough. Search Console connected, and a call showing your team how to add a project, update its status and swap a price sheet.

Project pages buyers trust

What every project page shows

  • Configurations, such as 1, 2 or 3 BHK, plots or shops, with carpet area as defined under RERA, not only super built-up area.
  • Floor and unit plans as images that zoom on a phone, with a PDF for printing.
  • Amenities, specifications and construction status, with dated site photos.
  • Location: a map, distances to schools, hospitals and transport that you have checked, and directions to the site office.
  • A price or starting price with what it includes, if you choose to publish it.

RERA details, displayed properly

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, advertisements for a registered project must show its RERA registration number and the address of the state RERA website, and a project page on your website is generally treated as advertising. I put both near the top of each project page, and agents' pages carry the agent's registration number. Some authorities add their own requirements, such as MahaRERA's QR code on advertisements, so check your state's current rules. A project that needs registration should not be marketed before it is registered, which rules out "pre-launch" pages for it. Renders are labelled as artist's impressions.

Lead capture: site visits, brochures and WhatsApp

  • Site-visit booking with a preferred date and time, so your sales team calls to confirm a slot rather than asking "when can you come?".
  • Gated brochure downloads that ask only for a name, phone and email, with the brochure sent to the email address given.
  • A short enquiry form with configuration and budget dropdowns. Studio Agama Interiors, an interior design studio I built a site for, asks for project stage, budget and time frame, so the team knows what each lead needs before calling; the same idea works for property buyers.
  • WhatsApp and tap-to-call on every page, with the project name pre-filled in the message.

Each enquiry is emailed instantly and can also go to a Google Sheet or your CRM, with a consent line and privacy notice under the form. Property leads cool quickly, so the first call should happen the same day; responding to enquiries fast covers alerts and auto-replies.

Landing pages, tracking and speed for ad campaigns

Builders often run Google and Meta ads around a launch. Each campaign should land on a page about that project alone, with a headline matching the ad, one form and no menu leading elsewhere; see landing page design. Form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls are tracked as conversions in GA4, Google Ads and Meta, so you can see which campaign brings site visits, not just clicks. Google Ads conversion tracking on WordPress explains the setup. Walkthrough videos are embedded from YouTube and load only when tapped, and renders are compressed, because a slow page wastes paid clicks on mobile data.

Local SEO and schema for builders and agents

Buyers search by locality and configuration, such as "2 BHK in Wakad" or "plots near Hinjewadi". A page for each project, and for agents a genuinely useful page for each locality you work in, matches those searches; copies with the area name swapped do not. Local landing pages without doorway pages explains the difference. The business gets RealEstateAgent or Organization schema, project pages get breadcrumbs, and your Google Business Profile links to the site. Keep project status current, because a sold-out project still shown as available costs trust.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I add property listings myself?

Yes. Projects and listings are managed from the dashboard. You can add photos, prices and status (available, sold) anytime.

Can buyers download a brochure after entering their details?

Yes. I can set up gated brochure downloads so you capture the name and phone number of every interested buyer.

Do you make landing pages for property ads?

Yes. I build fast, focused landing pages for each project that work well with Google Ads and Meta ads.

Can the site show multiple projects and locations?

Yes. The site can be organised by project, city, status (ongoing, completed) or property type.

Do I need to show the RERA number on my website?

Yes, for registered projects. RERA requires advertisements to show the project's registration number and the state RERA website address, and a project page on your website is generally treated as advertising. I add both to every project page. Check your state authority's rules too, as some add requirements such as a QR code.

Can I promote a pre-launch project on my website?

Not before it is registered, if the project needs RERA registration. The Act does not allow a promoter to advertise, market or invite bookings for such a project until it is registered. You can keep a general page about your company and completed projects, and add the new project once the registration number is issued. Confirm the current rules for your state with your RERA consultant or lawyer.

Should each project have its own microsite?

A large launch with its own ad budget can justify a microsite or dedicated landing pages. Most projects work well as pages on the main site, where buyers can also see your completed projects. I usually suggest project pages on the main site, plus focused landing pages for ad campaigns.

Should we publish prices on project pages?

It is your decision. A starting price with the configuration and carpet area answers the first question most buyers have and filters out enquiries outside their budget. If you prefer to share prices on request, say so plainly and make the price sheet a quick download or WhatsApp request.
