Hiring a freelance WordPress developer: how it works with me

Whether you run a business in India, a company abroad or an agency, this explains what hiring me involves, how I compare with your other options, and what you have in hand when the work is done.

From first message to finished work

  1. You send the brief. A WhatsApp message or the contact form is enough to start: what you need, your current website if there is one, and any deadline. If you want to be thorough, the website brief template covers everything I would otherwise ask.
  2. Questions, then a fixed quote. I reply with any questions, then send a written scope, a fixed price and a timeline, usually within 24 hours. The scope lists the pages or tasks, the features, the revision rounds and anything excluded.
  3. An advance to start. Projects usually begin with an advance, with the balance due before launch. Monthly plans are billed monthly.
  4. Work on a staging site. New builds and bigger changes happen on a private copy. I share the link so you can check progress on your own phone, and nothing reaches your live site until you approve it.
  5. Testing and launch. Pages are checked on real phones and desktops, forms are tested to make sure the emails arrive, stores get test orders, and speed and SEO basics are in place before anything goes live.
  6. Handover and support. A walkthrough on a video call, your logins confirmed and a backup taken, and I stay available for changes and fixes.

Which kind of engagement fits

  • A fixed-price project suits anything with a clear end point: a new website, a redesign, a WooCommerce store or a migration.
  • A batch of fixes suits a list of smaller jobs, such as a broken layout, a slow page and a form that stopped sending, quoted together as one fixed price.
  • A monthly maintenance plan suits a site that already works and needs updates, backups, monitoring and small content changes. See WordPress maintenance.
  • Ongoing support hours suit businesses that change their site often and want someone who already knows it.
  • White-label delivery suits agencies that sell websites under their own brand. See WordPress developer for agencies.

You can switch between them as your needs change.

What to check before you hire anyone, including me

  • Live work, and what they did on it. Open their sites on your phone and ask what they personally built. My case studies spell it out, from the magazine-style layout and deep category structure of India Automation Hub to the Our Temples directory, searchable by state and by deity, with registration for guests and temple owners.
  • Whose name the accounts are in. The domain, hosting and WordPress administrator account should belong to you, not the developer. The website ownership checklist lists every account to confirm.
  • A written scope and fair payment terms. Pages, features, revisions, exclusions and milestones should be agreed before work starts; see fair payment terms for a website project.
  • Licensed software. Premium themes and plugins should be genuine licences, never "nulled" copies.
  • How they communicate before you pay. Sensible questions about your brief are a good sign. A quote sent within minutes, with no questions at all, is not.

UK mornings overlap the Indian working day, and for US clients, feedback sent in your afternoon can be worked on while you sleep. What is included, and what is not

Every website project includes the written scope, the staging link, testing on real devices, speed optimization, SEO basics, a walkthrough and support after launch. These sit outside it unless the quote says otherwise:

  • Hosting, domain and premium licence fees, which are paid to the providers, ideally in your name.
  • Writing all your content from scratch, and photography. I structure the pages and polish your text, but the facts about your business have to come from you.
  • Work beyond the agreed scope. New pages or features are quoted before I start, so the invoice never surprises you. Bug or change request? explains where the line usually sits.

How long it takes

You get a timeline with every quote. As a rough guide, from the point content and access are ready:

  • A landing page: 3–5 working days.
  • A business website: a few weeks, depending on the number of pages.
  • A WooCommerce store: usually 2–4 weeks.
  • Speed optimization: usually 2–4 working days.
  • A hacked site: most cleanups within 24–48 hours of getting access.

What stretches these: content that is not ready, slow or scattered feedback, custom features such as bookings or a second language, waiting for payment gateway approval, and hosting access that takes days to arrive.

How to prepare

  • What the website or the fix needs to achieve: more enquiries, online sales or a faster site.
  • Two or three websites you like, and what you like about them.
  • Your WordPress, hosting and domain logins, or the name of whoever holds them.
  • Where your content stands: ready, half-written or not started.
  • Who makes the final decision, and any fixed deadline such as a launch or a campaign.

What you have when the work is done

  • A site on hosting in your name, with your own administrator login.
  • A video-call walkthrough, so you can edit text, images and pages yourself.
  • A full backup taken at launch.
  • Any temporary access I used removed, unless you keep me on for maintenance.

Can you join an ongoing project?

Yes. I can take over or support existing WordPress sites after a quick review of the setup.

Can I hire you for a small job, or only for full websites?

Small jobs are welcome: a broken layout, a slow page, a form that stopped sending or a few content changes. Send the list, and you get a fixed price for the batch before I start.

Will I be working with you or with a team?
