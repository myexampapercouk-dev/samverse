Building an interior designer website, in detail

An interior design website is judged in the first few photos. This is how I plan and build sites for interior designers, architects and design-and-build studios. For a deeper planning read, see websites for interior designers and architects.

Must-have features

  • Project pages, not one gallery. Each project gets its own page: space type, city, the brief in two or three lines, what you changed, and 10 to 30 photos in a logical order (entrance, living, kitchen, bedrooms).
  • Portfolio filters. Residential, commercial, hospitality, or by room type, so a client looking for an office fit-out does not scroll through bedrooms.
  • Fast images. WebP conversion, responsive sizes and lazy loading. Image weight is the main reason studio sites feel slow.
  • A process page. Consultation, concept, 3D views, execution, handover. Clients want to know what happens and when they pay.
  • A qualifying enquiry form. Property type, approximate area, city, timeline and an optional budget band.

Pages list

  1. Home: a few hero projects, services, process and a clear enquiry button.
  2. Portfolio, with filters, and a page for each project.
  3. Services: residential interiors, commercial, turnkey, consultation only.
  4. About the studio, with team and approach.
  5. Process and FAQs.
  6. Contact, with map, studio timings and appointment request.

Integrations

  • WhatsApp with a pre-filled message that names the page the visitor came from.
  • Google Maps embed and a link to your Google Business Profile.
  • Appointment requests for a site visit or studio consultation, through a form or a booking calendar.
  • Instagram link or feed for work in progress.

Content checklist

  • Professional photos of 4 to 8 completed projects (more can be added later).
  • For each project: location, space type, size if you want to share it, and a few lines on the brief.
  • Your services list, process stages and studio photos.
  • Logo, contact numbers and the studio address.

What is not included

Photography, 3D rendering and paid ads are separate. I can suggest what to shoot so the photos work on the site. Send me your current site or Instagram for a free review and a fixed quote.

FAQ

Common questions

Can I add new projects myself?

Yes. Each project uses the same template: title, location, space type, a short story and the photos. Your team fills it in from the WordPress dashboard and the portfolio and filters update on their own.

Will lots of high-resolution photos make the site slow?

Not if they are handled properly. I convert uploads to WebP, serve smaller sizes to phones and lazy-load images below the first screen. Upload the best originals you have and the site does the resizing.

Should I show prices or budgets?

That is your call. Many studios show a starting range or ask for a budget band in the enquiry form instead. I do not put any figure on the site that you have not given me.

Can the site link to my Instagram and Houzz profiles?

Yes. Instagram can appear as a feed or a link, and directory profiles can be linked from the footer. Your website stays the place where the full project story lives.

Do architects need anything different?

Usually a stronger emphasis on drawings, process stages and project data such as built-up area. The same project template can carry plans and elevations alongside photos.

How long does it take?

Usually 1 to 3 weeks once I have photos and short notes for your first projects. The number of projects you launch with is the main factor.
