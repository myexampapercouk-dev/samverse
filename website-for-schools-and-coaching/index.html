Building a school or coaching institute website, in detail

Admission decisions start with a phone search, often by a parent comparing three or four institutes in one evening. This is how I plan and build websites for schools, junior colleges and coaching institutes: the admission flow, course and batch pages, honest results, notices, photo consent and local search. What parents and students look for covers the visitor's side.

An education website project, step by step

  1. Free review and a fixed quote. Send your current site or prospectus, your courses or classes, branches and the admission dates you are working towards.
  2. Page plan. We agree the courses, admission steps, results format and which pages need Hindi or a regional language.
  3. Content and consent. Course details, faculty profiles, results and photos, with written consent collected for every student shown.
  4. Build on staging. Course templates, the enquiry form, notice board, events and galleries, all editable by your staff.
  5. Testing. Test enquiries sent to each branch, pages checked on budget phones, and Search Console connected.
  6. Launch and staff training. A video call showing your office team how to post notices, results and gallery albums.

Admissions and enquiry flows

An admissions page should answer everything a parent would otherwise phone about: eligibility, the steps in order, documents needed and key dates, with dates your staff can change in one place. The enquiry form then asks only what your counsellor needs before calling:

  • Student name, class or course of interest, and the parent's phone number.
  • The preferred branch, so the enquiry reaches the right office.
  • A "how did you hear about us?" question. Studio Agama Interiors uses one on its enquiry form, which shows which channels bring enquiries.

Each enquiry is emailed instantly, can open a WhatsApp chat, and can also go to a Google Sheet or CRM; multi-step forms help when you need more detail without a long form. For demo classes, a booking form with a date and session dropdown works well: Dr. Nitish Gupta's clinic site asks patients for a preferred date and time slot in the same way. During admission season, paid campaigns deserve their own landing pages.

Course, batch and fee pages

Give each course or class group its own page with the syllabus covered, duration, batch timings, mode (classroom, online or hybrid), medium of instruction, batch size and the faculty who teach it. Batches change often, so they sit in a simple table staff can edit. Dr. Sudhir Arora's course page is a useful pattern from another field: it says group sessions run in Hindi on Saturday mornings and in English on Sunday afternoons, and states that there is a one-time enrolment fee with no monthly or hidden charges, answering the money question before anyone asks.

Whether you publish fee amounts is your decision. Either way, explain the fee structure: what is included, whether instalments are possible and your refund policy, and say clearly if figures are shared on enquiry.

Results and toppers, handled honestly

Results persuade parents more than anything else, which is why inflated claims backfire when someone checks. On the sites I build, results pages follow a few rules:

  • Each result names the exam, the year and the course the student actually took with you, such as a full classroom programme or a short test series.
  • Names and photos appear only with the student's written consent, and a parent's for minors.
  • Only your own students, and percentages only when you can show how they were calculated.

India's consumer protection authority has issued guidelines on coaching advertisements that cover consent and disclosure of the course taken, so check the current version with your adviser.

Notices, events and galleries

The notice board is set up as its own section with categories such as circulars, holidays and exam schedules, so old notices move to an archive instead of cluttering the homepage. Circulars can be attached as PDFs, with the key points also typed on the page so they read well on phones. Events get a calendar, and each event gets its own gallery album with photos compressed so the page loads on mobile data; preparing photos for a website explains how.

Photo consent for children

Children's photos need more care than any other content on the site. I recommend a photo consent line in your admission form, with an option to opt out, and a register of students who must not appear. Avoid pairing a child's full name with their photo, choose group shots over close-ups, and remove any photo quickly when a parent asks. India's data protection law expects verifiable parental consent for children's personal data; the DPDP Act and your website explains the basics in plain English.

Local SEO for coaching institutes

Students search by exam and area, such as "NEET coaching in Kothrud". Each branch gets its own page with its address, batches, faculty and directions, linked to its own Google Business Profile. Branch pages must be genuinely different, not copies with the area name swapped; see local landing pages without doorway pages. Reviews come from real students and parents you ask, never written for you. For ongoing search work, see WordPress SEO services.

Hindi and regional language options

Many parents read Hindi, Marathi or another regional language more comfortably than English. You do not need to translate everything: admissions, fees, contact and notices are usually enough to start. Each language gets its own URLs and fonts tested for Devanagari or the relevant script. Our Temples shows temple names and sections such as history and festivals in Hindi and Telugu as well as English. Multilingual WordPress websites covers the setup.

How long it takes

A coaching institute or small school site usually takes 1 to 2 weeks once content is ready. A larger school or a multi-branch institute with online payments takes 2 to 4 weeks, and Hindi or regional versions or an LMS add more. Collecting consent and results often takes longer than the build, so start well before admission season.

FAQ

Common questions

Can we post notices and results ourselves?

Yes. Staff can post notices, events and results from the dashboard without any technical knowledge.

Can students apply or enquire online?

Yes. I set up admission and course enquiry forms that email you instantly, with WhatsApp follow-up.

Can you add online fee payment?

Yes. I can integrate a payment gateway such as Razorpay so fees can be paid online.

Can the site support online classes or course sales?

Yes. WordPress LMS plugins can handle video courses, quizzes and paid enrolments if you need them.

Should we show our fees on the website?

It is your decision. Some institutes publish the full fee structure, others explain what the fee covers and share figures on enquiry. Either way, say what is included, whether instalments are possible and what your refund policy is, so parents are not surprised later.

Can we publish toppers' names and photos?

Yes, with written consent from the student, and from a parent for minors. Each result should name the exam, the year and the course the student took with you. Only your own students should appear, and anything you cannot back up should be left out.

Can each branch have its own page?

Yes. Each branch page gets its own address, batches, faculty, timings and map, linked to that branch's Google Business Profile, and enquiries from it can go straight to that branch's office.

Can the website replace our WhatsApp groups for notices?

It works best alongside them. The website keeps a permanent, searchable record of every circular and holiday, and staff can share the notice link in WhatsApp groups, so parents who join later can still find older notices.
