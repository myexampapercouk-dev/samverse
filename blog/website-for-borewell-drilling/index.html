Websites for Borewell Drilling and Water Well Services

What borewell drilling and water well businesses need online: clear services, areas served, an honest survey process, real equipment and careful permit wording.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Give each service its own page and explain how charges are worked out, even if you don't publish exact rates.
  • Describe your survey method, process and real equipment honestly, and never promise that a borewell will find water.
  • Word permit information carefully, make calls and WhatsApp location sharing effortless, and list only the areas you actually serve.

People look for a borewell contractor at important moments: a new house is being built, a farm needs water before the season, a factory's supply is running short, or an existing borewell has dried up or its pump has failed. Most of them search on a phone, often from the site itself, and call whoever looks local, experienced and straightforward. Your website should answer their first questions quickly and make it easy to call, send a WhatsApp message or book a site visit.

What customers want to know before they call

  • Do you work in my area? Village, taluka, town or district, not just "all over the state"
  • Which work do you do? New drilling, flushing, pump installation, repairs or recharge structures
  • How are charges worked out? Often per foot of drilling, plus casing, the pump and other extras
  • When can the rig come, and how long will the job take?
  • Will we definitely get water? An honest answer here builds more trust than a confident promise

You don't have to publish exact rates, especially when they vary with depth and rock conditions, but explaining how pricing works saves time on every call. See whether to show prices on your website.

Service pages that match real jobs

Give each main service its own page instead of one long list. Typical pages include:

ServiceWhat the page should explain
New borewell drillingResidential, agricultural and commercial work, the diameters you drill, casing options and what the quote includes
Flushing, cleaning and deepeningWhen these can help a weak borewell, and when they are unlikely to
Submersible pump installation and repairPump types and brands you work with, starters and control panels, lowering and lifting pumps
Site survey and point selectionThe method you actually use, and what it can and cannot tell you
Borewell recharge and rainwater harvestingRecharge pits and structures you build, and the space they need
Capping old borewellsSafely sealing failed or unused borewells

That last service matters. Open, abandoned borewells are a serious danger to children and animals, and courts and authorities have issued safety guidelines about them. A page explaining how you cap and seal old bores is useful, responsible and a good reason for people to call.

Explain your process and site survey

Many customers have never had a borewell drilled before. A simple process page eases their worries:

  1. Call or WhatsApp with a location pin and the type of property
  2. Site visit and survey to choose a drilling point and check rig access
  3. Quotation explaining the per-foot rate, casing, pump and any extras
  4. Permissions check, so the customer knows what may be needed before work starts
  5. Drilling and casing, with an idea of how long the rig will be on site
  6. Yield check and pump installation
  7. Handover with a written record of depth, casing length and the water observed

Describe your survey method honestly. Whether you use geophysical equipment, local experience or both, say what it is and don't suggest that any method guarantees water. Groundwater depends on local geology, so publish a clear policy on what happens if a bore is dry or low-yielding, including how charges are worked out. It prevents disputes later.

A short "before the rig arrives" checklist also helps: clear access for the rig and support vehicle, enough space to work, a power supply for the pump, and a word with the neighbours about noise.

Show the equipment you actually have

Customers and builders judge your capability by your machines. Show:

  • Your own rigs and compressors, photographed on real sites, ideally with your company name visible
  • The rig type, and the depth and diameter range it can genuinely handle
  • Support equipment such as pump-lifting gear or a borewell inspection camera, only if you own it

Avoid manufacturer brochure photos or stock images of rigs that aren't yours. If you work with partner rig owners for some jobs, say so plainly. Customers notice when the machine that turns up doesn't match the website.

Permits and rules: choose your words carefully

Groundwater rules in India vary by state and by area. Depending on the location and what the water will be used for, a new borewell may need registration or permission, some areas restrict new drilling, and some states require drilling agencies or rigs to be registered. Rules also change, so your website should guide people without overpromising.

  • Write "we can guide you on the permissions usually needed in our area" rather than "no permission needed" or "we handle all approvals"
  • If your agency or rig is registered with a state authority, show the registration details
  • Avoid "100% water guarantee" or "success assured" claims
  • Advise customers to check current requirements with the groundwater department or local authority

This is general information, not legal advice. If you're unsure what applies in your area, ask a local consultant or the authority itself.

Make calls and WhatsApp effortless

Most borewell enquiries arrive by phone, so make contact the easiest thing on every page:

  • A tap-to-call button that stays visible on mobile
  • A WhatsApp button with a pre-filled message, inviting people to share their location pin and a photo of the site or the existing borewell
  • A short form for those who prefer it: location, property type, new borewell or repair, depth of nearby borewells if known, and a preferred visit date
  • A callback promise you can keep, because your team is often busy at a rig when the phone rings

Many customers are in rural areas or are more comfortable in their own language, so consider Hindi or regional-language pages; see multilingual WordPress websites.

Areas served, local SEO and speed

Customers search for things like "borewell drilling near me", "borewell contractor in {district}" or "submersible pump repair {town}". To show up for them:

  • Set up your Google Business Profile as a service-area business, listing the districts and towns you cover; see SEO for service-area businesses
  • List your service areas clearly on the website, and mention any travel charges for distant sites
  • Create area pages only where you have genuinely worked, with local job photos and useful notes, rather than copies with the town name swapped
  • Ask satisfied customers for Google reviews that mention the type of work you did

Keep pages light. Customers often open your site on patchy mobile data in the field, so compress photos and avoid heavy sliders; see website speed on Indian mobile networks.
