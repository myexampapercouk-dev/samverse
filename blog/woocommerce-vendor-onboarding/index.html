WooCommerce Vendor Onboarding: A Step-by-Step Seller Onboarding Process for Multi-Vendor Stores

How to set up WooCommerce vendor onboarding: registration forms, KYC and GST checks, approval, store setup, payouts and the seller onboarding process.

Key takeaways
  • Good vendor onboarding is a fixed sequence: application, verification, approval, store setup, first product review and payout setup.
  • Collect GST, PAN and bank details at registration, but keep the first form short and ask for documents after basic approval.
  • Review each new seller's first products manually and send a welcome checklist so vendors go live correctly the first time.

WooCommerce vendor onboarding is the process a new seller goes through on your multi-vendor store, from filling in a registration form to making their first sale. In WooCommerce it is handled by a marketplace plugin such as Dokan, WCFM or WC Vendors, plus the rules you set for verification, approval, store setup and payouts.

The plugin gives you the screens. The process is up to you, and a weak one leads to half-finished shops, wrong product listings and payout disputes. Below is a seller onboarding process that works for Indian marketplaces, step by step.

What vendor onboarding needs to achieve

A good onboarding flow does four things:

  • Filters out sellers you do not want before they can list anything.
  • Collects the legal and payout details you need, such as GST, PAN and bank account, once and correctly.
  • Gets approved sellers to a live, compliant shop quickly, because sellers who stall in setup rarely come back.
  • Sets expectations about commission, shipping, returns and response times before the first order, not after a dispute.

If you are still choosing the platform, start with our guide to building a WooCommerce multi-vendor marketplace, which compares the main plugins.

The WooCommerce seller onboarding process, step by step

Step 1: Vendor registration form

Most marketplace plugins add a "become a vendor" option to the My Account registration form or give you a separate vendor registration page. Keep this first form short: name, business or shop name, email, mobile number, product category and city. A long form with document uploads at this stage scares away genuine sellers who simply want to know whether they qualify.

Step 2: Application review and approval

Set new vendor accounts to "pending" rather than auto-approved. In Dokan this is a setting under Selling Options; WCFM and WC Vendors have similar options. Review each application against simple criteria: do they sell in categories you allow, do they look like a real business, can they ship to your service areas? Send an approval or rejection email either way.

Step 3: KYC and business verification

After approval, ask for verification documents through the vendor dashboard. For an Indian marketplace this typically means:

  • GSTIN (mandatory for most sellers on an e-commerce operator; check with your CA for exemptions).
  • PAN of the business or proprietor.
  • Bank account details with a cancelled cheque or bank statement.
  • Pickup address for shipping.
  • Any category licences, for example FSSAI for food.

Some plugins include verification modules; otherwise a custom field set or a form plugin with file upload does the job. Store documents securely and limit who can see them.

Step 4: Store setup

The vendor now sets up their shop page: logo, banner, store name, description, policies and shipping settings. Give them a setup wizard if your plugin has one, and a written checklist either way. Decide which settings vendors control and which you lock, such as commission rate, allowed shipping methods and product types.

Step 5: First product review

Set new products to "pending review" for new vendors. Check titles, images, category, attributes, price, tax class and stock before approval. Variable products need extra care, since wrong attributes cause messy filters; see our guide to WooCommerce product variations. After a vendor has a few clean listings, you can let their products publish directly.

Step 6: Payout and commission setup

Confirm how and when vendors get paid: commission percentage or fixed fee, payout schedule, minimum withdrawal, and how refunds and returns are deducted. In India, split payments through a gateway such as Razorpay Route or Cashfree are common for automating this. Our comparison of payment gateways in India covers which ones support marketplaces.

Step 7: Go live and first order follow-up

When the shop is approved, send a go-live email with links to the vendor dashboard, order handling steps and support contact. Watch the vendor's first few orders closely: shipping times, packaging complaints and cancellations show early whether the seller will work out.

GST and compliance points to settle early

When you run a marketplace, you are an e-commerce operator under Indian GST law, which brings obligations such as TCS collection on vendor sales. Settle with your CA before launch who issues invoices (usually the vendor, under their GSTIN), how TCS is deducted from payouts, and what reports you need each month. Your onboarding must collect the data those reports need. Our post on WooCommerce GST invoices in India explains the invoicing side.

Common vendor onboarding mistakes

  • Auto-approving everyone, which fills the marketplace with duplicate, low-quality or prohibited listings.
  • Asking for every document upfront, so genuine sellers abandon the form.
  • No product review for new sellers, leading to wrong categories, missing tax classes and poor images.
  • Unclear commission and return rules, which become disputes after the first refund.
  • Giving vendors too much control, for example letting them change shipping rules that break checkout.
  • Plugin overload. Stacking add-ons for every onboarding feature slows the vendor dashboard; see WooCommerce speed optimisation.

A vendor welcome checklist you can copy

Send this list with the approval email, or pin it on the vendor dashboard. It cuts the number of support questions and gets shops live faster.

  1. Upload your GST certificate, PAN and bank proof in the Verification section.
  2. Add your shop logo (square), banner and a short description of what you sell.
  3. Fill in your pickup address and confirm the pincodes you can ship to.
  4. Read the commission, payout and returns policy and accept it in the dashboard.
  5. Add your first three products using the product template: clear title, at least three photos on a plain background, correct category, price inclusive of GST and accurate stock.
  6. Wait for product approval; we check new sellers' first listings before they go live.
  7. Place a test order on your own shop if we ask you to, so you see how notifications arrive.
  8. Keep your mobile number active for order alerts and respond to orders within the agreed time.

A checklist like this also gives your own team a standard to review against. When a vendor emails to ask why their shop is not live yet, you can point to the exact step that is still incomplete instead of investigating from scratch every time. Track each vendor's progress in a simple sheet or a custom admin column so stalled applications get a reminder.

Not every multi-supplier store needs vendor accounts. If you keep control of listings and pricing, see WooCommerce with multiple suppliers for a simpler setup.

Plugin or custom onboarding?

For most new marketplaces, the built-in onboarding of Dokan, WCFM or WC Vendors plus a few custom fields and emails is enough. Custom development makes sense when you need document checks against an external API, a multi-stage approval with different staff roles, or onboarding that pulls data into your ERP or CRM. Start simple, run the process with your first twenty or thirty sellers, and automate the steps that actually take your time.

FAQs

What is WooCommerce vendor onboarding?

It is the process that takes a new seller on a WooCommerce multi-vendor store from registration through verification, approval, store setup and product review to their first sale, usually run through a marketplace plugin.

What is a good seller onboarding process for a marketplace?

Short registration, manual application review, KYC and GST verification, guided store setup, review of the first products, clear payout and commission setup, then a go-live email and close watch on early orders.

Which plugin is best for WooCommerce vendor registration?

Dokan, WCFM Marketplace and WC Vendors all include vendor registration and approval. The best choice depends on the dashboard your sellers prefer, the payout options you need and how much customisation you plan.

Should I manually approve new vendors?

Yes, at least at the start. Manual approval of vendors and their first products keeps quality high and stops prohibited or duplicate listings.

What documents should I collect from vendors in India?

Typically GSTIN, PAN, bank account proof, pickup address and any category licence such as FSSAI. Confirm the exact list with your CA based on your categories.

How do vendors get paid in a WooCommerce marketplace?

Either you collect payments and pay vendors on a schedule after deducting commission, or a split-payment gateway sends each vendor their share automatically. Refunds and TCS deductions should be part of the payout rules from day one.
