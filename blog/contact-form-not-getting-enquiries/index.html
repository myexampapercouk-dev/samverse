Why Your Website Contact Form Isn't Getting Enquiries: Broken, Blocked or Ignored?

Contact form not getting enquiries? Find out if it is broken, blocked by spam filters or ignored by visitors, then fix email delivery, spam checks and design.

Key takeaways
  • Submit your own form today and check the email arrives; many forms fail silently because WordPress sends unauthenticated email that lands in spam or nowhere.
  • Send form emails through SMTP from an address on your own domain, keep SPF and DKIM in place, and store every submission so nothing depends on email alone.
  • If the form works but nobody uses it, shorten it, put it where visitors decide, offer WhatsApp and phone alongside, and show proof and a reply time next to it.

When a website contact form isn't getting enquiries, it's one of three problems: the form is broken (submissions fail or the emails never arrive), it's blocked (spam protection or filters stop real people or their messages), or it's ignored (visitors don't trust it, can't find it or find it too much effort). Start by submitting the form yourself and checking the email arrives in the right inbox. That one test tells you which problem you have.

A form that silently stops sending is one of the most expensive problems a business website can have, because nothing looks wrong. When I build a site, every form is tested to make sure the emails arrive before launch; see WordPress website development. If you're sending ad traffic to a page and want a form built to convert, see landing page design.

First: test your form properly

  1. Open your site in a private window on your phone, using mobile data.
  2. Fill in the form as a customer would, with a personal email address that isn't the one receiving enquiries.
  3. Check whether a success message or thank-you page appears.
  4. Check the receiving inbox, including spam, promotions and any filters, within a few minutes.
  5. Check your form plugin's stored entries, if it keeps them, to see whether the submission was recorded.

Submissions failing: errors, spinners and dead buttons

  • A plugin or theme update broke it. Check whether the problem started after an update, and test with other plugins switched off on a staging copy.
  • JavaScript errors. Open the browser console (F12) while submitting. A script delayed or combined by an optimisation plugin often stops the form's own scripts from running.
  • Page caching and security tokens. Some form plugins add a security token to the page that expires after a while. If your cache serves an old copy for days, submissions can fail with a vague error. Exclude the contact page from the cache or shorten the cache lifetime.
  • A firewall blocking the request. A security plugin, host firewall or Cloudflare rule can block form submissions, especially messages that contain links. Check its logs.
  • Broken spam protection. A reCAPTCHA key registered for another domain, or v2 keys where v3 is expected, stops every submission; see reCAPTCHA not working on WordPress.

Emails not arriving, or landing in spam

This is where most broken forms fail. By default, WordPress sends email through the server's PHP mail function, with no authentication. Gmail, Outlook and Zoho treat that kind of mail with suspicion, so it goes to spam or is quietly rejected.

Send through SMTP

Install an SMTP plugin such as WP Mail SMTP, FluentSMTP or Post SMTP and connect it to a proper mailbox or sending service: Google Workspace, Zoho Mail, your host's email, or a transactional service such as Brevo. Then send a test email from the plugin's tools page. WordPress not sending emails? Fix it with SMTP walks through the setup.

Fix the From address

Many forms are set to send "from" the visitor's own email address. That looks logical, but it fails email authentication, because your server isn't allowed to send as gmail.com or someone else's company domain. Send from an address on your own domain, such as enquiries@yourdomain.com, and put the visitor's email in Reply-To, so clicking reply still reaches them. In Contact Form 7, that means the Mail tab's From field and a Reply-To line under Additional headers:

From: [_site_title] <enquiries@yourdomain.com>
Additional headers: Reply-To: [your-email]

Check SPF, DKIM and DMARC

Your domain's DNS should have an SPF record that includes whoever sends your mail, DKIM signing for that sender, and a DMARC record on top. Without them, more of your messages land in spam. See SPF, DKIM and DMARC explained.

Check where it's going

  • Is the recipient address current? Forms often still send to an ex-employee, a previous developer or an address at a domain that has lapsed.
  • Filters and forwarding: check for Gmail filters or forwarding rules that archive or redirect the messages.
  • Email logging: FluentSMTP logs every email it sends, and WP Mail SMTP offers logging on its paid plans at the time of writing, so you can see whether WordPress sent each message.

Store every submission

Don't let email be the only copy. Most form plugins can save entries in the dashboard: Contact Form 7 with the Flamingo plugin, Elementor Pro's Collect Submissions action, and the entries screens in Fluent Forms, Gravity Forms and paid WPForms plans. A connection to a Google Sheet or your CRM adds a second copy.

Spam protection, mobile and validation problems

When the form works for you but customers say it failed, something is stopping real people:

  • Puzzle CAPTCHAs that ask visitors to pick out traffic lights put some genuine people off, especially on phones.
  • reCAPTCHA v3 scores set too strictly can reject real visitors as bots, and on some cookie consent setups the script doesn't load for visitors who decline cookies.
  • Keyword and link filters in anti-spam plugins sometimes flag ordinary messages.
  • Phone validation that rejects +91, spaces or a leading zero. Accept the formats Indian visitors actually type, and use a phone field so mobiles show the number keypad.
  • A hidden submit button behind a cookie banner, chat widget or WhatsApp button on small screens.
  • Error messages that only appear at the top of a long form, so visitors never see what went wrong.
  • Required fields people can't answer, such as a company name for a household customer or a budget they haven't decided yet.

Lighter spam protection usually works better: a hidden honeypot field, a time check that drops anything submitted within a few seconds of the page loading, and Cloudflare Turnstile, which is free and usually invisible to visitors. The contact form on this site uses a honeypot, a simple addition question and a three-second time check, with no reCAPTCHA at all. How to stop contact form spam covers each method.

Why people don't fill it in

If the form works perfectly and enquiries are still low, the problem is persuasion:

  • Too many fields. Ask only what you need to reply: often a name, phone or email and a short message. A dropdown for the service, like the one on the Vansh Group site I built, gives you context without extra typing, so enquiries arrive pre-qualified.
  • Hard to find. If the form lives only on the contact page, many visitors never see it. Put a short form or a clear button on every service page and at the end of key articles.
  • No reason to trust you. Testimonials, client logos, a real address and a clear promise such as "we reply within one working day" next to the form make people comfortable sharing their details.
  • No alternative. Many Indian customers would rather WhatsApp or call. Offer both next to the form, with tap-to-call on mobile.
  • A vague button. "Submit" says nothing. "Get my quote" or "Request a call back" says what happens next.

The page around the form matters as much as the form itself: designing a contact page that gets enquiries covers layout, and ways to get more enquiries from your website covers the rest of the site.

Make sure you never miss an enquiry again

  • Send each enquiry to email and a second place, such as a Google Sheet, your CRM or WhatsApp.
  • Show a clear thank-you message or page, so people know it worked and you can track conversions.
  • Track successful submissions as a key event in Google Analytics, so a sudden drop to zero is obvious.
  • Test the form after every plugin, theme or WordPress update, and once a month regardless.
  • Reply quickly: the first business to respond often wins the job.

The form on this site is built around that idea: a serverless function emails each enquiry, then the browser opens WhatsApp with the same details already typed in, so if the email step ever fails, the enquiry still reaches me.

And confirm every submission properly with a thank-you page.

FAQs

Why am I not receiving emails from my website contact form?

Usually because WordPress is sending through the server's unauthenticated mail function, so messages are rejected or filtered as spam. Set up SMTP with a mailbox on your domain, send from your own address with the visitor in Reply-To, and check your SPF and DKIM records.

Why does my form say "message sent" but no email arrives?

The form worked; the email delivery didn't. Check the spam folder and the recipient address, look at the plugin's stored entries to confirm the submission, and set up SMTP with email logging to see what happens to each message.

Why do my contact form emails go to spam?

Most often the From address is the visitor's email, or the mail isn't authenticated with SPF and DKIM. Send from an address on your own domain through SMTP, and add the visitor's email as Reply-To.

How do I test if my contact form is working?

Submit it yourself from a phone on mobile data, using an email address other than the one that receives enquiries, then check the inbox, the spam folder and the form's stored entries. Repeat after every update and at least once a month.

Does reCAPTCHA stop real people from sending enquiries?

It can. Puzzle challenges put some visitors off, strict v3 scores can reject real people, and the script may not load for visitors who decline cookies on some setups. A honeypot, a time check or Cloudflare Turnstile is often enough.

My form works, so why is my website not getting enquiries?

Then visitors aren't being persuaded, or there aren't enough of the right ones. Check your traffic in Analytics first, then shorten the form, add WhatsApp and phone, show proof and a reply time near it, and put calls to action on every service page.
