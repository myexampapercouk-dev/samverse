WooCommerce Inventory and Stock Management: Settings, Variations, Alerts and Syncing With Tally or a Shop

WooCommerce stock management explained: inventory settings, variation stock, low-stock alerts, backorders, ATUM and syncing with Tally or marketplaces.

Key takeaways
  • WooCommerce has solid built-in inventory management: turn it on under Settings, Products, Inventory, then set quantity, SKU and thresholds on each product or variation.
  • Most stock problems come from setup: manage stock left unticked on variations, orders stuck in pending payment, or backorders allowed by accident.
  • If you also sell in a shop, on marketplaces or keep stock in Tally, pick one system as the master, use identical SKUs everywhere and keep a small buffer for sync delays.

WooCommerce has inventory management built in. Turn on stock management under WooCommerce, Settings, Products, Inventory, then give each product or variation a SKU and a stock quantity, and set your low-stock and out-of-stock thresholds, backorder rules and notification emails. For a store that sells only on its website, that's usually all you need. If you also sell from a shop, on marketplaces or keep stock in Tally, you need a sync routine or tool so the website never sells something you don't have.

Order and stock management is part of every store I set up as a WooCommerce developer, along with importing your existing product list from a spreadsheet. This guide shows you how to set it up yourself and how to fix the problems that cause overselling.

Menu names below match recent WooCommerce versions; WooCommerce is moving to a new product editor, so some screens may look different on your store.

Step 1: Turn on stock management for the store

Go to WooCommerce, Settings, Products, Inventory. These settings apply store-wide:

SettingWhat it doesSensible starting point
Manage stockTurns stock tracking on for the storeOn
Hold stock (minutes)Reserves stock for unpaid orders, then cancels themLong enough for a customer to finish a UPI or card payment
Notifications and recipientEmails when stock is low or runs outOn, sent to whoever reorders stock
Low stock thresholdThe level that triggers the low-stock emailYour reorder point, overridden per product where needed
Out of stock thresholdThe level at which a product is marked out of stock0, or a small buffer if you also sell elsewhere
Out of stock visibilityHides out-of-stock products from shop and category pagesOff if items come back; on if they clutter browsing
Stock display formatShows "12 in stock", "Only 2 left" or nothingOnly show when low

The out-of-stock threshold is underused. If you keep a few units for the shop counter or to cover damaged items, set it to 2 or 3, and the website stops selling before you actually run out.

Step 2: Set stock on each product

Open a product and go to Product data, Inventory:

  • SKU: a unique code for every product and variation, such as KURTA-BLU-M. Use the same SKU in Tally, on marketplaces and in your shop, because imports and sync tools match products by SKU.
  • Manage stock: tick it to track a quantity. Leave it unticked for made-to-order items and set the stock status by hand instead.
  • Stock quantity: what you physically have available to sell online.
  • Allow backorders: no, allow but notify the customer, or allow.
  • Low stock threshold: overrides the store-wide level for fast or slow sellers.

For quick changes, use Quick Edit or Bulk Edit on the Products list, which shows a stock column. For hundreds of products, export a CSV, update the quantities in a spreadsheet and import it back with the "Update existing products" option ticked.

Managing stock for variations (sizes and colours)

Variable products can track stock in two ways:

  • Per variation: tick Manage stock inside each variation and enter its own quantity. This is right for clothing, shoes and anything where size M can sell out while size L doesn't.
  • Shared at product level: tick Manage stock on the parent product's Inventory tab and leave it unticked on the variations, and they all draw from one pool. This suits options that don't use separate stock, such as a gift wrap choice.

The variations screen has bulk actions for setting stock and toggling stock management across all variations at once, which saves a lot of clicking. A common mistake is a variable product where stock is managed on a few variations but not the rest, so some sizes never run out. More on setting variations up in WooCommerce product variations.

How orders change stock

Knowing when WooCommerce reduces and restores stock explains most "my stock is wrong" problems. In current versions, it typically works like this, though some payment gateway plugins behave differently:

Order statusEffect on stock
Pending paymentHeld for the hold-stock time, then the order is cancelled and stock released
On hold (for example, bank transfer awaiting payment)Reduced
Processing (paid, or cash on delivery)Reduced
CompletedAlready reduced, no further change
CancelledRestored
RefundedRestored only if you tick the option to restock refunded items

If paid orders stay in Pending payment, stock is released when the hold time runs out, and you can oversell. That usually means the payment gateway's webhook isn't reaching your site; see fixing WooCommerce payment gateway problems in India.

Low-stock alerts and the stock report

  • Low-stock and out-of-stock emails go to the notification recipient in the inventory settings. Use an address someone actually reads, not the developer's.
  • WooCommerce, Analytics, Stock lists every product with filters for low stock, out of stock and on backorder. A weekly look at it is a simple reorder routine.
  • The WooCommerce mobile app shows orders and product stock on your phone.
  • Once a month, count what's on the shelf and compare it with the website. Small differences from damaged items, returns and shop sales add up.

Backorders and pre-orders

Allowing backorders lets customers order when stock is at zero, and the quantity goes negative. It suits made-to-order products and items you can restock quickly. Choose "Allow, but notify customer" so the product page and cart say the item is on backorder, and state a realistic dispatch time. If you're taking orders before stock arrives, see pre-orders and backorders in WooCommerce for payment timing and honest dates.

Out of stock: hide, show or redirect?

  • Show it if it's coming back, with a "notify me when back in stock" form from a back-in-stock plugin. The page keeps its search rankings and captures demand.
  • Hide it from category pages if out-of-stock items clutter browsing, but keep the product page live.
  • Redirect it with a 301 to the closest replacement or its category if it's discontinued for good.

Keeping stock in sync with a shop, Tally or marketplaces

WooCommerce can only stop overselling if its numbers are right. When stock also sells elsewhere, decide which system is the master, use identical SKUs everywhere and keep a buffer with the out-of-stock threshold to absorb sync delays.

A physical shop

A POS system that connects to WooCommerce updates stock as you sell at the counter. For low volumes, a daily routine works: note counter sales and update WooCommerce each evening with Bulk Edit or a CSV import.

Tally

Many Indian businesses keep stock and GST billing in Tally. Connector tools from several vendors sync Tally and WooCommerce, typically pushing stock to the website and pulling orders back for invoicing. Without one, export stock from Tally to a spreadsheet, match it to WooCommerce by SKU and import it with "Update existing products". Agree how often this happens, and who does it. For tax and invoices on the WooCommerce side, see GST invoices in WooCommerce.

Amazon, Flipkart and other marketplaces

Multi-channel tools such as Unicommerce, EasyEcom or Browntape, or a marketplace's own connector, sync one stock figure across your website and marketplaces. Check features and pricing with each vendor. The trade-offs of selling in both places are in own website vs marketplaces.

When an inventory plugin like ATUM helps

The built-in tools track quantities, not purchasing. ATUM Inventory Management for WooCommerce adds a Stock Central screen to view and edit stock for all products in one table, plus suppliers, purchase orders, inbound stock and stock logs in its free version at the time of writing. Paid add-ons cover things like multiple warehouses and products made from components. It's worth it if you reorder from suppliers regularly or stock several locations; a store with a few dozen products rarely needs it.

Common stock problems and fixes

  • Stock doesn't reduce after orders: Manage stock isn't ticked on the product or variation, or orders are stuck in Pending payment.
  • Stock went negative: backorders were allowed, often through a bulk edit or import.
  • Overselling during a sale: sync delays with other channels, or a hold-stock time shorter than slow payments. Raise the out-of-stock threshold for the sale.
  • Refunded items not back in stock: the restock option wasn't ticked; adjust the quantity by hand.

A clear daily routine for orders helps keep stock right too; see a WooCommerce order management workflow.

FAQs

How do I manage stock in WooCommerce?

Turn on Manage stock under WooCommerce, Settings, Products, Inventory, then open each product, tick Manage stock on its Inventory tab and enter the quantity and a SKU. Set the low-stock threshold and notification email so you know when to reorder.

How do I set stock for each variation in WooCommerce?

Open the product's Variations tab, expand each variation, tick Manage stock and enter its quantity. Use the bulk actions menu to set stock for all variations at once. Leave the parent product's Manage stock unticked unless the variations should share one pool.

Why is my WooCommerce stock not reducing?

Usually Manage stock isn't ticked on that product or variation, or orders are stuck in Pending payment because the payment gateway isn't confirming them. Check the order status of recent orders first.

Can WooCommerce sync stock with Tally?

Not out of the box. Third-party connectors can sync stock and orders between Tally and WooCommerce, or you can export stock from Tally and import it into WooCommerce by SKU on a regular schedule.

Do I need an inventory plugin like ATUM?

Only if you manage suppliers, purchase orders or several warehouses. For most small stores, WooCommerce's built-in stock management, the Stock report and low-stock emails are enough.
