WooCommerce Shipping Setup for India: Zones, Weight-Based Rates, Free Shipping, COD and Courier Integrations

Set up WooCommerce shipping for India: zones by state and pin code, flat and weight-based rates, free-shipping thresholds, COD rules and courier integrations.

Key takeaways
  • Enter packed weights and box dimensions for every product, because couriers charge the higher of actual and volumetric weight and missing weights break rate rules.
  • Order zones from most specific to least, such as local pin codes, your state, remote regions and the rest of India, since WooCommerce uses the first zone that matches.
  • Allow COD only on the shipping methods and pin codes your courier supports, connect an aggregator or courier for labels and tracking, and test real pin codes before launch.

WooCommerce shipping lives under WooCommerce > Settings > Shipping. Create shipping zones from the most specific to the least (your local pin codes, your state, any remote regions, then the rest of India), add methods to each zone (flat rate, free shipping or local pickup), and enter accurate packed weights and box dimensions for every product. Add a plugin if you need weight-based rates, decide which zones allow cash on delivery, connect a courier or shipping aggregator for labels and tracking, and test with real pin codes before launch.

Shipping is where many Indian stores quietly lose money, either by undercharging on heavy parcels or by putting buyers off with high charges. In my WooCommerce development work, COD options and shipping rules are set up and tested to match how you actually dispatch orders.

Step 1: weights, dimensions and shipping classes

Every rate calculation depends on product data. Set the units to kg and cm in WooCommerce > Settings > Products, then fill in the weight and dimensions on each product's Shipping tab, including each variation that differs.

Use the packed weight and the box size, not the product's own. Couriers generally charge the higher of the actual weight and the volumetric weight, which is often length × width × height in centimetres divided by 5,000; check the divisor on your courier's rate card. A 40 × 30 × 20 cm box works out at 4.8 kg volumetric, so it's billed as 4.8 kg even if the contents weigh 2 kg. Many couriers also bill in 500 g slabs, so an extra 50 g can push a parcel into the next slab.

If some products need different handling, create shipping classes such as "Bulky" or "Fragile" under Shipping > Classes and assign them on each product.

Step 2: create shipping zones in the right order

Go to Shipping > Shipping zones and click Add zone. Each zone has a name, the regions it covers (countries, states or pin codes) and its shipping methods. The rule that matters most: WooCommerce checks zones from top to bottom and uses the first one that matches the customer's address. Put the most specific zones first and drag them into order.

To match pin codes, choose "Limit to specific ZIP/postcodes" and enter one per line. Wildcards and ranges both work, such as 411* for most Pune city pin codes or 411001...411062 for a range. A typical set-up for a store in Pune:

1Pune localIndia, limited to the pin codes you deliver to yourselfLocal pickup, own delivery at a flat rate
2MaharashtraMaharashtraFlat rate, free shipping above your threshold
3Remote regionsStates and territories your courier charges more forA higher flat rate or weight-based rate

Some couriers charge more for regions such as the north-eastern states, Jammu and Kashmir, Ladakh and the island territories; your rate card shows whether a separate zone is worth it. Shipping abroad needs its own zones and much more besides; see selling internationally from an Indian store.

Step 3: add shipping methods

  • Flat rate: the cost box accepts simple formulas. [qty] is the number of items, [cost] is the order total, and [fee] adds a percentage with optional minimum and maximum. Flat rate also has a "Shipping class costs" section, with a choice of charging per class or per order.
  • Free shipping: choose what unlocks it: a minimum order amount, a free-shipping coupon, either or both. There's also a box to apply the minimum before coupon discounts, which decides whether a coupon can push an order below the threshold.
  • Local pickup: lets nearby customers collect at no charge. Newer WooCommerce versions using the block checkout manage pickup locations on a separate Local pickup settings screen.

A couple of flat rate formulas, with placeholder numbers to replace with your own courier costs:

50 + ( 10 * [qty] )
[fee percent="5" min_fee="40" max_fee="200"]

The first charges a base amount plus an amount per item; the second charges 5% of the order total, never less than 40 or more than 200.

Weight-based rates

WooCommerce has no weight-based rate table out of the box. The usual options, at the time of writing, are WooCommerce's own paid Table Rate Shipping extension, a plugin such as Flexible Shipping (whose free version handles weight-based rules), or live rates from a courier integration. Mirror your courier's slabs per zone, such as up to 500 g, up to 1 kg, then each extra 500 g. Any product without a weight counts as zero and gets the cheapest rate, so check the product list for blanks before launch.

Free-shipping thresholds that protect your margin

  • Set the threshold a little above your typical order value, so it nudges shoppers to add one more item.
  • Check the margin at the threshold still covers your worst case, such as a heavy parcel to a remote region.
  • Leave bulky shipping classes or remote zones out of free shipping if they would make orders unprofitable.
  • Show how close the shopper is, such as "Add a little more for free delivery", in the cart.

By default WooCommerce shows paid options alongside free shipping, which looks odd. A developer can hide the paid ones whenever free shipping applies; note that this snippet hides local pickup too, so adjust it if pickup should stay:

add_filter( 'woocommerce_package_rates', 'my_only_free_shipping', 100 );

function my_only_free_shipping( $rates ) {
  $free = array();
  foreach ( $rates as $rate_id => $rate ) {
    if ( 'free_shipping' === $rate->method_id ) {
      $free[ $rate_id ] = $rate;
    }
  }
  return ! empty( $free ) ? $free : $rates;
}

Cash on delivery rules

In WooCommerce > Settings > Payments > Cash on delivery, the "Enable for shipping methods" setting is more useful than it looks. Because each zone has its own methods, allowing COD only on certain methods effectively limits COD to certain zones, with no extra plugin. Order value limits, COD fees and pin code rules need a plugin or custom code, and your courier must accept COD for those pin codes too. Reducing refused deliveries is covered in cash on delivery without losing money.

Pin code serviceability

Zones limited to specific pin codes stop orders to areas you can't serve, but WooCommerce's default "no shipping options" message at checkout is unfriendly and comes late. A checker on the product page tells shoppers about delivery and COD before they reach checkout, using your own pin code list or your courier's live serviceability data; see pin code delivery checkers for WooCommerce.

Couriers and shipping aggregators

Aggregators let you ship with several couriers from one dashboard; a direct courier account can suit stores with steady volumes. At the time of writing, Shiprocket, Delhivery and iThink Logistics all offer ways to connect a WooCommerce store, through a plugin or a channel connection in their own dashboard. Depending on the provider, an integration can:

  • Pull new orders from WooCommerce automatically
  • Assign a courier, generate the airway bill and print labels
  • Schedule pickups
  • Send the tracking number back to the WooCommerce order and update its status
  • Show live rates or serviceability at checkout

Before choosing, compare rates by slab and zone, the volumetric divisor, COD charges and how quickly COD money is remitted, return-to-origin charges, how weight disputes are handled and how reliable pickups are in your area. Photographing packed parcels on the scale with their dimensions helps when a courier disputes a weight. Many integrations connect using WooCommerce REST API keys, created under Settings > Advanced > REST API; revoke a key when you stop using that service. Shipping, packaging and RTO costs belong in your budget too; see WooCommerce store running costs.

Tracking, emails and your shipping policy

  • Add the tracking number and courier link to each order, through your integration or a tracking plugin, so the customer's email includes them; order notifications by email, SMS and WhatsApp covers which updates to send.
  • Publish a shipping policy covering zones, charges, dispatch times, delivery estimates, COD rules, failed deliveries and damaged parcels.
  • Ask your CA how delivery charges should be taxed, then set the shipping tax class to match; see WooCommerce GST setup.

Test before launch

  1. Under Shipping > Shipping settings, turn on debug mode temporarily; the cart then shows which zone matched. Turn it off afterwards.
  2. Test pin codes from your city, your state, a metro elsewhere, a remote region and an invalid code.
  3. Try a light cart, a heavy cart, a bulky item and a cart just under and just over the free-shipping threshold, with and without a coupon.
  4. Check COD appears only where it should.
  5. Place a test order and confirm the courier integration picks it up.

These checks sit alongside the payment and email tests in the WooCommerce store launch checklist.

FAQs

How do I set up shipping zones in WooCommerce for India?

Go to WooCommerce > Settings > Shipping > Shipping zones and add zones from most to least specific: local pin codes, your state, any remote regions, then India as a whole. WooCommerce uses the first zone that matches the address, so the order matters.

How do I charge shipping by weight in WooCommerce?

Enter packed weights for every product, then add a weight-based rates plugin such as Table Rate Shipping or Flexible Shipping, or use live rates from a courier integration. WooCommerce's built-in methods don't calculate by weight on their own.

How do I offer free shipping above a certain order value?

Add the Free shipping method to the zone and set it to require a minimum order amount. Decide whether coupons count towards the minimum, and consider hiding paid options when free shipping applies.

Can I connect Shiprocket or Delhivery to WooCommerce?

At the time of writing, both offer ways to connect a WooCommerce store, as do other aggregators such as iThink Logistics. Integrations typically sync orders, generate labels and send tracking back, but check each provider's current features.

How do I stop orders from pin codes I can't deliver to?

Limit your zones to the pin codes you serve so checkout offers no shipping elsewhere, and add a pin code checker on product pages so shoppers find out before they reach checkout.

Why is WooCommerce showing the wrong shipping charge?

Usually because a broader zone sits above a specific one, products are missing weights or shipping classes, or an old rate is cached. Turn on shipping debug mode to see which zone and method matched.

Want shipping that charges correctly and doesn't scare buyers off? Zones, weight-based rates, COD rules and a courier connection can all be set up and tested with real pin codes as part of WooCommerce development.
