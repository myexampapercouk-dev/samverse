Websites for Yoga Studios and Teachers: Classes, Schedules and Online Sessions

What yoga studios and teachers need online: beginner-friendly class pages, schedules, online sessions, memberships, real credentials and careful health claims.

Sameer Gupta 28 September 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Explain each style and level in plain words, with a "Your first class" page, so beginners feel confident enough to book.
  • Show time zones for online batches, send session links only after booking, and publish clear membership and refund rules.
  • List only real certifications and avoid claiming yoga cures medical conditions; encourage students to check with their doctor.

Someone looking for a yoga class is rarely just comparing prices. A beginner wants to know whether they will cope, someone with back pain wants to know whether it is safe, and a student abroad wants to know whether your online batch fits their time zone. A yoga website has to answer these questions calmly and clearly. Our guide to websites for gyms and fitness studios covers the general fitness side; this one focuses on what is specific to yoga studios and independent yoga teachers.

Studio or independent teacher?

The structure of the site depends on how you work:

AspectYoga studioIndependent teacher
Main focusTimetable, styles, teachers and membershipsYou: your training, approach and availability
Typical offersGroup classes, class packs, workshops, teacher trainingPrivate sessions, home visits, corporate classes, online batches
Main actionBook a trial classBook a first session or a short call
Typical searches"yoga classes in {area}""yoga teacher for home in {city}", "online yoga teacher"

Teachers building a name for themselves can borrow ideas from personal brand websites for professionals.

Class pages that reassure beginners

Instead of a bare list of class names, give each style or class type its own short page or section:

  • What it is: Hatha, Vinyasa, Ashtanga, Yin, prenatal, kids or chair yoga, explained in plain words
  • Level: beginner, all levels or experienced, and whether newcomers can join any week
  • What to expect: pace, duration, and how much breathwork, chanting or meditation is included
  • What to bring: mat, clothing, water, and whether to avoid a heavy meal beforehand
  • Who should check first: for example during pregnancy, after surgery or with an injury

A short "Your first class" page that answers common worries (flexibility, age, fitness level, what happens when you arrive) often does more for enquiries than any discount.

Schedules, time zones and online sessions

  • An editable timetable: the teacher or studio staff should be able to change a class, mark a substitute or cancel a session from a phone in minutes
  • Morning and evening batches clearly separated, since that is how many students choose
  • Time zones: if you teach students abroad, state the time zone next to every online class (for example "7:00 am IST") and consider showing the time for your main overseas markets too
  • Private links: send Zoom or Google Meet links only after booking or payment, by email or WhatsApp, never on a public page
  • Recordings: if you offer replays, keep them in a members-only area rather than on public video pages

Trials, private sessions and group classes can all be booked online with automatic reminders; see online appointment booking.

Memberships, packs and payments

Yoga pricing usually mixes several formats, and the website should make them easy to compare:

  • Drop-in or trial classes
  • Class packs with a clear validity period
  • Monthly or quarterly memberships for in-studio, online or both
  • Workshops, retreats and teacher training courses, priced separately

Collect payments by UPI, cards and net banking through a payment gateway, and check whether your gateway plan accepts international cards if you have overseas students. Write clear pause, freeze and refund rules for memberships and publish them before anyone pays. Our guide to accepting online payments on WordPress explains the options.

Teacher credentials, described honestly

Students increasingly check who will be teaching them. For each teacher, include:

  • A real photo and a short, personal introduction
  • Where and how they trained, and the certifications they hold, such as Yoga Alliance registration or Yoga Certification Board certification, with levels stated accurately
  • Years of teaching and the groups they specialise in, such as beginners, seniors or prenatal students
  • The languages they teach in

List only certifications a teacher actually holds, and keep them current. Vague labels such as "expert" or "world-famous" mean little to a new student; clear training details mean a lot.

Health claims and student safety

People often search for yoga in connection with back pain, stress, PCOS, thyroid problems or diabetes. It is fine to describe classes designed for particular groups, but be careful with the wording:

  • Avoid saying yoga "cures" or "reverses" a disease, or promising specific medical results
  • Encourage students with medical conditions, injuries or pregnancies to consult their doctor before starting
  • Use a short health questionnaire at sign-up and limit who can see the answers, since health details deserve extra care
  • Share student stories only with consent, and keep them about experience rather than medical outcomes

Claims about treating medical conditions can fall under advertising rules, so if you run therapeutic programmes, have the wording reviewed by someone qualified.

Local SEO and seasonal moments

  • Target searches such as "yoga classes in {area}", "morning yoga batch near me", "prenatal yoga in {city}" and "online yoga classes for beginners"
  • Keep your Google Business Profile updated with class times, photos of the studio and genuine reviews
  • Write helpful articles that answer beginner questions, rather than copied lists of poses
  • Plan events and pages around International Day of Yoga on 21 June, the new year and summer batches for children
  • Give teacher training and retreats their own landing pages, with dates, syllabus, accommodation, eligibility and an application form

A calm design that still loads fast

A yoga website should feel calm, but calm doesn't mean heavy. Large background videos and full-screen sliders slow pages down on mobile data. Use a few good photos of your real space and teachers, embed class videos carefully, and keep the timetable, WhatsApp button and "Book a trial" link easy to reach on a phone.
