Real Estate Project Microsites: What to Include, How to Capture Leads and When a Landing Page Is Enough

What a real estate project microsite needs: floor plans, amenities, location, price on request, RERA details, brochure and site-visit forms, call tracking.

Key takeaways
  • A project microsite should answer a buyer's first questions fast: configurations with carpet area, floor plans, location, price or price on request, RERA details and how to book a visit.
  • Most projects work best as a page on the developer's main site plus focused ad landing pages; a separate microsite earns its place for a large launch with its own budget.
  • Capture leads with short forms, a gated brochure, site-visit booking, tap-to-call and WhatsApp, and track every one by campaign so you know which ads bring visits.

A real estate project microsite is a focused website, or section of one, for a single housing or commercial project, built to turn ad clicks and brochure requests into site visits. It should cover the project overview, configurations with carpet areas, floor plans, amenities, a location map with real distances, a starting price or a clear price-on-request option, the RERA registration details, a brochure download and site-visit booking. Every enquiry, call and WhatsApp click should be tracked back to the campaign that produced it.

Project pages are the core of my real estate website design work: galleries, floor plans, amenities, location maps and RERA details on every project, with site-visit forms and brochure downloads that capture serious buyers. For most projects, I suggest project pages on the developer's main site plus focused landing pages for ad campaigns, and a separate microsite when a large launch has its own budget. This guide covers how to decide, what to include and how to measure what works.

Microsite, landing page or project page on the main site?

These terms get used loosely, so it's worth being precise about what each one is for:

OptionWhat it isBest forWatch out for
Project page on the main siteA page or small section at an address like example.com/projects/project-name/Most projects; builds the developer's brand and search visibility over timeNeeds a main site that already looks credible
Ad landing pageA single focused page for one campaign, with one form and no menu leading elsewhereGoogle and Meta ads, where every click costs moneyUsually kept out of Google so it doesn't compete with the project page
Project micrositeA separate multi-page site, often on its own domain, branded around the projectLarge launches, joint ventures or projects marketed under their own brandStarts with no search history, and needs its own hosting, upkeep and renewals

A common pattern is all three working together: the project page on the main site for buyers who search the developer or project name, landing pages for each ad campaign, and a microsite only when the project's brand and budget justify it. When a project sells out, redirect its microsite to the project page on the main site and keep the domain renewed, so old brochures and links still work.

The structure of a project microsite

Buyers arrive with the same questions in roughly the same order. Lay the site out to answer them:

OverviewLand area, towers, units, possession timeline, approvalsPlain facts, not slogans
Configurations1, 2 or 3 BHK, plots or shops, with carpet area as defined under RERADon't show only super built-up area

RERA details and honest claims

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, advertisements for a registered project generally need to show its RERA registration number and the state RERA website address, and a project page or microsite is generally treated as advertising. Some state authorities add their own requirements, such as a QR code on advertisements in Maharashtra. Check current RERA advertising rules for your state before launch, and have your advisers confirm the wording.

  • Show the registration number and RERA website near the top of the page and in the footer of every page.
  • Don't market a project that needs registration before it is registered, so no "pre-launch" microsites for it.
  • Label renders and sample flat photos as artist's impressions.
  • State carpet area, and keep specifications and amenities consistent with the approved plans and the agreement.
  • Avoid claims you can't support, such as guaranteed returns or travel times.

Showing prices or price on request

It's your decision. A starting price with the configuration and carpet area answers the first question most buyers have and filters out enquiries outside their budget. If you prefer to share prices on request, say so plainly and make the price sheet a quick download or WhatsApp request, rather than hiding it behind several steps. Whichever you choose, keep the prices on every page, ad and brochure consistent, and update them when they change.

Lead capture: forms, brochures and site visits

  • Short enquiry forms: name, phone, configuration and budget as dropdowns. Every extra field costs enquiries on a phone. Studio Agama Interiors, an interior design studio I built a site for, asks for project stage, budget and time frame, so the team knows what each lead needs before calling; the same idea works for property buyers.
  • A gated brochure: ask only for name, phone and email, then send the brochure to the email given, which also confirms the address is real. A brochure PDF should still be findable and well titled; see SEO for PDFs and brochures.
  • Site-visit booking with a preferred date and time, so your sales team calls to confirm a slot rather than starting from scratch.
  • Tap-to-call and WhatsApp on every screen, with the project name already in the WhatsApp message.
  • Instant delivery: every enquiry emailed to sales straight away, and sent to a Google Sheet or your CRM, with a consent line and privacy notice under the form.

Property leads cool quickly, so the first call should happen the same day; responding to enquiries fast covers alerts and auto-replies.

Tracking calls, WhatsApp and campaigns

A microsite that can't tell you which campaign brought a site visit wastes budget. Track form submissions, phone taps and WhatsApp clicks as conversions in GA4, Google Ads and Meta, and tag every ad link with UTM parameters. Pre-filled links make each source identifiable:

<a href="tel:+919800000000">Call sales</a>
<a href="https://wa.me/919800000000?text=Hi%2C%20I%20would%20like%20details%20of%20Example%20Heights%202%20BHK">WhatsApp us</a>

Tracking a tap only tells you someone tried to call. To know which calls connected and which campaign they came from, a call tracking service with separate numbers per source helps; see call tracking for small businesses. Give channel partners their own links or a code field, so their leads are credited correctly. Google Ads conversion tracking on WordPress walks through the setup.

Speed for ad traffic

Most microsite visitors arrive from a mobile ad, often on mobile data. Heavy render galleries, autoplay videos and large sliders delay the page while the ad meter runs. Compress renders and serve them as WebP, load galleries below the first screen lazily, embed walkthrough videos so they load only when tapped, and test the page on a real phone over 4G before the campaign starts. After launch

  • Post construction updates with dates on a regular schedule.
  • Update inventory and prices when they change, and mark sold-out configurations.
  • Review which campaigns bring site visits, not just form fills, and shift budget accordingly.
  • When the project completes, keep the page as a case study for the developer, or redirect the microsite to it.

FAQs

Does every real estate project need its own microsite?

No. Most projects work well as pages on the developer's main site, where buyers can also see completed projects, plus focused landing pages for ad campaigns. A separate microsite makes sense for a large launch with its own brand and budget.

Do I need to show the RERA number on a project microsite?

For a registered project, RERA generally requires advertisements to show the registration number and the state RERA website, and a website is generally treated as advertising. Check your state authority's current rules, as some add requirements such as a QR code.

Should a project microsite have its own domain?

Only if the project is marketed as a brand in its own right. A page or section on the main site benefits from the site's existing search history, while a new domain starts from nothing and needs its own renewals and upkeep.

What is the difference between a microsite and a landing page?

A landing page is a single page built for one campaign, with one goal and no distractions. A microsite is a small multi-page site for the whole project, covering plans, amenities, location, updates and more.

What should happen to the microsite after the project sells out?

Redirect it to the project's page on the main site, which becomes a completed-project showcase, and keep the domain renewed so old brochures, QR codes and links still lead somewhere useful.
