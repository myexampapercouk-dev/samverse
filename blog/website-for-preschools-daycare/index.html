Websites for Preschools, Playschools and Daycare Centres: Safety, Admissions, Visits and Photo Consent

Plan a preschool or daycare website parents trust: safety and staff details, programmes by age, timings, fees, visit booking and photo consent for children.

Key takeaways
  • Lead with what parents check first: safety, who looks after the children, facilities, daily routine and timings, described as you actually run them rather than with vague claims.
  • Make the main action a school visit: a short enquiry form with the child's age, programme and a preferred visit slot, followed up quickly on WhatsApp.
  • Publish children's photos only with written parental consent, avoid pairing names with faces, and keep daily photos and CCTV in private parent channels, never on public pages.

A preschool or daycare website has one main job: to reassure a parent enough to book a visit. Parents check safety, who looks after the children, the facilities, the daily routine and the timings before they look at anything else, and they notice quickly when a site makes claims it cannot back up. The strongest sites describe how the centre really runs, make booking a visit easy on a phone, and treat children's photos and data with more care than any other business website would.

This guide covers playschools, nursery and kindergarten sections, daycare and after-school centres, whether independent or franchise outlets. If you want a site built around admission enquiries, notices and galleries, with photo consent planned in from the start, see my websites for schools and coaching institutes. For older children and school admissions, what parents look for in school websites covers the wider picture.

What parents check first

Choosing a first school or a daycare is an anxious decision, often made by two working parents comparing centres on their phones late in the evening. Their questions are practical:

  • Is it safe? Entry control, who can pick a child up, supervision, and what happens if a child is unwell or hurt
  • Who will look after my child? The teachers and caregivers, their training, and how many adults there are per group
  • What does a day look like? Activities, play, meals, naps and outdoor time
  • Does it fit our timings? Session hours, extended daycare, holidays and late pickup
  • What does it cost? Fees, or at least what the fee covers
  • How far is it? Location, landmark, parking and drop-off arrangements

Put the location, timings, a "Book a visit" button and one-tap WhatsApp at the top of the homepage. Everything else can follow in the order above.

Programmes by age group

Parents search by age and need: "playgroup for 2 year old", "nursery admission near me", "daycare for working parents". Give each programme its own section with the age band and the cut-off date you use, session timings, group size and the number of adults as you actually staff it, a typical day, and how you share progress with parents. Some states have set minimum ages for nursery and Class 1 admissions, so check your state's current rules and say clearly which date you use to calculate age.

If you follow a particular approach, such as play-based learning or Montessori-inspired activities, explain what it means in your classrooms in plain words. Only use a named method or affiliation if your teachers are genuinely trained in it, and say where.

Daycare needs extra detail

  • Full-day, half-day and flexible options, with the exact opening and closing times
  • Meals: whether you provide them or parents send food, a sample weekly menu, and how you handle allergies
  • Nap times, bedding and what parents need to send
  • Late pickup rules, holiday care and the days you close
  • After-school care: pickup from nearby schools, if you offer it, with the schools you cover

Safety, staff and hygiene: show, don't claim

"Safe and secure environment" appears on almost every preschool website and tells a parent nothing. Describe the measures instead, and only the ones you actually have:

  • Entry and pickup: who can enter, how visitors are logged, and how authorised pickup works, such as a registered list of adults with photos
  • Supervision: how many adults are with each group, and who accompanies children to the washroom
  • Staff checks and training: background verification and first aid training, stated only if you do them
  • CCTV: which areas are covered and who can view the footage
  • Health: your sick-child policy, how medicines are given, and how parents are told about an injury
  • Hygiene: cleaning routines for toys, washrooms and the kitchen
  • Emergencies: fire safety, evacuation drills and the nearest hospital you would use

Avoid claims nobody can verify, such as "100% safe", "best preschool in the city" or "internationally certified" without saying by whom. Registration and licensing rules for preschools and creches vary by state, so mention a registration only if you hold it, and check the current requirements with your adviser.

Admission enquiries and visit booking

Very few parents enrol without seeing the centre, so the visit is the real conversion. A short form works best:

  • Parent's name and phone number for WhatsApp
  • The child's age, or month and year of birth, and the programme
  • Preferred start month and, for chains, the centre
  • A preferred visit date and time slot
  • An optional "how did you hear about us?" question

A preferred date and slot means your coordinator receives a specific request rather than a bare "please call me". Dr. Nitish Gupta's clinic website, which I built, asks patients for a preferred date and session in the same way. Confirm the visit on WhatsApp, say what will happen during it and how long it takes, and explain your settling-in period for new children. Leave birth certificates, address proofs and medical forms for the admission stage, collected in person or through a secure process, not a public web form.

Fees: show them or explain them

Whether you publish fee amounts is your decision. Either way, explain the structure: the admission or registration fee, term or monthly fees, meals, transport, activity charges, any refundable deposit, and whether instalments are possible. If figures are shared on enquiry, say so plainly. Should you show prices on your website? covers the trade-offs.

Children's photos and parental consent

Photos of happy children sell a preschool better than anything else, which is exactly why they need care. India's data protection law expects verifiable parental consent before processing children's personal data; India's DPDP Act and your website explains the basics, and your lawyer can confirm what applies to you.

  • Add a photo consent section to the admission form, with a clear option to opt out of website and social media use
  • Keep a list of children who must not appear, and check it before anything is published
  • Never put a child's full name next to their photo
  • Prefer group activity shots, hands at work and photos from behind over close-up portraits
  • Remove a photo quickly when a parent asks
  • Share daily photos and any CCTV access through a private parent app or group, never on public pages

Facility photos taken before the children arrive avoid most of the problem for the pages that matter most.

Parent communication after admission

Once a child joins, the website becomes a reference for current parents: the holiday calendar, event notices, newsletters, the term's theme and reminders about things to send. If you use a parent app for daily updates, mention it on the admissions page as a benefit, and link to it rather than duplicating it. A notices page staff can update in minutes keeps a permanent record that new parents can find, which WhatsApp groups do not. Franchise outlets and multiple centres

Many preschools are franchise outlets of a national or regional brand. The brand's guidelines usually control the name, logo, curriculum descriptions and sometimes the website itself, so check what you are allowed to build. Even within the brand's site, each centre needs its own details: address, timings, team, photos and its own enquiry routing. Owners of several centres should give each one a genuinely different page linked to its own Google Business Profile; see websites for businesses with multiple branches. If you run the brand itself, websites for franchise brands covers outlet locators and franchise enquiries.

Getting found by local parents

Parents search close to home: "preschool near me", "daycare in Baner" or "playschool in Kharadi". To appear:

  • Complete a Google Business Profile for each centre with the closest category, such as preschool or day care centre, real photos of the rooms and accurate hours
  • Keep the name, address, phone and timings identical on the website and the profile
  • Ask real parents for reviews once they have settled in, and never write them yourself
  • Use the area name naturally in page titles and headings, for example "Playschool and Daycare in Wakad"
  • Add structured data with schema.org's Preschool or ChildCare types where they fit

Common mistakes on preschool websites

  • Close-up photos of identifiable children with no record of consent
  • Safety described with adjectives instead of actual measures
  • No timings, so working parents cannot tell whether daycare covers their day
  • Last year's admission dates and fees still live
  • A long admission form asking for documents before the parent has even visited
  • A heavy video slider that takes ages to load on mobile data

FAQs

Should a preschool show its fees online?

It is your choice. Publishing fees filters out parents outside your range; sharing them on enquiry starts a conversation. Either way, explain what the fee includes, any one-time charges and your refund policy.

Can parents book a school visit on the website?

Yes. A short form with the child's age, programme and a preferred date and slot, confirmed on WhatsApp, works well. Larger chains can route each request to the right centre.

Can we post photos of children on our website?

Only with written parental consent, and with an easy way to withdraw it. Avoid naming children, prefer group or activity shots, and keep daily photos in private parent channels.

Do we need a website if we have a franchise brand page?

Often the franchise agreement decides this. Where you are allowed your own site or centre page, it lets you show your own team, facilities and timings and rank for searches in your area.

Can our staff update notices and holidays themselves?

They should be able to. Ask for a notices section and holiday calendar staff can edit from the dashboard, with a short training session at handover.
