Online Donations for NGOs and Trusts: 80G Receipts, FCRA and Payment Setup

How Indian NGOs, trusts and temples can accept online donations on their website: donation forms, payment gateways, 80G receipt details, PAN collection, foreign donations and FCRA, and transparency.

Key takeaways
  • Use short donation forms with suggested amounts, monthly giving and UPI.
  • Collect PAN and address for 80G receipts and follow your CA's reporting process.
  • Only accept foreign donations with FCRA registration; publish reports and impact.

A donation page can bring steady support for an NGO, trust or temple, but only if giving is easy, donors trust you, and the paperwork is handled correctly. This guide covers the website side. For tax and legal requirements, always confirm with your chartered accountant.

A donation form that works

  • Suggested amounts with a custom amount option
  • One-time and monthly giving
  • Name, email and phone
  • PAN and address, needed for donors who want an 80G tax receipt
  • An option to dedicate the donation (in memory of, on behalf of)
  • A clear note on how the money will be used

Keep the form short; ask for PAN and address only from donors who want the tax benefit.

Payment options

  • UPI, cards, net banking and wallets through a payment gateway that supports non-profits
  • Recurring donations through card mandates or UPI AutoPay
  • Bank transfer details for large donors

See accepting online payments in India.

80G receipts

If your organisation has 80G registration, donors can claim a tax deduction. Your website should:

  • Clearly state your 80G status and registration details
  • Collect the donor details your CA says are required, typically name, address and PAN
  • Send an automatic acknowledgement email with transaction details

Organisations are also required to report donations to the Income Tax Department, after which donors receive their official certificate. Your CA will guide the exact process and timelines. Note that large cash donations aren't eligible for the deduction, which is another reason to encourage online payment.

Foreign donations and FCRA

Indian NGOs generally need FCRA registration to accept donations from foreign sources, and foreign contributions must be received in the designated FCRA bank account. If you don't have FCRA registration, make it clear that you can only accept donations from Indian citizens and residents, and add a nationality check to the form. Take professional advice on FCRA compliance.

Build trust and transparency

  • Registration details: trust or society registration, 12A, 80G and FCRA (if applicable)
  • Annual reports and audited financials
  • Impact stories with photos, with consent
  • How donations are used, such as "₹500 provides…" only if accurate
  • Trustees and team

Thank donors properly

A thank-you page, an immediate email receipt and regular updates on impact encourage repeat giving; see thank-you pages.
