Websites for Security and Facility Management Companies: Service Pages, PSARA and Other Registrations, Sector Pages, Tender Enquiries and Recruitment

Plan a security or facility management website: guarding, housekeeping and technical services, licences shown only if held, tenders and recruitment.

Key takeaways
  • Write the site to answer a vendor evaluation: services, licences, statutory registrations, supervision and references, each on a page a procurement team can check.
  • Show a PSARA licence, labour registrations or ISO certificates only if you hold them, with numbers, states and validity dates kept current.
  • Run two separate journeys, one for clients sending tenders and RFQs and one for job seekers, because both arrive every day and want completely different things.

Before a society committee or a factory's admin head invites a security or facility management company to quote, someone is usually asked to check the company out online. They read your website like a vendor evaluation, looking for licences, registrations, supervision and references, while on the same day guards, housekeepers and technicians read it looking for a job. The sites that work answer the evaluation questions page by page and give recruitment its own clearly separated section.

This is a straightforward build of the kind I describe on the WordPress website development page: service and sector pages your team can edit, enquiry forms and WhatsApp, a downloadable company profile, and SEO foundations in place at launch. Because buyers often start with searches such as "security agency in Pune" or "housekeeping services for offices", page titles, headings, schema and internal links matching those searches decide whether buyers ever reach you; that work is what my WordPress SEO services cover.

The questions a buyer is told to check

Many organisations evaluate security and facility vendors against a checklist, formal or informal. Your website should let them tick most of it off before the first meeting:

  1. Which services do you provide in-house, and which through partners?
  2. Are you licensed and registered for this work in our state?
  3. How do you recruit, verify and train staff?
  4. Who supervises the site, how often, and how are incidents reported?
  5. Do you comply with wage, provident fund and insurance obligations for deployed staff?
  6. Which similar sites do you already manage, and can we speak to them?
  7. How quickly can you deploy, and how are replacements handled when someone is absent?

Each question deserves a clear answer somewhere on the site, in plain language, not a slogan about "trusted security solutions".

Service pages for guarding, housekeeping and technical work

Give each service its own page, written around what the client receives rather than what you call it internally.

Manned guarding

Explain the roles you supply, such as unarmed guards, supervisors, lady guards, reception and access control staff, and event or crowd management teams. Mention armed guards or ex-servicemen only where you genuinely provide them and hold any licences that requires. Describe shift patterns, how relievers cover weekly offs and leave, and what equipment guards carry.

Housekeeping and soft services

List what is covered: daily cleaning, washroom upkeep, mechanised floor cleaning, pantry and office assistance, and periodic deep cleaning. Name the equipment and the type of cleaning chemicals you use where buyers ask, and say whether you supply consumables or the client does. If cleaning for homes or one-off jobs is also part of your business, websites for cleaning services covers that retail side.

Technical services

Electricians, plumbers, HVAC technicians, DG and STP operators and lift attendants are often part of an integrated facility contract. State the trades you supply and the certificates or licences your technicians hold where a trade requires them, and avoid listing services you would subcontract without saying so. If you also install CCTV and access control systems, websites for CCTV and security system installers deals with that business.

PSARA and other registrations, shown only if held

Private security agencies in India are licensed under the Private Security Agencies (Regulation) Act, 2005, by the controlling authority of each state, and an agency working in several states needs a licence in each of them. Buyers are increasingly told to check, so show what you hold and where:

RegistrationWhat to showWhere it belongs
PSARA licenceLicence number, the state that issued it and its validity, for each stateCompliance page, and the footer of each state or city page
GST registrationGSTIN for each state where you are registeredFooter and compliance page
EPF and ESIC registrationsEstablishment code numbersCompliance page
Labour law licences and registrationsThe licences you hold as a contractor, as your adviser confirms they applyCompliance page, or on request
ISO or similar certificationsStandard, certifying body and validityCompliance page, only if certified
Government e-Marketplace registrationThat you are registered as a seller, if you areTenders page

Labour regulation has been changing as the new labour codes take effect, so ask your compliance adviser which registrations apply to you before writing this page, and date it. Never display a licence that has lapsed or is "applied for" as if it were granted.

A compliance documents page

Procurement teams ask for the same documents on every tender: registration certificates, licences, PAN, GST, EPF and ESIC registrations, ISO certificates, insurance policies and sometimes audited financials. A compliance page that lists them, with validity dates, saves your team hours. Publish what is safe to publish, such as certificates with numbers and dates, and mark the rest "available on request", sent after a qualified enquiry. Do not publish anything containing employees' personal details. If the company profile is a PDF, give it a proper title and text so it can be found; SEO for PDFs and brochures explains how.

Sector pages built from sites you already manage

A housing society, a hospital and a warehouse buy the same services for different reasons. Write a page for each sector you genuinely serve, describing that sector's particular needs and how you handle them:

  • Housing societies: visitor and delivery management, resident complaints, night patrols and staff who speak the local language
  • Hospitals: patient attendant management, infection-aware housekeeping, emergency department crowd control
  • Factories and warehouses: gate and material movement checks, vehicle registers, fire safety rounds
  • Offices and IT parks: access control, front desk staff, pantry and housekeeping, out-of-hours cover
  • Retail and malls: loss prevention, crowd handling at peak times, opening and closing routines

Back each sector page with a short case study from a site you manage, shared with the client's permission: the brief, the team deployed, how supervision works and what changed. How to write case studies has a structure that works. Client logos also need permission, and many hospitals and corporates will say no, which is fine; a described site without a name still helps.

Tender, RFQ and site survey enquiries

Large contracts usually start with a site survey or a tender document, not a casual phone call. Give them their own page and form, asking for:

  • Organisation, contact person, designation and phone
  • Service needed: guarding, housekeeping, technical or integrated
  • Site type, address, built-up area and number of entry points
  • Approximate headcount and shifts, if known
  • Start date, and whether a tender document or reference number exists
  • An upload for the tender or scope document

When I built the Vansh Group website, enquiries were pre-sorted by a dropdown asking which service the buyer wanted, and a company profile download was placed beside the phone and email buttons. The same two features suit a facility company, where routing a housekeeping enquiry to the wrong manager can cost a day.

Careers pages that recruit and filter

Security and facility companies hire constantly, and job seekers may well outnumber clients among your visitors. Keep them in a separate careers section so client pages stay focused:

  • Openings by city and role, with the shift, eligibility and documents needed for verification
  • The training new staff receive before deployment
  • An apply-by-WhatsApp button and a short form, because many applicants use only a phone
  • Hindi or regional language versions of the key pages, if most applicants prefer them
  • A clear statement that you do not charge job seekers any fee, if that is your policy, and a warning about fraudsters using your name

Job listings marked up with job posting schema can appear in Google's job search features, which brings applicants without advertising. Websites for recruitment agencies covers candidate data and fraud warnings in more detail.

Supervision, reporting and incident handling

Buyers who have had a bad vendor worry most about what happens after the contract is signed. Describe your actual arrangements: field officer visits and night checks, attendance and patrolling systems if you use them, daily or monthly reports, the escalation matrix with named roles, and how incidents are recorded and reported. If you use a mobile app for patrol checkpoints or attendance, show a screenshot with client details removed. Promise only what you do on every site.

City pages and branches

Write a page for each city where you have a branch or deploy staff regularly, with the local office address, the PSARA licence for that state, the sectors you serve there and a photo of the local team. Each branch should also have its own Google Business Profile matching that page. Pages for cities where you have no presence add risk without adding trust.

FAQs

Should a security agency show its PSARA licence on the website?

Yes, if you hold it. Show the licence number, issuing state and validity for each state you operate in, and update the page when licences are renewed.

Should we publish compliance documents for download?

Publish certificates that are safe to share, with validity dates, and offer the rest on request after a qualified enquiry. Never publish employees' personal details.

Can the website accept tender documents?

Yes. A tender enquiry form can accept uploads with file type and size limits, and notify the right manager.

How should we handle the flood of job applications?

Give careers its own section with openings by city and role, a short form and a WhatsApp option, so client enquiries are not buried among applications.

Can we list client logos?

Only with each client's permission. Where permission is not given, describe the site and sector without naming the client.
