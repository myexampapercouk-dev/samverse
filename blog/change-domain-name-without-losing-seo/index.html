How to Change Your Domain Name Without Losing SEO: Redirects, Change of Address and Checks

How to change domain without losing SEO: keep the old domain registered, 301-redirect every old URL to its matching page on the new domain, and tell Google with the Change of Address tool in Search Console. Then update canonicals, sitemaps, internal links, profiles and your best backlinks, and expect some short-term fluctuation rather than a lasting drop.

A steep, lasting fall after a move almost always means redirects are missing, chained or pointing at the wrong pages. The checklist below is the order I follow, and the sections after it explain each step in detail.

Businesses change domains for good reasons: a rebrand, a shorter name, a .com for international buyers, or finally getting the name they wanted. This guide is about moving your website to a different domain name. If you are keeping the same name and only changing where it is registered, see how to transfer a domain to another registrar.

How to change domain without losing SEO: the 10-step checklist

  1. Keep the old domain. Leave it on auto-renew for years; if it expires, every redirect and old backlink dies with it.
  2. Crawl the old site. Export every URL that returns 200, including posts, pages, products, categories and PDFs.
  3. Add URLs from Search Console and backlinks. Pages with clicks or links matter most, even if they are no longer in your menus.
  4. Build a redirect map. One row per old URL, one matching new URL. Keep paths identical wherever you can.
  5. Set up the new site on staging. Same content, same structure, new domain in the WordPress settings or store settings.
  6. Launch and switch on 301s. Server-level or plugin rules that send each old URL to its new twin in one hop.
  7. Verify both domains in Search Console. Submit the new sitemap, then use Change of Address on the old property.
  8. Update everything you control. Canonicals, internal links, Google Business Profile, social profiles, ads, email signatures.
  9. Ask for key backlinks to be updated. Directories, partners and press mentions that still point at the old domain.
  10. Monitor for months. Check redirects, 404s, indexing and clicks weekly at first, then monthly.

Is a domain change worth it?

Your domain carries history: links from other websites, brand searches, bookmarks, printed material and customers' memories. A move usually brings a period of ranking fluctuation while Google processes it, and it can take weeks or months to settle. Before committing, ask:

  • Is the new name clearly better for the business, not just slightly nicer?
  • Can you keep the old domain registered for years, ideally for good? If it expires, every redirect breaks and someone else can buy it.
  • Can you avoid a redesign or platform change at the same time? Changing one big thing at a time makes problems far easier to diagnose.

Pick a quieter season too. Moving a gifting store's domain just before Diwali is asking for trouble. If you are weighing a .in against a .com, read .in vs .com domains first, and if the move is part of a wider rebrand, see what to update on your website after a rebrand.

Plan before you move

  1. List every URL on the old site: pages, posts, products, categories and any PDFs that get traffic. Use your XML sitemap, a crawler and Search Console's Performance report.
  2. Record benchmarks: organic clicks, top pages, key search terms and monthly enquiries.
  3. Export your top backlinks from Search Console's Links report; you will ask the most valuable sites to update them later.
  4. Prepare the new domain: hosting, an SSL certificate and a copy of the site, kept out of search results until launch.
  5. Plan your email: if business email uses the old domain, decide whether to move it, forward it or run both for a while, and set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC for the new domain.

Redesigning at the same time? Also follow the steps to redesign a website without losing rankings, so titles, content and key pages carry over intact.

Set up page-by-page 301 redirects

Redirects carry your rankings and links across. Each old URL should permanently (301) redirect to its equivalent page on the new domain.

  • Map one-to-one: the old /services/ac-repair/ page should go to the new /services/ac-repair/ page, not to the new homepage. Sending everything to the homepage loses much of the value.
  • Keep the same paths if you can. Then a single server-level rule can redirect the whole domain while preserving each path.
  • Avoid chains: the http, https, www and non-www versions of the old domain should each go straight to the final new URL in one hop.
  • Keep the old domain pointing at a server that serves the redirects, and leave them in place for at least a year, ideally permanently.

For when to use permanent and temporary redirects, see 301 vs 302 redirects.

Update the new site itself

  • Change WordPress's site address settings and run a search-and-replace on the database with a tool that handles serialised data safely, so internal links, images and settings use the new domain.
  • Check canonical tags point to the new domain, not the old one.
  • Remove any noindex setting or password protection left over from preparation.
  • Generate a fresh XML sitemap and make sure robots.txt is not blocking anything important.
  • Update structured data, such as business name, URL and logo, if the brand has changed.
  • Test forms, payments, email notifications and tracking on the new domain.

Use the Change of Address tool in Search Console

Google Search Console has a Change of Address tool built for this situation. The rough sequence:

  1. Verify the new domain in Search Console using the same Google account that owns the old property.
  2. Make sure the 301 redirects are live, as the tool checks that the old site redirects to the new one.
  3. In the old property's settings, open Change of Address, choose the new property and submit.
  4. Submit the new XML sitemap in the new property.

The tool is meant for moving a whole site from one domain to another, not for switching to HTTPS or moving individual pages. It supports your redirects; it does not replace them. Check Google's current documentation when you do it, as the steps can change. Keep the old property verified so you can watch it wind down.

Shopify and WooCommerce stores

On Shopify, you add the new domain in the admin, set it as primary, and Shopify redirects the other connected domains to it, keeping paths the same. Keep the old domain connected to the store rather than removing it, so those redirects continue. On WooCommerce, the steps above apply, plus extra checks: payment gateway webhooks and callback URLs, the domain approved in your Razorpay or other gateway account, shipping and invoice plugins, and Google Merchant Center product feeds. Place a test order end to end before announcing the change.

Telling customers about the new domain

Redirects handle search engines, but people notice a new address too, and some will wonder whether a new domain is a scam, especially if they receive emails from it. A short, clear announcement prevents confusion:

  • A banner or notice on the new site for a few weeks: "We have moved to [new domain]. Same team, same services."
  • An email or WhatsApp broadcast to existing customers explaining the change, sent from a known number or address.
  • A line on the About page mentioning the old name or domain, so people searching for it understand the link.
  • An auto-reply or forwarding on the old email addresses for several months, so no enquiry gets lost.
  • A note to your accountant, bank and payment gateway if invoices or receipts show the website address.

For online stores, warn customers that order emails will now come from the new domain, so they are not marked as spam or ignored. Keep both domains' email authentication records in place while messages from the old domain are still being sent.

Update links and profiles everywhere

  • Your Google Business Profile website and appointment links.
  • Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube and WhatsApp Business profiles.
  • Directories and listings such as Justdial, IndiaMART and industry associations.
  • Your most valuable backlinks from partners, suppliers and press, with a polite request to update them.
  • Final URLs in Google Ads and Meta ads, the website set in GA4 and Merchant Center, and your payment gateway account.
  • Email signatures, invoices, letterheads, visiting cards, vehicle branding and printed QR codes.

Printed QR codes keep working only as long as the old domain keeps redirecting. For keeping listings consistent, see business directories and citations in India.

Old links hardcoded with http or the old domain can trigger browser warnings after the switch; see how to fix a mixed content warning in WordPress.

Monitor after the move

  • First week: crawl a list of old URLs and confirm each redirects in a single hop to the right page. Check the new property's Pages report for errors.
  • First few months: watch impressions and clicks rise on the new domain as they fall on the old one. Some dip is normal.
  • Enquiries: compare form submissions, calls and WhatsApp clicks against your benchmark, not just traffic.
  • 404 errors: fix any old URLs that land on "page not found" by adding the missing redirects.

If traffic falls sharply and stays down, work through this traffic drop checklist.

FAQs

How do I change domain without losing SEO?

Keep the old domain, map every old URL to its new equivalent, set up one-hop 301 redirects, update canonicals and sitemaps, and use the Change of Address tool in Search Console. Then update profiles and key backlinks and monitor both properties for several months.

How long does it take for rankings to recover after a domain change?

It varies. Many sites settle within weeks to a few months if redirects are correct; there is no guaranteed timeline.

Can I let the old domain expire after moving?

No. If it expires, all redirects stop and someone else could buy it. Keep renewing it, ideally permanently.

Should I redirect all old pages to the new homepage?

No. Redirect each old page to its matching new page; homepage-only redirects lose much of the value.

Does changing from .in to .com affect Indian rankings?

The extension itself matters less than a clean move. With proper redirects and Search Console set up, an Indian business can rank on either.
