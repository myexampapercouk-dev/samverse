WooCommerce Maintenance Checklist: Daily, Weekly, Monthly and Quarterly Tasks for Indian Online Stores

A WooCommerce maintenance checklist by frequency: orders, payment reconciliation, stock, staging updates, backups, checkout tests, GST invoices, cleanup.

Key takeaways
  • Split store maintenance by frequency: a few minutes daily on orders and payments, a weekly test order, monthly updates on staging, and quarterly restores, audits and cleanup.
  • Reconcile gateway settlements against orders regularly, because a payment that succeeded at the gateway but left the order pending is easy to miss.
  • Back up stores more often than brochure sites and never restore or push a full database over a live store without first saving the orders placed since.

A WooCommerce store needs maintenance at four rhythms. Daily: check new, pending and failed orders, payment alerts and low stock. Weekly: place a test order, confirm order emails and backups, and scan the logs. Monthly: apply updates on a staging copy first, test every payment method and shipping rule, reconcile gateway settlements with orders, and spot-check GST invoices. Quarterly: test a backup restore, audit plugins and staff accounts, clean the database and review tax, policy and hosting settings.

These are store-specific tasks on top of normal WordPress maintenance. They exist because payments, shipping, stock and order emails can each fail quietly after an update, and a broken checkout costs sales every hour it goes unnoticed. Daily: five minutes on orders, payments and stock

  • Orders by status. Look at Processing for what needs dispatch, On hold for bank transfers to confirm, and Pending payment and Failed for problems. A cluster of failed orders from different customers usually means a gateway or checkout issue, not bad luck.
  • Gateway alerts. Payment providers email about failed webhooks, disputes and settlement issues. Someone should read them every day.
  • Stock. Check low-stock alerts so bestsellers don't show as unavailable or get oversold.
  • COD orders, if you offer them, confirmed before packing.

Weekly: prove the store still sells

  • A test order. Use a low-priced hidden test product, pay with UPI or a card, then refund it. This exercises the cart, checkout, payment, order status and emails in one go.
  • Stuck orders. If the gateway dashboard shows a successful payment but the order is still Pending payment, the gateway's webhook or callback is probably not reaching your site. Payment gateway not working covers the common causes.
  • Order emails. Check the new-order alert to you and the confirmation to the customer arrive, and not in spam.
  • Backups. Confirm they ran and that a copy lives away from your hosting server.
  • Logs. Under WooCommerce, Status, Logs, look for new fatal errors or gateway errors.
  • Uptime alerts, if you use monitoring: check any downtime was explained, not just recovered.

Monthly: updates, reconciliation and invoices

Updates on staging first

Update WooCommerce, extensions, the gateway plugin and the theme on a staging site, run a test order, then repeat on the live store at a quiet time. If WooCommerce asks to run a database update, take a fresh backup first. Update the most important plugins one at a time so you know which one caused any problem.

Outdated template files

The System Status report under WooCommerce, Status flags theme template overrides older than your WooCommerce version. Outdated overrides can break product pages, the cart or checkout after an update.

Payment methods and shipping

On a phone, test UPI, cards, net banking, wallets and Cash on Delivery if offered. Try addresses in different zones or pin codes, check free-shipping thresholds and compare your rates with what your courier actually charges.

Payment reconciliation

Export the month's settlements from your gateway and compare them with completed orders. You are looking for:

  • Payments with no matching order, or orders marked paid with no settlement
  • Duplicate payments for one order
  • Refunds issued in WooCommerce but not processed at the gateway, or the other way round
  • Gateway fees and any GST on those fees, for your accountant
  • COD remittances from your courier matched against delivered COD orders

GST invoice spot-check

Open a handful of invoices, including an intra-state and an inter-state order, and check the GSTIN, HSN codes, invoice numbering sequence, and that tax is split as CGST and SGST or charged as IGST as expected. Rules and formats are for your CA to confirm; the check is that the store is still producing what was agreed after the month's updates. GST invoices for WooCommerce stores covers the setup.

Speed

Test a product page, the cart and checkout on mobile. New plugins, larger images and tracking scripts creep in over time. WooCommerce speed optimisation covers what to fix when pages slow down.

Housekeeping

  • Scheduled actions: under WooCommerce, Status, Scheduled Actions, a growing pile of failed or past-due actions points to a problem worth investigating.
  • Coupons and sales: remove expired coupons and check scheduled sale prices start and end as planned.

Quarterly: restore, audit and clean

  • Test a restore of a recent backup on staging. An untested backup may fail when you need it most.
  • Audit plugins and extensions: remove what you no longer use, and check premium licences are active and in your name so updates keep arriving.
  • Staff accounts: remove people who have left, and give order staff the Shop Manager role rather than Administrator.
  • Security review: admin users, two-factor login, file changes and checkout scripts. The WooCommerce security checklist goes further.
  • Database cleanup: clear expired transients and old sessions, review which tables are growing, and decide what to do with years of old orders and logs. See database optimisation.
  • PHP version and hosting resources: a store needs more capacity as the catalogue and order history grow.
  • Tax, invoice and policy settings: confirm GST settings with your CA if anything has changed, and check that shipping, returns and privacy policies still match how you work.
  • Renewals: domain, hosting, SSL and any paid gateway, shipping or invoicing services.

Backups and restores work differently for stores

Restoring yesterday's database on a store would wipe every order, customer account and stock change since. So:

  • Back up more often, or use a service that saves database changes in near real time
  • Never restore the whole live database to fix a design problem; restore only affected files, or fix it on staging
  • Never push a staging database over the live store without a plan for orders placed in between
  • If you must restore after a disaster, export recent orders first

Before festival sales and big campaigns

A couple of weeks before Diwali, Raksha Bandhan, an end-of-season sale or a large Instagram campaign:

  1. Finish updates, then freeze non-essential changes until the sale ends
  2. Test coupons, sale prices and free-shipping offers exactly as customers will use them
  3. Update stock and confirm your courier can handle extra pickups
  4. Ask your host whether your plan can cope with a traffic spike
  5. Take a fresh backup, and make sure someone is available if checkout breaks

Before a sale goes live, also update the sale page's search snippet; see the flash sale collection meta title and description templates.

Keep a maintenance log

A shared sheet with the date, task, plugin versions before and after, tests run and any issues. When something breaks, the log answers "what changed last?" in seconds, and it makes a handover to a new developer far easier.

FAQs

How often should I update WooCommerce?

Security updates as soon as they have been tested; regular updates monthly, on staging first, followed by a test order on the live store. Avoid updating just before or during a big sale.

Why is an order pending when the customer has paid?

Usually the gateway's webhook or callback did not reach your site, so WooCommerce never heard the payment succeeded. Check the gateway dashboard and logs before marking the order paid manually.

How often should a WooCommerce store be backed up?

More often than a brochure site, because orders change the database all day. Busy stores benefit from near-real-time database backups, with copies stored off the hosting server.

What is payment reconciliation in WooCommerce?

Comparing gateway settlements and COD remittances with your completed and refunded orders, to catch missing, duplicate or mismatched payments early.

Do I need to check GST invoices after updates?

A quick spot-check is wise, because invoice plugins and tax settings can be affected by updates. Confirm rates and formats with your CA.

What should a WooCommerce maintenance checklist include?

Daily order, payment and stock checks; a weekly test order and backup check; monthly updates on staging, payment and shipping tests, reconciliation and a GST invoice spot-check; and a quarterly restore test, user audit and clean-up. Keep a log so each task has a date and an owner.
