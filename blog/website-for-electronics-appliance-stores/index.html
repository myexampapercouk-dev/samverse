Websites for Electronics and Home Appliance Stores: Catalogue, Offers and Service

What electronics and home appliance stores need online: a spec-led catalogue, careful EMI and offer wording, installation and warranty details, and WhatsApp.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Build the catalogue around the specs buyers compare, with exact model numbers, dimensions and warranty terms.
  • Word EMI, cashback and exchange offers precisely, with lenders, dates and conditions, and remove expired offers.
  • Clear installation, delivery and branch details plus WhatsApp price checks turn online research into store sales.

People buying a TV, fridge, washing machine or AC rarely walk in cold. They compare models and prices on their phones, read reviews, check offers and only then decide where to buy. Many still choose a local store for delivery, installation, an exchange deal or simply someone to call if something goes wrong. Your website's job is to make sure they find you during that research and feel confident enough to visit, call or message.

This guide is for consumer electronics and home appliance retailers. If you mainly sell laptops, desktops and networking equipment to businesses, see websites for computer and IT hardware dealers instead.

How appliance buyers shop

  • They know roughly what they want (a 1.5 ton AC, a 55-inch TV, a front-load washer) but not which model
  • They compare specs, energy ratings and running costs, not just the price
  • They look for offers: bank discounts, EMI options and exchange deals
  • They want to know when it can be delivered and who will install it
  • The decision is often made by the whole family, by sharing links on WhatsApp

So your site needs accurate product information, clear offer terms, practical service details and a quick way to ask a question.

A catalogue built around real decisions

Organise products by category (TVs, refrigerators, washing machines, ACs, kitchen appliances, small appliances and audio) and then by the specs people actually use to choose:

  • Screen size and display type for TVs
  • Capacity in litres for fridges and in kilograms for washing machines
  • Tonnage, and inverter or non-inverter, for ACs
  • Energy star rating, brand and price band

On each product page, give the exact model number, key specs taken from the manufacturer and double-checked, dimensions (will it fit the kitchen niche?), what's in the box, power and installation requirements, and warranty terms. Add a short note in your own words on who the model suits, instead of pasting the brand's spec sheet.

Buying guides help too: "which AC tonnage for my room", "front-load vs top-load washing machines" or "what size fridge for a family of four". Keep them practical, and point to the manufacturer's guidance where the answer depends on room size, sunlight or usage.

Decide early whether to sell online or show a catalogue with enquiry buttons. Some brands have their own policies on how dealers advertise prices or sell online, so check your dealer agreements first. A catalogue with "get the best price" and WhatsApp buttons is a sensible start for many stores.

Offers, EMI and prices: word them carefully

Offers sell appliances, but loosely worded offers cause complaints and can mislead. Advertising in India is covered by consumer protection law, and the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) publishes guidelines on misleading claims, so check the current rules with your lawyer. Some sensible habits:

Risky wordingClearer approach
"No-cost EMI on everything"Name the eligible cards or finance partners and tenures, and mention that processing fees and approval conditions may apply as per the lender
"Flat cashback"State the bank, the minimum purchase, the validity dates and when the cashback is credited
"Lowest price guaranteed"Avoid it unless you have a written policy you can honour
"Best exchange value"Explain that exchange value depends on the old appliance's brand, age and condition
"Free installation"Say what standard installation covers and what costs extra

Put every offer on an offers page with start and end dates, link to the full terms, and remove expired offers promptly. Only name banks, finance companies and brands you actually have arrangements with, and make it clear whether prices include GST, delivery and installation.

Delivery, installation and warranty information

After-sales service is often the reason people buy from a local store, so make it easy to find:

  • Delivery: the areas you serve, typical delivery times, and whether you deliver to upper floors in buildings without lifts
  • Installation: who installs each category (your team or the brand's service partner), how soon, what standard installation includes, and typical extras such as additional AC piping, stands or wall mounts
  • Demo and setup: for TVs, washing machines and kitchen appliances
  • Warranty: the manufacturer's warranty and how to register and claim it, plus any extended warranty you sell and exactly who provides it
  • Exchange and disposal: how you take back old appliances
  • Invoices: a GST invoice for every sale, which customers often need for warranty claims

A short FAQ covering each of these cuts down repetitive calls.

Store locations and local pages

Searches like "electronics shop near me", "AC dealer in {area}" and "washing machine showroom in {city}" are where local stores can win. If you have more than one branch, give each its own page with the address, map, opening hours, phone and WhatsApp number, parking, photos and the categories or brands on display there. Match each page to its own Google Business Profile, and keep hours updated during festival seasons. The guide to websites for businesses with multiple branches explains how to do this without thin, duplicate pages.

WhatsApp enquiries and quick quotes

Many appliance buyers would rather message than call. Make that easy:

  • A "check price on WhatsApp" button on every product, with the model number pre-filled in the message
  • A short callback form for people who prefer a phone call
  • Replies within business hours that include the price, offer terms and delivery date in one message
  • Tracking, so you know which products and pages bring enquiries

See adding WhatsApp to your business website for setup tips. For Diwali, festive and summer AC sales, a dedicated landing page with the offers, terms and a single enquiry button works better than a homepage banner; planning seasonal campaigns covers the timing.

Trust, SEO and speed

  • Real photos of your showroom, team and delivery vans, not only brand images
  • Authorised dealer status only for brands where it's true, and brand logos used only with permission
  • Google reviews that mention delivery, installation and service
  • Your GST number, full address and contact details on the About and Contact pages
  • Product schema with price and availability, kept accurate as offers change
  • Compressed images and a lightweight theme, because large catalogues slow down quickly on mobile
