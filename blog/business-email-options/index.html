Business Email Options for Small Businesses: Google Workspace vs Microsoft 365 vs Zoho Mail vs Hosting Email

Compare business email options: Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, the Zoho Mail free plan and hosting email like Hostinger, plus how to choose and set up.

Key takeaways
  • Google Workspace suits teams who live in Gmail and Google Docs, Microsoft 365 suits Outlook and desktop Office users, and Zoho Mail is the budget choice with a small free plan.
  • Email that comes with web hosting is cheap but tied to your hosting account, so a hosting problem or migration can take your email down with it.
  • Whichever you pick, set up MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records properly, use aliases or groups for info@ and sales@, and keep the account in the business name.

For most small businesses, the choice comes down to four business email options. Google Workspace is the best fit if your team already uses Gmail, Google Docs and Drive. Microsoft 365 suits businesses that rely on Outlook, Word and Excel. Zoho Mail is the budget option, popular in India, with a small free plan. Email included with your web hosting, such as Hostinger's, is the cheapest but the least independent. My general advice: keep email separate from website hosting, so a hosting problem or a move never takes your email down.

Below I compare the options on cost model, features and the catches, then cover setup and switching. Email problems often surface when a website changes host, and checking that site emails actually arrive is part of every health check on my WordPress maintenance plans.

Google Workspace

Workspace gives you Gmail at your own domain, with Google's spam filtering, Calendar, Meet, Drive and Docs. Its strengths are reliability, search and easy sharing; the downside is the per-user price, which adds up as the team grows. Each user can have up to 30 aliases at the time of writing, so one person can receive mail for info@ and sales@ without paying for extra mailboxes.

Business or Enterprise editions?

Google sells Workspace in Business editions (Starter, Standard and Plus) for organisations up to 300 users, and Enterprise editions for larger organisations or those that need advanced security and compliance, such as data loss prevention, data regions and advanced device management. Like all the options here, it's a SaaS (software as a service) product: you pay a subscription and Google runs the servers. Almost every small business is well served by a Business edition; Business Plus adds Vault for retaining and searching email for legal purposes.

Microsoft 365

Microsoft 365 provides Exchange email through Outlook, plus Teams, OneDrive and the Office apps. The basic business plan includes web and mobile versions of Word and Excel; desktop apps come with higher plans. Choose it if your team works in Excel and Word files all day, or your clients expect Teams meetings. Shared mailboxes for addresses like accounts@ don't need a paid licence within Microsoft's size limits at the time of writing, which keeps costs down.

Zoho Mail

Zoho is an Indian company with data centres in India, and Zoho Mail is the most affordable of the three big providers. At the time of writing, its Forever Free plan covers up to five users on one domain with a modest amount of storage each, but only through the web and Zoho's mobile apps, without IMAP or POP access for other email apps. The paid Mail Lite and Premium plans add storage, email app access and more admin features at a low price per user. It's a sensible choice for small teams that mostly need email, and Zoho Workplace adds documents and meetings if you need them.

Email from your web hosting

Many hosts, including Hostinger and most cPanel hosts, include mailboxes or sell them cheaply. That's fine for one or two basic addresses, but there are catches:

  • Tied to hosting. If the hosting expires, is suspended or is migrated carelessly, email stops too.
  • Shared reputation. On shared servers, another customer sending spam can hurt delivery for everyone.
  • Weaker filtering and smaller mailboxes than the big providers, though this varies by host.

If you're on Hostinger and want to use its email, setting up business email on Hostinger walks through it step by step.

Other options

Privacy-focused services such as Proton Mail suit teams that want encrypted email. Some domain registrars resell Microsoft 365 or their own branded email; before buying, check whose service it actually is and whether you can move it later.

How to choose

  1. Which tools does your team already use? Gmail and Docs point to Workspace; Outlook and Excel point to Microsoft 365.
  2. How many real mailboxes do you need? Use aliases, groups or shared mailboxes for role addresses instead of paying for each one.
  3. Do you need email apps such as Outlook or the iPhone Mail app? That rules out Zoho's free plan.
  4. How much does reliable delivery matter? If quotes and invoices go by email, avoid relying on cheap shared hosting mail.
  5. Who will manage it? Make sure the admin account is in the business's name, not a staff member's or developer's, and add the renewal to your renewal checklist.

Setting up business email on your domain

  1. Sign up with the provider and verify your domain, usually with a TXT record.
  2. Create the mailboxes, aliases and groups.
  3. Change the MX records in your DNS to the provider's, and remove the old ones.
  4. Add SPF, DKIM and DMARC records so your mail isn't marked as spam.
  5. Migrate old email with the provider's import tool, then set up phones and computers.
  6. Send test emails to Gmail and Outlook addresses and check they land in the inbox.

The SPF record names the services allowed to send as your domain. Each provider gives its own value, and you should have only one SPF record, combining services if needed. Typical examples at the time of writing:

Google Workspace:  v=spf1 include:_spf.google.com ~all
Microsoft 365:     v=spf1 include:spf.protection.outlook.com -all
Zoho Mail (India): v=spf1 include:zoho.in ~all

SPF, DKIM and DMARC explained covers all three records and how to check them.

Switching email providers without losing mail

  • Create every mailbox and alias at the new provider before touching DNS.
  • Lower the TTL on your MX records a day ahead, so the change spreads quickly.
  • Switch the MX records, update SPF and add the new DKIM record.
  • Migrate old mail, and keep the old account for a few weeks to catch stragglers.

Moving website hosting at the same time? Copy every DNS record, not just the website ones; domain, hosting and SSL explained shows how the pieces connect, and my website migration service handles email records as part of the move.

Don't forget your website's emails

Contact forms and WooCommerce order emails are sent by your website, not your mailbox. Send them through an authenticated SMTP service or your email provider, or they often land in spam; see WordPress not sending emails.

FAQs

Which business email is best for a small business in India?

Google Workspace if your team likes Gmail and Google Docs, Microsoft 365 if you work in Outlook and Office, and Zoho Mail if cost matters most. Hosting email works for one or two basic mailboxes but is the least independent.

Is Zoho Mail really free for business email?

Zoho offers a free plan for a small number of users on one domain at the time of writing, with web and mobile app access only. If you need Outlook or another email app, more storage or more users, you'll need a paid plan. Check Zoho's site for current limits.

Google Workspace or Microsoft 365?

Both are reliable with excellent deliverability. Choose Workspace for browser-based collaboration in Docs and Drive, and Microsoft 365 if your work depends on Excel, Word or Teams, or you need desktop Office apps.

Can I use Gmail with my own domain for free?

Not properly. Google Workspace is the paid way to use Gmail with your domain. Workarounds that connect a hosting mailbox to a free Gmail account are less reliable, and Google has been withdrawing Gmail's option to fetch mail from other accounts.

What is the difference between an alias, a group and a shared mailbox?

An alias is an extra address that delivers into one person's mailbox. A group, or distribution list, sends each message to several people. A shared mailbox is a separate inbox that several people can open and reply from. Aliases and groups are usually free; whether shared mailboxes cost extra depends on the provider.

Should my email be with my web host?

It can be for a very small business, but separating them is safer. With email at Google, Microsoft or Zoho, changing or fixing your hosting never affects your inbox.
