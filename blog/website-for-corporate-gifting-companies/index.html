Website for Corporate Gifting Companies: Catalogues, Branding and Bulk Quotes

What a corporate gifting website needs: a catalogue by occasion and budget, clear branding options, festival campaign pages and a quote form for bulk orders.

Sameer Gupta 28 September 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Organise the catalogue by occasion, budget per piece and product type, and show minimum order quantity, branding methods and lead time on each product.
  • A quote form asking for quantity, budget, logo, deadline and delivery addresses, plus an enquiry cart and WhatsApp, turns visitors into bulk orders.
  • Publish Diwali and year-end gifting pages early on a permanent URL, with a clear last date to order for branded gifts.

Corporate gifting is a different business from a gift shop. Your buyers are HR teams, admin and procurement staff, marketing managers and founders, and they are ordering dozens or thousands of pieces with a logo, a budget per head and a fixed deadline. They usually compare several vendors before choosing one, so your website has to answer their practical questions quickly and make asking for a quote effortless. If you mainly sell flowers and gifts to individual shoppers, see websites for florists and gift shops instead.

What corporate buyers want to know first

BuyerTypical needWhat they look for on your site
HR and people teamsFestival gifts, welcome kits, work anniversary and award giftsIdeas by occasion, budget bands, delivery to every employee
Marketing and events teamsBranded merchandise for conferences, trade shows and launchesBranding options, lead times, quantities
Admin and procurementA reliable vendor with proper paperworkGST invoices, company details, payment terms
Founders and sales teamsPremium gifts for key clientsCurated hampers, packaging, personal notes

Across all of them, the same questions come up: what can I get within my budget, what is the minimum order quantity, can you add our logo, and can you deliver by my date? Answer those on your pages rather than making buyers ask.

A catalogue organised the way buyers shop

Corporate buyers rarely start with a specific product. They start with an occasion and a budget, so organise your catalogue around both:

  • By occasion: Diwali, New Year, employee onboarding, work anniversaries, client appreciation, events and conferences
  • By budget per piece: a few price bands that match how you actually quote
  • By product type: drinkware, bags, tech accessories, stationery, apparel, hampers and eco-friendly gifts
  • By recipient: employees, clients, event attendees

Each product page should show real photos (including a branded sample), the minimum order quantity, available branding methods, typical production time, packaging options and either tiered pricing or a clear "request a quote" button. Because prices depend on quantity, branding and packaging, most gifting businesses work on quotes. A WooCommerce site can combine a browsable catalogue with an enquiry cart, and still sell small ready-made orders online if that suits you.

A downloadable PDF catalogue is still useful for procurement teams who circulate options internally, but keep every product on a proper web page too. Web pages can be found on Google and updated in minutes; a PDF goes out of date the moment prices or stock change.

Make branding and customisation clear

Branding is a big part of what buyers are paying for, so explain it properly:

  • List the methods you offer, such as screen printing, laser engraving, embroidery, UV printing or debossing, and which products each suits
  • Show close-up photos of finished branded work, using client logos only where you have permission
  • Explain your mock-up process: when buyers see a digital proof, and how many revisions are included
  • Offer custom packaging, sleeves and printed message cards, plus personalisation with individual names if you can do it
  • Say which logo files you need, typically a vector file such as AI, EPS, SVG or PDF

A quote form that wins bulk orders

Your quote form is where enquiries become orders. Ask for enough detail to send a useful quote in your first reply:

  1. Products or occasion, or a "suggest options for me" choice
  2. Quantity and budget per piece
  3. Branding needed, with a logo upload
  4. Required delivery date
  5. Delivery to one office, several offices or individual home addresses
  6. Company name, contact person, phone and email

Let buyers shortlist several products and send them in one enquiry, rather than filling in a form for each. Add a WhatsApp button for quick questions, since many buyers prefer it for follow-ups; see WhatsApp on your business website. An automatic reply that says when you'll respond reassures buyers who are contacting several vendors at once.

Plan for Diwali and year-end peaks

For many gifting businesses, a large share of the year's orders arrives in the weeks around Diwali and the New Year, and companies often shortlist vendors well before then. Prepare early:

  • Publish a dedicated Diwali gifting page before buyers start searching, and update the same page each year instead of creating a new URL, so it builds up rankings over time
  • Show a clear "last date to order" for branded gifts, based on your real production and delivery times
  • Build focused landing pages for Google Ads or LinkedIn campaigns, with one offer and one quote form
  • Test the site's speed and forms before the rush, not during it

For a full planning timeline, see seasonal and festival campaigns.

Trust signals for business buyers

A company placing a large order is taking a risk on a vendor. Reduce that risk with:

  • Your registered business name, GST details, office address and phone number
  • Photos of your workspace, packing area and team
  • Short project stories: the brief, quantity, branding method and how you met the deadline
  • Your sample policy, including whether samples are charged
  • Clear payment terms, cancellation rules and what happens if items arrive damaged
  • Genuine reviews and, with permission, logos of companies you have supplied

SEO and speed

  • Target specific searches such as "corporate gifts in {city}", "Diwali corporate gifts", "employee welcome kit" and "customised gifts with logo"
  • Write your own product descriptions; many resellers use the same supplier photos and text, which gives Google little reason to rank one over another
  • Add helpful guides, such as gift ideas by budget or what to put in an onboarding kit
  • Keep your Google Business Profile updated if buyers can visit your office or showroom
  • Compress product images, because a single catalogue page can easily hold dozens of photos

WordPress with WooCommerce handles a large gifting catalogue, enquiry carts and seasonal campaign pages in one place your team can update. If you're planning a new site, see WooCommerce development, and for festival ad campaigns, landing page design.
