Websites for Custom T-Shirt Printing Businesses: Design Tools, Bulk Orders and Payments

How to plan a custom t-shirt printing website in India: product options, design upload, bulk quote forms, UPI and COD, GST, shipping and policies.

Key takeaways
  • A custom t-shirt printing website must let buyers choose product, size, colour and print method, upload artwork and see the price.
  • Bulk and corporate orders need their own quote form, and policies on artwork, proofs and returns prevent most disputes.
  • Shopify and WooCommerce both work; the choice depends on how complex your options and pricing rules are.

A custom t-shirt printing website in India should let customers choose a product, size, colour and print method, upload their design or create one in a simple tool, see the price, and pay by UPI, card or COD where you allow it. Bulk and corporate orders need a separate quote form. Both Shopify and WooCommerce can do this well; the right choice depends on how complex your options and pricing are.

This guide covers the features, platform choice, product structure, bulk orders, payments, shipping and the policies a printing business should make clear.

Who uses a custom t-shirt printing website

  • Individuals ordering one or a few printed tees as gifts, for birthdays or trips.
  • Colleges, clubs and events needing matching t-shirts for fests, marathons, sports teams and reunions.
  • Companies ordering branded polos and hoodies for staff, launches and giveaways.
  • Brands and creators wanting merchandise, sometimes print-on-demand without holding stock.

Individuals want an easy online order. Groups and companies want a quote, samples, size breakdowns and a firm delivery date. The site must handle both journeys without confusing either.

Product structure and options

Keep the catalogue simple. Create one product per base garment, such as round neck cotton tee, polo, oversized tee or hoodie, and use options for the rest:

  • Size: with a clear size chart in centimetres or inches.
  • Colour: with real photos of each colour, not just swatches.
  • Print method: such as screen print, DTF, DTG, sublimation or vinyl, explained in plain words.
  • Print position: front, back, sleeve, with price differences shown.
  • Quantity: with price breaks shown if you offer them.

Explain each print method on a separate page: what it suits, how it feels, durability and minimum quantities. Customers rarely know the difference, and a clear guide reduces questions and returns.

Design upload and online design tools

There are three common approaches:

  1. Artwork upload: the customer uploads a logo or image with notes. Simple and reliable; you send a proof before printing.
  2. Online customiser: the customer adds text, clipart or images onto a t-shirt preview. Great for individuals, but customisers can be heavy and slow on mobile, so test carefully.
  3. Design help: the customer describes what they want and your team creates the design, with a proof for approval.

Whatever you choose, state the artwork requirements clearly: preferred file types, minimum resolution, and what happens if the file is low quality. A proof approval step, by email or WhatsApp, prevents most disputes.

Bulk and corporate orders

Bulk orders are often the most valuable part of a printing business and rarely fit a normal cart. Give them a dedicated page and quote form with fields for:

  • Garment type and preferred fabric.
  • Total quantity and size breakdown.
  • Number of print colours and positions.
  • Logo or artwork upload.
  • Delivery city and required date.
  • GST details if they need a tax invoice.

A multi-step form keeps this manageable; see RFQ forms for B2B websites. Show past bulk work for colleges, events and companies, with permission, and explain sampling, approval and production steps on the page.

Payments, GST and COD in India

Offer UPI, cards and netbanking through a gateway such as Razorpay, Cashfree or PayU, all of which work with both WooCommerce and Shopify. See payment gateways on Shopify in India for the Shopify side.

COD is a harder choice for custom products, because a refused parcel cannot be resold. Common approaches include prepaid only for custom prints, COD for plain or ready-designed stock, or a partial advance for larger orders. Whatever you choose, state it clearly on product pages and at checkout. The guide on cash on delivery on Shopify in India covers the setup.

Corporate buyers usually need GST invoices with their GSTIN. Make sure your checkout can capture it and your invoices are set up correctly; ask your CA about the correct tax treatment for your products.

Shipping and production time

Customers often order for a fixed date, such as a trip, birthday or event. Be clear about:

  • Production time after proof approval, separate from shipping time.
  • Courier options and delivery estimates by region, through a shipping aggregator such as Shiprocket if you use one.
  • Express options if you offer them.
  • Cut-off dates before festivals and peak seasons.

The guide on Shopify shipping in India covers zones, rates and courier apps, and most of the principles apply to WooCommerce too.

Policies that prevent disputes

Custom printing has more room for disagreement than ready-made products. Publish clear policies on:

  • Returns and refunds for custom items, usually limited to printing errors or damage.
  • Colour differences between screens and fabric.
  • Size exchanges, if any, for printed items.
  • Copyright: customers must own or have rights to the designs they upload; you can refuse designs using other brands' logos or characters.
  • Proof approval and what happens if a customer approves a proof with mistakes.

Link these from product pages and the checkout, not only the footer.

Marketing and repeat orders

Many t-shirt orders come from Instagram, and buyers then move to the website or WhatsApp. Make that path smooth: link Instagram posts to matching product or collection pages, and keep WhatsApp available for quick questions. Show real customer photos and event shots with permission. For bulk clients, a simple reorder process, such as saving their artwork and size breakdown, makes it easy to come back next season.

Collection pages for occasions, such as college fest tees, corporate polos, couple t-shirts or trip tees, help both search and browsing. Make sure product pages load fast on mobile; heavy customiser scripts and large mock-up images are common culprits.

Managing orders behind the scenes

A printing business has more steps between payment and dispatch than a normal store, and the website should support them rather than leave everything in WhatsApp chats and notebooks. Useful order stages include:

  1. Awaiting artwork, when a customer paid but has not uploaded a usable file.
  2. Proof sent, waiting for the customer to approve.
  3. Approved, in production, with the expected completion date.
  4. Ready to ship, with label and tracking created.
  5. Shipped, with tracking shared automatically.

WooCommerce supports custom order statuses through plugins or a little custom code, and Shopify can handle similar stages using order tags, notes and apps. Either way, automatic emails or WhatsApp updates at each stage cut down on "where is my order" messages.

Keep the uploaded artwork attached to the order so production staff never have to search for files. For bulk clients, store the final approved artwork and size breakdown so a reorder next year is quick. If you outsource printing or work with a print-on-demand partner, check how orders and files pass between the systems before launch, and do a few test orders end to end.

FAQs

How do I build a custom t-shirt printing website in India?

Choose Shopify or WooCommerce, set up products with size, colour and print options, add artwork upload or a design tool, create a bulk quote form, and set up UPI, card and shipping.

Is Shopify or WooCommerce better for t-shirt printing?

Shopify is quicker to launch and maintain. WooCommerce offers more control for complex options and pricing. Both handle Indian payments and shipping.

Should I offer COD for custom t-shirts?

Many printers keep custom prints prepaid or take an advance, and allow COD only on ready designs, because refused custom parcels cannot be resold.

Do I need an online design tool?

Not always. Artwork upload with a proof step works well, especially for bulk orders. A customiser suits individual buyers but must be tested for speed on mobile.

How should I handle bulk t-shirt orders?

Use a separate quote form with quantity, size breakdown, print details, artwork upload, delivery date and GST details, followed by a sample or proof.

If you want a t-shirt printing store set up properly, see Shopify developer or WooCommerce developer services, and the WordPress and Shopify developer in India page.

What decides the cost of a custom t-shirt printing website?

Mainly the design tool or upload method, how many option combinations you sell, bulk pricing rules, payment and shipping integrations, and how many products you launch with. A store with simple uploads costs less than one with a full online designer.
