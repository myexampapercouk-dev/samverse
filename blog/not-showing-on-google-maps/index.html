Business Not Showing on Google Maps? What to Check and Fix

Why a business isn't showing on Google Maps or the local pack: verification, suspensions, duplicates, category, service areas, distance, reviews and NAP issues.

Key takeaways
  • First work out whether it is a profile problem (unverified, suspended, duplicate) or a ranking problem.
  • Check the primary category, business name, map pin and service-area settings before anything else.
  • Google does not publish exact ranking weights; distance cannot be changed, but reviews, consistent details and your website can.

You've set up a Google Business Profile, but when you search for your service you're nowhere on the map. Or you were there last month and now you've vanished. Before changing everything at once, work through the likely causes in order. Google doesn't publish exact ranking weights for local results, so nobody can promise a fix, but most "invisible on Maps" problems come down to a handful of things you can check yourself.

If the profile isn't fully set up yet, start with the Google Business Profile checklist. This guide is for troubleshooting a profile that exists but isn't showing.

First, check what's really happening

"Not showing" can mean quite different things:

  • You don't appear even for your exact business name. That points to a profile problem: unverified, suspended, duplicated or not yet published.
  • You appear for your name but not for "{service} near me". The profile works; it's a ranking question of category, distance, reviews and website.
  • You appear in some places but not others. That's usually distance, and it's normal.

Your own results are influenced by where you are and what you've searched before, so one search from your office tells you little. Search on your phone in a few locations, and ask customers in different areas what they see. If you can't find yourself by name at all, see how to rank for your own business name.

Is the profile verified and active?

Sign in to the Google account that manages the profile and check its status.

  • Not verified, or verification pending: unverified profiles generally don't show reliably. Complete whichever verification method Google offers you, which is often a short video showing your premises, signboard and proof of business.
  • Newly verified or recently edited: changes can take a while to go live, and some edits are reviewed first. Lots of edits in quick succession can trigger further reviews.
  • Suspended or disabled: Google may suspend a profile it believes breaks its guidelines, for example a keyword-stuffed name, an ineligible address, or an address shown for a business customers can't visit. Fix the cause first, then submit a reinstatement request. Don't create a new profile to get around it.
  • Lost access: if a former employee or agency set it up, request ownership through Google's process rather than creating a second listing.

Duplicates and outdated listings

Two listings for the same business split your reviews and confuse Google about which one is real. Duplicates often come from:

  • An old profile at a previous address
  • A listing created by a staff member, a previous agency or a customer
  • Separate profiles for a practice and an individual doctor, lawyer or consultant, where Google has specific rules about what's allowed

Search Maps for your name, phone number and old addresses. Report true duplicates through Google's support options, and ask for an old-address listing to be removed or merged rather than leaving it live.

Category, name and details

  • Primary category: one of the strongest relevance signals you control. A physiotherapy clinic listed only as "Medical clinic", or a caterer listed as "Restaurant", will struggle for the searches that matter. Pick the most specific match and add genuine secondary categories.
  • Business name: use your real trading name. Adding "Best AC Repair Pune" may bring a short-term lift, then a suspension when a competitor reports it.
  • Services and description: list what you actually do, in the words customers use.
  • Hours: wrong hours cost you calls and visits even when you do appear, so keep them and holiday hours accurate.

Address, service area and the map pin

  • Service-area businesses: if you hide your address there's no public pin, but the profile is still tied to the location you verified, and you'll usually show most strongly near it. Adding more service areas doesn't make you appear across all of them. See SEO for service-area businesses.
  • Shops and offices: check the pin sits on your actual building and entrance. A misplaced pin can put you in the wrong neighbourhood.
  • Ineligible addresses: virtual offices, PO boxes and shared spaces without your own staffed presence and signage are generally not allowed, and are a common reason for suspension.

Proximity: you can't rank everywhere

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. It doesn't publish how much each one counts, and the balance varies from search to search. What is clear is that distance matters a great deal, and it's the one factor you can't change.

  • A clinic in one part of a city rarely appears in the map pack for someone searching from the far side, however good its profile
  • "Near me" searches are judged from the searcher's location; a search that names an area is judged against that area
  • In crowded categories, the map pack may only reach a small radius around the searcher

Beyond that radius, your website ranking in the normal results is usually the better route: strong service pages, plus genuine area pages where you really have work to show.

Reviews, consistency and your website

When relevance and distance are similar, prominence decides, and you can build it steadily.

  • Reviews: a few old reviews against competitors with a steady flow of recent ones is a common gap. Ask every happy customer, reply to every review and never buy them; see how to get more Google reviews ethically.
  • NAP consistency: your name, address and phone number should match on your profile, website, Justdial, IndiaMART and other listings. An old number or address on several directories muddies the picture.
  • Your website: Google also draws on information about you from across the web. Link the profile to a relevant page that mentions your services and location, shows the same contact details, includes LocalBusiness schema and is indexed in Google.

A sensible order to work through

  1. Confirm the profile is verified, published and not suspended
  2. Find and deal with duplicates
  3. Fix the primary category, name and pin
  4. Make your details consistent across the web
  5. Build reviews and improve the linked page on your website
  6. Watch calls, direction requests and website clicks in the profile's performance data for a couple of months before judging

Be wary of anyone who guarantees a top-three map position. Nobody outside Google controls the ranking.
