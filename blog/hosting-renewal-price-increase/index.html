Hosting Renewal Price Shock: Why Your Hosting Renewal Costs So Much More, and What to Do About It

Hosting renewal charges much higher than you paid? Why intro prices end, how add-ons and GST add up, and when to renew, negotiate, downgrade or move hosts.

Key takeaways
  • The advertised hosting price is usually an introductory discount for the first term, often tied to paying for several years upfront; renewal is at the regular price, plus 18% GST.
  • Before renewing, remove add-ons you do not use, check whether a smaller plan fits, ask for a retention offer and compare the total cost over three to five years.
  • Moving hosts can save money, but the next host will renew at its regular price too; move for better speed and support, and never let the old plan lapse mid-move.

Hosting renewals cost more because the price you first paid was an introductory discount. Most hosts advertise a low monthly figure that applies only to the first term, usually when you pay for two to four years upfront, and then renew at the regular price, which can be several times higher. Add-ons bundled free for the first year, the end of a "free" domain and 18% GST on top make the renewal invoice look even worse. It's normal industry practice rather than a scam, but you don't have to accept the first invoice.

Before you click renew, check what you're paying for, ask for a better deal and compare the real cost of staying with the cost of moving. Where the renewal charges come from

Here's how a typical hosting bill changes between the first term and renewal:

Line itemFirst termAt renewal
Hosting planIntroductory price, lowest on the longest termRegular price, often several times the intro rate
DomainOften free for the first yearPaid every year, roughly ₹500 to ₹1,200 for a .com or .in
SSL certificateFree certificate includedUsually still free; paid upgrades are rarely needed
Business emailFree mailboxes or a trial on some plansCharged per mailbox once the offer ends
Backups, malware scanning, privacySometimes added at checkout or bundled freeRenewed automatically, often at full price
GST18%, sometimes left out of the advertised price18% on the full renewal amount

The advertised monthly price is often the total for a multi-year term divided by the number of months, so the "per month" figure you remember may never have been a monthly payment at all.

Why hosts price it this way

Shared hosting is a competitive market, and the introductory price is how hosts win new customers. They expect to earn the money back at renewal, because most customers don't move: migrating a website feels risky, and many owners don't know how. That's why the deepest discounts are on the longest terms, and why renewal prices are shown in smaller print. Some hosts, Hostinger among them, display the renewal price next to the offer at the time of writing; it's worth reading before you buy.

Step 1: Check what you're actually paying for

  1. Log into your hosting account and open the billing or services section.
  2. List every service due for renewal: hosting, domains, email, backups, security add-ons, domain privacy, SEO tools.
  3. Turn off auto-renew on anything you don't use. Paid malware scanners and backup add-ons are often unnecessary if your site has a security plugin and off-site backups.
  4. Check your resource usage in the control panel. If the site uses a fraction of the plan, a smaller plan may do.
  5. Note which domains are registered at the host. You can move domain renewals to a separate registrar; see transferring a domain.

Step 2: Choose your option

  • Renew for a longer term. If you're happy with speed and support, longer renewal terms usually cost less per month. Weigh that against being locked in.
  • Ask for a retention offer. Contact support or sales before the renewal date, say you're considering moving, and ask what they can do. Some hosts offer a discount or a longer term at a better rate; some won't.
  • Downgrade. If you're paying for capacity you don't use, move to a smaller plan before renewal.
  • Move to another host. New-customer pricing elsewhere may be cheaper for the next term, but factor in the migration and the next host's renewal price.
  • Move up instead. If the site is slow or keeps hitting limits, renewal is a good moment to move to better hosting rather than cheaper hosting; see shared vs VPS vs managed WordPress hosting.

Asking for a retention offer

Be brief and specific. Give your account or domain name, say the renewal price is higher than you expected and that you're comparing other hosts, and ask whether they can offer a better rate, a longer term at a lower price or a cheaper plan that suits your usage. Ask a few days before the renewal date, not after it has been charged, and get any offer confirmed in writing in the ticket or chat.

Step 3: Compare the total cost over three to five years

Comparing first-term prices is how the renewal shock happens. For each option, add up:

  1. The first term at the price you'll actually pay
  2. The renewal price for the remaining years
  3. Domain renewals, if they're at the host
  4. Email, if it's billed separately
  5. Add-ons you genuinely need
  6. 18% GST on all of it
  7. For a move: the migration cost or your time, and the risk

Is switching hosts worth it?

Moving every few years to chase introductory pricing can work, but each move takes effort and carries some risk to email, DNS and SSL if it's done carelessly. For a business site, reliability, speed and helpful support usually matter more than saving a small amount a year.

Moving makes sense when:

  • The site is slow, or goes down, and the host can't fix it
  • Support is slow or unhelpful when something breaks
  • The renewal price is well above what comparable hosts charge for similar plans, after counting their renewal prices too
  • You're paying for a bundle of services you don't need

Staying makes sense when the site is fast, support is good and the difference over several years is small. How to choose WordPress hosting in India lists what to check in a new host.

How to move hosting before the renewal date

  1. Start two to four weeks before renewal, so there's time to test without pressure.
  2. Buy the new hosting and note its renewal price this time.
  3. Take a full backup of files and database, and copy every DNS record, including email records.
  4. Migrate the site and test it on the new server before changing DNS, using a temporary address or your computer's hosts file.
  5. Move email if it's hosted with the old host, or better, move it to a separate email provider.
  6. Switch DNS and check the site, SSL, forms and email.
  7. Keep the old plan running for a week or so, then cancel its auto-renew.

Avoid the shock next time

  • Before you buy, write down the renewal price and the date the intro term ends.
  • Set a calendar reminder 30 days before every renewal.
  • Keep the domain at a separate registrar, so hosting decisions never put the domain at risk.
  • Keep the hosting account in the business's name, with a card that won't expire before the renewal.
  • Track all renewals in one place; see the domain, hosting and email renewal checklist.

Whatever you decide, don't let hosting expire while you think about it. Expired hosting takes your website, and any email on it, offline.

FAQs

Why is my hosting renewal so much more expensive?

Because the first term was an introductory discount, often requiring several years paid upfront. Renewal is at the regular price, and add-ons, a domain that was free for the first year and 18% GST all add to the invoice.

Are high hosting renewal charges a scam?

Usually not; it's standard industry pricing, and the renewal price is normally stated somewhere at purchase. It's worth checking the invoice comes from your real host, though, as fake renewal emails exist.

Can I negotiate my hosting renewal price?

Sometimes. Contact the host before the renewal date and ask for a retention offer or a better rate on a longer term. If they refuse, removing unused add-ons or downgrading may still cut the bill.

Is GST charged on web hosting in India?

Yes, hosting and domain services are charged with 18% GST. Some advertised prices exclude it, so check the final checkout amount when comparing hosts. A GST-registered business can usually claim it back as input tax credit; ask your accountant.

What happens if I don't renew my hosting?

Most hosts suspend the account shortly after the due date, which takes the website and any email hosted there offline. After a grace period, files, databases and mailboxes may be deleted for good. If you plan to move, finish the migration and download a full backup before the old plan ends.

Will moving hosts affect my SEO or email?

Not if it's done properly. Keep the same domain and URLs, test the site before switching DNS, copy every DNS record including MX, SPF and DKIM, and keep the old hosting live until everything is confirmed.
