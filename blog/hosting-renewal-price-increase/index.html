Why Your Hosting Renewal Costs So Much More (and What to Do About It)

Why hosting and domain renewals often cost far more than the first term, how introductory pricing works, add-ons to watch, how to budget, and when to renew, negotiate or move hosts.

Sameer Gupta 28 September 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • Introductory hosting prices usually apply to the first term only.
  • Compare total cost over several years and remove unused add-ons.
  • At renewal, renew longer, ask for a deal, downgrade or move carefully, but never let it lapse.

You bought hosting at an attractive price, and two or three years later the renewal invoice is much higher. This is common across popular hosting companies, including big names used by Indian businesses. It isn't usually a scam, but it catches many owners by surprise.

How introductory pricing works

  • The advertised low monthly price usually applies only to the first term
  • The lowest price often requires paying for several years upfront
  • Renewal is at the regular price, which can be several times the introductory rate
  • A "free domain" is typically free for the first year only

Add-ons that increase the bill

  • Domain privacy, backups, security scanners and SSL upgrades added at checkout
  • Email plans billed separately
  • Auto-renewal of add-ons you don't use

Review your account and remove what you don't need before renewal.

How to budget properly

Look up the renewal price before you buy, and compare the total cost over three to five years, not just the first term. Include domain, email and any premium plugins. See WordPress website cost in India.

Your options at renewal

  • Renew for a longer term if you're happy with the service; longer terms usually cost less per month
  • Ask for a discount: some hosts offer retention deals if you contact them
  • Move to another host: new-customer pricing elsewhere may be cheaper, but factor in migration effort and the next renewal
  • Downgrade if you're paying for resources you don't use

Is moving worth it?

Switching hosts every few years just for introductory pricing can work, but each migration carries some risk and effort. For a business site, reliability, speed and support often matter more than saving a small amount. See choosing WordPress hosting and migrating safely.

Don't let it lapse

Whatever you decide, don't let hosting or domain expire while you think about it. An expired account takes your site and email offline. Keep a renewal checklist.
