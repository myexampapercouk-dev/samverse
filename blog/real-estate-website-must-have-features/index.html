Real Estate Website Must-Haves: 14 Features Builders, Developers and Brokers Need to Generate Site Visits

The 14 features a real estate website needs: project pages, floor plans, price on request, site visits, brochures, RERA details, CRM and call tracking.

Key takeaways
  • Build every project page from one template, with configurations, carpet areas, zoomable floor plans, checked location facts, RERA details and a clear price or price-on-request route.
  • Make the next step easy and measurable: site-visit booking with slots, a brochure gated only where it earns a lead, WhatsApp and calls, all landing in one sheet or CRM tagged by source.
  • Builders, plotted developers and brokers need different extras, so launch with the essentials for your business and add calculators, portals and tours only when they solve a real problem.

A real estate website needs fewer features than most builders are sold, but the ones it has must answer a buyer's shortlist questions quickly: which configurations are on offer and at what carpet area, what the plans look like, roughly what it costs, where exactly the site is, whether the project is RERA registered and how to book a visit. Everything else exists to make that visit more likely, and to tell you which ad or channel produced it.

These are the features I build into a real estate website design project: project and property pages, floor plans and price lists, galleries, site-visit booking, gated brochure downloads, location maps, a RERA and approvals section and landing pages for ads. Below I go through 14 features in the order a buyer meets them, then show which matter most for a builder, a portfolio developer, a plotted-land developer and a broker.

For the page structure of a single launch, see real estate project microsites. This guide covers the website as a whole.

Features that help buyers shortlist

Buyers usually compare several projects, often on a phone, before visiting any of them. These six features decide whether yours stays on the list.

1. One page per project, built from one template

Every project gets its own address, such as /projects/project-name/, and every project page shows the same facts in the same order: status, configurations, plans, price, amenities, location, RERA details and the booking button. A fixed template lets a buyer comparing two of your projects find the possession date in the same place on both, and stops your team leaving sections out. Put a status label (under construction, ready to move, completed) at the top.

2. Configurations with carpet area

List each unit type in a small table: configuration, carpet area, any balcony or terrace area shown separately,. State carpet area as defined under RERA rather than only super built-up area, and use one unit of measurement across the site and the brochure.

3. Floor plans that work on a phone

Plans locked inside a PDF are hard to read on a phone. Put the unit plans, a typical floor plan and the site layout on the page as images that open full screen and zoom with two fingers, with the dimensions legible, and offer the PDF as well for printing. For plotted developments, a layout plan showing plot numbers, sizes and road widths does the same job. If you mark sold plots on it, decide who keeps it updated.

4. A price, a starting price or price on request

There are three workable choices, and each needs a little design:

  • Full price list: suits ready-to-move units and plotted land with fixed rates. Say what the price includes and what is extra, such as parking, registration and stamp duty.
  • Starting price: a "from" price for each configuration, next to its carpet area, tells buyers at once whether the project is within reach, so fewer enquiries come from people outside your range.
  • Price on request: state it openly, and let buyers get the price sheet with one tap on WhatsApp or by email, rather than filling in a form and waiting for a call.

Whichever you choose, the price on the website, the brochure and the ads must match, and one person should own updating all three.

5. Location facts you have checked

Show a map, the site office address and distances to the schools, hospitals, stations, highways and business districts buyers ask about, measured rather than guessed. Load the embedded map only when tapped so it doesn't slow the page, and add a directions link that opens Google Maps on the visitor's phone.

6. Walkthroughs and virtual tours

A walkthrough video, a sample flat video or a 360-degree tour lets a buyer in another city, or abroad, judge layout and light before travelling. Embed videos from YouTube so they load only when tapped. Label renders and animated walkthroughs as artist's impressions, and add real site and sample flat footage as soon as it exists.

Features that turn interest into site visits

7. Site-visit booking with slots

A "book a site visit" form should ask for a preferred date, a time slot (morning, afternoon or evening works better than exact times), the number of people and, if you offer it, whether they need a pickup. A confirmation on screen, followed by one on WhatsApp or email, reduces no-shows.

8. A brochure gated only where it earns a lead

Gating everything behind a form annoys buyers who are still comparing, and a gated PDF cannot be found on Google. A balance that works for many builders:

  • Floor plans, amenities and location on the page, open to everyone
  • The full brochure and price sheet behind a short form asking for name, phone and email, sent to the email address given
  • A plain project overview PDF left ungated, with a proper title, so it can appear in search; see SEO for PDFs and brochures

Some developers add OTP verification on the phone number to cut fake leads. It also costs some genuine ones, so add it only if junk enquiries are a real problem.

9. WhatsApp and tap-to-call on every screen

Many buyers would rather message than fill in a form. A WhatsApp button with the project name already in the message, and a phone number that dials on tap, should stay visible on mobile without covering the content.

10. Leads that land in one place, tagged by source

Every form, brochure request and site-visit booking should reach your sales team instantly and also go into one list: a Google Sheet for a small team, or the CRM you already use. Each lead should carry its project, the page it came from and the campaign's UTM tags, and each channel partner needs a tagged link or a partner code field, so the leads they send are attributed to them; connecting forms to a CRM explains the options. A tap on the call button only shows that someone tried to ring; call tracking, which gives each source its own number, shows which calls connected and which campaign produced them.

Features that build trust before the first call

11. RERA details on every project page

RERA generally treats a project page as an advertisement, and advertisements for a registered project generally have to carry its registration number and the address of the state RERA website. Put both near the top of each project page, add a broker's own agent registration number to their pages, and keep a project off the site until it is registered if it needs registration. Some states add requirements such as a QR code, so check your state authority's current rules with your advisers.

12. Completed projects with honest status

Buyers trust a builder who shows what has already been delivered. Give each completed project a page with real photos, the location and its handover status, and keep it after the project sells out, because it becomes your strongest proof for the next launch. Only state possession dates, unit counts or years in business that you can support.

13. Dated construction updates

For an under-construction project, dated site photos every month or quarter answer the question every buyer has: is work actually progressing? A year-old update suggests the opposite, so add this only if someone will keep it going.

14. A dashboard your team can update

Projects, prices, statuses, plans and updates should be editable by your team without a developer. In WordPress this usually means storing projects as a custom post type with a field for each fact, so the template stays consistent; custom post types and fields explains how that works. Marking a configuration as sold out should take a minute, not a support request.

Features that can wait, and ones to skip

  • EMI calculators: useful on project pages if the interest rate, tenure and loan amount are editable and the result is labelled as an estimate. Name partner banks only if you genuinely have tie-ups. Lead generation calculators shows how to keep the assumptions honest.
  • A buyer login: a portal showing payment schedules, documents and construction updates for booked customers can be valuable for larger developers, but it is a custom build with its own security and data protection responsibilities. Add it after the public site is working.
  • Pages for NRI buyers: worth it if you genuinely sell abroad, with video-call site visits and callbacks arranged around time zones.
  • Regional languages: a Marathi, Hindi or other language version helps in some markets, but only if your sales team can answer enquiries in that language.
  • Skip: autoplay video headers, heavy sliders, pop-ups that appear before the page has loaded and chatbots that cannot answer price or availability questions. They slow the page and bury the booking button.

Visitors from property ads mostly arrive on phones, so every feature on this list has to load quickly over mobile data. When a campaign needs a page of its own, landing page design covers fast single-project pages built for ads.

FAQs

What is the most important feature on a real estate website?

A complete page for each project, with configurations, carpet areas, floor plans, location, RERA details and a site-visit button. Every other feature supports that page.

Should a builder show prices on the website?

That is your call. A starting price for each configuration filters enquiries by budget, while price on request works well enough if buyers can get the price sheet quickly on WhatsApp or by email. Keep the website, brochure and ads consistent either way.

Is a virtual tour worth it for a project website?

For launches, and for buyers in other cities or abroad, a walkthrough video or 360-degree tour often is, as long as it loads only when tapped and renders are labelled as artist's impressions. For a resale listing, a short phone video is usually enough.

Do I need a CRM for my real estate website?

Not at the start. A Google Sheet that receives every lead with its project and source is enough for a small team. Once several people handle leads across projects and campaigns, a CRM makes follow-up and reporting easier, and website forms can feed it directly.
