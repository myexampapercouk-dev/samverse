Websites for Computer, Laptop and IT Hardware Dealers: Retail and B2B Paths, Large Catalogues, Price on Request, Brand Authorisation, AMC and GST Quotes

Plan a computer dealer website for walk-in buyers and companies: a catalogue that scales, price on request, precise brand status, AMC pages and GST quotes.

Key takeaways
  • Retail and business buyers want different things, so give each a clear path from the homepage: stock and store visits for one, quotes and service for the other.
  • Hardware prices move too often for a fixed price list, so many dealers show specifications with price on request and sell only stable items online.
  • State brand authorisation exactly as your partner agreement allows, and explain warranty, AMC and GST invoicing plainly, because these decide business orders.

A computer and IT hardware dealer's website has to serve two buyers with almost nothing in common: a student who needs a laptop this weekend, and an office manager who needs forty machines, a server and a working network before a team moves in. The site works when each finds their path in one tap, the catalogue is organised the way buyers search, and prices, brand status and after-sales service are described accurately.

For most dealers that means a WooCommerce catalogue that can run in enquiry mode, sell online, or mix the two, which is what I build under WooCommerce development. If you mainly want a credible company site with solution pages and a quote form, without products, a standard WordPress website does the job. If repairs are your main business, see websites for mobile and laptop repair shops instead.

Two buyers, two paths

Retail buyerBusiness buyer
Usually needsOne laptop, desktop, printer, monitor or accessoryBulk devices, servers, storage, networking, licences, support
Checks firstIs it in stock, what does it cost, where is the storeRange, delivery capacity, GST invoices, service, references
Decides withSpecifications, warranty and a store visitA written quote, compared against two or three others
Next stepCall, WhatsApp, visit or buy onlineRequest a quote or book a requirement call
Comes back forAccessories, upgrades, repairsExpansion, AMC renewals, replacements

Put both choices near the top of the homepage, for example "Shop laptops and accessories" and "IT for your business", and give each its own navigation. A retail buyer should not have to scroll past server racks, and a procurement manager should not land in a festive offer banner.

A catalogue built to scale

A dealer catalogue can grow to thousands of products, most of which change or disappear within a year. Plan the structure before adding anything.

Categories and attributes

  • Categories by product type: laptops, desktops and all-in-ones, workstations, servers and storage, networking, printers and scanners, monitors, UPS and power, peripherals, software licences
  • Attributes for filtering: brand, processor family, memory, storage type and size, screen size, operating system, warranty period
  • Use-case collections: laptops for students, for design and video work, for office staff, for gaming, chosen by your team

Consistent attributes are what make filters useful; a filter that shows "8GB", "8 GB" and "8gb RAM" as three options is worse than no filter. WooCommerce product filters covers how to set them up.

Importing and maintaining products

Nobody should add a thousand products by hand. Keep a master spreadsheet with your codes, specifications and stock status, and import or update it in bulk. Decide in advance what happens when a model is discontinued: keep the page with a "no longer available, see these alternatives" note if it gets search traffic, or redirect it to the nearest current model. Deleting pages that people have found through Google throws that traffic away.

Write your own descriptions

Copying manufacturer text makes your pages identical to hundreds of others. A short paragraph in your own words about who a model suits, plus the specification table, is enough to stand apart. Category pages deserve a few useful sentences too, such as what to consider when choosing a business laptop.

Showing prices when they change every week

Hardware prices move with exchange rates, distributor schemes and component shortages. A fixed price published in January can be wrong by February. Dealers generally choose between three approaches, often mixing them by category:

ApproachSuitsHow it appears
Live price, buy onlineAccessories, consumables, stable itemsNormal product page with cart and checkout
Price on requestLaptops, desktops, servers, anything that moves oftenSpecifications, stock status and a "Get best price" button
Catalogue onlyProject items, networking, enterprise kitSpecifications and a link to add to a quote list

WooCommerce can run all three in one store. Whatever you show must be accurate: a price on the site that the store will not honour damages trust and can create consumer complaints. Should you show prices on your website? discusses the trade-offs. Stock status needs the same care; if you cannot keep it accurate, show "check availability" rather than "in stock".

Brand authorisation, stated precisely

Buyers, especially companies, care whether you are an authorised partner, because it affects warranty support, genuine stock and escalation. Getting the wording right matters:

  • Use the exact partner title each brand gives you, and only while that agreement is current
  • If authorisation covers certain product lines or regions only, say so
  • Use brand logos only as the partner programme allows, usually from its official partner kit
  • For brands you sell without authorisation, say plainly that you source from distributors, and explain how warranty claims are handled
  • Remove claims promptly when an agreement lapses

Say clearly that products are new and genuine, and label refurbished or open-box stock as such, with its grading and warranty.

Business quotes that arrive complete

For business buyers, the quote request is the main conversion. Make it possible to send a whole requirement in one go:

  1. A "quote list" where buyers add several products, with quantities, from the catalogue.
  2. A free-text box for items not on the site.
  3. Company name, GSTIN, billing and delivery addresses.
  4. Required delivery date and location.
  5. Services needed: installation, imaging, data migration, cabling, on-site setup.
  6. An upload option for a requirement list or BOQ.

RFQ forms for B2B websites covers layout and follow-up. For regular business customers, WooCommerce can also show account-specific or tiered pricing after login; wholesale and dealer pricing explains the options.

GST invoices and input tax credit

Business buyers need a proper GST invoice to claim input tax credit, so say clearly that you issue one, and collect the GSTIN at checkout or in the quote form. For online orders, the store can calculate CGST and SGST or IGST by delivery state and generate invoices with your GSTIN and HSN codes; WooCommerce GST setup and invoices goes through it. Your CA should check the tax rates and invoice layout before the store goes live.

AMC and service pages

Annual maintenance contracts and after-sales support are what keep corporate clients for years. Give them proper pages rather than a line on the contact page.

  • What an AMC covers: preventive visits, breakdown support, which devices, whether parts are included (comprehensive) or charged (non-comprehensive)
  • Response approach: how support requests are logged and how quickly you aim to respond, stated as a target you meet
  • On-site and remote support: what can be fixed remotely and what needs a visit
  • Warranty help: how you assist with manufacturer warranty claims for products you sold
  • Request form: a support form for existing customers with device, issue and location, plus a WhatsApp number

Include an enquiry form for AMC quotes that asks for the number and types of devices, locations and current setup.

Solutions pages for system integrators

If you design and install as well as sell, buyers want to see solutions rather than SKUs: new office setup, networking and Wi-Fi, structured cabling, CCTV integration, servers and backup, device rental. Each page should describe the kind of work, the steps from survey to handover, and the sectors where you genuinely have experience. Client names and logos should appear only with permission. If you are registered on government procurement portals, say so on the relevant page with the details as registered.

Keep a big catalogue fast

Large catalogues slow down quickly: many images, heavy filters, and plugins added over time. Compress and resize product images, use caching and good hosting, limit the number of products loaded per page, and test category pages on a phone over mobile data. A slow catalogue loses retail buyers before they reach a product.

Local search

Most dealer searches are local: "laptop dealer in {area}", "computer shop near me", "networking company in {city}", "IT AMC services {city}". A complete Google Business Profile with store photos and accurate categories, category and solution pages with the city named naturally, and product schema on catalogue pages give the site a fair chance in those results.

Corporate clients replacing old machines often ask about safe disposal. If e-waste collection and recycling is your main business, see websites for scrap, recycling and e-waste companies.

FAQs

Should a computer dealer sell online or just show a catalogue?

Many do both: accessories and stable items sold online, laptops and business products shown with price on request. WooCommerce handles both in one store.

How do I show prices that change every week?

Use price on request for volatile products, and only publish prices you can keep accurate. A "Get best price" button that opens WhatsApp or a form works well.

Can I use brand logos on my website?

Only where your partner agreement allows it, usually from the brand's official partner material, and only for the brands and lines you are authorised for.

Can business customers request a quote for several items at once?

Yes. A quote list lets them add products and quantities, attach a requirement list and submit everything with their GSTIN in one request.

Can the website handle GST invoices?

Yes, for online orders WooCommerce can calculate GST by delivery state and generate invoices with your GSTIN and HSN codes. Confirm the setup with your CA.
