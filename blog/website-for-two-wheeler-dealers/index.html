Websites for Two-Wheeler Dealers: New and Used Bikes, Model Pages Within Brand Rules, Test Rides, Service Booking, Finance Enquiries, EVs and Several Showrooms

What a bike and scooter dealer website needs: new and used listings, model pages within brand rules, test rides, service booking, finance and EVs.

Key takeaways
  • Read your dealership agreement before planning the site, because it usually decides what you may say about models, prices, offers and the brand.
  • Treat new models and used bikes as two different catalogues, each with its own page layout, enquiry button and level of detail.
  • Test ride and service booking, careful finance wording and a page for every showroom and service point are what turn local searches into visits.

Two-wheeler buyers compare models on their phones, then look for a nearby showroom that will give them a straight on-road quote and a test ride. A dealer website should work within the manufacturer's rules, keep new models and used bikes as separate catalogues, make test rides and service easy to book, word finance enquiries carefully, present electric models alongside petrol ones and give every showroom and service point its own page.

The build is a business website with model pages, booking forms, WhatsApp and call tracking, of the sort I describe under WordPress website development. Launches, festive offers and exchange campaigns are better served by their own landing pages, so ad clicks land on the offer they were promised and you can see which campaign brought each enquiry.

If you sell only electric scooters and motorcycles, websites for EV dealers goes into range, charging and subsidy wording in more depth.

Start with your dealership agreement

Authorised dealers rarely have a free hand. Before planning pages, pull out the agreement and any brand guidelines and answer these questions with your area manager if needed.

Question to checkWhy it changes the website
May we have our own website and domain, and may the brand name appear in it?Some manufacturers restrict domains, or provide dealer pages of their own
Which official images, videos and specifications may we use?Decides whether model pages use brand assets, your own photos, or both
May we publish prices and offers, and do they need approval?Decides whether you show ex-showroom prices or only take quote requests
Are there rules on paid ads using the brand or model names?Affects campaign landing pages and ad wording
Does the manufacturer run its own test ride or service booking that we must link to?Avoids two booking systems competing for the same customer

Multi-brand and used-vehicle dealers should never imply an authorisation they do not hold. Saying you stock or service a make is fine; using its logo as your own is not.

New and used: two different catalogues

A new model page sells a model that many people will buy in many colours. A used listing sells one specific vehicle. Mixing them in one template produces pages that do neither job well.

New model pageUsed bike listing
What it describesA model with variants and coloursOne vehicle with its own history
Key detailsVariants, specifications from official data, colours, availabilityYear, kilometres, owners, insurance validity, service history, condition notes
PhotosOfficial images where allowed, plus your own showroom photosYour own photos of that bike from all sides, including any wear
Main buttonGet on-road price, book a test rideEnquire about this bike, book a viewing
LifespanStays up while the model is soldMarked sold or removed the day it goes

Used listings need a quick way for staff to add and mark vehicles sold from a phone, or the stock soon looks stale. Websites for car dealers and workshops covers used stock listings and condition wording in more detail; the same principles apply to bikes.

Model pages inside brand rules

  • Write an introduction for your local buyers in your own words: who the model suits, such as college commuters or delivery riders, and what people in your area ask about it
  • Take specifications from the manufacturer's current official information, and recheck them when a model is updated
  • Show variants and what changes between them in a small table
  • Say honestly whether a variant or colour is in stock or has a waiting period
  • For prices, follow your agreement: either label figures as ex-showroom with a "last updated" date, or offer an on-road quote form asking for model, variant and pin code
  • Link accessories, helmets and extended warranty options, if you offer them, from the model page

On-road prices include registration, insurance and other charges that vary by state and change from time to time, which is why many dealers prefer to reply with an itemised quote on WhatsApp. For the wider pros and cons, read whether to show prices on a website.

Test ride booking

A test ride is often the step that settles a purchase. Keep the booking short:

  1. Choose the model, from those actually available for test rides at that showroom
  2. Pick a showroom, a date and a time window
  3. Choose showroom ride or doorstep ride, if you offer doorstep rides, with the areas covered
  4. Enter name and mobile number, and confirm they hold a valid licence for that class of vehicle
  5. Receive a WhatsApp confirmation with the address and a reminder to bring the licence and wear a helmet

Follow up the same day after the ride with the on-road quote and finance options, while the ride is fresh in the buyer's mind.

Service booking and the workshop

Service keeps customers coming back for years after the sale. A good service section includes:

  • An online booking form with registration number, model, kilometres, service type and preferred date
  • An explanation of scheduled services based on the manufacturer's schedule, including any free services that come with the vehicle under its terms
  • Pickup and drop, if you offer it, with areas and charges stated upfront
  • A note that you send an estimate for anything beyond the scheduled service, and wait for approval
  • Service reminders by WhatsApp, sent only with the customer's consent

If your workshop is at a different address from the showroom, give it its own page and Google Business Profile, because people search for service separately from sales.

Finance enquiries, worded carefully

Many two-wheelers are bought on EMI, so a finance enquiry form is worth having. The wording matters:

  • Say that you help arrange finance through banks and finance companies, and that approval, interest rate and terms are decided by the lender
  • Name financing partners only where you have an actual arrangement
  • If you show an example EMI, state the assumptions (amount, tenure and rate) and that it is only an example
  • Avoid "guaranteed approval", "zero down payment for all" or "no documents needed"
  • Ask only for model, preferred down payment, employment type, city and phone; collect documents later through a secure route, not the website form

For exchange, ask for the old vehicle's make, model, year, kilometres and a few photos, and explain that the value is confirmed after inspection.

Electric models alongside petrol

Many dealers now sell electric scooters next to petrol models, sometimes from a different brand. Give EVs their own section rather than mixing them into the scooter list, and answer the questions EV buyers ask: range and its testing basis, charging at home, battery warranty and service. Be careful with subsidy or incentive information, which changes; date it and link to the official source.

Accessories, helmets and riding gear

Accessories add to every sale and bring in people who are not buying a vehicle. Even a simple enquiry catalogue helps: helmets, riding jackets, gloves, seat covers, crash guards, phone mounts and genuine accessories for each model. Helmets for road use in India generally need to meet the mandatory standard, so list certification details only as the manufacturer provides them. If accessories are a big line for you, an online shop may be worth it; websites for car accessories shops covers fitment and catalogue questions that also apply to bikes.

Several showrooms and service points

Dealers with more than one location need a page for each, not one contact page with a list of addresses.

FAQs

Can an authorised dealer have its own website?

Usually yes, within the manufacturer's rules on domains, logos, images, prices and offers. Check your agreement and brand guidelines before building.

Should we show on-road prices?

On-road prices vary by city and change often, so many dealers show ex-showroom prices with a date, or offer an itemised on-road quote on request.

Can customers book test rides and services online?

Yes. Short forms with model, showroom, date and time, followed by WhatsApp confirmation, work well for both.

Can we list used bikes on the same website?

Yes, as a separate catalogue with each vehicle's own details and photos, marked sold as soon as it goes.

How should finance offers be worded?

Say you help arrange finance, that the lender decides approval and terms, name only actual partners, and label any EMI as an example with its assumptions.
