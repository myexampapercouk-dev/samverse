Gravity Forms vs Contact Form 7: Features, Limits and Which One Your WordPress Site Needs

Key takeaways
  • Contact Form 7 is free and fine for simple enquiry forms; Gravity Forms is a paid plugin built for complex, multi-step and data-heavy forms.
  • Gravity Forms includes saved entries, conditional logic, calculations and many integrations; Contact Form 7 needs add-ons for most of these.
  • Choose by the job: a basic contact page rarely needs Gravity Forms, while applications, quotes and workflows rarely work well in Contact Form 7.

Gravity Forms vs Contact Form 7 comes down to complexity: Contact Form 7 is a free, lightweight plugin for simple contact forms, while Gravity Forms is a paid plugin for complex forms with saved entries, conditional logic, calculations, multi-page layouts and integrations. For a basic enquiry form, Contact Form 7 is enough; for applications, quotes or workflows, Gravity Forms is the better tool.

Below is a practical comparison from building and maintaining both on business sites, plus guidance on which to pick.

Where Contact Form 7 wins

  • Cost. It is free, and so are most of its add-ons.
  • Weight. It is light, and with a little configuration it can load its scripts only on pages that contain a form.
  • Developer control. The markup-based forms are quick for a developer to write and easy to keep in version control.
  • Simple needs. Name, phone, email, message and a submit button is exactly what CF7 was built for.

The weak spots are well known: entries are not saved by default, spam needs extra protection, and email delivery depends on your host unless you set up SMTP. Fix those three and CF7 is dependable. Our guides on stopping contact form spam and WordPress not sending emails cover both fixes.

Where Gravity Forms wins

  • Complex forms. Admission applications, loan or insurance enquiries, job applications and long quote forms are much easier to build and maintain.
  • Conditional logic everywhere. Show fields, skip pages and route notifications to different teams based on answers, for example sending service enquiries to one inbox and sales enquiries to another.
  • Calculations and quotes. Build simple estimate calculators using number fields and formulas.
  • Entries as a mini database. Search, filter, add notes, mark as read and export to CSV from the dashboard.
  • Integrations. Official and third-party add-ons connect to CRMs, email platforms, spreadsheets and payment processors, so leads do not sit in an inbox. See connecting website forms to a CRM.
  • Developer hooks. Gravity Forms has extensive actions and filters for custom behaviour. If that term is new, our explainer on WordPress hooks, actions and filters covers the basics.

Real-world examples: which plugin each form needs

Abstract feature lists are less useful than real form types. Here is how I would match common business forms to each plugin.

A clinic appointment request

Name, phone, preferred date, doctor and a short message. Contact Form 7 handles this well. If the clinic wants requests for each doctor routed to a different receptionist, Gravity Forms conditional notifications make that simpler, though CF7 can do it with a little customisation.

A coaching institute admission form

Student details, parent details, course choice, previous marks, photo upload and a document upload, often over several steps. This is a Gravity Forms job. Multi-page layout, saved partial progress via an add-on, and searchable entries for the admissions team all matter here.

A manufacturer's request for quote

Product selection, quantities, specifications, delivery location and drawing upload. Gravity Forms handles product lists, conditional fields per product type and routing to the right sales person. Our guide to RFQ forms for B2B websites explains what to ask.

A simple "contact us" page

Contact Form 7, set up properly. Paying for Gravity Forms here adds cost without benefit.

A service cost estimator

Visitors choose options and see an estimated range before submitting. Gravity Forms calculations do this without custom code; in Contact Form 7 it means add-ons or custom JavaScript.

The pattern is consistent: the more the form branches, calculates or needs to be worked through by a team afterwards, the more Gravity Forms earns its licence.

Performance: does Gravity Forms slow your site?

Both plugins are reasonably efficient when set up properly. Problems usually come from loading form scripts and styles on every page, or from adding many add-ons. With either plugin:

  • Load form assets only on pages that contain a form, using the plugin setting or a script manager.
  • Avoid loading reCAPTCHA site-wide; it is one of the heavier scripts on many sites.
  • Exclude form pages from aggressive JavaScript delay if the form stops working after optimisation.

If your site is slow overall, the form is rarely the main cause. Our WordPress speed optimisation service looks at the full picture.

Switching from Contact Form 7 to Gravity Forms

  1. List every page, widget and popup using a CF7 shortcode.
  2. Export any stored Flamingo entries.
  3. Rebuild each form in Gravity Forms, adding logic where it simplifies the form.
  4. Set up notifications, confirmations and a thank-you page redirect.
  5. Reconnect conversion tracking for Google Ads and Meta; see Google Ads conversion tracking setup.
  6. Replace the shortcodes, test on mobile, and keep CF7 active for a short overlap before removing it.

Moving the other way, from Gravity Forms to CF7, is unusual and only makes sense when the forms have become simpler than the tool. Remember to export entries first, because they will not carry across.

Maintenance and long-term cost

The licence is only part of the cost. Think about who will maintain the forms over the next few years.

  • Contact Form 7 costs nothing to license, but every extra feature means another add-on from a different author. Each add-on is one more thing to update, and one more thing that can break after a WordPress or PHP upgrade. Sites with five or six CF7 add-ons often end up harder to maintain than one Gravity Forms install.
  • Gravity Forms keeps most features in one product with official add-ons from the same team, which usually means fewer compatibility surprises. The trade-off is a renewal you must keep active for updates and support. An expired licence on a business-critical form plugin is a security risk, not a saving.
  • Editing by the owner. If staff need to add a field or change who receives notifications, the Gravity Forms visual editor is far easier for them than CF7 markup, which saves developer time on small changes.

Never use a nulled copy of Gravity Forms to avoid the licence. Pirated premium plugins are a common route for malware; our article on nulled themes and plugins explains the risk.

Common mistakes with either plugin

  • Not setting up SMTP, so notifications silently land in spam or never send.
  • Sending notifications from the visitor's email address, which fails email authentication. Use your own domain address as the sender and the visitor's address as reply-to.
  • No thank-you page or confirmation, so visitors are unsure the form worked and ad platforms cannot count conversions.
  • Asking for too many fields on a first contact form.
  • Never testing the form after plugin or theme updates.

FAQs

Is Gravity Forms better than Contact Form 7?

For complex forms, yes: it includes saved entries, conditional logic, multi-page forms, calculations and integrations. For a simple contact form, Contact Form 7 does the same job for free.

Is Gravity Forms free?

No. Gravity Forms is a paid plugin with annual licences. Contact Form 7 is free.

Does Contact Form 7 have conditional logic?

Not built in. Third-party add-ons provide it, but complex logic is much easier to build and maintain in Gravity Forms.

Which is better for multi-step forms, Gravity Forms or Contact Form 7?

Gravity Forms, which has multi-page forms with progress bars built in. Contact Form 7 needs an add-on and is harder to maintain for long forms.

Can I import Contact Form 7 forms into Gravity Forms?

There is no reliable direct import for most setups. Rebuilding the forms is usually quicker and gives you the chance to tidy fields and add logic.

Which uses fewer resources, Gravity Forms or Contact Form 7?

Both are light when their assets load only on form pages. Contact Form 7 is smaller, but the difference is rarely what makes a site slow.
