How to Create a Meet the Team Page for a Small Business

How to create a meet the team page for a small business in India: who to include, real photos, honest bios, staff consent, and what to do if you work solo.

Key takeaways
  • A meet the team page builds trust by showing the real people behind the business, with genuine photos, roles and short bios focused on how each person helps customers.
  • Only list real people with their consent, state qualifications accurately, and update the page when someone joins or leaves.
  • If you work solo, say so honestly: an about page focused on your process and experience works better than inventing a team.

A meet the team page shows visitors the real people behind your business: who they are, what they do and why they can be trusted. To create one, choose who to include, use genuine photos, write short bios focused on how each person helps customers, get each person's consent, and keep the page up to date.

For small businesses in India, where many buyers still prefer to know who they are dealing with before calling, a good team page can be the difference between an enquiry and a closed tab. This guide covers what to include, how to write bios, and how to handle the page honestly if you are a solo business.

Why a meet the team page matters

People buy from people, especially for services. A clinic, a CA firm, an interior design studio or a coaching institute is really selling the expertise of specific individuals. When visitors can see those individuals, three things happen:

  • Trust increases, because there are real faces and names behind the brand.
  • Expertise becomes visible, since bios can show qualifications, experience and specialisms.
  • The first call feels easier, because the customer already knows who they will speak to.

Team pages also help with search. Google looks for signs of experience and expertise, particularly on health, legal and finance topics. Clear author and team information supports that, alongside good content. The guide on E-E-A-T for small business websites explains the idea in more depth.

How to create a meet the team page: step by step

  1. Decide who to include. Usually founders, senior staff and anyone customers deal with directly: doctors, consultants, designers, trainers or sales leads.
  2. Get consent. Ask each person before publishing their photo, name and details. Keep a simple record of their agreement.
  3. Arrange real photos. A consistent style, similar background and good lighting matter more than an expensive shoot.
  4. Write short bios. Two to four sentences each, focused on what the person does for customers.
  5. Choose a layout. A grid of cards works for most businesses; a longer profile page per person suits doctors, lawyers and consultants.
  6. Link it properly. Add the page to the main menu or the about page, and link individual profiles from relevant service pages.
  7. Set a review reminder so the page is updated when someone joins or leaves.

Writing team bios that build trust

Most bios fail because they are either too formal or too vague. "A passionate professional with years of experience" tells the reader nothing. Instead, write about what the person actually does and who they help.

A simple structure for each bio:

  • What they do: "Leads kitchen and wardrobe design projects for apartments."
  • What they are good at: "Focuses on storage planning for compact homes."
  • Relevant background: a qualification or a type of project, stated accurately.
  • Something human (optional): a line about languages spoken or what they enjoy.

Write in the third person for a formal business, or first person if the brand is personal and friendly. Keep the same style across the page. And never inflate qualifications or claim memberships, awards or certifications the person does not genuinely hold; it damages trust quickly, and for regulated professions it can create real problems.

Photos: real people, consistent style

Stock photos on a team page are worse than no photos. Visitors can tell, and it suggests the team is not real. Use genuine photos of each person, ideally taken on the same day with similar framing and background.

Practical tips:

  • Shoot in natural light near a window if a professional photographer is not available.
  • Keep backgrounds simple and consistent.
  • Crop all photos to the same ratio, such as square or 4:5.
  • Compress images before upload so the page stays fast on mobile data.
  • Write descriptive alt text, such as the person's name and role.

If someone does not want a photo online, respect that. Use a simple placeholder with initials, or leave them off the page.

Consent, privacy and staff turnover

Publishing staff details is a privacy matter. Ask for consent, explain where the information will appear, and let people choose what to share. Avoid personal phone numbers and personal email addresses; use role-based contacts instead. India's data protection rules are evolving, so check current requirements with your lawyer if you handle a lot of personal data.

Staff turnover is the other practical issue. A team page that still shows someone who left a year ago looks careless. When someone leaves, remove their profile promptly, and if they had their own profile URL, redirect it to the main team page rather than leaving a broken link.

If you work solo: be honest

Many freelancers and small businesses are run by one person. Inventing a team to look bigger is tempting, but it backfires: clients find out on the first call, and fake profiles undermine everything else on the site.

A solo business has real advantages to explain instead: the client deals directly with the person doing the work, there is no handover between departments, and decisions are quick. Write an about page that covers your process, your experience and the kind of work you take on. If you work with trusted specialists for certain jobs, such as a photographer or copywriter, say that in general terms rather than presenting them as staff.

Building a team page in WordPress or Shopify

In WordPress, a team page can be built with blocks or a page builder using a simple grid of image, name, role and bio. For larger teams, a custom post type for team members makes updates easier: each person becomes an entry with fields, and the page displays them automatically. Individual profile pages can then link from relevant service pages, which is useful for clinics and consultancies.

On Shopify, a team section can be added to an about page using theme sections or metaobjects, which keeps the layout consistent with the rest of the store. Either way, keep the page light and fast, with compressed images and no heavy sliders.

Team pages for different types of business

The right format depends on what customers are really choosing. A clinic patient chooses a doctor; a homeowner chooses a design studio; a parent chooses a teacher. Shape the page around that decision.

  • Clinics and hospitals: a profile per doctor with specialisation, qualifications as held, languages, consultation days and a booking link.
  • CA, legal and consulting firms: partner profiles with practice areas, plus a short line on support staff as a group.
  • Coaching institutes and schools: faculty cards by subject, with teaching focus rather than long histories.
  • Interior, architecture and agency studios: a small founder section plus the team as a group photo if individual profiles feel too formal.
  • Shops and restaurants: often a founder story is enough; staff profiles rarely influence buying.

Pages like these also pair well with other trust signals, such as reviews and clear contact details.

Common mistakes on meet the team pages

  • Stock photos or mismatched selfies.
  • Bios full of buzzwords and no specifics.
  • People who left still listed.
  • Qualifications stated loosely or inaccurately.
  • The page hidden in the footer where nobody finds it.
  • No link from the team page to the contact page, so interested visitors have nowhere to go next.

FAQs

How do I create a meet the team page for a small business?

Choose who to include, get consent, use real and consistent photos, write short bios focused on how each person helps customers, and link the page from your menu or about page.

Should a small business have a team page?

If customers deal with specific people, yes. It builds trust and helps visitors feel comfortable before they call or book.

What should a team member bio include?

Their role, what they do for customers, relevant qualifications stated accurately, and optionally languages spoken or a personal line.

Can I use stock photos on a team page?

No. Stock photos make the team look fake. Use real photos, or initials placeholders for anyone who prefers not to be shown.

What if I run my business alone?

Say so. A strong about page explaining your process and experience builds more trust than an invented team.
