Website Analytics for Business Owners: The GA4 and Search Console Metrics That Actually Matter, and a Monthly Routine

Website analytics metrics that matter for small businesses: GA4 key events, sources, landing pages, Search Console data and a 30-minute monthly routine.

Key takeaways
  • Start with key events, such as enquiries, calls, WhatsApp clicks and orders, then look at which sources and landing pages produce them.
  • Use Search Console for what people searched and which pages earn clicks, and treat page views, time on site and follower counts as context, not results.
  • A 30-minute monthly routine comparing key events, sources, landing pages and search queries with last month and last year beats a detailed report nobody reads.

For a small business website, the analytics metrics that matter are key events (enquiries, calls, WhatsApp clicks, bookings or orders), the traffic sources and landing pages that produce them, engaged sessions on your important pages, and, from Search Console, the searches and pages that earn clicks from Google. Page views, time on site and follower counts are context at best. Check a handful of these once a month, against last month and the same month last year, and act on one thing each time.

Google Analytics 4 shows dozens of reports, and it's easy to get lost in numbers that don't matter. When I set up SEO for a WordPress site through WordPress SEO services, Search Console and GA4 are set up in your own Google account, with notes on what to watch; this guide is those notes, expanded.

1. Key events: enquiries, calls, WhatsApp clicks and orders

A key event is an action you have marked as important in GA4; Google called these conversions until 2024. For most businesses the list is short:

  • Enquiry or quote form submitted, counted on a thank-you page or the form's success event.
  • Phone number tapped on mobile.
  • WhatsApp button clicked.
  • Booking made or order placed.

Key event rate, the share of sessions or users with a key event, tells you whether the site persuades visitors. If traffic grows but the rate falls, the new visitors may be less relevant, or a page has stopped working. GA4 doesn't track forms, calls or WhatsApp clicks as key events on its own; GA4 events explained shows how to add and mark them.

2. Traffic sources that bring enquiries

The Traffic acquisition report groups sessions by channel: organic search, paid search, organic social, referral, direct and others. The column to read is key events, not sessions. A channel with modest traffic that produces most of your enquiries deserves more effort than a busy one that produces none.

Social and campaign links often land in the wrong channel unless they are tagged, so add UTM parameters to links in Instagram bios, emails, WhatsApp broadcasts and QR codes; UTM tags explained covers the naming.

3. Landing pages

The Landing page report shows the pages where sessions start, with sessions, engagement and key events for each. Look for two patterns:

  • High traffic, few key events: the page attracts people but doesn't persuade them. Check the call to action, proof, speed and whether the page matches what visitors searched for.
  • Few visits, strong key event rate: the page works. Link to it from related pages and improve its search visibility.

4. Engaged sessions and engagement rate

GA4 counts a session as engaged if it lasts longer than ten seconds, includes a key event, or views at least two pages. Engagement rate is engaged sessions divided by all sessions; GA4's bounce rate is simply the opposite.

Use it to compare like with like: one landing page against another, or one channel against another. A very low engagement rate on a key page suggests a mismatch between what people expected and what they found, or a page that loads slowly. Don't chase a target figure, because a contact page that answers the question quickly can have a low engagement time and still be doing its job.

5. Device split

On many small business sites in India, phones bring most of the visits, so check your own split first. Then compare the key event rate on mobile with desktop. If mobile is much lower, test the site on a mid-range Android phone: tap targets, form fields, WhatsApp and call buttons, and pop-ups covering the content.

Search Console: what GA4 can't tell you

GA4 doesn't show the searches people typed into Google. Search Console does, for the last 16 months:

  • Queries: the searches your site appeared for, with clicks and impressions.
  • Pages: which pages earn clicks, and which appear often but rarely get clicked.
  • CTR: a page with many impressions and a low click-through rate may need a better title and description.
  • Average position: useful per query or per page, misleading as a site-wide average. Pages averaging positions 8 to 20 for relevant searches are often the best candidates for improvement.
  • Indexing: check that your important pages are indexed, and read the reasons for those that aren't.

Why the numbers never match exactly

  • GA4 undercounts visitors who decline cookies, use ad blockers or leave before the tag loads.
  • Search Console clicks and GA4 organic sessions differ, because they count different things in different ways, and Search Console hides some rare queries for privacy.
  • Search Console data arrives a couple of days late, so don't judge yesterday.
  • Small numbers swing: going from two enquiries to four is not doubling your business.

Trends matter more than exact figures. If GA4 suddenly shows nothing at all, the tag has probably broken; see GA4 not showing data on WordPress.

One setting is worth changing on day one: GA4 keeps detailed data for explorations for only two months by default. Set data retention to the longest option in the Admin area, so you can compare this year with last.

A 30-minute monthly routine

  1. Key events: total enquiries, calls, WhatsApp clicks and orders, against last month and the same month last year, since festivals, admissions and weather make many businesses seasonal.
  2. Sources: key events by channel. Which channel grew, which fell?
  3. Landing pages: the top ten by sessions, with their key events. Note one page with traffic but few enquiries.
  4. Search Console: clicks and impressions trend, top queries, and queries with many impressions but few clicks.
  5. Indexing: any new errors on important pages.
  6. Your own records: enquiries and sales from your lead sheet or CRM, by source. This is the number that matters most.
  7. One action: pick one improvement for the month, write it down, and check its effect next month.

If you'd rather not open three tools, Looker Studio, Google's free dashboard tool, can show GA4 and Search Console figures on one page.

FAQs

What website metrics should a small business track?

Key events such as enquiries, calls, WhatsApp clicks and orders; the sources and landing pages that produce them; engagement on important pages; and Search Console clicks, queries and indexing.

What is a key event in GA4?

An action you have marked as important, such as a form submission or purchase. GA4 called these conversions until 2024; Google Ads still uses the word conversion for actions it counts for bidding.

What is a good engagement rate in GA4?

There is no universal figure, because it depends on the page and the visitor's purpose. Compare pages and channels on your own site, and investigate the ones well below the rest.

Why does GA4 show fewer visits than Search Console shows clicks?

The tools count differently. GA4 misses visitors who decline cookies or block tracking, and it only records a visit once its tag loads. Watch the trend in each tool rather than expecting them to match.

How often should I check website analytics?

Once a month for the routine above is enough for most small businesses, with a quick weekly look at enquiries if you are running ads or have just launched changes.
