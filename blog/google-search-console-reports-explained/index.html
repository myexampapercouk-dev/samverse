Google Search Console Reports Explained: A Tour of Every Report, What It Shows and What to Do About It

Key takeaways
  • Performance, Pages, URL Inspection and Sitemaps do most of the work for a small business; Manual actions and Security issues should simply stay empty.
  • Each report answers a different question: how you appear in search, whether pages are indexed, how Google sees one URL, and whether speed, HTTPS or structured data need fixing.
  • A short monthly routine across the reports catches indexing problems, lost pages and new opportunities long before they show up as lost enquiries.

Google Search Console has around a dozen reports, but for a small business four do most of the work: Performance shows the searches you appear for, Pages shows what is and isn't indexed, URL Inspection checks any single page, and Sitemaps confirms Google can read your list of pages. Core Web Vitals, HTTPS, Enhancements and Links point to specific fixes, while Manual actions and Security issues should simply stay empty. The table below lists every report, and the sections after it explain what to do with each one.

If Search Console isn't set up yet, start with setting up GA4 and Search Console. Google moves and renames reports from time to time, so menu names here are correct at the time of writing.

Performance: how you appear in search

The Performance report has tabs for Search results and, if your site appears there, Discover and News. Turn on all four metrics: clicks, impressions, average CTR and average position. Then use the filters and tabs:

  • Queries: the words people searched before your site appeared. Some rare queries are hidden for privacy, so totals won't always add up.
  • Pages: which URLs appeared. Click one, then switch to Queries, to see every search that page shows up for.
  • Countries and devices: useful when most customers are in one city or most traffic is mobile.
  • Search appearance: visibility from rich results such as product snippets, where you have them.
  • Date compare: compare with the previous period, or the same period last year for seasonal businesses.

What to do: rewrite titles for page-one queries with low CTR, strengthen pages averaging positions around 8 to 20, and note questions you don't answer yet. How to find content ideas in Search Console turns this report into a step-by-step method. Average position is averaged across many searches, places and devices, so treat it as a trend, not an exact rank.

URL Inspection: one page, in detail

Paste any URL from your property into the search bar at the top. The result shows whether the page is on Google, how Google found it, when it was last crawled, whether crawling and indexing were allowed, and two canonicals: the one your page declares and the one Google selected. If they differ, Google has decided another URL is the original.

  • Test live URL fetches the page now, which is how you confirm a fix before asking Google to recrawl.
  • View crawled page shows the HTML Google saw, useful when content loads by JavaScript.
  • Request indexing asks Google to crawl a new or updated page. There is a daily limit, so use it for important pages, not every URL.

Pages: what is indexed and why not

The Pages report splits your known URLs into indexed and not indexed, with a reason for each group, such as "Crawled, currently not indexed", "Excluded by noindex tag" or "Duplicate without user-selected canonical". The number of not-indexed URLs isn't a score: thank-you pages, feeds, redirects and filtered URLs are meant to stay out. What matters is whether your service pages, products and key articles are in. Search Console page indexing errors explained covers each status and its fix. After fixing a status, click Validate fix so Google rechecks the affected URLs.

Sitemaps: your list of pages

Submit your sitemap address once, usually sitemap_index.xml from an SEO plugin or wp-sitemap.xml from WordPress itself, and check the status reads Success with a sensible number of discovered pages. Resubmitting every week doesn't speed anything up. If it shows "Couldn't fetch" or an HTTP error, see how to fix a sitemap Search Console couldn't fetch.

Removals: emergencies only

The Removals tool temporarily hides URLs from Google's results, for roughly six months at the time of writing. It is useful when spam pages from a hack or a leaked staging site appear in search while you clean up. It doesn't delete anything from your site or remove pages permanently: for that, the page must return a 404 or 410, or carry a noindex tag, before the temporary removal ends. The same section lists outdated content and SafeSearch requests made by others.

Core Web Vitals and HTTPS

Core Web Vitals groups your URLs as good, needs improvement or poor for loading, responsiveness and visual stability, separately for mobile and desktop, using data from real Chrome visitors. Small or new sites often see "not enough data", which is normal. When a group of URLs is flagged, it usually means one template is slow, so fixing that template fixes the group. Core Web Vitals explained covers what each metric measures.

The HTTPS report lists indexed pages served over plain HTTP. On a properly set up WordPress site the list should be empty; if not, check your site address settings, redirects and certificate.

Enhancements and Shopping: structured data

When Google finds supported structured data, it creates a report per type, such as Breadcrumbs, Product snippets or Merchant listings, with valid and invalid items and example URLs. There is also a report for structured data Google couldn't parse at all. Fix errors at the template, retest and validate; how to test structured data walks through the tools and common errors.

Manual actions and Security issues

Both should say no issues detected. A manual action means a human reviewer found a breach of Google's spam policies, such as unnatural links, thin or scraped content or user-generated spam; the notice says whether it affects the whole site or some pages. Security issues means Google detected hacked content, malware or deceptive pages, often with a browser warning for visitors. In both cases, fix the cause completely, then request reconsideration or a review from the same report and explain what you changed. If you see a security issue, "This site may be hacked" in Google explains the clean-up and review steps.

Links: external and internal

The Links report shows top linked pages, top linking sites and top linking text for external links, plus your most internally linked pages. It is a sample, not a complete list, and it updates slowly. Use it to check which pages other sites link to, to find important pages with few internal links, and to spot old URLs that still attract links and need a redirect. Seeing unfamiliar spammy domains is usually harmless; backlink audits and the disavow tool explains when they actually matter.

Settings: crawl stats, users and ownership

  • Crawl stats: how often Google crawls, response times and response codes. A rise in server errors or slow responses here often explains pages stuck as discovered but not crawled.
  • Users and permissions: give your developer or agency their own access as a full or restricted user, rather than sharing your Google login. Keep the business as the owner.
  • Change of address: used when moving to a new domain, alongside 301 redirects.

A 15-minute monthly routine

  1. Performance: compare the last 28 days with the previous period and note any page with a big change.
  2. Pick two pages averaging positions 8 to 20 for useful queries, and plan improvements.
  3. Pages: confirm key pages are indexed and no new status includes important URLs.
  4. Core Web Vitals, HTTPS and Enhancements: any new issues?
  5. Manual actions and Security issues: confirm both are empty.

If clicks fall suddenly, work through the traffic drop checklist before changing anything.

FAQs

Is Google Search Console free?

Yes. It is free for any site you can verify, and it is the only place to see the actual searches your site appears for in Google.

Which Search Console report matters most?

For most businesses, Performance and Pages: one shows how people find you, the other whether your important pages are in Google at all. URL Inspection is the tool for checking any single page.

How long until a new site shows data?

Data starts collecting once the property is verified, usually appearing within a few days. A new site may show few impressions at first because Google hasn't indexed or ranked much yet.

Why does the Core Web Vitals report say not enough data?

It uses real visitor data from Chrome users, and smaller sites often don't have enough visits for a report. Use PageSpeed Insights lab tests in the meantime.

Should I give my developer access to Search Console?

Yes, as a user added under Settings with the access level they need. Never share your Google password, and keep your business account as the owner.
