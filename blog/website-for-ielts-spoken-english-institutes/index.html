Websites for IELTS, PTE and Spoken English Institutes: Courses by Goal, Demo Class Booking, Batch Timings, Mock Tests and Careful Exam Wording

Plan an English institute website by goal: IELTS, PTE, spoken and corporate English pages, demo booking, live batch timings and no band-score promises.

Key takeaways
  • Organise the site by what the learner wants to achieve, such as study abroad, a job interview or workplace confidence, rather than by your internal course codes.
  • A free demo class or level check with a short booking form is the most useful enquiry route an English institute can offer.
  • Refer to exam names factually, avoid official logos and words like authorised unless you hold an agreement, and never promise a band score.

An English institute's website has one job before any other: help each visitor find the course that matches their goal and book a demo class or level check. Arrange courses by goal (an IELTS or PTE score for a university or visa plan, spoken English for confidence or interviews, workplace English for companies), keep batch timings current, explain online and classroom formats honestly and keep exam names and results within careful wording.

The site itself is a standard education build of the kind I describe on the schools and coaching institutes page: pages for each course and its batches, enquiry forms that also open a WhatsApp chat, results shown only with consent, and notices staff can post themselves. Because most students start with a search such as "IELTS coaching near me" or "spoken English classes in Kothrud", the search side matters just as much, and that is covered under WordPress SEO services.

If your main business is university applications and visas, with test preparation as a side service, start with websites for overseas education consultants instead.

Sort courses by the learner's goal

People rarely arrive knowing your course names. They arrive with a reason. Put those reasons in the main menu and on the homepage, and let each one lead to the right course pages.

Learner's goalCourses it leads toWhat the page must answer first
Study abroad or migration with a test scoreIELTS Academic, IELTS General Training, PTE preparationWhich test or module suits their plan, and how many weeks of preparation you suggest from their level
Speaking confidentlySpoken English from beginner, intermediate fluency, public speakingWhether beginners are welcome, batch size and how much of each class is speaking
Interviews and jobsInterview English, email and workplace communicationWhat is practised: mock interviews, self-introduction, group discussion
Company trainingCorporate English programmesWho it suits, where it runs and how progress is reported to HR
School studentsGrammar, reading and speaking for childrenAge groups, timings and who teaches

Keep the beginner spoken English route visibly separate from test preparation. A nervous learner who lands on a page full of band descriptors often leaves without enquiring.

What a course page should contain

Each course gets its own page, written for the person in the first column above. A practical order:

  1. Who it is for, in one or two sentences, and the starting level you expect
  2. What happens in class: skills covered, speaking practice, how writing is corrected and returned
  3. Duration and schedule: total weeks, hours per week, weekday or weekend
  4. Formats: classroom, live online, or both, with a link to how online classes work
  5. What is included: material, practice tests, one-to-one feedback sessions
  6. Fees, or what decides them, plus instalments and your refund policy
  7. Next batch dates pulled from the same timetable as the rest of the site
  8. A "book a free demo" button that remembers which course the visitor was reading

Wording around exam names

IELTS, PTE and similar exam names generally belong to their test owners and are commonly protected as trademarks. You can usually say, factually, that you prepare students for those exams. Problems start when a site suggests a connection it does not have. The owners' current brand rules are the final word, so check them, but as a working guide:

Avoid unless you hold a current agreementSafer factual wording
"Official IELTS centre" or "authorised PTE partner""Preparation classes for the IELTS and PTE exams"
The test owners' logos in your headerExam names in plain text only
"Book your IELTS exam with us""We can explain how to book the test on the official website"
Copied official test descriptionsYour own explanation, dated, with a link to the official test information

Exam formats, fees and rules change from time to time. Put a "last checked" date on every exam explainer and ask students to confirm details on the official website before booking a test.

Demo classes and level checks

A free demo class or a short level check gives visitors something useful before they pay, which is why it works better than a generic "contact us" form. Keep the route short:

  1. The visitor picks a goal or course, then a demo slot or a level check
  2. The form asks only for name, mobile number, goal, rough current level and preferred time
  3. For test learners, an optional field for target score and planned test month
  4. A confirmation on screen and on WhatsApp, with the address or the online class link
  5. A reminder a few hours before, and a follow-up message after the demo with the next batch dates

Booking tools range from a simple form with manual confirmation to a calendar with fixed slots; online appointment booking compares them. Ask for consent before adding someone to WhatsApp broadcasts, and see WhatsApp on your business website for the button and click tracking.

Batch timings that never go stale

Timings decide many enquiries, particularly for college students and working learners. The common failure is a timetable image uploaded months ago. Instead:

  • Keep one timetable in the dashboard, with each batch as an entry: course, days, time, format, branch and start date
  • Show it on the timetable page and, filtered, on each course page, so a change happens once
  • Let batches disappear automatically after their start date, or move to "running, join on request"
  • Show times in IST, and add the learner's time zone for online batches if you teach people abroad
  • If you show seats left, update it honestly or leave it out

Online, classroom or both: say exactly how each works

"Online classes available" tells a learner very little. Describe the format so they can judge whether it suits them.

Question learners askClassroomLive online
How is speaking practised?Pair work and trainer feedback in classBreakout rooms or one-to-one calls, as you actually run them
How is writing corrected?Handed in and returned with commentsUploaded, returned with typed or recorded feedback
Are sessions recorded?Usually notState whether recordings are shared and for how long
What if I miss a class?Your make-up policyYour make-up policy
Practice testsAt the centre, timedOnline or at the centre, as you offer

If you also sell recorded self-study courses, those need their own pages and a login area; online course websites on WordPress covers that setup.

Mock tests: useful, but not a prediction

Bookable mock tests bring in learners who are close to their exam and want to see where they stand. Explain what each mock includes (full or sectional, speaking with a trainer or not, how soon results are shared) and who marks it. Then say plainly that a mock score is your trainers' estimate under practice conditions and does not predict the real result. A short free reading or listening quiz on the site can work as a first step, as long as the questions are your own.

Results and reviews without promises

  • Publish a student's score only with their written consent, and with a parent's consent for minors
  • Cover candidate numbers, dates of birth and other personal details on any score report you show
  • Give context where the student agrees, such as starting level and weeks of preparation, rather than presenting top scores as typical
  • Never promise a band score, a visa or a job; at the time of writing, consumer protection guidance in India specifically addresses misleading coaching advertisements, so check the current version before writing any results copy
  • Video reviews from learners in their own words often say more than score cards; collecting and displaying testimonials covers consent and presentation

Corporate English programmes

An HR or L&D manager reads differently from a student. Give corporate training its own page that explains which roles it suits, whether you assess employees first, where and when sessions run, group sizes, and how attendance and progress are reported. A form asking for company name, number of learners, city and preferred format is enough to start a proposal. Training firms with a broader catalogue face similar buyers; see websites for corporate training companies.

Pages worth building for search

  • One page per course and per branch, each with its own batches and map
  • Explainers you keep updated, such as the difference between the Academic and General Training modules, with the date checked
  • Spoken English articles that answer real worries, such as how to stop translating in your head or how to prepare a self-introduction
  • A Google Business Profile for each centre, with class photos, timings and reviews
  • Fast pages with compressed audio and PDF files, because learners browse on phones

FAQs

Can we use the IELTS or PTE name on our website?

Generally you can say factually that you prepare students for those exams. Avoid official logos and words such as official, authorised or partner unless you hold a current agreement, and check the test owners' brand rules.

Should we publish fees?

It is your choice. If you do not publish figures, explain what the fee covers, whether instalments are possible and your refund policy, and share the amount after the level check.

How should a demo class be booked online?

A short form with goal, level, mobile number and preferred time, followed by a WhatsApp confirmation and a reminder, works well for most institutes.

Can we show students' band scores?

Yes, with written consent, personal details hidden and honest context. Do not present the best scores as what every student achieves.

Can the site handle both online and classroom batches?

Yes. Each batch is an entry with its format, branch and timings, shown on the timetable page and on the matching course pages.
