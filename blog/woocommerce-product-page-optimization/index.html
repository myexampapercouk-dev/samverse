WooCommerce Product Page Optimization: Photos, Pricing, Delivery Details and Layouts That Help Shoppers Buy

Optimize WooCommerce product pages: mobile layout, photos, GST-inclusive pricing, delivery and COD details, variations, reviews, speed and product schema.

Key takeaways
  • Answer four questions on the first phone screen: what it is, the full price, when it will arrive and whether the store can be trusted.
  • Use consistent photos, clear variation swatches, specifications as attributes and a one-line delivery, COD and returns summary right beside Add to cart.
  • Keep the page fast, add honest reviews, and use GA4 views, add-to-carts and purchases per product to decide what to change next, one change at a time.

A WooCommerce product page sells when a shopper can answer four questions on a phone without hunting: what is it, what will it cost in total, when will it arrive, and can I trust this store? Put sharp photos, a descriptive title, the GST-inclusive price, the variation picker, delivery and cash on delivery details and a clear Add to cart button near the top. Follow with a scannable description, specifications, reviews and a short returns summary, and keep the whole page fast.

Most shoppers buy on their phones, so in my WooCommerce development work product pages, cart and checkout are kept light and simple. This guide covers what goes on the page and where; the checkout itself is covered in WooCommerce checkout optimisation.

The first screen on a phone

Open one of your product pages on an ordinary Android phone and look at what appears before you scroll. Ideally that's the main image, title, price, star rating, variation picker and Add to cart button. Things that commonly push them out of view:

  • Stacked announcement bars, large headers and promotional banners
  • Pop-ups and chat bubbles covering the button
  • Oversized image galleries that fill the whole screen

A sticky Add to cart bar that appears as the shopper scrolls helps on long pages; many themes include one. How you change the layout depends on your set-up: block themes edit the Single Product template under Appearance > Editor > Templates, Elementor Pro's Theme Builder has a single product template, and classic themes usually need a child theme and WooCommerce hooks.

Photos and video

  • A consistent main image on the same plain background across the catalogue, so category pages look tidy.
  • Then the photos that answer questions: other angles, close-ups of texture or stitching, something that shows scale, the product in use, and what's in the box.
  • One aspect ratio for every image, usually square, so the gallery doesn't jump as photos load.
  • A photo for each variation. In Product data > Variations, each variation can have its own image, which swaps in when the shopper picks that colour.
  • Zoom and lightbox: WooCommerce includes gallery zoom, lightbox and slider features that most themes switch on; check yours does.
  • Short videos for texture, fit or how something works, embedded from YouTube or as a small file, without autoplaying sound.

Resize and compress every image before upload; image optimisation for WordPress explains how without losing quality.

Title, price and short description

  • Titles that say what it is: product type, the key attribute and the size or quantity, such as "Cold-pressed groundnut oil, 1 litre glass bottle", rather than a brand-only name.
  • Prices inclusive of GST, so the total doesn't jump at checkout. WooCommerce shows the regular price struck through beside a sale price automatically.
  • Delivery cost upfront: a line such as "Free delivery on orders above" your threshold, or the flat charge, beside the price.
  • The short description (the Product short description box) appears near the price on most themes. Use two to four lines or bullets with the facts that decide the purchase.
  • Packaged goods: if you sell pre-packaged products, Indian packaging rules generally require details such as MRP, net quantity and country of origin to be shown on online listings too. Check the current requirements with your CA or a lawyer.

For the words themselves, see how to write product descriptions that sell.

Variations that don't confuse

  • Use colour and size swatches instead of dropdowns where a plugin or your theme supports them.
  • Mark or hide combinations that are out of stock, so nobody picks one and hits an error.
  • Pre-select the most popular option with Default Form Values, so the exact price shows immediately instead of a range.
  • Put a size guide link right beside the size picker.

Setting attributes and variations up cleanly is covered in WooCommerce product variations.

Delivery, COD and returns beside the button

Shoppers often message a store to ask these things, so answer them where the decision happens:

  • A pin code checker showing whether you deliver there, an estimated delivery date and whether COD is available; see pin code delivery checkers.
  • Dispatch time, such as "Dispatched within two working days", if you can keep that promise.
  • Payment options in plain words: UPI, cards, net banking and COD where offered.
  • A one-line returns summary with a link to the full policy.
  • A WhatsApp button that opens a chat with the product name already typed in.

Description, specifications and FAQs

  • Description: lead with why it matters, then the details, uses and care instructions, using short paragraphs and subheadings. Write your own; copied manufacturer text appears on every competitor's site.
  • Specifications as attributes: add them in Product data > Attributes with "Visible on the product page" ticked, and WooCommerce shows them as a tidy table. Consistent attributes also power product filters.
  • Product FAQs: turn the questions customers ask on WhatsApp into short answers on the page.
  • Tabs or one scrolling page: on phones, shoppers often miss content hidden in tabs. Accordions or a single scrolling page usually work better.

Reviews and trust

  • In WooCommerce > Settings > Products, enable reviews and star ratings, and show the "verified owner" label. You can also limit reviews to verified buyers.
  • Ask for a review after delivery, not after payment, and invite photos.
  • Show low-stock messages only when they're true; the stock display setting in Inventory can show quantities only when stock runs low.
  • Never write, buy or incentivise fake reviews. Collecting and moderating real ones is covered in WooCommerce product reviews.

Cross-sells without clutter

WooCommerce's Linked Products tab has upsells, shown on the product page, and cross-sells, shown in the cart, while related products are picked automatically from categories and tags. A few well-chosen suggestions help; a wall of them distracts from the product being sold. Bundles, upsells and cross-sells explains when each works.

Speed and search

  • Don't lazy-load the main product image. It's usually the largest element on the page, and delaying it makes the page feel slow. Lazy-load gallery images and everything further down instead.
  • Watch the extras: sliders, review widgets, chat tools and tracking scripts add up quickly on product pages. WooCommerce speed optimisation covers hosting, caching and scripts.
  • Product schema: WooCommerce outputs basic product structured data, and SEO plugins extend it, so price, availability and ratings can appear in Google results. Test it in Google's Rich Results Test.
  • Unique titles and descriptions for every product; WooCommerce SEO covers product and category pages in depth.

Measure, then change one thing at a time

With GA4 e-commerce tracking in place, the e-commerce purchases report shows how often each product is viewed, added to cart and bought. Read it like this:

  • Many views, few add-to-carts: the page isn't convincing. Look at photos, price clarity, delivery information and reviews.
  • Many add-to-carts, few purchases: something at the cart or checkout puts people off, often an unexpected delivery charge.
  • Few views: a traffic and search problem, not a page problem.

Change one thing, give it a few weeks, and compare. Then watch someone who has never seen your store buy a product on their own phone; wherever they hesitate is the next thing to fix.

FAQs

What should a WooCommerce product page include?

Clear photos, a descriptive title, the GST-inclusive price, variation options, delivery and COD details, an Add to cart button, a short summary, a full description with specifications, genuine reviews and a link to your returns policy.

How do I change the WooCommerce product page layout?

With a block theme, edit the Single Product template under Appearance > Editor. With Elementor Pro, build a single product template in the Theme Builder. Classic themes usually need a child theme and WooCommerce hooks, which is developer work.

How many product photos should I use?

Enough to answer the questions a shopper would ask if the product were in their hands: the front, other angles, close-ups, scale, the product in use and what's included. Keep them consistent in size and background.

Should product descriptions be long or short?

Both, in the right places. Put a short summary of the deciding facts near the price, and a fuller description with specifications and FAQs lower down for shoppers who want detail.

Why do shoppers view my products but not add them to cart?

Usually because a question is left unanswered: the total price, delivery time, COD, sizing or trust. Check the first phone screen, add delivery and returns details beside the button, and improve photos and reviews.
