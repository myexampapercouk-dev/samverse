Seasonal and Festival Campaigns on Your Website: Landing Pages, Offers, Gift Guides, Speed and Tracking for Diwali, Rakhi, Navratri and Wedding Season

Plan festival and seasonal campaigns on your website: a permanent page per festival, coupons and scheduling, gift guides, speed under traffic and tracking.

Key takeaways
  • Give each festival one permanent page, such as a Diwali gifts page, and update it every year instead of creating a new URL that has to start from zero each season.
  • Prepare weeks ahead: offers and coupons scheduled, order-by dates on every page, gift guides by budget and recipient, and the site tested for speed and checkout before traffic rises.
  • Tag every campaign link and track enquiries, WhatsApp clicks and orders, then review what worked while it is fresh so next year starts from evidence, not memory.

A festival campaign on your website works best when it lives on one permanent page per festival, goes live several weeks before people start buying, and has its offer, deadlines and delivery cut-offs stated clearly. Behind it you need coupons or sale prices scheduled in advance, a site that stays fast when traffic rises, and tracking on every link you share, so you know whether Instagram, WhatsApp broadcasts or Google Ads brought the orders and enquiries.

Campaign pages are what my landing page design service is built for: one clear message and one goal, a lead form and WhatsApp, a thank-you page, and GA4 events, Meta Pixel and Google Ads conversion tracking set up so you can see what each campaign produced. For online stores, festival offers run on coupons, scheduled sale prices and a checkout that holds up under load, which is part of WooCommerce store development.

This guide covers planning a season end to end, for shops, service businesses and B2B companies alike.

Your own analytics are a better guide than any calendar. Compare last year's orders and enquiries by week, and use Google Trends to see when searches for your festival products start to rise in your state.

One permanent page per festival

The most common mistake is creating a new page every year, with a new address, then deleting it after the festival. Each new URL starts from zero: Google has to discover and index it, and any links or shares it earned last year are lost. Use one address per festival and update it every year:

https://example.com/diwali-gifts/              reused and updated every year
https://example.com/diwali-sale-2026/          starts from zero, dies after one season
  • Before the season: update the products, prices, offer and order-by dates, and change the year in the title if you use one.
  • After the season: keep the page live with a line saying the offer has ended, the best of your regular range, and an option to be told about next year's collection. Don't delete it or redirect it to the homepage.
  • Ad-only versions: if you run ads with offer-specific variations, those pages can be kept out of Google, so they don't compete with the permanent page you want to rank.

What a festival landing page needs

  1. A headline with the offer and the occasion, such as "Diwali corporate gift hampers, delivered across Pune".
  2. The deadline that matters: order-by dates for delivery before the festival, last booking slots, or when the offer ends.
  3. Products or packages for the season, with prices or price ranges, not your whole catalogue.
  4. Offer terms in plain words: minimum order, which products, and whether it combines with other discounts.
  5. Proof: genuine reviews, photos of last year's hampers or events, and anything that builds trust with a first-time buyer.
  6. One clear action, with WhatsApp, a short form and a phone number as routes to the same enquiry or order.
  7. A short FAQ on delivery areas, bulk orders, customisation and returns.

If you send ad traffic to the page, the headline should repeat what the ad promised; landing page mistakes that waste ad budget covers what usually goes wrong.

Offers, coupons and scheduling in WordPress

Set offers up in advance so nobody has to remember to switch them on at midnight:

  • Scheduled sale prices. On a WooCommerce product, the Schedule link next to the sale price lets you set start and end dates, so the festival price appears and disappears on time.
  • Coupons with limits. WooCommerce coupons can have an expiry date, a minimum spend, usage limits per customer and product or category restrictions. WooCommerce coupon types and discounts covers every setting, including festival-sale tips.
  • Scheduled pages and posts. In the WordPress editor, change "Immediately" next to Publish to a date and time to publish a page or announcement automatically.
  • Banners and notices. A site-wide banner can be scheduled with a block visibility plugin, or put on a dated checklist so it is removed when the offer ends.

Test the offer before launch: apply each coupon, try combinations you didn't intend, and place a test order. Countdown timers are fine if the deadline is real; a timer that resets for every visitor damages trust.

Gift guides that make choosing easy

Festival buyers often know who they are buying for and how much they want to spend, but not what to buy. Gift guides answer that:

  • By budget: under a set amount, mid-range and premium, using price bands that suit your range
  • By recipient: parents, siblings, colleagues, clients, children
  • Ready-made hampers and bundles, with a photo of exactly what is inside
  • Gift options at checkout: a gift message and delivery to a different address
  • Gift cards for last-minute buyers who have missed the delivery cut-off; see selling gift cards in WooCommerce
  • A bulk enquiry form for corporate orders, asking for quantity, delivery date and branding

Speed and stability when traffic rises

A festival is the worst time for your site to slow down or for checkout to fail. Do the work beforehand:

  • Test speed on a phone for the festival page, a product page, the cart and checkout, using mobile data rather than office Wi-Fi.
  • Check caching and a CDN, remembering that cart, checkout and account pages can't be cached and depend on your hosting.
  • Compress the new campaign images and avoid heavy sliders or autoplay video at the top of the page.
  • Update plugins and test before the season, then pause non-urgent updates during the peak.
  • Take a full backup, confirm uptime monitoring is on, and place a test payment through every method.
  • Check stock levels and low-stock alerts, so bestsellers don't oversell.

If your store already feels slow, fix it well before the season; WooCommerce speed optimisation explains where the time usually goes.

WhatsApp, Google Ads and tracking

  • Tag every link you share in WhatsApp broadcasts, Instagram, emails and QR codes with UTM parameters, so GA4 shows which channel brought each visit; see UTM tags explained.
  • Track what matters: form submissions, WhatsApp clicks, phone taps and purchases as key events in GA4.
  • Set up Google Ads conversion tracking before the campaign starts, so automated bidding has real conversions to learn from; Google Ads conversion tracking on WordPress covers the options.
  • Send WhatsApp messages only to people who agreed to hear from you, and keep festival messages few and useful.
  • Update your Google Business Profile with festival opening hours and an offer post; see Google Business Profile posts and offers.

A campaign timeline

WhenWhat to do
Two months beforeReview last year's results, decide the offer and products, and brief photos and copy
Six to four weeks beforeUpdate the permanent festival page, build gift guides, set up coupons and scheduled prices, and test speed
Three to two weeks beforeSet up tracking and ads, update your Business Profile, and message past customers who opted in
During the seasonWatch orders, stock and site uptime daily, reply to enquiries quickly, and update order-by dates as they pass
Straight afterMark the offer as ended, review results by channel and product, and note what to change next year

B2B and wedding businesses often need a longer lead time, since corporate gifting budgets and wedding bookings are decided well before the date.

After the season

Review results while they are fresh: orders or enquiries by channel, conversion rate on the festival page, best-selling products or packages, coupon use, and any problems with delivery, stock or the site. Write down three things to repeat and three to change. Next year's campaign then starts from evidence rather than memory.

FAQs

When should I start my Diwali campaign on the website?

Have the festival page updated and live several weeks before Diwali, earlier for corporate gifting and anything that needs delivery across India. Your own analytics from last year show when buying actually started for your business.

Should I create a new festival page every year?

No. Keep one permanent address, such as a Diwali gifts page, and update it every year. It keeps whatever visibility and links it has earned instead of starting from zero.

Should festival landing pages be indexed by Google?

Your permanent festival page, yes, because people search for festival products and services. Short-lived variations built only for ads can be kept out of Google so they don't compete with it.

How do I schedule a festival sale in WooCommerce?

Use the Schedule option next to the sale price on each product for start and end dates, and set an expiry date and usage limits on any coupons. Test both before the season starts.

Will my website cope with festival traffic?

Most well-built sites on decent hosting cope if they are cached and tested in advance. Stores need extra care, because cart and checkout pages can't be cached, so test checkout on a phone before the rush.
