Setting Up Cloudflare's Free Plan on WordPress: SSL, Caching and Common Problems

Set up Cloudflare's free plan on WordPress safely: nameservers, the right SSL mode, cache exclusions, APO, security settings and fixes for 52x errors.

Key takeaways
  • On the free plan you normally change nameservers, so copy every DNS record first, keep MX and mail hostnames DNS only, and turn off DNSSEC at the registrar before switching.
  • Use Full (strict) SSL with a valid certificate on your server; Flexible makes Cloudflare talk to your server over HTTP, which causes redirect loops and mixed content.
  • Leave HTML caching off unless you can bypass wp-admin, cart, checkout and logged-in users, test Bot Fight Mode against payment callbacks, and read 52x errors as problems reaching your server.

Cloudflare's free plan puts a global network between your visitors and your hosting. Set up well, it takes load off your server, serves files from nearby locations and filters junk traffic. Set up in a hurry, it causes redirect loops and lost email. Cloudflare changes its dashboard and plan features from time to time, so treat menu names here as a guide and check its current documentation as you go.

Full setup or partial setup?

  • Full setup (changing nameservers): Cloudflare becomes your DNS provider. You copy your DNS records into Cloudflare, then change the nameservers at your registrar to the two Cloudflare gives you. This is the normal route on the free plan.
  • Partial (CNAME) setup: your DNS stays where it is and only chosen hostnames point to Cloudflare. At the time of writing this is limited to higher paid plans, so check before planning around it.

If your host offers its own Cloudflare integration, read its guide first to avoid two conflicting setups. Each DNS record in Cloudflare is either proxied (orange cloud: traffic passes through Cloudflare) or DNS only (grey cloud). Website records are normally proxied. Mail records must not be.

Moving your DNS without breaking email

Cloudflare scans your existing DNS records when you add a site, but it can miss some. Compare its list line by line with your current zone before changing nameservers, especially:

  • MX records for Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Zoho or your host's mail
  • TXT records for SPF, DKIM, DMARC and site verifications
  • Mail hostnames such as mail, webmail or smtp, which should be DNS only
  • Subdomains for a shop, booking system, staging site or app

If DNSSEC is on at your registrar, turn it off before changing nameservers and re-enable it through Cloudflare once the zone is active; old DNSSEC settings can stop the domain resolving. Nameserver changes take a while to spread, so switch at a quiet time and leave the old zone untouched until everything works.

SSL/TLS modes, and why Flexible causes loops

Cloudflare encrypts the connection between the visitor and Cloudflare. The SSL/TLS mode decides what happens on the second leg, between Cloudflare and your hosting:

ModeCloudflare to your serverUse it?
FlexiblePlain HTTP, unencryptedAvoid for WordPress
FullHTTPS, but the certificate isn't checkedOnly as a short stopgap
Full (strict)HTTPS with a valid certificateYes, the target for every site

Flexible causes the classic loop. Your site redirects HTTP to HTTPS, but Cloudflare always asks your server over HTTP, so the server keeps replying "go to HTTPS" until the browser shows ERR_TOO_MANY_REDIRECTS. Flexible can also make WordPress think visitors are on HTTP, causing mixed content. If you're stuck, see how to fix ERR_TOO_MANY_REDIRECTS.

Full (strict) needs a valid certificate on your server: the free Let's Encrypt certificate most hosts provide, or a free Cloudflare Origin CA certificate. Origin certificates are trusted only by Cloudflare, so if you later switch the record to DNS only, visitors will see warnings. Cloudflare has also been introducing an automatic mode, so check which mode your site actually uses.

Caching: what to cache and what to bypass

By default, Cloudflare caches static files such as images, CSS and JavaScript and passes HTML pages through to your server. That's safe for WordPress and a good start, alongside a caching plugin or your host's cache; see WordPress caching explained.

If you add a rule to cache HTML too, exclude anything personal or changing:

  • /wp-admin/ and wp-login.php
  • WooCommerce cart, checkout and My Account pages
  • Logged-in users and shoppers with items in their cart, usually detected by cookies
  • Search results, previews and form submissions

Matching on cookies may not be available on every plan, which is one reason many owners leave HTML caching off on the free plan or use APO. Purge the cache after design changes, use Development Mode while editing, and test switches such as Rocket Loader on key pages, because they can break some scripts.

APO for WordPress

Automatic Platform Optimization (APO) is Cloudflare's WordPress option for caching whole pages on its network. It works with the official Cloudflare WordPress plugin, which tells Cloudflare when content changes, and is designed to skip the cache for logged-in users and common WooCommerce pages. For free-plan sites it's a paid add-on (included with some paid plans at the time of writing), so check current pricing and compatibility notes. It helps most when visitors are spread across regions, but it won't fix a slow server: the admin, checkout and uncached pages still depend on it.

Security settings worth knowing

  • Always Use HTTPS: redirects HTTP to HTTPS at Cloudflare. Keep one HTTPS redirect in charge rather than stacking rules in Cloudflare, your host and a plugin.
  • Bot Fight Mode: challenges traffic that looks automated, but can also block payment gateway callbacks, uptime monitors or integrations, and on the free plan you may not be able to make exceptions. If something breaks, test with it off.
  • WAF and custom rules: the free plan includes some managed protection and a small number of custom rules, useful for challenging visits to wp-login.php or blocking xmlrpc.php if nothing needs it.
  • Under Attack mode: challenges every visitor during an attack. Turn it off afterwards.

With all traffic arriving via Cloudflare, check that your host or security plugin reads the real visitor IP, or a login limiter can block everyone at once.

Common problems after switching

  • Too many redirects: usually Flexible SSL or duplicate HTTPS and www redirects. Move to Full (strict) and keep one redirect.
  • Mixed content: old http:// addresses in content, theme settings or the database. Automatic HTTPS Rewrites can cover some, but fix the source; see fixing mixed content warnings.
  • Email stopped: MX, SPF or DKIM records weren't copied, or mail hostnames were proxied.

Cloudflare's 52x errors mean it couldn't get a proper answer from your server:

ErrorUsual meaningFirst check
520Empty or unexpected responseServer error logs, crashing PHP, a security module
521Connection refusedIs the web server running? Is the host's firewall blocking Cloudflare?
522Connection timed outWrong IP in DNS, an overloaded server or a firewall dropping requests
523Origin unreachableThe A record points to an old or wrong IP
524Server took too long to respondSlow pages, long imports or exports, heavy reports
525 / 526SSL handshake failed / invalid certificateInstall or renew the certificate on your server

Send your host the Ray ID from the error page, the time and the URL. For timeouts that start on the server, see 502 and 504 errors on WordPress.
