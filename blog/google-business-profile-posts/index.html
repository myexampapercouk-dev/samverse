How to Use Google Business Profile Posts, Offers and Photos to Win Local Customers

How to use Google Business Profile posts, offers, events, photos and questions to turn profile views into calls and visits, with post ideas and a routine.

Key takeaways
  • Posts, offers and fresh photos mainly help convert people already comparing profiles; Google has not said posting improves map rankings.
  • Use updates for news, offers for dated deals and events for dated happenings, each with a real photo, one message and one clear button linking to the right page.
  • A short weekly routine of replying to reviews, posting once and adding photos keeps a profile active, and UTM-tagged links show what it brings in.

Setting up your Google Business Profile properly is step one, and our Google Business Profile checklist covers it. But a complete profile that hasn't changed in two years can look like a business that has quietly closed. This guide is about step two: using posts, offers, photos and other features to keep your profile active and persuade the people looking at it to call, visit or book.

Why an active profile matters

When someone searches for "bakery near me" or "physiotherapist in Andheri", they usually compare two or three profiles in the map results before deciding. At that moment, recent photos, a current offer and fresh replies to reviews all say "this business is open, busy and cares about its customers".

Be realistic about rankings, though. Google hasn't said that posting improves your position in the map results, and your category, distance, reviews and profile completeness matter far more. Treat posts as a way to win the customers who are already looking at your profile, not as a ranking trick.

The three types of post

Post typeBest forExample
UpdateNews, recent work, tips, new services and announcements"We now offer home sample collection across Sectors 50 to 57"
OfferTime-limited discounts or deals, with dates and terms"Free AC check-up with every service booked before 15 April"
EventSomething happening on specific dates"Festive craft market at our store this weekend"

Each post can include a photo or short video, some text and a button such as Book, Order online, Learn more or Call now. Offers and events run between the dates you set, while updates move down your profile as newer ones appear, so a steady rhythm works better than a burst of posts once a year.

What to post: ideas by business type

  • Clinics: a new doctor joining, extended hours, a health camp, or a simple explainer on when to see a specialist, keeping medical claims careful and accurate
  • Restaurants and cafés: a new dish, the weekend special, festival menus, party catering, and a link to order directly from your website
  • Salons, gyms and studios: new services, batch timings, bridal or festive packages and trainer introductions
  • Contractors and home services: a finished project with before-and-after photos, or seasonal reminders like pre-monsoon waterproofing and pre-summer AC servicing
  • Shops: new stock, festival collections and extended hours during wedding season
  • B2B and professional firms: a completed project, a new certification or a helpful reminder about an upcoming deadline your clients care about

Plan posts around the moments your customers care about, such as festivals, exam season, monsoon or the financial year-end, alongside your website's own campaigns. See seasonal and festival campaigns on your website.

How to write posts people act on

  • Lead with the point. Only the first few lines show in the preview, so start with the news or offer, not "Greetings from ...".
  • One message per post, with one clear button.
  • Use a real photo of your work, team, food or premises. Stock images and text-heavy graphics get cropped awkwardly and look like adverts.
  • Link to the most relevant page, such as the service page, menu or booking page, not always your homepage.
  • Tag your links with UTM parameters so you can see profile visitors separately in Google Analytics. See UTM tags explained.
  • Keep offers honest and specific: state the dates, what's included and any conditions in the terms.
  • Use the call button rather than typing your phone number into the text, and follow Google's content policies, because posts that break them can be rejected or removed.

Photos and videos: keep them coming

Photos are often the first thing people look at on a profile, and customers can add their own too. Keep yours current:

  • A clear exterior photo, so people recognise your entrance or signboard when they arrive
  • Interior, team and "at work" photos that show the real place and people
  • Your products, dishes or finished projects, added as they happen
  • Short videos, such as a walkthrough of your premises or a quick look at how you work

Check the photos customers have added. Most help, but if one is misleading, offensive or not of your business, you can report it to Google. Don't try to bury honest but unflattering photos under a flood of new ones; fix whatever they show instead.

Questions, products and menus

Questions

Google has been changing how questions work on Business Profiles. Some profiles still show a public questions-and-answers section, while elsewhere Google increasingly answers questions itself using AI, drawing on your profile details, reviews and website. Check what your own profile shows. Either way, the fix is the same: keep your details complete and accurate, and answer common questions (parking, UPI payments, home visits, delivery areas) clearly on your website and in your profile.

Products and services

The products and services sections let you show items with a photo, a description and optionally a price or price range. They suit shops, bakeries and service businesses with standard packages, and unlike posts, they stay in place until you change them.

Menus

Restaurants and cafés can add a menu to their profile. Keep it in step with the menu on your website and update both when prices or dishes change, because customers notice when what they saw online doesn't match the bill.

Measure what's working

Your profile's performance view shows how many people saw it and how many called, asked for directions, clicked through to your website or booked. Check it monthly and compare it with what you posted:

  • Did website clicks rise in the weeks you posted offers?
  • Which posts and photos do customers mention when they call?
  • In Google Analytics, how many visits and enquiries came through your tagged profile links?

A simple weekly routine

  1. Reply to new reviews, positive and negative; see how to get more Google reviews
  2. Publish one post: an update, offer or event
  3. Add two or three new photos
  4. Check your hours, especially before holidays
  5. Once a month, review performance and adjust what you post

This needn't take long. Many businesses add a trusted staff member as a manager so the routine doesn't depend on the owner; just make sure you stay the primary owner of the profile.
