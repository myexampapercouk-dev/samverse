How to Use Google Business Profile Posts, Offers and Photos to Win Local Customers

How to use Google Business Profile posts, offers, events and photos to win local customers in India, with post ideas, a weekly routine and common mistakes.

Key takeaways
  • Google Business Profile posts, offers and photos show real activity and give searchers a reason to call, visit or click through to your website.
  • A simple weekly routine of one useful post and a few fresh photos is more effective than occasional bursts of activity.
  • Link each post to the matching page on your website, with clear tracking, so you can see which posts actually bring enquiries.

Google Business Profile posts are short updates, offers and event notices that appear on your profile in Google Search and Maps. To use them well, post something useful every week, add real photos regularly, link each post to the right page on your website, and answer questions and reviews promptly. Together these show Google and customers that the business is active and worth contacting.

For local businesses in India, from clinics and salons to hardware shops and coaching classes, the profile is often the first thing a customer sees. This guide covers the types of posts, what to write, a simple weekly routine and the mistakes to avoid.

What Google Business Profile posts are

Posts are mini-updates published from your profile dashboard or directly from Google Search when you are signed in as the owner. Each post can include text, a photo or video, and a button such as "Call now", "Book", "Order online" or "Learn more" linking to your website.

The main post types are:

TypeBest forExample
UpdateNews, tips, new services, recent work"New weekend batch for Class 10 maths starting next month"
OfferPromotions with a start and end date"Free AC servicing check with any repair this week"
EventWorkshops, open days, sales, launches"Free dental check-up camp on Sunday"

Older posts move down the profile as new ones are added, so regular posting keeps fresh content visible. Google changes how and where posts are displayed from time to time, so check the current layout in your own profile rather than relying on old screenshots.

Why posts and photos matter for local customers

Posts are unlikely to be a large ranking factor on their own. Their real value is conversion: when someone compares three businesses on Maps, the one with recent posts, current photos and quick replies looks open, active and reliable. That is often what tips the decision.

Posts also let you:

  • Promote seasonal offers before Diwali, wedding season, monsoon or exams.
  • Highlight services customers may not know you offer.
  • Answer common questions before customers call.
  • Send interested searchers to a specific page on your website rather than the homepage.

If the profile is not appearing at all, posts will not help yet; fix that first with the guide on why a business is not showing on Google Maps.

Google Business Profile post ideas

Running out of ideas is the main reason businesses stop posting. Keep a list like this and rotate through it:

  1. Recent work: a finished kitchen, a new haircut style, a repaired machine, with a short caption.
  2. Tips: "Three things to check before booking a pest control service".
  3. Service spotlights: one service per post, with a link to its page on your website.
  4. Seasonal offers: festivals, school terms, monsoon preparation, summer services.
  5. Events: workshops, demo days, health camps, open houses.
  6. Behind the scenes: your workshop, kitchen, clinic or team at work, with consent.
  7. Answers to common questions: timings, parking, payment options such as UPI, home visits.
  8. New stock or products for shops, with an "Order online" button to your store.
  9. Review highlights: thank a customer for a review, without sharing personal details.

How to write a good post

  • Lead with the point: the first line shows in the preview, so make it the offer or news.
  • Keep it short: a few sentences are enough.
  • Mention the area naturally, such as "home visits across Kothrud and Karve Nagar", when it is genuinely relevant.
  • Use a real photo, not a stock image or a poster with lots of text.
  • Add one clear button to a relevant page, not just the homepage.
  • Avoid phone numbers in post text; use the call button instead, as Google may reject posts with contact details in the text.

Write in the language your customers search in. Some businesses post in English and Hindi or a regional language on alternate weeks, which can work well if both audiences matter.

Photos: the most underused part of the profile

Photos often influence customers more than text. Add them regularly, not just once at setup:

  • Exterior photos that help people recognise the shop or building.
  • Interior photos showing the space, cleanliness and atmosphere.
  • Work and product photos, updated as new work is done.
  • Team photos, with consent.
  • A logo and cover photo that match your website.

Customers also add photos, and you cannot always control those. Adding good photos of your own keeps the overall impression accurate. The guide on Google Business Profile photos covers sizes, types and what to avoid.

A simple weekly routine

Consistency matters more than volume. A routine that takes a short time each week:

  1. Monday: publish one post (rotate update, offer and tip).
  2. Midweek: add a few new photos of recent work.
  3. Daily or every other day: reply to new reviews and questions.
  4. Monthly: check hours, holiday timings, services and phone number are correct.
  5. Monthly: check the profile performance view for calls, direction requests and website clicks.

Reviews are part of the same routine; see how to get more Google reviews and how to handle negative reviews.

Linking posts to your website and tracking results

A post is most useful when the button leads to a page that matches it: an offer post to an offer landing page, a service post to that service page, a product post to the product in your store. Sending everyone to the homepage wastes the intent.

To see what works, add UTM parameters to the post link, for example a source of "google", a medium of "gbp-post" and a campaign name per post. Then you can see visits and enquiries from posts in Google Analytics, separate from regular search traffic. If the landing page is slow or confusing, fix that first; a post can only bring people to the door.

For shops selling online, posts can link to a WooCommerce or Shopify product or collection page with UPI and COD clearly shown, so a Maps visitor can buy without visiting.

Products, services and the Q&A section

Posts are only one part of the profile. Two other sections deserve the same attention because they appear in the same space and answer buying questions directly.

Products and services. Add your main services with short descriptions, and for shops, add key products or categories with photos. This helps customers see at a glance whether you offer what they need, such as "inverter battery replacement" or "bridal makeup". Keep the names close to the words customers actually use, and avoid listing services you do not offer just to appear in more searches.

Questions and answers. Where the feature is available on your profile, anyone can ask a question, and anyone can answer it, which means a wrong answer from a stranger can sit there unnoticed. Check it regularly and reply as the owner. Some businesses seed it by asking and answering common questions themselves, such as parking, home visits, payment methods or whether appointments are needed.

Attributes and hours. Keep holiday hours updated before festivals, and set attributes like wheelchair access or online appointments accurately. A customer who arrives to a closed shop after trusting your listing rarely comes back.

All of this should match your website exactly: the same name, address, phone number, hours and service names. Mismatches confuse customers and weaken the signals Google uses to trust the listing. The Google Business Profile checklist covers the full setup.

Common mistakes with profile posts

  • Posting in bursts, then going quiet for months.
  • Posters full of text that are hard to read on a phone.
  • Expired offers left visible after the end date.
  • Every button pointing to the homepage.
  • Keyword-stuffed posts that read badly for customers.
  • Ignoring questions and reviews while still posting.

FAQs

How often should I post on Google Business Profile?

Once a week is a sensible target for most local businesses. Regular, useful posts matter more than posting several times a day.

Do Google Business Profile posts help ranking?

They are unlikely to move rankings much on their own. Their main value is showing an active business and turning profile views into calls, visits and website clicks.

What are good Google Business Profile post ideas?

Recent work, tips, service spotlights, seasonal offers, events, behind-the-scenes photos and answers to common customer questions.

Why was my Google Business Profile post rejected?

Common reasons include phone numbers in the text, prohibited content, misleading offers or low-quality images. Check Google's current content policies and edit the post.

Should my posts link to my website?

Yes. Link each post to the most relevant page, and add UTM tracking so you can see which posts bring enquiries.
