How to Design a Contact Page That Turns Visitors Into Enquiries

How to design a contact page that gets enquiries: which contact options to show, short forms, WhatsApp and click-to-call, maps, hours, reply times and tracking.

Key takeaways
  • Lead with one or two contact options your best customers actually use, such as click-to-call or WhatsApp, and keep the form short.
  • Show your address, landmark, map and hours, and tell visitors honestly when and how you will reply.
  • Place a few trust signals beside the form and track form submissions, call taps and WhatsApp clicks.

Your contact page is where interested visitors go when they're ready to act. Yet on many business websites it's an afterthought: a form, an address and a stock photo of a telephone headset. If the page is confusing, slow or feels unattended, people who were ready to enquire go back to Google and call the next business. This guide covers how to design the page itself. If your form is set up but submissions aren't arriving, start with why your contact form isn't getting enquiries instead.

What visitors want from a contact page

Someone who opens your contact page has usually decided they're interested. Now they're asking three quick questions:

  • Can I reach you the way I prefer? Some people want to call, some want WhatsApp, some want to write.
  • Will anyone actually reply, and when?
  • Are you real and nearby? An address, map and hours answer this at a glance.

Design the page to answer all three within one screen on a phone. A short headline such as "Talk to us about your project" and one line on what happens next works better than a paragraph about your company history.

Choose your contact options deliberately

Listing every possible channel with equal weight makes people hesitate. Pick one or two primary options based on how your best customers actually get in touch, and show the rest as secondary.

Click-to-callUrgent or local services such as repairs, clinics and pest controlShow the hours when someone answers

If different people handle different requests, such as sales, support and job applications, say so clearly so each enquiry reaches the right person first time.

Keep the form short and friendly

The form is for people who would rather write than talk. Ask only for what you need to reply well:

  • Name
  • Phone number (using a field that opens the number keypad on mobile) or email
  • An optional dropdown for the type of enquiry, if it helps you route or prioritise
  • A message box with a helpful prompt, such as "Tell us what you need and your location"

Put clear labels above each field rather than placeholder text that disappears when people start typing, and use a specific button label like "Send enquiry" instead of "Submit". Make error messages explain exactly what to fix. Save longer qualifying questions for the follow-up call.

Spam is a real nuisance on contact pages, but hard puzzles block genuine customers too. Invisible methods are usually the better first step; see how to stop contact form spam.

Make WhatsApp and click-to-call work properly

On mobile, most people would rather tap than type. A few details make a big difference:

  • Make the phone number a tappable call link, and show it in full so desktop visitors can dial it from their phone
  • Link the WhatsApp button to your business number with a short pre-filled message, such as "Hi, I'm enquiring from your website about..."
  • Say when calls and messages are answered, for example "Mon to Sat, 9am to 7pm"
  • Make sure whoever answers knows what to ask and where to record the lead
  • Keep buttons large enough to tap, and check a floating chat widget isn't covering them

Add your address, map and hours

For businesses that customers visit, location details often matter more than the form. Include:

  • Your full address, written exactly as it appears on your Google Business Profile
  • A nearby landmark, since many Indian addresses are easier to find that way ("opposite the metro station, first floor above the bank")
  • Opening hours, plus a note about holidays and festival closures
  • Parking or access information where it's relevant
  • A map, or a "Get directions" link that opens Google Maps

Embedded maps can slow a page down, so load them carefully; see how to add Google Maps without slowing your site. If you visit customers at their location and don't want your address public, list the areas you cover instead.

Set expectations about replies

Uncertainty stops people from getting in touch. Tell visitors what happens next:

  1. When they'll hear back, such as "We reply within one working day", but only if you can genuinely keep that promise
  2. Who will contact them and how: a call, a WhatsApp message or an email
  3. What the next step usually is, such as a site visit, a short call or a written quote

Repeat the promise in the confirmation message or thank-you page after the form is sent, and in an automatic email reply if you use one. Then keep it. A fast, helpful reply wins more work than any design change.

Put trust signals near the form

The contact page is a decision point, so a little reassurance goes a long way:

  • One or two short testimonials, or your Google rating with a link to the reviews
  • A real photo of your team, office or shopfront rather than a stock image
  • Your registered business name, and your GSTIN where business customers expect it
  • A short privacy note, such as "We'll only use your details to reply to your enquiry", linking to your privacy policy

Avoid clutter. A few honest signals beside the form work better than a wall of badges and logos.

Track every enquiry

You can't improve a contact page if you don't know what it produces. Set up tracking for:

  • Form submissions, ideally confirmed with a thank-you page or a tracked event
  • Taps on the phone number and the WhatsApp button
  • Clicks on "Get directions" and any booking link

A tap on a call or WhatsApp button doesn't always mean a conversation happened, so also ask new customers how they found you. See call tracking for small businesses for ways to connect calls and chats to your website. Finally, test the form yourself every month and after plugin updates, because a silently broken form is an expensive problem.
