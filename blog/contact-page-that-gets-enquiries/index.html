How to Design a Contact Page That Turns Visitors Into Enquiries

Contact page design that gets enquiries: which contact options to show, short forms, WhatsApp and click-to-call, maps, hours, reply times and tracking.

Key takeaways
  • A contact page that gets enquiries offers the options your customers prefer, usually a short form, click-to-call and WhatsApp, with clear hours and reply times.
  • Keep the form short, ask only what you need to respond well, and send people to a thank-you page that confirms what happens next.
  • Track form submissions, calls and WhatsApp clicks separately, and make sure every enquiry reaches someone who replies quickly.

A contact page that gets enquiries makes it easy for a ready visitor to reach you in the way they prefer. In India that usually means a short form, a tap-to-call number and a WhatsApp button, plus your address and map if customers visit, your working hours and an honest note on how quickly you reply. Remove anything that slows them down, and track every enquiry so none are missed.

This guide covers contact page design that turns visitors into enquiries: what to include, how to design the form, where WhatsApp fits, and the mistakes that quietly lose leads.

Why the contact page matters

Visitors who reach the contact page have already decided they might want to talk to you. That makes it one of the most valuable pages on the site, yet it is often an afterthought: a generic form, an email address and a map. Small changes here can make a real difference, because you are not persuading cold traffic, just removing friction for interested people.

It also works as a trust page. Visitors check whether you are a real business with a real address, phone number and working hours before they get in touch.

Offer the contact options your customers prefer

Different customers want different channels. A homeowner with a leaking pipe wants to call. A busy professional prefers WhatsApp. A procurement manager sends an email or a detailed form. Offer the main channels and make each one work well:

  • Phone: one main number, clickable on mobile, with the hours it is answered.
  • WhatsApp: a button that opens a chat with a short pre-filled message. See WhatsApp on your business website.
  • Form: for enquiries that need detail, files or a quote.
  • Email: visible for those who prefer it, protected from spam bots where possible.

Avoid showing so many options that the page looks cluttered. If you have several departments or branches, group them clearly, such as "Sales", "Support" and "Branches".

Designing a contact form that people complete

Every extra field reduces the number of people who finish the form. Ask only what you need to give a useful first reply:

  1. Name.
  2. Phone or email (or both, if you will genuinely use both).
  3. What they need, as a dropdown if your services are clear-cut.
  4. A message box for details.

Add more fields only when they clearly help you respond, such as location for a service business, or project size and timeline for a B2B enquiry. For longer enquiries, a multi-step form keeps each step short; see multi-step forms for lead qualification.

Small details matter:

  • Use a specific button label, such as "Send enquiry" or "Get a quote", not just "Submit".
  • Use the right input types so mobile keyboards show numbers for phone fields.
  • Show clear error messages next to the field, not only at the top.
  • Add a short privacy line explaining how you use their details.
  • Protect the form from spam without making it hard for humans; see stopping contact form spam.

Contact page layout for mobile

Most visitors in India arrive on a phone. On mobile, the order matters:

  1. Headline and one line on what happens next.
  2. Call and WhatsApp buttons, side by side.
  3. The form.
  4. Address, map, hours and email.

Keep the map lower down, because embedded maps are heavy and can slow the page. A static image of the map linking to Google Maps is a lighter option. The guide on adding Google Maps to your website compares the approaches.

After the form: thank-you page and reply

What happens after someone clicks send shapes their impression as much as the page itself. Instead of a small "Message sent" line, send people to a thank-you page that:

  • Confirms the enquiry was received.
  • Says when and how you will reply.
  • Offers WhatsApp for anything urgent.
  • Suggests one useful next step, such as a relevant case study or guide.

A thank-you page also makes tracking easier. See thank-you pages for forms.

Then reply fast. An enquiry answered within the hour often goes further than one answered the next day, because the customer is likely to have contacted others too. Set up instant alerts by email or WhatsApp and a simple routine for following up; see how to respond to website enquiries fast.

Tracking enquiries properly

You cannot improve what you do not measure. Track these separately in Google Analytics 4:

  • Form submissions, ideally through thank-you page views or a form event.
  • Clicks on the call button.
  • Clicks on the WhatsApp button.
  • Clicks on the email address.

Then check monthly which pages and traffic sources bring enquiries. If many people visit the contact page but few contact you, something on it is putting them off. If the form gets submissions but you rarely receive them, the problem is delivery; see contact form not getting enquiries.

Writing the words on the page

Most contact pages say only "Contact us" and "Get in touch". A few lines of plain, specific copy do much more, because they answer the questions people have just before they decide to write.

  • Who will reply: "You will hear back from the person who will handle your project" or "Our front desk replies to all booking requests".
  • What to include: "Share your area, the problem and a photo if you can" helps people write useful first messages.
  • What happens next: a call, a quote by email, a site visit or an appointment slot.
  • What it costs to ask: if the first consultation or quote is free, say so clearly; if there is a visit charge, explain it.
  • Languages: if you speak Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati or another language, mention it. Many customers feel more comfortable writing when they know this.

Keep the tone warm and direct, in the same voice as the rest of the site. Avoid pressure tactics like countdown timers or "only two slots left" unless they are genuinely true. A calm, clear page builds more trust than an urgent one, and the people who contact you will arrive with better expectations, which makes the first conversation easier for both sides.

Common contact page mistakes

  • Long forms asking for budget, company size and more before any conversation.
  • Phone numbers shown as images, which cannot be tapped.
  • A personal Gmail address that looks less professional than a domain email.
  • Out-of-date hours or an old address.
  • Forms that send emails to an inbox nobody checks.
  • No mention of what happens after the enquiry.
  • Pop-ups and chat widgets covering the form on mobile.

Contact pages for different businesses

Local services and shops should lead with call and WhatsApp, then address, map, hours and parking. B2B and manufacturers should lead with a detailed enquiry or RFQ form, plus department emails and office addresses. Online stores on WooCommerce or Shopify should separate order support (with an order number field) from general enquiries, and link to delivery and return policies so customers find answers without writing in.

The contact page is only the last step. If few visitors get that far, look at the wider site with how to get more enquiries from your website.

FAQs

What should a contact page include?

A short intro on what happens next, a brief form, click-to-call, WhatsApp, a business email, your address and map if customers visit, working hours and an honest reply time.

How many fields should a contact form have?

As few as possible: usually name, phone or email, what they need and a message. Add fields only if they help you reply better.

How do I design a contact page that gets enquiries?

Put call and WhatsApp buttons first on mobile, keep the form short, set expectations about replies, add a thank-you page and track every enquiry type.

Should I add WhatsApp to my contact page?

For most Indian businesses, yes. Many customers prefer it to forms or calls, especially on mobile.

Why is my contact form not sending enquiries?

Usually email delivery problems, spam filtering or form plugin errors. Set up SMTP, test the form regularly and store submissions on the site as a backup.
