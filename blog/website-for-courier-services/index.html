Websites for Courier and Parcel Services: Pickup Booking, Rate Pages, Tracking, Franchise Enquiries and Business Accounts

Key takeaways
  • A courier website has three jobs: let people book a pickup, let them check what a parcel will cost, and let them track it, so build those three paths before anything else.
  • Branch and area pages with real addresses, timings and pin codes served win local searches like courier near me far better than one generic home page.
  • Business customers want a separate path: a monthly account enquiry, bulk rates on request, COD remittance terms and integration details, written plainly.

A website for a courier or parcel service should let a customer do three things in under a minute: book a pickup, see roughly what a parcel will cost, and track a shipment. Add branch pages with real addresses and pin codes, a WhatsApp button, a clear list of what you will not carry, and a separate path for business accounts, and the site starts bringing in bookings instead of just existing.

Most local courier franchises, city parcel services and small express companies in India still run on phone calls and walk-ins. That works until a customer searches "courier near me" at 7 pm, finds three options, and picks the one whose site answers their question. Here is how to plan the site so that is you.

If you are a franchisee of a national courier brand, check the brand's rules before building your own site. Many brands restrict use of their logo and name, and some do not allow franchisees to run separate websites at all. Never present yourself as the brand's head office; say clearly that you are an authorised franchise or booking centre only if your agreement says so.

Pages a courier website needs

  • Home: a tracking box, a Book Pickup button, a WhatsApp button and your service areas, all visible on a phone without scrolling.
  • Services: one page each for domestic parcels, documents, international shipments (if you offer them), same-day city delivery, heavy or bulk cargo, and e-commerce shipping.
  • Rates or rate calculator: how pricing works by weight, volumetric weight and zone, with sample rates if you are comfortable publishing them.
  • Track shipment: a working tracking page or a clear link to where tracking happens.
  • Branches: one page per branch with address, map, timings, phone and pin codes served.
  • Business accounts: what monthly accounts include and an enquiry form.
  • Prohibited and restricted items: what you cannot carry and why.
  • Packing guide, claims and FAQs: how to pack, what happens if something is damaged or lost, and common questions.
  • About and contact: registered business name, GSTIN, office address and how long you have operated.

Pickup booking that actually gets used

A pickup form should ask only what you need to send a rider: sender name and phone, pickup address and pin code, destination city or pin code, approximate weight and size, number of parcels, content type (documents, clothes, electronics and so on) and a preferred pickup slot. Anything more, such as receiver details or declared value, can be collected at pickup.

Send the booking to email and WhatsApp at the same time so the branch sees it within minutes. An auto-reply that confirms the request and says when you will call back stops customers from booking with someone else while they wait. If forms are arriving but nobody replies, read why contact forms stop bringing enquiries, because the problem is often delivery of the email rather than the form itself.

For a service with many riders, a simple WordPress booking plugin with pickup slots works. Larger operators may connect the form to their dispatch software. Start simple; the form should work reliably before it gets clever.

Rate pages: how much to publish

"Courier charges" and "parcel rates per kg" are common searches. You do not have to publish a full rate card, but you should explain how pricing works:

  • Charges depend on weight, destination zone and speed of service.
  • Volumetric weight: large light boxes are charged on size. Show the formula you use, for example length times width times height divided by your stated divisor, and give a worked example.
  • What is extra: fuel surcharge, COD fee, insurance or declared-value cover, packing material, remote area charges, GST.
  • Sample prices for common cases (a 500 g document to a metro city, a 5 kg parcel within the state), clearly marked as starting from and dated.

A simple calculator that takes weight, size and destination pin code is useful if your rates are stable. If they change often, a "get a quote on WhatsApp" button is more honest than a calculator showing old numbers. Never show a price you will not honour.

Tracking

Tracking is the most visited page on most courier sites. Options:

  • Your own system: if your software has an API, a developer can show tracking status on your own page.
  • Partner tracking: if you book through larger carriers, link to their tracking with the right number format, and explain which number the customer should use.
  • Manual: a WhatsApp link with a prefilled message such as "Track my shipment, AWB number:" so staff can reply quickly.

Whatever you choose, put the tracking box on the home page and make it work well on mobile. Explain what each status means, especially "in transit", "out for delivery" and "RTO" (return to origin), because confused receivers call the branch.

Branch and area pages for local search

People search "courier service in Andheri" or "parcel service near me". A page per real branch, with its own address, timings, phone, map and the pin codes it serves, matches these searches far better than one city page. Link each branch page to its Google Business Profile and keep name, address and phone identical on both.

Do not create pages for areas where you have no branch and no pickup service; those pages rarely rank and mislead customers. The local SEO guide for small businesses in India explains how to choose which area pages are worth writing.

Reviews matter a lot for couriers because trust is the main worry. Ask customers for a review after successful deliveries and reply to complaints politely. See how to get more Google reviews and how to handle negative reviews.

Business accounts and e-commerce sellers

Business customers are worth more than walk-ins, so give them their own page. Explain:

  • Who an account suits: online sellers, offices, distributors, clinics sending samples.
  • What is included: scheduled pickups, monthly billing, GST invoices, a single contact person.
  • COD: whether you offer it, the remittance cycle and any COD charge.
  • Returns (RTO) handling and how undelivered parcels are charged.
  • Integration: whether you work with shipping aggregators or can provide tracking links for a store's order emails.

Use a short enquiry form that asks for monthly volume, typical parcel weight, destinations and whether COD is needed. Say "rates on request based on volume" instead of inventing discounts. Many sellers run stores on Shopify or WooCommerce; they will ask whether your tracking can appear in their order notifications.

WhatsApp and calls

Most courier customers prefer to message or call. Add a WhatsApp button with a prefilled message ("I want to book a pickup from pin code"), a tap-to-call button on mobile, and branch numbers on each branch page. The guide to WhatsApp on a business website covers button placement and the WhatsApp Business app. Track button taps so you know which pages produce bookings; call tracking for small businesses explains simple ways to do it.

Trust, claims and prohibited items

Parcel fraud and fake courier calls are common, so trust signals carry weight:

  • Registered business name, GSTIN and a real office address.
  • Photos of your branch, vehicles and staff (with their permission).
  • A plain claims process: what is covered, the liability limit, how to report damage, time limits and documents needed.
  • A fraud warning: state that you never ask for OTPs, UPI PINs or payments to personal accounts to release a parcel.
  • A prohibited items list. Common examples include cash, explosives, flammable liquids, narcotics and hazardous chemicals; your own list depends on your carriers and the rules that apply, so take it from them rather than copying another site.

International shipping brings customs and documentation. Explain which documents senders need (such as invoice and ID) and that customs duty is decided by the destination country, without promising clearance times you cannot control.

Compliance and privacy

Courier businesses collect names, addresses and phone numbers of both senders and receivers. Keep forms on HTTPS, store bookings only as long as needed, and publish a privacy policy that says how you use this data. DPDP Act basics for websites and privacy policy and cookie basics in India cover what to include. Licensing and registration rules for courier and postal-type services can change, so confirm your obligations with your adviser rather than relying on a website guide.

Platform and speed

FAQs

What should a courier service website include?

A tracking box, pickup booking, a rate explanation or calculator, branch pages with addresses and pin codes, business account information, a prohibited items list, a claims process and WhatsApp and call buttons.

Should a courier website show rates?

Explain how rates work and give dated sample prices if you can honour them. If rates change often, offer a quick quote on WhatsApp instead of showing figures that may be out of date.

Can a courier franchise have its own website?

It depends on the franchise agreement. Check the brand's rules on logos, names and websites first, and never present your site as the brand's official head office.

How do I add shipment tracking to my website?

If your software has an API, a developer can show tracking on your own page. Otherwise link to the carrier's tracking page with clear instructions, or offer tracking through a prefilled WhatsApp message.

How can a courier business get more local customers online?

Create a page for each real branch, keep the Google Business Profile complete and consistent, collect reviews after deliveries and make booking and WhatsApp easy to find on mobile.

How do I attract e-commerce sellers as courier clients?

Give them a dedicated page covering pickups, COD remittance, returns, GST invoicing and tracking links, with an enquiry form asking for monthly volume and destinations.
