How to Write Product Descriptions That Sell and Rank: A Framework, Specs Tables and Examples for Indian Stores

How to write product descriptions that sell and rank: a simple framework, specs tables, real benefits, unique copy and SEO without keyword stuffing.

Key takeaways
  • Open with who the product is for and the main benefit, then give scannable details, a specifications table, care and delivery notes, and answers to common doubts.
  • Write your own copy instead of the manufacturer's text, use the words customers search with once or twice naturally, and keep facts accurate.

A product description that sells answers a shopper's questions in the order they ask them: what it is and who it's for, why it's worth buying, the exact details and specifications, how to use and care for it, and what happens about delivery and returns. Write it yourself rather than copying the manufacturer's text, lead with the main benefit, put the facts in bullets and a specifications table, and use the words customers search with naturally, without repeating them for Google.

When I set up a store through WooCommerce development, the product template, attributes and specification layout are planned before products go in; the product facts and descriptions come from you, because you know the products best. This guide gives you a framework for writing them.

The description is one part of the product page. For photos, prices, delivery details and layout, see WooCommerce product page optimization. All product examples below are fictional.

Short description vs description in WooCommerce

WooCommerce gives each product two text areas, and they do different jobs:

Product short descriptionProduct description
Where it showsNear the title, price and Add to cart button on most themesIn the Description tab or section further down
LengthTwo to four lines or bulletsAs long as the product needs
JobThe facts that decide the purchaseThe full story: benefits, details, use, care and doubts
Example contentSize, key material or ingredient, the main benefit, delivery noteWho it's for, how it's made, how to use it, FAQs

Specifications belong in neither box. Add them as attributes under Product data > Attributes with "Visible on the product page" ticked, and WooCommerce shows them as a tidy table in the Additional information tab, along with weight and dimensions. Consistent attributes also power category filters.

A product description framework

  1. The hook: one or two sentences on who it's for and the main benefit.
  2. Why it's different: how it's made, sourced or designed, in specific terms.
  3. Key features as benefits: three to six bullets, each feature followed by what it means for the buyer.
  4. Specifications: size, weight, material or ingredients, as attributes.
  5. Use and care: how to use, store, wash or maintain it.
  6. What's in the box or pack contents, where it isn't obvious.
  7. Delivery and returns: a line each, linking to your full policy.
  8. FAQs: the questions customers ask on WhatsApp, answered in a sentence or two.

Not every product needs every part. A greeting card needs a hook, size and paper type; a mixer grinder needs all eight.

Example 1: a food product (fictional)

Before: "Premium quality groundnut oil. Best for cooking. Totally pure."

After:

Cold-pressed groundnut oil for everyday cooking, with the nutty taste of oil from a traditional wooden press. We press small batches of groundnuts at low speed without heat or chemicals, then let the oil settle naturally instead of refining it.

  • Good for tadka, deep frying and chutneys, so one oil covers most of the kitchen.
  • Glass bottle, so the oil doesn't pick up the smell of plastic.
  • A little sediment at the bottom is natural; it shows the oil hasn't been refined.

The "after" version names the product the way people search for it, explains what makes it different and turns each fact into a reason to buy. Notice it drops "totally pure" and "best", which say nothing a shopper can check, and keeps claims you could explain if asked. Food products also have their own labelling rules, so check what must be shown online.

Example 2: a fashion product (fictional)

Before: "Beautiful cotton saree. Latest design. Free size."

After:

A handloom cotton saree in indigo with a narrow mustard border, light enough for a full working day in summer. Woven on a pit loom, so small irregularities in the weave are part of the fabric, not defects.

  • Soft, breathable cotton that gets softer with each wash.
  • Comes with an unstitched blouse piece in the border colour.
  • Natural dye: wash separately in cold water for the first few washes.

Then the specifications table: length, blouse piece length, fabric, weave and care. For clothing and footwear, the doubts are fit, colour accuracy and fabric feel, so a size guide, a note on how the colour looks in daylight and real customer photos do more than adjectives.

Specifications: make them a table, not a paragraph

Shoppers comparing products scan for numbers. A specifications table might look like this for a fictional mixer grinder:

SpecificationDetail
MotorWattage as stated by the manufacturer
JarsNumber, capacity and use of each jar
BodyMaterial and colour
WarrantyWhat is covered, for how long, and who honours it
In the boxEverything included

Use the same attribute names and units across a category, such as always "Capacity (litres)", so shoppers can compare and filters work. Pre-packaged goods sold online are generally expected to show details such as MRP, net quantity and country of origin too; check the current rules with your CA or a lawyer.

Benefits without hype

A feature is what the product is; a benefit is what it does for the buyer. Add "so that" after each feature and finish the sentence: "a glass bottle, so the oil doesn't smell of plastic". If you can't finish it, the feature may not matter to buyers. Keep benefits honest: "may help" or a health promise needs evidence and can break advertising rules, and regulated products such as food, cosmetics and supplements have stricter limits on claims.

Write your own copy, not the manufacturer's

Manufacturer and supplier descriptions appear on every other store selling the same item. Copying them isn't a penalty in itself, but it gives Google no reason to show your page instead of the others; duplicate content explained covers why. Rewrite in your own words and add what only you can say: how you test or pack it, who it suits, what customers ask about, and photos you took.

SEO without keyword stuffing

  • Product title: product type, key attribute and size or quantity, such as "Handloom cotton saree, indigo with mustard border", rather than a brand-only name.
  • The words people search with: use the main phrase in the title and once or twice naturally in the description. If customers say "kurta set" rather than "ethnic co-ord", use theirs.
  • Natural variations: material, use and occasion words, such as "office wear" or "for gifting", help you match longer searches without repetition.
  • Meta title and description: written in your SEO plugin, so the search snippet sells the click; see writing meta titles and descriptions.
  • Image alt text: describe the photo plainly, such as "indigo cotton saree draped, showing the border".

Category pages need their own short introductions as well; the WooCommerce SEO guide covers categories, schema and filters.

Variable products and large catalogues

For products with sizes or colours, write one description for the product and use each variation's own description field only for real differences, such as a fabric change in one colour; WooCommerce product variations covers the setup. For a large catalogue:

  1. Write full descriptions for your best sellers and products with the most search interest first.
  2. Create a template per category with the same sections and attribute names.
  3. Fill the rest using the template, adding at least a few specific lines per product.
  4. Edit in bulk by exporting products to CSV, updating descriptions in a spreadsheet and importing them back, after a backup.

If you draft with AI tools, edit and fact-check every description, and never let them invent ingredients, materials or results; see using AI tools for website content responsibly.

FAQs

How long should a product description be?

Long enough to answer the questions that stop someone buying, and no longer. Simple products may need a short paragraph and a table; technical or expensive products need more detail and FAQs.

Can I use the manufacturer's product description?

You can, but it won't help you stand out in search or with shoppers, because the same text is on every other store. Rewrite it in your own words and add details only you know.

What is the difference between the short description and the description in WooCommerce?

The short description appears near the price and button, so keep it to the deciding facts. The main description sits lower down and tells the full story, with use, care and FAQs.

How do I add keywords without stuffing them?

Use the main phrase in the product title and once or twice in the description, then write naturally about materials, uses and occasions. If a sentence sounds odd read aloud, rewrite it.

Should product descriptions mention delivery and returns?

Yes, briefly. A line each on delivery time and the return window, linking to your full returns and refunds policy, answers common doubts at the moment of purchase.
