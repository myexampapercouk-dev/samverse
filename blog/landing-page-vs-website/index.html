Landing Page vs Website: The Key Differences, Where Squeeze Pages Fit, and Which You Need for Your Ads

Landing page vs website: the key differences, landing page vs homepage, squeeze pages explained, and when to send ad clicks to each to get more leads.

Key takeaways
  • A website serves many visitors with many pages; a landing page is one focused page for one audience, one offer and one action, usually for an ad campaign.
  • Sending paid clicks to your homepage usually wastes budget; a landing page or a focused service page that repeats the ad's promise converts better.
  • Most businesses running ads need both: the website for credibility and search traffic, landing pages to turn paid clicks into enquiries.

A website is your business's full presence online: many pages for many kinds of visitors, built to inform, build trust and rank in search. A landing page is a single page built for one audience, one offer and one action, usually the destination of a Google or Meta ad. For paid campaigns, send clicks to a landing page (or a very focused service page) that matches the ad; for credibility, search traffic and returning visitors, you need the website. Most businesses that advertise need both.

Landing page vs homepage: why ad clicks shouldn't go to your homepage

Your homepage has to serve everyone: new and returning customers, job seekers, suppliers, people checking your address. It links to every section of the site and talks about all your services at once. Someone who clicked an ad for "AC repair in Kothrud" lands on a page about everything, has to find the right service, and often leaves.

A landing page fixes that with message match. The headline repeats what the ad promised, the content answers that visitor's questions, and there's one obvious next step. Fewer choices usually means more enquiries from the same budget, which is why homepages make poor ad destinations. If you're about to start campaigns, run through whether your website is ready for Google Ads first.

Squeeze page vs landing page

Every squeeze page is a landing page, but not every landing page is a squeeze page. A squeeze page strips everything back to a single field and a reason to fill it in, usually a lead magnet such as a checklist or guide. It suits building an email or WhatsApp list cheaply. A lead generation landing page asks for more and gives more: details of the service, proof and answers, because the visitor is closer to buying. Microsites

Between the two sits the microsite: a few pages for one project or campaign, such as a new housing project with its own floor plans, amenities and location pages. See real estate project microsites.

When a landing page is the right choice

  • Google Ads or Meta ads for a specific service, offer or location
  • Seasonal and festival campaigns, such as a Diwali offer
  • Launching a single product or service, or testing demand before building full pages
  • Events, webinars and admissions periods
  • Testing headlines and offers against each other; see A/B testing landing pages

When to send traffic to your website instead

  • Brand searches: people searching your business name already know you; your homepage or contact page is fine.
  • Retargeting: people who've visited before can go to a specific service page.
  • Strong service pages: if a service page is focused on one service and has a clear call to action, it can work as a landing page.
  • Click-to-WhatsApp ads: these skip the page entirely; see click-to-WhatsApp ads vs your website for when that works.

Do you need a website if you have a landing page?

A landing page on its own can work for a single offer or an early test. But many people who see an ad and land on a page will look you up before they call: your Google Business Profile, your website, your reviews. If there's nothing to find, trust drops. Ad platforms also expect the page to say who you are, how to contact you and how you use the data you collect.

The reverse is also true. A website without landing pages is fine if your service pages are focused enough to act as landing pages. A business site with good service pages, plus a landing page per major campaign, covers both jobs. If you need the website side, see WordPress website development.

What makes a landing page convert

  1. A headline that matches the ad's wording and offer
  2. A call to action on the first screen, with WhatsApp and tap-to-call alongside the form
  3. Three to five clear benefits, written for that one audience
  4. Real proof: genuine reviews, photos of your work, client logos you're allowed to use
  5. A short form asking only what you need to call back
  6. An FAQ that answers price, timing and trust objections
  7. Fast loading on mobile data, where most ad clicks happen
  8. Conversion tracking and a thank-you page, so you know which ads bring leads; see thank-you pages for forms

Should landing pages be in Google search?

Usually not, if they're built only for ads. Campaign pages often repeat what your service pages already say, and keeping them out of Google (with a noindex setting) stops them competing with the pages you want to rank. Ads can still send visitors to a page that isn't indexed. Landing pages built to rank, such as location pages with genuinely useful content, are a different thing and should be indexed.

Building landing pages on WordPress

You can build a landing page on your existing WordPress site, using a blank template such as Elementor Canvas so the menu and footer don't appear, or on a separate subdomain set up for campaigns. Your own site is simpler to manage and shares your domain's trust; a separate setup keeps campaign pages and tracking scripts away from the main site. Either way, keep the page light so it loads fast on mobile. Most of my landing pages are ready in three to five working days once the content and offer are clear.

FAQs

What is the difference between a landing page and a website?

A website has many pages serving many visitors and purposes, with full navigation. A landing page is a single page for one audience and one action, usually with no menu, built for a specific ad or campaign.

What is the difference between a squeeze page and a landing page?

A squeeze page is a type of landing page with one narrow goal: capturing an email address or phone number, usually in exchange for a free resource. Other landing pages, such as lead generation or sales pages, carry more detail because they ask for a bigger commitment.

Is a landing page better than a website for Google Ads?

Usually, yes. A landing page that repeats the ad's promise and offers one clear action tends to convert better than a homepage. A focused service page with a strong call to action can also work well.

Can a landing page replace a website?

For a single offer or an early test, it can for a while. For most businesses, a website is still needed for search traffic and credibility, because people check you out before they get in touch.

Is a landing page the same as a homepage?

No. A homepage introduces your whole business and links to everything. A landing page focuses on one offer and removes distractions, so visitors from an ad take one action.

Do I need a separate landing page for each ad?

Only when the ads promise different things, such as a different service, offer or location. Ad groups with the same promise can share one page. What matters is that every visitor sees the words their ad used, so they know they're in the right place.
