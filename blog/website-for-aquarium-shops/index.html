Websites for Aquarium and Pet Fish Shops: Livestock, Tanks and Maintenance

How aquarium and pet fish shops can keep livestock lists current, sell tanks and setups, promote maintenance services and set clear local delivery limits.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Keep livestock lists simple enough for staff to update weekly, with a visible "last updated" date and enquiry buttons.
  • Sell tanks, equipment and food online, and give maintenance contracts and custom tanks their own pages and quote forms.
  • Be clear about where live fish can be delivered, how they are packed and what happens if one arrives dead or unwell.

An aquarium shop is a strange mix of businesses: part pet shop, part equipment store, part installation and maintenance service. Fishkeepers want to know what's in your tanks this week, beginners want help choosing their first setup, and offices and restaurants want someone to keep their display tank looking good. A website that handles all three, without a stale stock list, can bring in steady local enquiries.

This guide is for shops that focus on fish and aquariums. If you sell supplies for dogs, cats and other pets, or run grooming and boarding, see websites for pet shops, grooming and boarding.

What fishkeepers look for

  • Which fish, shrimp and plants you have in stock right now
  • Whether you stock the equipment and food they already use
  • Help with compatibility, tank size and setting up a new aquarium
  • Whether you deliver live fish to their area, or they need to visit
  • Maintenance and cleaning services for home and office tanks
  • Photos of your shop and display tanks, which hint at how well you care for livestock

Livestock lists that are easy to keep current

Livestock changes every week. A beautifully designed fish catalogue that nobody updates is worse than none, because customers travel for fish that sold out a month ago. Design for easy updating rather than perfection:

  • A simple stock list grouped by type (community fish, cichlids, livebearers, shrimp, plants, marine if you keep it), which staff can update from a phone in a few minutes
  • A visible "last updated" date at the top, so visitors know how fresh it is
  • Key details per species: common and scientific name, approximate size, temperament and basic needs
  • "New arrivals" posts when a shipment comes in, which can be shared to WhatsApp and Instagram
  • "Ask about this fish" buttons rather than add-to-cart for most livestock, so you can check the buyer's tank first

Only list species you can legally sell. Some animals are protected or restricted, and shops dealing in live animals may come under animal welfare rules that include registration, so check current requirements with a lawyer and display registration details where required.

Tanks, setups and equipment

Dry goods are where an online store makes most sense. Organise them the way fishkeepers shop:

CategoryExamplesHelpful filters or details
Tanks and cabinetsReady-made tanks, custom-built tanks, standsDimensions, volume, glass thickness, lead time for custom builds
Filtration and equipmentFilters, heaters, air pumps, lights, CO2 kitsSuitable tank size, power use, brand
Food and careFlakes, pellets, frozen food, water conditioners, test kitsFish type, pack size
Decor and substrateGravel, sand, plant soil, rocks, driftwoodWeight, colour, freshwater or marine

Starter packages are popular: a tank, filter, heater, light and substrate sized to work together, with a note on which fish suit it. Sell pack sizes and tank sizes as variations rather than separate products, and keep stock accurate so you don't sell what you no longer have; see WooCommerce inventory management.

Custom tanks need a quote form instead of a cart: dimensions, location in the room, freshwater or marine, cabinet needed and budget.

Maintenance and setup services

Regular maintenance contracts for homes, offices, clinics, hotels and restaurants can be steady income. Give them a proper page:

  • What a visit includes, such as water change, glass cleaning, filter cleaning and a health check of livestock
  • Visit frequency options and whether you offer annual contracts
  • Set-up services for new tanks, including cycling advice before fish are added
  • Shifting and relocating existing aquariums, if you do it
  • Before and after photos of tanks you maintain, with the owner's permission

The booking form should ask for tank size, freshwater or marine, location, current problems and a photo upload, so you can quote and plan the visit.

Care guides that bring visitors

Beginners search a lot before they buy: how to set up a first aquarium, which fish live peacefully together, why water turns cloudy, how often to change water. Short, practical guides written from your shop's experience answer those searches and bring people to your door.

  • Link each guide to the products and services it mentions
  • Keep advice general and accurate, and suggest visiting the shop with a water sample for specific problems
  • Avoid promising cures for fish diseases; describe what a product is intended for and follow the label

Local delivery limits for live fish

Live fish don't travel like a parcel. Be clear about what you deliver and where:

  • Live fish and plants: state the areas and time windows you deliver within, or say that livestock is pick-up only
  • Packing: explain how fish are bagged and how long they can safely stay packed
  • Arrival policy: what happens if a fish arrives dead or unwell, and what proof you need, written plainly
  • Dry goods: these can usually be shipped further; a pin code checker shows customers instantly what reaches them; see pin code delivery checkers

Many couriers restrict or refuse live animals, so check before promising anything outside your own delivery area.

WhatsApp enquiries and local SEO

Most aquarium questions are easiest to answer with a photo: "what's wrong with my fish?", "will this fit in my tank?", "is this fish in stock?". A WhatsApp button on livestock, service and product pages makes that easy; see WhatsApp on your business website. Use quick replies for common questions and record enquiries so you can follow up when requested fish arrive.

People search locally for "aquarium shop near me", "aquarium maintenance in {area}" and "custom fish tank {city}". Keep your Google Business Profile hours accurate, post new arrivals and tank photos, and ask happy customers for reviews. Keep the site fast on mobile by compressing tank photos and avoiding heavy video backgrounds.
