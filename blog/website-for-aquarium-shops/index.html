Websites for Aquarium Shops: Livestock Lists, a WooCommerce Store for Dry Goods, Setup and Maintenance Bookings, and Care Guides

Plan an aquarium shop website: livestock vs dry goods, local-only live fish delivery, WooCommerce categories, maintenance bookings and care guides.

Key takeaways
  • Treat livestock and dry goods as two different businesses on one site: keep permanent species pages with a simple stock status, and sell tanks, equipment and food through a normal online store.
  • Offer live fish for store pickup or your own local delivery only, set up as a separate shipping class, and state your arrival policy plainly; courier shipping of live animals is a special case to check first.
  • Setup and maintenance services, species and care guides, a complete Business Profile and WhatsApp for photo questions bring the local enquiries that a stock list alone never will.

An aquarium shop website has to handle three very different things: livestock that changes every week and is best sold in store or delivered locally, dry goods such as tanks, filters and food that can be sold online like any other product, and setup and maintenance services that need a booking form rather than a cart. The shops that do well online keep those three apart, then use species and care guides, local search and WhatsApp to bring fishkeepers through the door.

WooCommerce suits this mix because livestock, products and services can live on one site with different rules for each. My WooCommerce development service covers the store foundations: product catalogue and categories, Indian payment gateways, cash on delivery and shipping rules, order and stock management and a WhatsApp chat button. If you mainly sell services and in-store stock, a services-first site through WordPress website development may be enough.

This guide is for shops built around fish and aquariums. If dog and cat supplies, grooming or boarding are the main business, see websites for pet shops, grooming and boarding.

Mixing them causes most of the problems on aquarium websites: live fish in the same cart as a courier-shipped filter, maintenance sold as a fixed-price product, or a stock list that nobody can update.

Species pages that stay accurate

The common mistake is a livestock catalogue built like a normal store, where staff add a product when fish arrive and delete it when they sell out. Within a month the list is wrong, and every deleted page throws away whatever it had started to earn in Google.

A better pattern is a permanent page for every species you regularly keep, with a simple stock status that staff can switch from a phone: in stock, arriving soon or currently unavailable. When a species is out, the page stays live with a "tell me when it's back" button that opens WhatsApp with the species name already typed. Each page carries the details fishkeepers check before buying:

  • Common name and scientific name, since hobbyists search by both
  • Approximate size when sold and typical adult size
  • Temperament, the smallest tank you would recommend, and whether it is best kept in a group
  • Typical water conditions and diet, written as general guidance
  • Good and bad tank mates, linking to their species pages
  • A real photo from your tanks where possible, with stock photos labelled as representative

For shoaling fish, sell sensible group quantities, such as pairs or groups, as variations, and say why. Add a visible "stock updated" date to your weekly arrivals list. Only list species you can legally sell; some animals are protected or restricted, and shops dealing in live animals may need registration under animal welfare rules, so check the current requirements with a lawyer and display registration details where required.

Live fish: store pickup or local delivery only

Live fish don't travel like a parcel. Long journeys, summer heat and rough handling all put them at risk, and many couriers restrict or refuse live animals. The sensible default is store pickup plus your own delivery within areas you can reach quickly. If you ever consider shipping livestock further, check the courier's current terms and the welfare rules that apply first, and treat it as a special case rather than a checkout option.

In WooCommerce this is straightforward to enforce:

  1. Give livestock and live plants their own shipping class.
  2. Create a local delivery zone limited to the pin codes you serve, plus a local pickup method.
  3. Allow the livestock class only on those two methods, so a courier option never appears for fish.
  4. Offer delivery slots, so nobody leaves bagged fish at a locked door.
  5. Show a pin code checker on livestock pages; see pin code delivery checkers.

Dry goods can then use normal courier rates; WooCommerce shipping setup for India covers zones and classes. Glass tanks are heavy and fragile, so many shops deliver them locally too. Write an arrival policy in plain words: how fish are bagged, acclimatisation steps, what to do if a fish arrives dead or unwell, how soon to report it and what photo or video you need. The same thinking applies to live plants; websites for plant nurseries covers selling living stock locally.

A WooCommerce store for dry goods

Dry goods are where online sales make sense. Organise categories the way fishkeepers shop, with filters that answer "will this suit my tank?":

  • Tanks and cabinets: length, volume in litres, glass thickness, rimless or framed, cabinet included
  • Filtration: filter type (sponge, internal, hang-on, canister), rated tank volume, flow rate
  • Lighting: fits tank length, suitable for planted tanks
  • Heating and cooling: heaters by wattage and tank volume, plus fans and chillers for hot months
  • Air and power: air pumps, and battery-backed pumps for power cuts
  • Food: fish type, floating or sinking, pack size
  • Water care and test kits: conditioners, bacteria starters, test kits
  • Plants, CO2 and substrate: plant soil, sand, gravel, hardscape by weight
  • Marine, only if you genuinely stock it: salt, RO units, marine test kits

Sell pack sizes as variations, and add a "suits tanks up to" field so filters, heaters and lights can be filtered by tank size. Link products together: a tank page should suggest the right filter, heater and light, and starter kits work well as bundles; see WooCommerce bundles and upsells. Custom-built tanks need a quote form instead of a cart: dimensions, room location, freshwater, planted or marine, cabinet and budget.

Setup and maintenance services with booking

Maintenance contracts for homes, offices, clinics, restaurants and hotels can be the steadiest income an aquarium shop has, so give each service its own page:

  • New tank setup: what you install, and an honest explanation that a new tank needs time to mature before fish go in
  • Regular maintenance: what a visit includes, such as a water change, glass and filter cleaning and a livestock check, with frequency options and annual contracts
  • Shifting and relocation of existing tanks, if you do it
  • Problem visits for cloudy water, algae or equipment failure
  • Custom tanks and aquascaping, with a gallery of your own work and owners' permission

The booking form should ask for tank size, freshwater, planted or marine, the type of location, the area or pin code, the current problem, photo uploads and a preferred slot. Photos let you quote accurately before travelling. Show "starting from" prices or "quote after photos", whichever suits how you work; online appointment booking compares booking tools and reminders.

Species and care guides as content

Beginners research for weeks before buying a tank, and their questions are searches you can answer from experience. Guides that tend to fit an Indian aquarium shop:

  • Setting up a first aquarium, step by step, including why a new tank needs time before fish go in
  • Keeping a tank cool in summer, and what to do during power cuts
  • Testing tap or borewell water before a water change
  • Compatibility guides: which fish live together, and which don't
  • Choosing a filter, heater or light for a given tank size

Link each guide to the species pages, products and services it mentions, and suggest bringing a water sample to the shop for specific problems. Keep advice general and accurate: describe what a treatment is intended for and follow the label, rather than promising cures.

Local SEO and WhatsApp

Most aquarium customers live within driving distance, and they search with local words: "aquarium shop near me", "fish tank maintenance in {area}", "custom aquarium in {city}". Choose the closest primary category in your Google Business Profile, keep hours accurate, add photos of your display tanks and shop floor, post new arrivals, and ask happy customers for reviews after a setup or a successful first month.

WhatsApp suits this trade because almost every question is easier with a photo: "what's wrong with my fish?", "will this fit my tank?", "is this in stock?". Put an "Ask on WhatsApp" button on every species, product and service page, with the page name pre-filled, and keep an opt-in list for new arrival updates; see WhatsApp on your business website. Compress tank photos and avoid heavy video backgrounds, so pages stay quick on mobile data.

Common mistakes on aquarium shop websites

  • A stock list with no update date, or one last updated months ago
  • Deleting species pages when fish sell out
  • Live fish offered with courier shipping at checkout
  • Maintenance sold as a fixed-price product without knowing the tank
  • Stock photos of perfect tanks that look nothing like the shop
  • Disease advice that reads like a guaranteed cure

FAQs

Can an aquarium shop sell live fish online?

You can take orders and reservations online, but delivery is best limited to store pickup and your own local delivery. Courier shipping of live animals is restricted by many couriers, so check their current terms and the rules that apply before offering it.

Should every fish species have its own page?

Every species you keep regularly, yes. A permanent page with care details and a stock status builds search visibility over time, and customers can ask to be told when it is back.

How do I keep the livestock list up to date?

Make updating a phone task: staff switch a species between in stock, arriving soon and unavailable, and a weekly arrivals list shows the date it was last updated.

Do I need a full online store, or just a catalogue?

Many shops start with an online store for dry goods and enquiry-only species pages for livestock. If you mainly sell services and in-store stock, a services-first site with WhatsApp enquiries may be enough to begin with.

Should I show maintenance prices on the website?

Showing "starting from" prices by tank size, or asking for photos before quoting, both work. Explain what changes the price, such as tank size, type, location and visit frequency.
