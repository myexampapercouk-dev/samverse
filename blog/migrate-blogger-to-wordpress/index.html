How to Move From Blogger to WordPress Step by Step: Export, Import, Images, Permalinks, Redirects, Custom Domain and SEO Checks

Move from Blogger to WordPress step by step: export and import posts, move images, keep or redirect Blogger URLs, switch your domain and check SEO.

Key takeaways
  • Export your Blogger content as a backup file, import it into WordPress on staging, then pull every image into the media library so nothing still loads from Blogger.
  • Matching the Blogger post URL pattern in WordPress permalinks avoids most redirects; pages, labels and anything else that changes need 301 redirects to the closest new page.
  • A custom domain moves cleanly with a DNS change, while a blogspot.com address cannot send server redirects, so keep the old blog live and plan for a slower handover.

To move from Blogger to WordPress, download a backup of your Blogger content, import it into a new WordPress site on staging, and copy every image into the WordPress media library. Then either match Blogger's post URLs in your WordPress permalink settings or redirect each old address with a 301, point your custom domain to the new host, and check Search Console for errors in the weeks after. If your blog lives on a blogspot.com address rather than your own domain, the move needs extra planning, because Blogger can't send server redirects to another site.

Step 1: Export and audit your Blogger blog

  1. In Blogger, go to Settings, find the Manage blog section, choose Back up content and download the file. Keep it safe; it's your full backup.
  2. Save a list of every URL. Blogger's sitemap, usually at https://www.example.com/sitemap.xml, and Search Console's Pages report are good sources.
  3. Export your top pages and queries from Search Console as a baseline, so you can compare traffic after the move.
  4. Note anything the export won't carry: gadgets, a custom template, ad placements, a contact form or email subscription widgets.
  5. If you use a custom domain, copy every DNS record for it, especially email records.

Step 2: Set up WordPress on staging

Buy hosting in your own name, install WordPress on a staging address and block search engines from it; how to choose WordPress hosting in India covers what to check. Choose a lightweight theme, and decide your permalink structure before importing anything (see Step 5), because changing it later means another round of redirects.

Step 3: Import posts, pages and comments

WordPress lists a Blogger importer under Tools > Import. Install it, run it and upload your Blogger backup file. It brings in posts, comments and labels, and lets you assign the posts to a WordPress user. Importers change over time, so if it struggles with a large blog, a migration plugin or a converter that turns the Blogger file into a standard WordPress import file are alternatives.

After the import, compare the number of posts with Blogger's dashboard, then open a sample of old and new posts side by side. Check headings, line breaks, tables, embedded videos and code. Then:

  • Recreate static pages such as About and Contact if they didn't come across.
  • Merge or rename categories created from labels, and set a default category.
  • Delete spam comments, and decide whether comments stay open; should you enable comments on WordPress? helps you decide.
  • Add a contact form, and remove leftover Blogger-specific shortcodes or scripts from posts.

Step 4: Bring images into WordPress

Images in Blogger posts are hosted on Google's servers, at addresses such as blogger.googleusercontent.com or bp.blogspot.com. After the import, posts may still load them from there, which leaves your site depending on Blogger. Use a plugin that imports external images into the media library and rewrites the image addresses in each post, then check a sample of posts. Large photos are a good moment to resize and compress; image optimization for WordPress shows how.

Step 5: Keep or redirect your Blogger URLs

This is the step that protects your rankings and backlinks. Commonly seen Blogger patterns, which can vary by blog, are:

Typical Blogger URLWordPress equivalentWhat to do
/2024/05/post-name.htmlA postMatch it with a custom permalink, or redirect each post
/p/about.htmlA page such as /about/Redirect each page
/search/label/RecipesA category such as /category/recipes/Redirect your main labels one by one
/feeds/posts/default/feed/Redirect, so feed readers keep working
Any URL ending in ?m=1The same postMake sure redirects ignore the mobile parameter

The easiest option for posts is to keep the same addresses. Under Settings > Permalinks, choose Custom Structure and enter:

/%year%/%monthnum%/%postname%.html

That works when each imported post keeps its original date and its slug matches the last part of the Blogger URL, so check a sample before relying on it. Blogger often shortens long titles in URLs, and an importer may create a different slug. If you prefer cleaner URLs, use pattern redirects instead. In the Redirection plugin, for example, tick the regex option, set the query parameter handling to ignore parameters, and use rules like these:

Source:  ^/([0-9]{4})/([0-9]{2})/([^/]+)[.]html$
Target:  https://example.com/$3/

Source:  ^/p/([^/]+)[.]html$
Target:  https://example.com/$1/

A pattern rule only works when the new slug matches the old one. For posts whose slugs differ, add individual redirects. Test every rule against your full URL list, never redirect everything to the homepage, and avoid redirect chains.

Step 6: Point your custom domain to the new host

Blogger custom domains are usually connected with a CNAME record pointing to ghs.google.com, plus records for the bare domain. To switch:

  1. Lower the DNS TTL a day ahead.
  2. Move WordPress from staging to the live hosting.
  3. Change the domain's records, or nameservers, to point to the new host, keeping your email records exactly as they were.
  4. Issue SSL, force https and turn on the redirects.
  5. Test a sample of old URLs straight away, including the ?m=1 versions.
  6. Remove the custom domain from Blogger's settings once the new site is live, but keep the blog itself.

If your blog is on blogspot.com

You can't add server redirects to a blogspot.com address, and Blogger's own redirect settings only work within the same blog. The usual approach is:

  • Keep the Blogger blog published, never deleted or made private.
  • Edit the Blogger theme so each post points readers to its new address, with a canonical link or a redirect script that maps old posts to new ones.
  • Update links you control: social profiles, YouTube descriptions, email signatures and guest posts.
  • Ask sites that link to your best posts to update the link.

Google can follow script and meta refresh redirects in many cases, but they're weaker signals than a server 301, and Search Console's Change of Address tool expects proper redirects. Expect the handover to take longer and some traffic to dip. Changing your domain without losing SEO covers the wider process.

Step 7: SEO checks before and after launch

  • Install an SEO plugin and add titles and meta descriptions to your top posts, carrying over Blogger search descriptions.
  • Search posts for links to your old blogspot.com address, and update them with a search-and-replace plugin.
  • Check images have alt text and load from your own domain.
  • Switch off the staging "discourage search engines" setting, submit the new sitemap in Search Console and request indexing for key posts.
  • Watch the Pages report for 404s in the first weeks and add redirects for anything missed.

After the move

Add back analytics, ads and email sign-up forms, set up caching, backups and security, and keep your Blogger backup file. Rankings can move for a few weeks while Google recrawls; a steady fall on particular posts usually points to a missing redirect or changed content on those posts.

FAQs

Will I lose my Google rankings moving from Blogger to WordPress?

Not if you keep your custom domain and match or redirect every old URL. Short-term movement is normal. Blogs on blogspot.com face more risk, because they can't use server redirects.

Can I keep the same URLs as Blogger?

Often, yes. A custom permalink of /%year%/%monthnum%/%postname%.html matches Blogger's post pattern, provided dates and slugs match. Pages and labels still need redirects.

Do comments move to WordPress?

Blogger comments are usually imported along with the posts. If you used a third-party comment system, check how it exports, and remove spam before reopening comments.

Should I delete my Blogger blog after moving?

No. Keep the backup file and, for a blogspot.com address, keep the blog published so its redirects and links keep working.

What happens to AdSense when I move to WordPress?

You add the ad code to the new site, check the site is listed correctly in your AdSense account, and add an ads.txt file if needed. Check AdSense's current requirements for the new setup.
