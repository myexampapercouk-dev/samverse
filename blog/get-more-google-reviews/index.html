How to Get More Google Reviews for Your Business, the Ethical Way: Review Links, QR Codes, WhatsApp Templates and Staff Routines

How to get more Google reviews ethically: your review link and QR code, when to ask, WhatsApp and SMS templates, staff routines and what Google forbids.

Key takeaways
  • Most happy customers will leave a review if they are asked personally, at the right moment, with a direct link, so make asking part of every handover, visit or delivery.
  • Don't offer anything in exchange, don't ask only the customers you expect to be happy, and never post reviews yourself; Google's policies forbid all three, at the time of writing.
  • Reply to every review, good or bad, and show genuine reviews on your website with a link to your profile, so a steady flow of reviews keeps working for you.

The way to get more Google reviews is to ask every customer, personally, right after a good experience, with a direct link that opens the review box in one tap. Make it a routine for whoever serves the customer, send the link on WhatsApp or show a QR code at the counter, and reply to every review you receive. Don't offer anything in return, don't ask only the customers you think are happy and never write reviews yourself: those break Google's policies and can cost you the reviews you have.

Business Profile setup and improvement is part of my WordPress SEO services, and the rule I follow there is simple: reviews come from real customers you ask, never bought or written for you. This guide covers the practical side of asking well, with templates you can adapt.

Why reviews matter

Reviews are one of the signals Google uses for prominence in local results, and they are often the deciding factor when someone compares three businesses in the map pack. People look at how many reviews you have, how recent they are, what they say and how you reply. A steady flow of recent, genuine reviews does more for you than a large batch from two years ago.

Step 1: Get your review link and QR code

  1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your Business Profile and search for your business name on Google, or open the profile in the Google Maps app.
  2. Find the option to ask for reviews. At the time of writing it is called "Ask for reviews" or "Get more reviews", depending on where you manage the profile.
  3. Copy the short link it gives you. Opening it takes a customer straight to the review box for your business.
  4. Some views also offer a QR code. If yours doesn't, create one from the link with a reputable QR generator, and test it on two or three phones before printing.
  5. Save the link where your team can reach it in seconds: a WhatsApp Business quick reply, your email signature and a note on the counter phone.

Before you start asking, make sure the profile itself is complete and accurate, since reviewers will see it; the Google Business Profile checklist covers every setting.

Print the QR code where customers have a moment to spare: the billing counter, a table tent, the bill folder, a thank-you card in deliveries or the back of a visiting card. QR codes for your business covers printing, testing and tracking scans.

Step 2: Ask at the right moment

Ask when the customer is happiest: just after the problem is solved, the order arrives or they thank you. Asking weeks later rarely works, and asking before the job is finished feels pushy.

BusinessWhen to askHow
Restaurant or cafeWhen the bill is settled, or the day after a deliveryQR code on the bill folder or table; WhatsApp for delivery customers
ClinicAfter a follow-up visit goes well, or when a patient thanks youA card at the front desk or a polite WhatsApp; never during treatment
Home services such as AC repair, pest control or plumbingWhen the job is done and paid forThe technician mentions it; the office sends the link the same day
Retail shop or showroomAt billingQR code at the counter, or the link with the e-bill on WhatsApp
B2B and professional servicesAt handover or a successful milestoneA personal message or email from the owner
Online storeOnce the product has arrived and been usedAn email or WhatsApp a few days after delivery

Doctors, lawyers and other regulated professionals should check their professional body's rules on testimonials and soliciting feedback, and never mention a patient's or client's case when asking or replying.

Step 3: Ask personally, and keep it short

A message from a person, using the customer's name, works far better than an automated blast. Adapt these rather than pasting them unchanged.

WhatsApp, in English

Hi [Name], thank you for choosing [Business]. If you were happy with [the service or product], would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It helps other people find us. Here's the link: [review link]. Thanks, [Your name]

WhatsApp, in Hinglish

Namaste [Name] ji, [Business] choose karne ke liye dhanyavaad. Agar aapko hamari service achhi lagi ho, toh kya aap Google par ek chhota sa review de sakte hain? Isse doosre customers ko hamare baare mein pata chalta hai: [review link]. [Your name]

SMS

Thanks for visiting [Business], [Name]. If you have a minute, a Google review would really help us: [review link]

Email after a project

Hi [Name], thank you for working with us on [project]. If you're happy with how it went, a short Google review describing your experience would mean a lot and helps others decide. You can leave one here: [review link]. And if anything could have gone better, please reply and tell me directly. [Your name]

One gentle reminder

Hi [Name], just a gentle reminder in case my last message got buried. If you have a minute, here's the review link: [review link]. No worries if not. Thank you again!

Send one reminder at most. Don't suggest a star rating or tell customers what to write, and send the same message to every customer, not only the ones you expect to be happy.

Step 4: Make asking a staff routine

Reviews grow when asking is part of the job, not something the owner remembers occasionally.

  • Decide who asks: the person who served the customer, since they have the relationship.
  • Give them one line to say, such as "If you were happy with today, a Google review really helps us. The QR code is right here."
  • Make the link instant with a WhatsApp quick reply and a QR code at every counter or in every technician's phone.
  • Track it simply: a weekly count of customers asked and reviews received, so you can see whether the routine is working.
  • Recognise staff for asking, not for the ratings they get, so nobody feels pressure to push customers.
  • Let customers review in their own time on their own phones. A batch of reviews written one after another on a shop tablet or the shop's Wi-Fi can look unnatural, and older Google guidance advised against review stations at the business, so check the current policy before setting one up.

What Google's policies forbid

Google's rules on reviews change from time to time, so check its current Business Profile and review content policies. At the time of writing, these practices are against them:

PracticeWhy it's a problem
Offering discounts, gifts, cashback or contest entries for reviewsIncentivised reviews aren't allowed, even if you don't ask for five stars
Asking only customers you think are happy, or filtering them firstSelectively asking for positive reviews, often called review gating, is prohibited
Reviews by owners, staff, family or friendsA conflict of interest, and easy for Google to spot
Buying reviews or joining review-exchange groupsFake engagement; WhatsApp groups that swap reviews count too
Asking customers to remove or change a review in return for a refundTreated the same as paying for a review

Google removes reviews it detects as fake and can restrict profiles or show warnings to customers, and consumer protection rules in India and elsewhere increasingly treat fake reviews as misleading. Be wary of anyone selling review packages or "guaranteed" ratings.

Reply to every review

Replies show future customers how you treat people. Thank positive reviewers by name and mention something specific, such as "Glad the AC is cooling properly again before the summer, Rahul", so the reply doesn't look automated. For negative reviews, reply calmly, apologise where it is fair and move the conversation to a call; how to handle negative reviews has templates for common situations. Never reveal personal details, and for clinics and law firms never confirm someone was a patient or client.

Show your reviews on your website too

Genuine Google reviews on your service pages and near your contact form reassure visitors who arrive from ads or social media. Show them word for word, link to your profile so people can read the rest, and keep the widget light so it doesn't slow the page. Don't add review star markup for reviews about your own business, since Google doesn't show stars for those. For longer quotes collected directly, see collecting and displaying customer testimonials.

Your website can also make asking easier: add a "Review us on Google" link to your contact page, email signature and the follow-up email after an order or project.

FAQs

How do I get my Google review link?

Sign in to the account that manages your Business Profile, open the profile on Google Search or Maps and use the "Ask for reviews" option. Copy the short link it gives you and share it on WhatsApp, SMS or email, or turn it into a QR code.

Can I offer a discount for a Google review?

No. Offering anything in exchange for a review breaks Google's policies, even if you don't ask for a positive one. Thank customers for their business instead and ask without conditions.

Can I ask only my happy customers for reviews?

No. Selectively asking for positive reviews is against Google's policies. Ask every customer in the same way; if someone is unhappy, you'll hear it either way, and you can fix the problem.

Why did some of my Google reviews disappear?

Google's systems remove reviews they judge to be fake, incentivised or against policy, and sometimes genuine reviews get caught, for example several posted from the same place in a short time. Let customers review from their own phones in their own time.

How many Google reviews do I need?

There is no magic number. A steady flow of recent, genuine reviews with thoughtful replies matters more than a total, and what counts as strong depends on your competitors in your area.
