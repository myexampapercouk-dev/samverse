Key takeaways
  • Start with the words customers use on calls and WhatsApp, then expand them with Search Console, Google autocomplete, People also ask and Keyword Planner ranges.
  • Collect local modifiers, city spellings and Hinglish or Marathi variants, map every keyword group to one page by intent, and prioritise searches close to a buying decision that you can realistically win.
  • Keep a simple keyword map so two pages never target the same search, and revisit it every few months with fresh Search Console data.

Keyword research for a small business means finding the words customers actually type when they need what you sell, then deciding which page on your site answers each group of searches. Free tools are enough: Search Console shows what you already appear for, Google autocomplete and People also ask show how people phrase things, and Keyword Planner gives rough volume ranges. Prioritise searches close to a buying decision, include local and Hindi or Marathi variants, and give each group one page.

Mapping each target search to one page, so two pages don't compete for the same phrase, is where on-page work starts in my WordPress SEO services. At the end you get a map of which page targets which search, and the content worth adding next. This guide shows how to build that map yourself.

Finding the words is the first step; matching each one to the right type of page is the next, covered in search intent explained. To see what competitors already rank for, use how to analyse your competitors' SEO.

A free-tools workflow

  1. Start with customers. Write down the exact words people use on calls, WhatsApp, emails and at the counter, and the phrases in your reviews. "AC thanda nahi kar raha" and "gas khatam ho gaya" tell you more than any tool.
  2. Check Search Console. In the Performance report, set the last three months and look at the Queries tab. These are searches you already appear for; queries with impressions but few clicks are the quickest wins. Finding content ideas in Search Console covers filters and regex for question searches.
  3. Use Google autocomplete. In a private window on your phone, type each seed term and note the suggestions. Add words like "price", "charges", "near", "best", "for" and your area, and try the seed followed by each letter of the alphabet.
  4. Read People also ask and related searches. Each question is a potential FAQ, section or article.
  5. Get rough volumes from Keyword Planner. It is free with a Google Ads account, at the time of writing, and you don't have to run ads to use it. Set the location to your city or state. It shows broad ranges, and very specific searches often show little or no volume even when people do search them, so use it to compare, not to decide alone.
  6. Check seasonality in Google Trends. Compare terms over time and by state, useful for festivals, exams, weddings, summer and the monsoon.
  7. Look at who ranks. Search your main terms and note the page types and businesses on the first page.
  8. Put it all in one sheet: keyword, intent, the page it belongs to and a priority.

Local modifiers, city spellings and Hindi or Marathi variants

Indian searches are rarely tidy. Collect the variations your customers use:

  • Areas and suburbs: Kothrud, Baner, Wakad, Hinjewadi, Pimpri-Chinchwad, not just "Pune"
  • City names and spellings: Bengaluru and Bangalore, Gurugram and Gurgaon, Mumbai and Bombay; search engines usually connect these, but check which one ranking pages use
  • Hinglish: "ac repair wala near me", "website banane ka kharcha"; question words often come at the end
  • Devanagari: some customers type in Hindi or Marathi script, especially for local services and religious or cultural purchases
  • "Near me": common on phones, but Google works out location itself; don't stuff "near me" into headings

One page can cover close variants; you don't need a page per spelling. Build separate Hindi or Marathi pages only if you can serve customers properly in that language, with translations written or checked by a fluent speaker; multilingual WordPress websites in Hindi and English covers how.

Group keywords that a single page can answer well. "AC repair Pune", "AC service Kothrud" and "AC mechanic near me" usually belong on one service page; "AC not cooling" belongs in a guide that links to it.

Prioritise by intent, relevance and difficulty

Score each group simply, for example high, medium or low on four questions:

  • Value: does it lead to a service you want more of, in an area you serve?
  • Intent: is the searcher ready to call or book, or just researching?
  • Difficulty: who ranks now? If the first page is all national marketplaces and large directories, it is hard. If small local businesses with thin pages rank, it is winnable. A map pack means your Business Profile matters as much as the page.
  • Effort: can you improve an existing page, or do you need a new one?

Start with high-value, high-intent groups you can realistically win, usually specific service-plus-area searches, then add guides for the questions customers ask before buying.

Avoid keyword cannibalisation

Cannibalisation happens when two of your pages target the same search, so neither ranks as well as one strong page would. Prevent it with the keyword map: every group has exactly one page. To check, look in Search Console for queries where two of your URLs take turns appearing. Fix it by merging the weaker page into the stronger one with a 301 redirect, or by sharpening each page's focus and linking between them with consistent anchor text. Copies of the same page for different areas are a common cause and can be treated as doorway pages; see local landing pages without doorway pages.

Use keywords naturally, and review regularly

Put the main phrase in the page title, the H1, the URL and early in the text, use variants where they fit, and write the rest for people. The on-page SEO checklist covers the rest of each page. Every few months, check Search Console for new queries, update the map, and improve the pages that are close to page one.

FAQs

How do I do keyword research for free?

List the words customers use, then expand them with Search Console's Queries report, Google autocomplete, People also ask, related searches, Keyword Planner ranges and Google Trends. Put everything in one sheet with the page each group belongs to.

Is Google Keyword Planner free?

At the time of writing it is free with a Google Ads account, and you don't need to run ads. It shows broad ranges rather than exact numbers, so use it to compare keywords.

Does search volume matter for a local business?

Less than intent. A search with very little reported volume can still bring customers if it says exactly what you sell in your area.

How many keywords should one page target?

One main keyword group plus its close variants. If two groups need different answers, they need different pages.

What is keyword cannibalisation?

It is when two or more of your pages target the same search, so they compete and neither ranks as well as one strong page would. A keyword map with one page per group prevents it.

Should I target Hindi or Marathi keywords?

If your customers search that way, yes: cover Hinglish phrasing in FAQs and content, and build separate language pages only when you can serve customers properly in that language.
