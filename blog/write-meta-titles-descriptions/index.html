How to Write Meta Titles and Descriptions That Get Clicks (With E-commerce Examples)

How to write meta titles and descriptions that get clicks: lengths, formulas, WordPress setup, and examples for product, category and flash sale pages.

Key takeaways

A meta title (the title tag) is the clickable headline Google shows for your page, and the meta description is the short summary underneath it. Write titles of roughly 50 to 60 characters that start with the page's main search term and end with your brand, and descriptions of roughly 120 to 155 characters that say what the page offers and give a specific reason to click. Make every one unique.

Below are the formulas I use, how to set them in Yoast and Rank Math, and a full section on e-commerce: category pages, product pages and flash sale collections, with examples. If you'd rather have someone write and test them across your whole site, it's part of my WordPress SEO services.

What meta titles and descriptions do

Both sit in the page's HTML head rather than on the visible page:

<title>AC Repair in Pune: Same-Day Visits | ABC Cooling</title>
<meta name="description" content="AC repair, servicing and gas refilling across Pune, with same-day visits in most areas. Book a technician on WhatsApp.">
  • The title is one of the clearest clues Google uses to understand what a page is about, and it's the headline searchers read first.
  • The meta description doesn't directly affect rankings. Its job is to persuade the searcher to click your result rather than the next one.

Google may rewrite a title that is too long or doesn't match the page, and often builds its own snippet from page text. Accurate tags get used more often.

How long should meta titles and descriptions be?

Google truncates by pixel width rather than a fixed character count, so treat these as guides:

ElementAim forIf it's longer
Meta titleAbout 50–60 charactersCut off with an ellipsis, or rewritten by Google
Meta descriptionAbout 120–155 charactersCut off; on mobile the visible part can be shorter
H1 heading on the pageNo strict limitNot truncated, but it should cover the same topic as the title

Wide letters such as W and M take more space than i or l, so a title in capitals can be cut off well before 60 characters. Yoast SEO and Rank Math show a length bar and a search preview as you type. And short is fine: a 40-character title that says exactly what the page is beats a padded 60-character one.

How to write a meta title

The formula I start with for most pages:

Main search term + one specific detail or benefit | Brand
  1. Find the main query. Check Search Console for the words the page already gets impressions for, or do some quick keyword research. Use the searcher's words ("AC repair", not "cooling solutions").
  2. Put it first. The start of the title is what people scan and what survives truncation.
  3. Add one specific detail: a location, a number, a format ("checklist", "price list") or a real differentiator.
  4. Add your brand at the end if there's room. On the homepage, the brand can go first.

How to write meta descriptions that get clicks

Writing good meta descriptions is mostly about being specific. A pattern that works for most pages:

What the page offers + who or where it's for + a concrete detail + a next step
  • Answer the search. If someone searched "AC service charges Pune", say the page lists charges, not that you "offer quality services".
  • Use the main term naturally. Google bolds words that match the query.
  • Give a real reason to click: a price list, delivery areas, cash on delivery, a checklist or a free quote, as long as the page backs it up.
  • End with a call to action that fits the intent: "See the checklist", "Book on WhatsApp", "Shop the range".

Weak: "Welcome to ABC Cooling. We are a leading company providing the best AC services. Contact us for more details."

Better: "AC repair, servicing and gas refilling across Pune. Fixed visit charges, trained technicians and same-day slots in most areas. Book on WhatsApp."

Meta titles and descriptions for e-commerce pages

Stores have hundreds or thousands of pages, so ecommerce meta titles usually come from a mix of templates and hand-written tags. I hand-write the pages that bring in most of the revenue (homepage, top categories, best sellers and sale pages) and use a template for the rest. The wider store setup is in WooCommerce SEO.

Category and collection pages

Category pages (collections on Shopify) usually target the broadest searches, such as "cotton kurtis for women". Treat them like landing pages:

  • Use the plural product type plus the modifier shoppers use: material, gender, use or size range
  • Use the description for range and practical details: styles, sizes, delivery, COD and returns
  • Add a short intro paragraph on the category page itself, so the tags have content to match

Product pages

  • Lead with the product name as people search for it, then the key attribute: size, weight, model or material
  • Don't copy the manufacturer's text, which appears on every store selling the item; see writing product descriptions that sell
  • Avoid typing a fixed price into the description unless it updates automatically. Google can show price, stock and ratings from product structured data instead

Meta title and description for a sale or flash sale collection

A flash sale collection is the trickiest page to write meta tags for, because the offer changes and Google may not recrawl the page before the sale ends. What works:

  1. Keep one permanent sale URL, such as a "Sale" product category, and reuse it for every event instead of creating a new page for each Diwali or end-of-season sale. It builds up links and history, and leaves no dead sale pages behind.
  2. Put "sale" and the strongest true offer in the title: the maximum discount, the category on offer or the occasion.
  3. Avoid hard deadlines in the meta tags. "Ends tonight" can still be showing in Google days later. Use "limited time" or "while stock lasts", and put the exact end time on the page.
  4. Update the tags when each sale starts and ends, switching to an evergreen version between sales, then request indexing in Search Console's URL Inspection tool.

The discounts themselves are set up with WooCommerce sale prices or coupons; see WooCommerce coupons and discounts.

Writing meta tags at scale with templates

Yoast SEO and Rank Math let you set a default title and description per content type using variables:

  • Yoast SEO: Yoast SEO > Settings > Content types > Products, and Categories & tags > Product categories
  • Rank Math: Rank Math SEO > Titles & Meta > Products, and Product Categories

A simple product template in Rank Math's syntax (Yoast uses double percent signs, such as %%title%%):

Title: %title% %sep% %sitename%
Description: Buy %title% online. %excerpt%

Variable names differ between plugins and versions, so pick them from the plugin's variable list. Templates beat empty descriptions, but your important pages still deserve hand-written tags.

How to add meta titles and descriptions in WordPress

WordPress has no meta description field of its own, so you need an SEO plugin:

  • Yoast SEO: edit the page or product, open the Yoast SEO box below the editor and fill in SEO title and Meta description.
  • Rank Math: open the Rank Math panel from the editor's top bar and click Edit Snippet.
  • Categories: go to Products > Categories (or Posts > Categories), edit the category and scroll down to the SEO section.
  • The WooCommerce Shop page: edit it under Pages; with some setups it's controlled from the product archive settings instead.

Then view the page source (right-click, View Page Source, search for <title>) and confirm there is exactly one title tag and one meta description. Two SEO plugins, or a theme adding its own tags, can create duplicates.

Common mistakes

  • The same title or description on many pages, often from a theme default; see duplicate content explained
  • Keyword stuffing ("website design, website designer, website design company…")
  • Titles that promise something the page doesn't deliver
  • The brand name first on every page, pushing the useful words out of view
  • Sale wording left in the snippet after the sale ends

Test and improve with Search Console

  1. Open Performance > Search results and switch on Average CTR and Average position.
  2. In the Pages tab, sort by impressions. Pages with plenty of impressions but a low click-through rate for their position are your best candidates.
  3. Click a page, then the Queries tab, to see what people searched, and make sure the tags answer those queries in their words.
  4. Rewrite, note the date, and compare the four weeks before and after at similar positions.

More in Search Console reports explained. Titles and descriptions are one part of the on-page SEO checklist, so check the rest of the page too.

FAQs

How long should a meta title and meta description be?

Roughly 50 to 60 characters for the title and 120 to 155 for the description. Google cuts them off by pixel width, so titles with wide letters or capitals get truncated sooner. Use the preview in Yoast or Rank Math to check.

How do I write a meta title and description for a flash sale collection?

Start the title with "Flash Sale" or "Sale", add the strongest true offer and the product category, and end with your brand. In the description, say what's discounted and use "limited time" or "while stock lasts" rather than an exact deadline, because Google may show the snippet after the sale ends. Reuse one permanent sale URL and update the tags for each event.

Do meta descriptions help SEO rankings?

Not directly; Google has said it doesn't use them as a ranking factor. They still matter because a clear, relevant description can win more clicks from the same position.

Why is Google showing a different title or description from the one I wrote?

Google rewrites titles that are too long, repetitive or don't match the page, and often writes its own snippet when page text fits the search better. Keep your tags accurate and close to the page's main heading and content, and they'll be used more often.

Do I need a unique meta description for every product?

Ideally, but on a large store it isn't realistic to write every one by hand. Hand-write your top categories and best sellers, and use a template with the product name and short description for the rest. That's far better than empty or identical descriptions.
