Logo, Favicon and Brand Basics for Your Website: File Formats, Sizes, Social Share Images and Consistency

Logo, favicon and brand basics for your website: logo formats and sizes, favicon and app icons, social share images, logo schema and keeping it consistent.

Key takeaways
  • Get your logo as an SVG plus PNG versions, in horizontal, stacked, icon-only and reversed forms, and keep the original design files in your business name.
  • One simple square icon of at least 512 pixels covers the favicon and phone home-screen icons; WordPress generates the smaller sizes from it.
  • Set a 1200 by 630 social share image and an organisation logo in your SEO plugin, and use the same name, logo and colours everywhere customers see you.

For a website, you need your logo as an SVG file plus transparent PNG versions, in a horizontal layout for the header, a stacked or square layout, an icon-only mark and a light version for dark backgrounds. From the icon you make a favicon and app icons, ideally from one square image of at least 512 pixels, and you add a social share image of 1200 by 630 pixels so links look right on WhatsApp and LinkedIn. Then use the same name, logo, colours and fonts everywhere customers see you.

These basics are easy to skip when a business is new, and fiddly to fix later. Whether you're planning a website redesign, a rebrand or your first site, collecting the files below first makes the project faster and keeps the result consistent. If your brand already lives in a Figma file, most of this can come straight from it.

The logo files your website needs

Most small businesses have their logo as a single JPEG from WhatsApp or a PDF letterhead. That isn't enough for a website, where the logo appears at different sizes, on light and dark backgrounds, and as a tiny icon in a browser tab.

Ask your designer for the original source files too, whether Illustrator, Figma or EPS, along with the font names and licences. Keep them somewhere the business controls, not only in the designer's account; the website ownership checklist lists brand assets alongside your domain and hosting.

Getting the logo right in the header

  • Use SVG where you can. It stays sharp at any size and is usually tiny in file size. WordPress blocks SVG uploads by default for security reasons, so use a plugin that sanitises SVG files, such as Safe SVG, rather than switching the protection off.
  • If you use PNG or WebP, export it at twice the size it displays, then let the theme scale it down, so it isn't blurry on modern phones.
  • Size it for phones first. A wide logo with a tagline becomes unreadable at mobile header height. A version without the tagline, or the stacked logo, often works better on small screens.
  • Give it a width and height, so the header doesn't jump as the logo loads; see fixing layout shift.
  • Alt text should be your business name, such as "Sharma Dental Clinic", and the logo should link to the homepage.

In most themes the logo is set under Appearance > Customize > Site Identity. In a block theme, it's the Site Logo block in the header template, and in Elementor it's under Site Settings > Site Identity.

Favicon and app icons

The favicon is the small icon in browser tabs, bookmarks and history. Google also shows it next to your site in mobile search results, so a blank or default icon looks unfinished. The same image becomes the icon when someone saves your site to their phone's home screen.

Designing a favicon that works at 16 pixels

  • Use the icon-only mark or a single letter, not the full logo with words.
  • Keep it bold and simple, with strong contrast and a solid or transparent background.
  • Preview it at actual size in a browser tab, in both light and dark browser themes.

Setting it in WordPress

WordPress calls it the Site Icon. Upload one square image of at least 512 by 512 pixels under Appearance > Customize > Site Identity; recent WordPress versions also offer it under Settings > General, and Elementor has it in Site Settings. WordPress then generates the smaller sizes and adds the right tags for browsers, iPhones and Android phones.

On a custom-coded site, you add the tags yourself. A modern minimal set looks like this:

<link rel="icon" href="https://example.com/favicon.ico" sizes="32x32">
<link rel="icon" href="https://example.com/icon.svg" type="image/svg+xml">
<link rel="apple-touch-icon" href="https://example.com/apple-touch-icon.png">
<link rel="manifest" href="https://example.com/site.webmanifest">

Browsers cache favicons heavily, so a new one can take a while to appear even after you've changed it. Google can also take time to pick it up in search results, and at the time of writing it recommends a square icon larger than 48 by 48 pixels at a stable, crawlable address.

Social share images

When someone pastes your link into WhatsApp, LinkedIn or Facebook, the preview shows a title, a description and an image taken from your page's Open Graph tags. Without one, the platform picks a random image or shows nothing.

  1. Create a branded default image at 1200 by 630 pixels, with your logo and a short line about what you do. Keep important text away from the edges, since some apps crop it.
  2. Set it as the default in your SEO plugin: in Yoast SEO and Rank Math it's in the social or Open Graph settings.
  3. Give key pages and blog posts their own share images where it helps, such as a service page or a popular article.
  4. Test a link with Facebook's Sharing Debugger or LinkedIn's Post Inspector, which also clear old cached previews.

Help Google show the right logo

Google can use the logo you specify in Organization structured data, for example in a knowledge panel for your brand. SEO plugins add this for you when you fill in the organisation name and logo in their settings. The output looks something like this:

<script type="application/ld+json">
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Organization",
  "name": "Example Interiors",
  "url": "https://example.com/",
  "logo": "https://example.com/wp-content/uploads/example-logo-512.png"
}
</script>

Use a square or near-square version on a plain background, at least 112 by 112 pixels per Google's guidelines at the time of writing. Schema markup explained covers which other structured data is worth adding.

Colours, fonts and imagery: a one-page brand sheet

You don't need a 40-page brand book. A single page your designer, developer and printer can all use is enough:

  • Logo versions and the minimum size each should be shown at.
  • Colours as hex codes: a primary, an accent, text and background colours. How to choose website colours and fonts covers picking them and checking contrast.
  • Fonts for headings and body text, with the weights you use.
  • Photo style: real photos of your team, work and premises, with similar lighting and editing; see preparing photos for your website.
  • Tone of voice: a few words on how you write, such as plain, friendly and direct.

On WordPress, the colours and fonts go into global styles, in the theme, the block editor's Styles panel or Elementor's Site Settings, so one change updates the whole site.

Consistency across every channel

Customers see your brand in many places before they contact you. Use the same business name, logo and colours on your website, Google Business Profile, social profiles, WhatsApp Business profile, email signature, invoices and signboard. Consistent business details also help Google match your website to your profile in local search. When the logo changes, update all of them together; rebranding your website has a full checklist, including protecting your SEO if the name changes too.

Before your website project: a checklist

  1. Logo in SVG and PNG, in horizontal, stacked, icon-only and reversed versions.
  2. A square icon of at least 512 pixels for the favicon.
  3. Brand colours as hex codes and font names, with licences.
  4. A default social share image, or the elements to make one.
  5. Real photos, and two or three websites whose style you like.

Put these in your website brief so your developer can quote accurately.

FAQs

What size should a website logo be?

There is no single size, because themes display logos differently. Use SVG if possible. For PNG, export at twice the size the logo appears in the header, so a logo shown 200 pixels wide is exported 400 pixels wide.

What size should a favicon be?

Start with a square image of at least 512 by 512 pixels. WordPress and favicon generators create the smaller sizes, including 32 by 32 for browser tabs and 180 by 180 for iPhones.

Why isn't my new favicon showing?

Browsers cache favicons for a long time. Clear your cache or try a private window. If Google still shows the old one in search, it updates when it recrawls your homepage, which can take a while.

Can I use a JPEG logo on my website?

You can, but JPEG has no transparency, so it shows a box on coloured backgrounds, and text edges blur. Ask your designer for SVG and transparent PNG versions.

Do I need a new logo before I redesign my website?

Not necessarily. If your current logo is clear and works small, a redesign can use it. If you're changing it, finalise the logo before the website design starts, so the colours and layout are built around it.

If your website no longer matches your brand, or you're rebranding, a website redesign brings the logo, colours and fonts into one consistent, mobile-first design without losing the rankings you have. If you already have a finished design, see Figma to WordPress.
