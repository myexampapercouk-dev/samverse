How to Redesign Your Website Without Losing Google Rankings: The SEO Migration Checklist

Redesign your website and keep your rankings: benchmark, crawl, map every URL, set up 301s, keep content parity, block staging and monitor after launch.

Key takeaways
  • Benchmark and crawl the old site first, then map every URL that changes to its closest new equivalent with a single 301 redirect, never a blanket redirect to the homepage.
  • Keep the content, titles, headings, internal links and schema of the pages that already rank, and keep staging out of Google with a password rather than relying on noindex.
  • On launch day remove the noindex, test every old URL and check canonicals, then watch Search Console daily for two weeks and fix 404s and drops quickly.

To redesign a website without losing rankings, protect what Google already ranks: keep URLs where you can, 301-redirect every URL that changes to its closest new equivalent, keep the content, titles and internal links of pages that bring traffic, keep the staging site out of Google, and remove that block at launch. Then watch Search Console closely for the first few weeks and fix anything that slips.

Most ranking drops after a redesign come from skipped steps, not from the new design itself. This is the redesign-specific checklist I follow on website redesign projects. If the redesign also brings a new business name, see rebranding your website; if the domain changes too, follow changing your domain without losing SEO as well.

Phase 1: Benchmark before you touch anything

You can't tell whether a redesign hurt rankings without a record of where you started. Save these in one spreadsheet, dated:

  • Search Console > Performance: export pages and queries with clicks and impressions for the longest range available. These are the pages that need the most care.
  • GA4 landing pages: sessions and key events by page, so you know which pages bring enquiries, not just visits.
  • Search Console > Links > Top linked pages: pages other sites link to. Losing these URLs without redirects throws away those links.
  • Positions for your important searches, checked manually or with a rank tracker.
  • Speed: PageSpeed Insights results for your homepage, a service page and a blog post.

Phase 2: Crawl and inventory every URL

Crawl the old site with a tool such as Screaming Frog SEO Spider, which has a free version for smaller sites, then add URLs from your XML sitemap, the Search Console export, GA4 landing pages and your linked pages list. Crawlers only find pages that are linked, so these extra sources catch orphan pages, old PDFs and campaign pages that still get visits.

For every URL, decide one action:

  • Keep: the page stays at the same URL
  • Move: the page continues at a new URL, with a 301 redirect
  • Merge: its content joins another page, and it redirects there
  • Remove: no equivalent exists; only acceptable for pages with no meaningful traffic or links

On Shopify the URLs usually survive a redesign, but titles, templates and app embeds do not always; see changing a Shopify theme without losing SEO.

Phase 3: Build the URL map and redirects

The safest URL is the one that doesn't change. A WordPress-to-WordPress redesign can usually keep every important slug. Moving from Wix, Blogger or a hand-coded site often forces changes, which is when the map matters most; see moving from Wix to WordPress for a platform-change example.

Old URLNew URLAction
/about-us//about-us/Keep
/services/solar-installation.html/solar-installation/301
/products/inverter-batteries//batteries/301 (merged)
/diwali-offer-2019/NoneRemove (no traffic or links)
  • Redirect to the closest equivalent, page by page. Sending everything to the homepage loses relevance, and Google tends to treat it as a soft 404.
  • Avoid chains. If an old redirect points to a URL that is now changing, update it to point straight at the final URL.
  • Use 301s for permanent moves, not 302s; see 301 vs 302 redirects.
  • Keep your existing HTTPS and www rules working alongside the new redirects.

On WordPress, the Redirection plugin or Rank Math's redirections module handle most sites. For long lists, server-level rules in .htaccess on Apache or LiteSpeed hosting are faster:

Redirect 301 /services/solar-installation.html https://www.example.com/solar-installation/
Redirect 301 /products/inverter-batteries/ https://www.example.com/batteries/

Phase 4: Keep content parity on pages that rank

A redesign changes how pages look. It shouldn't quietly remove what made them rank. For every page in your benchmark's top list, check that the new version keeps:

  • The substance of the copy: sections, details, FAQs and examples. Restyle long text with headings, columns or accordions rather than deleting it.
  • Title tags and meta descriptions, carried over or improved deliberately, not reset to defaults.
  • The H1 and main headings that match what people search.
  • Internal links: a slimmer menu can leave ranking service pages with no links pointing at them. Add them to the footer, hub pages or in-content links.
  • Images and alt text, and image URLs if images bring traffic from Google Images.
  • Structured data: LocalBusiness, breadcrumbs, FAQ, product or article schema.
  • Canonical tags, hreflang on multilingual sites, and robots settings.
  • Tracking: GA4, key events, Google Ads and Meta tags.

A practical check is to crawl the old site and the staging site and compare titles, H1s, word counts and indexability side by side.

Phase 5: Keep staging out of Google

WordPress's "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" setting (Settings > Reading) adds a noindex tag. It works, but it's risky: the setting lives in the database, so if the staging site is copied to live, the noindex goes live with it. A password on the staging site, which most hosts offer, keeps out search engines and everyone else, and can't be indexed by accident.

If a staging subdomain is already in Google, password-protect it and use the Removals tool in a Search Console property for that subdomain. More on safe testing in staging sites explained.

Phase 6: Launch-day checklist

  1. Untick "Discourage search engines" and check the page source of key pages for a noindex tag.
  2. Check robots.txt doesn't contain Disallow: / from staging.
  3. Run your full old-URL list through a crawler in list mode: each should return one 301 to a page that returns 200.
  4. Check canonical tags, internal links and image addresses use the live domain, not staging. A database search and replace fixes leftovers, for example with WP-CLI: wp search-replace 'staging.example.com' 'www.example.com' --dry-run, then without --dry-run once the preview looks right.
  5. Confirm HTTPS works everywhere and only one version of the domain loads.
  6. Submit the XML sitemap in Search Console and inspect your most important URLs with URL Inspection; see getting pages indexed faster.
  7. Test forms, email delivery, WhatsApp and call buttons, and check GA4 Realtime for key events.
  8. Clear caching plugin, host and CDN caches, then run PageSpeed Insights on key templates and compare with your benchmark.

Phase 7: Monitor for the first two months

  • Daily for two weeks: the Search Console Pages report for "Not found (404)", "Excluded by noindex tag" and redirect errors, plus spot checks with URL Inspection.
  • Weekly for the next six weeks: Performance with date comparison (compare equal periods with the same weekdays), clicks by page against your benchmark, and positions for your tracked searches.
  • Fix fast: redirect any 404 that has traffic or links, and for pages that lose clicks, compare the old content (the Wayback Machine helps) with the new page.

Some movement for a few weeks is normal while Google recrawls. A sharp site-wide drop usually points to a noindex, robots or canonical problem; see Search Console indexing errors and the traffic drop checklist.

FAQs

Will redesigning my website affect SEO?

It can, in either direction. A redesign that keeps URLs, content and internal links while making the site faster and clearer often helps. One that changes URLs without redirects or strips content usually hurts. The difference is the process, not the design.

How do I redesign my website and keep my rankings?

Benchmark your top pages, crawl every URL, keep URLs where possible, 301-redirect the rest to their closest equivalents, keep the content and on-page SEO of ranking pages, password-protect staging, run launch-day checks and monitor Search Console daily for two weeks.

Should I keep the same URLs when redesigning?

Yes, wherever you can. Unchanged URLs need no redirects and keep their history. Change a URL only when there's a clear reason, such as a platform move or merging duplicate pages, and redirect it.

How long do rankings take to settle after a redesign?

Usually a few weeks as Google recrawls the site and processes redirects. Problems such as missing redirects or a leftover noindex show up in Search Console within days, which is why the first two weeks need daily checks.

Can I change my WordPress theme without losing SEO?

Yes, if URLs stay the same and the new theme keeps your content, headings, internal links and schema. Test it on staging first, because some themes move content into builder layouts, drop breadcrumbs or load much more slowly.

Should I redirect deleted pages to the homepage?

No. Redirect each page to its closest equivalent. If nothing relevant exists, letting it return a 404 or 410 is better than a homepage redirect.
