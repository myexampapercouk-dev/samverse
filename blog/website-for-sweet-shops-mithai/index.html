Websites for Sweet Shops and Mithai Brands: Festival Seasons, Gift Boxes, Corporate Diwali Orders, Delivery Radius and Shelf Life, and Online or WhatsApp Ordering

Plan a sweet shop or mithai website: festival gift boxes, corporate Diwali orders, delivery radius by shelf life, online or WhatsApp ordering and FSSAI.

Key takeaways
  • Plan the website around the festival calendar, with gift box and pre-order pages ready weeks before each season rather than days.
  • Let shelf life decide delivery: fresh milk sweets within your own delivery radius, dry fruit sweets and namkeen by courier further away.
  • Give corporate Diwali buyers their own page and form, and choose between a full online checkout and WhatsApp ordering based on how your counter actually works.

A sweet shop's website earns most of its keep in a few busy weeks: Diwali, Raksha Bandhan, Holi, Eid, Ganesh Chaturthi and the wedding season. Built well, it takes gift box orders and corporate enquiries before the rush, shows clearly what can be delivered where, and lets regular customers order without queuing. Built badly, it is a menu photo nobody updates.

Online ordering for a mithai shop usually runs on a WooCommerce store: a product range in categories, payments through gateways such as Razorpay, COD where you allow it, delivery rules, order alerts and festive coupons. The shop side, with your menu, branches and timings, has a lot in common with the websites I build for restaurants and cafes. A sweet shop sits between the two, and the decisions below are the ones that make it work.

The festival year, and what the site should be doing in each part of it

Festival dates move with the calendar each year, so plan from the actual dates, but the pattern is predictable:

SeasonWhat customers wantWhat the site should have ready
Raksha BandhanSmall gift boxes, often sent to siblings in other citiesCourier-friendly boxes with dry fruit sweets, a cut-off date for delivery before the day
Ganesh ChaturthiModak and prasad orders in quantityPre-order by date and pick-up slot, with quantity options
Navratri and DussehraFasting-friendly sweets and namkeen, event ordersA clearly labelled fasting range, with ingredients listed
DiwaliGift boxes for family, corporate gifting in bulkGift box range, corporate order page, early pre-orders and delivery slot limits
Wedding seasonLarge orders, custom boxes, return giftsAn event and wedding enquiry form with date, quantity and box options
Holi and EidGujiya, sevaiyan and festive specialsSeasonal product pages that can be switched on and off

Keep a permanent page for each festival and bring it back each year with updated products, rather than creating a new page every time. The address builds up search history, and customers who bookmarked it find it again. Planning seasonal and festival campaigns explains the timing in more detail.

Shelf life decides how far you can deliver

A fresh rasmalai and a box of kaju katli cannot be sold the same way. Group your products by how long they stay good, and let that drive delivery options:

  • Fresh milk-based sweets such as rasgulla, rasmalai, kalakand and fresh peda: same-day or next-day delivery within your own delivery radius, or pick-up only.
  • Sweets that keep for several days, such as many ladoos, burfis and kaju katli: local delivery and, for some, courier to nearby cities.
  • Dry fruit sweets, namkeen and packaged items with a longer shelf life: suitable for courier across India, and for gifting to other cities.

Show the shelf life and storage instructions on every product page, based on your own testing and what your labels state. The "best before" information on packaged food is subject to labelling rules, so take advice on what you print and keep the website consistent with the label.

Delivery radius is easiest to manage by pin code: customers enter theirs and see what can reach them and when. A pin code delivery checker in WooCommerce shows how that works, and WooCommerce shipping setup for India covers rates for local delivery and courier.

Online checkout or WhatsApp ordering?

Both work, and many shops use both. The choice depends on how your counter and kitchen run:

Full online checkoutWhatsApp ordering from the website
Best forGift boxes, packaged sweets, courier orders, customers in other citiesFresh sweets by weight, mixed boxes, regular local customers
PaymentTaken at checkout by UPI, card or net bankingPayment link or cash on delivery after confirmation
Staff effortLow per order, once products are set up properlySomeone must answer and confirm each order
Risk at festival timeToo many orders for one slot unless limits are setMessages pile up faster than staff can answer

A common arrangement is a proper checkout for gift boxes and packaged products, with a WhatsApp button for loose sweets by weight. During peak days, limit orders per delivery slot so you never accept more than the kitchen and riders can handle, and set a clear cut-off for same-day orders.

Gift boxes that sell without a phone call

Gift boxes are the easiest products to sell online because they are fixed. Each one needs:

  • A photo of the box closed and open, with the actual sweets inside
  • The contents by name and the total net weight
  • Whether it is vegetarian, and whether it contains nuts, khoya, ghee or sugar-free options
  • The shelf life and how it is packed for courier
  • A gift message field, and separate sender and recipient details
  • Delivery options available for the chosen pin code

Offer a few sizes rather than dozens of near-identical boxes. Product bundles and add-ons, such as a card or a diya set, can raise the order value; WooCommerce bundles and upsells covers the options.

Corporate Diwali orders need their own route

A company ordering three hundred boxes does not want to use a shopping cart. Give corporate buyers a page that explains what you offer, such as branded sleeves, custom box contents, delivery to multiple addresses and GST invoices in the company's name, and a form that asks:

  1. Company name, contact person and GSTIN
  2. Approximate number of boxes and budget per box
  3. Preferred contents or box type
  4. Branding needed: logo on the box or sleeve, a printed card
  5. Delivery: one office, several offices, or individual employee addresses
  6. The date by which delivery must be complete

State your last date for accepting corporate orders each season, because custom printing takes time. If corporate gifting is a large part of your business, websites for corporate gifting companies covers bulk ordering in more depth.

FSSAI details and food information

Food businesses are generally expected to display their FSSAI licence or registration number to customers, and that includes sales made online. Show the number on every page and again at checkout, and keep it in step with your current licence. List ingredients and common allergens on each product, mark sugar-free or jaggery sweets accurately, and avoid health claims such as "guilt-free" or "good for diabetics" unless you have taken advice. The requirements depend on your licence type and change over time, so confirm them with your FSSAI consultant before launch.

Photography: the counter, not a stock photo

Mithai is bought on looks. Photograph your own sweets in daylight or soft, even light, on plain plates or in your own boxes, and show the texture close up. A few photos of the counter, the kitchen at work and the branch exterior help regular customers recognise you and new ones trust you. Compress every image before upload so pages stay fast on mobile data. Preparing photos for your website walks through sizes and formats.

Your story, ingredients and kitchen

Many mithai shops have been run by the same family for decades, and customers like knowing that. A short "about" page can say when and where the shop started, which sweets it is known for, where you source milk, khoya and dry fruits, and whether you cook in pure ghee or vanaspati for each range. Be specific and accurate here: if only some sweets use desi ghee, say which. A note on hygiene in the kitchen, with real photos, answers a question many customers have but rarely ask.

More than one branch

Shops with several branches should give each one its own page with address, hours, map, phone and any branch-only items, and connect it to its own Google Business Profile. If orders are fulfilled from the nearest branch, the pin code check can route them. Keep prices consistent across branches online, or explain clearly if they differ.

FAQs

Can a sweet shop sell mithai online across India?

Longer-lasting sweets, dry fruit sweets and namkeen can be couriered, with proper packing. Fresh milk sweets are usually best kept to local delivery or pick-up.

How do we stop too many orders arriving for Diwali day?

Set a limit on orders per delivery slot, open pre-orders early, and show a cut-off date after which only pick-up is available. The store can close slots automatically when they fill.

Do we need to show the FSSAI number on our website?

If you sell food online, generally yes. Display it sitewide and beside the checkout, and confirm current requirements for your licence type with your FSSAI consultant.

Should we take orders on WhatsApp or through the website?

Many shops do both: online checkout for gift boxes and packaged items, and WhatsApp for loose sweets by weight. Choose based on how many staff can answer messages at peak time.

Can corporate customers get GST invoices?

Yes. The store can generate GST invoices with the company's details, and bulk orders can be invoiced from a quote. Ask your CA to confirm rates and invoice format.
