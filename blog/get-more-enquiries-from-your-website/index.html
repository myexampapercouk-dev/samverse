Website Not Getting Enquiries? 12 Ways to Get More Enquiries From Your Business Website

Website not getting enquiries? Find out why, then use 12 practical ways to get more: clearer headlines, WhatsApp, shorter forms, real proof, speed and tracking.

Key takeaways
  • First work out whether you have too few visitors, visitors who do not act, or enquiries that are lost or untracked, because each needs a different fix.
  • Say what you do and for whom in the headline, put one clear call to action on every page, and offer WhatsApp, a tap-to-call number and a short form.
  • Real proof, a page for each service, a fast mobile site and tracking every form, call and WhatsApp click turn the same traffic into more enquiries.

If your website gets visitors but few enquiries, the problem is rarely the amount of traffic alone. Usually the site doesn't make it instantly clear what you offer, doesn't give visitors an easy way to get in touch, or doesn't give them enough reason to trust you. Fix those three first: a clear headline, an obvious call to action with WhatsApp and phone beside the form, and real proof of your work.

Below is how to work out why your website isn't getting enquiries, then twelve practical fixes, most of which you can make in a day, with examples from sites I've built. If you're running ads to a page that isn't converting, landing page design covers pages built around one offer; if the whole site needs rebuilding around enquiries, see WordPress website development.

Why is my website not getting enquiries?

Start by working out which of four problems you have, because the fixes are different:

What you seeLikely problemStart with
Very few visitors in Google Analytics or Search ConsoleNot enough of the right people find the siteLocal SEO, your Google Business Profile and a page for each service
Steady visitors, almost no enquiriesThe site isn't persuading them or making contact easyFixes 1 to 9 below
Enquiries suddenly dropped to zeroSomething broke: the form, email delivery, tracking or rankingsTest your form today
Plenty of enquiries, mostly the wrong kindThe site attracts or invites poor-fit leadsClearer service pages, pricing signals and a qualifying question on the form

If enquiries stopped suddenly, read why your contact form isn't getting enquiries first; a form that silently stopped sending is common and easy to miss. For the traffic side, especially if you serve one city, see local SEO for small businesses in India. The rest of this guide is about turning the visitors you already have into enquiries.

Test your site like a customer

Take fifteen minutes with your own phone, on mobile data:

  1. Open your homepage and give yourself five seconds. Can you tell what the business does, where, and for whom?
  2. Without scrolling, is there a button that tells you what to do next?
  3. Tap the phone number. Does it start a call?
  4. Tap the WhatsApp button. Does it open a chat with a sensible message already typed?
  5. Submit the contact form. Does a thank-you message appear, and does the email arrive?
  6. Open one of your service pages from a Google result. Does it have its own call to action, or does it dead-end?

Whatever fails here is where to start.

Make it instantly clear what you do

1. Write a headline that says what you do and for whom

"Welcome to our website" wastes your most valuable space. Try "Orthopaedic clinic in Pune: same-week appointments" or "Industrial automation panels manufactured in India". A visitor should understand you within a few seconds and see their own problem or location in the words.

2. Put one main call to action above the fold

Pick the single action you most want, such as "Get a free quote", "Book an appointment" or "Request a call back", and make it a clear button that's visible without scrolling on mobile. Other options can sit beside it, but one should stand out.

Remove friction

3. Add WhatsApp with a pre-filled message

Many people in India would rather send a WhatsApp message than fill in a form or call. A button that opens a chat with a message such as "Hi, I'd like a quote for..." already typed makes the first step almost effortless, and can tell your team which page the enquiry came from. See WhatsApp on your business website.

4. Make calling easy

Phone numbers should be tappable on mobile. For buyers who want to talk at a time that suits them, a call-back request works well: Sahni Power Solutions, a generator rental site I built, keeps "Request a Call Back" in the header and an "Enquire Now" button at the end of each section.

5. Shorten your forms

Every extra field is another reason to give up. Ask only what you need to reply, usually a name, phone number and a short message, and use a dropdown for the service instead of making people type it. On the Vansh Group site I built, a service-type dropdown means enquiries arrive already sorted by service.

6. Speed up the site on mobile

Slow pages lose visitors before they see your offer, and many of your visitors will be on phones, often on patchy mobile data. Compress images, use caching and decent hosting, and test your key pages in PageSpeed Insights on mobile.

Build trust

7. Show real proof

Client logos (with permission), named testimonials, Google reviews, project photos and case studies reassure visitors that you're genuine and good at what you do. Put proof next to your calls to action, not only on a separate page. The website trust signals checklist covers credentials, policies and contact details too.

8. Use real photos and real people

Photos of your team, premises, factory or finished projects build more trust than stock images, and a named person behind the business makes enquiring feel less risky.

9. Answer the questions people hesitate over

Price, timelines, process, service area and what happens after they enquire are the usual hesitations. A short FAQ on each service page answers them before visitors have to ask. If you can't publish prices, explaining what affects the cost, or giving a starting point, still helps people decide whether to get in touch.

Guide visitors to act

10. Give each service its own page

One "Services" page with a list of bullet points can't speak to everyone. A dedicated page for each service lets you describe that customer's problem, show relevant proof and rank for that specific search. See how to write service pages that rank and convert.

11. Repeat your call to action

Visitors decide at different points, so add a call to action after each main section and at the end of every page and article, not just in the header. The Studio Agama Interiors site I built repeats its "Get A Free Quote" button all the way down the homepage.

12. Track enquiries, and reply fast

Set up Google Analytics to count form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and phone taps as key events, so you know which pages and campaigns bring enquiries. A "How did you hear about us?" question on the form backs up the numbers. Then reply quickly, because the first business to respond often wins the job; see how to respond to website enquiries fast.

Other ways in for visitors who aren't ready

Not everyone is ready to enquire today. Something useful in return can start the conversation:

  • A self-assessment, like the free anxiety self-assessment on the Dr. Sudhir Arora site I built, which leads naturally to a consultation.
  • A calculator or estimator. The website cost calculator on this site sends the visitor's selections to WhatsApp, so the conversation starts with a brief.
  • A brochure or company profile for B2B buyers who need something to share internally, as on the Vansh Group site.

Fix the enquiry handling, not only the website

Sometimes the website is producing enquiries and they are being lost after they arrive. Before redesigning anything, check what happens to a form submission or a WhatsApp message once it leaves the site.

Send a test enquiry and time the reply

Fill in your own form and send a message to your own WhatsApp number from a different phone. Note whether the form email arrives at all, whether it lands in spam, and how long it takes for someone to reply. Form emails failing silently is a common and easily fixed problem on WordPress sites, usually solved by sending mail through a proper SMTP service.

Make sure enquiries reach a person

Route form notifications to an inbox that is checked daily, and to more than one person if possible. Store a copy of each submission in the website database or a simple spreadsheet so nothing is lost if an email goes astray.

Reply with what the visitor asked for

A fast, specific reply that answers the question and suggests a next step (a call, a visit or a quote) converts far better than a generic "thank you for contacting us". Keep a few saved reply templates for your most common enquiries.

Track which page each enquiry came from

Add a hidden field or a thank-you page so you can see which page, campaign or city page produced each enquiry. Over a few months this shows which pages deserve more effort and which pages bring time-wasters.

FAQs

Why is my website not getting enquiries?

Either too few of the right people visit, or visitors don't see a clear reason and an easy way to contact you. Check your traffic first, then your headline, calls to action, contact options and proof. If enquiries stopped suddenly, test your form and email delivery straight away.

How can I get more enquiries from my website quickly?

The quickest changes are a headline that says what you do and where, a WhatsApp button with a pre-filled message, tappable phone numbers, a shorter form and real testimonials near the call to action. Most can be done in a day.

Is a WhatsApp button better than a contact form?

Not better, but different. Many Indian customers prefer WhatsApp, while others want to write details in a form or talk on the phone. Offer all three side by side, and track which one each enquiry used.

How many fields should an enquiry form have?

As few as you need to reply well: usually a name, a phone number or email and a message, perhaps with a service dropdown. Add qualifying questions only if your team genuinely needs the answers before calling.

Do I need more traffic or a better website?

If visitors arrive but don't enquire, more traffic just means more people leaving. Fix the site first, then spend on SEO or ads. If almost nobody visits, you need both.

Why are my website form enquiries not reaching my email?

On WordPress this is usually because the site sends mail through the server without proper authentication, so it is blocked or lands in spam. Sending through an SMTP service and saving submissions in the database usually fixes it.
