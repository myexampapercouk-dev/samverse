Website Trust Signals Checklist: What Makes Visitors Confident Enough to Enquire

A practical checklist of website trust signals, from contact details and real photos to reviews, policies, security and credentials, that help visitors enquire.

Sameer Gupta 28 September 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Show a full address, an answered phone number, WhatsApp and an email on your own domain so visitors know you are a real business.
  • Real team photos, specific testimonials and verifiable credentials build far more trust than stock images or vague claims.
  • Outdated offers, broken forms, missing policies and security warnings quietly undo the trust the rest of your website builds.

Before someone fills in your form or taps the WhatsApp button, they quietly ask one question: "Are these people real, and can I trust them?" Most visitors never tell you why they left, and missing trust signals are a common reason. Use this checklist to go through your website the way a cautious first-time customer would.

Contact details that prove you're a real business

Hidden or incomplete contact details are one of the quickest ways to lose a careful visitor. Check that your site shows:

  • A full business address, with a map on the contact page, matching your Google Business Profile
  • A phone number that someone actually answers during business hours, tappable on mobile
  • A WhatsApp button linked to your business number, not a staff member's personal number
  • An email address on your own domain rather than a free Gmail or Yahoo address
  • Your registered business name, and your GSTIN if you're registered and sell to other businesses
  • Opening hours, and how quickly you usually reply

Put the phone number and WhatsApp button in the header or footer of every page, not only on the contact page.

For the contact page itself, from which options to show to setting reply-time expectations, see how to design a contact page that turns visitors into enquiries.

Real people and real photos

People buy from people. Stock photos of smiling models in suits are easy to recognise, and they make a genuine business look like a template.

  • Photos of the owner and team, with names and roles
  • Photos of your office, shop, clinic, factory or vehicles
  • Photos of real projects, products and deliveries; phone photos are fine if they're sharp and well lit
  • A short founder message or video explaining who you are and how you work
  • An About page with your story and experience; see how to write an About page that builds trust

Reviews, testimonials and proof of work

  • Testimonials with a full name (or first name and city), a photo or company name, and a specific detail about what you did
  • Your Google rating and a link to your Google reviews, kept current
  • Case studies or project galleries showing what you actually delivered
  • Client logos, but only with permission and only for clients you've genuinely worked with
  • Polite replies to reviews, including negative ones, which show you take feedback seriously

Place proof close to the point of decision: next to the enquiry form, on service pages and near prices. For more, see how to collect and display testimonials.

Credentials, registrations and memberships

Credentials reassure visitors that you're qualified and accountable. Show the ones that genuinely apply to you:

  • Professional qualifications and registrations for doctors, CAs, architects or engineers, shown in the way your professional body's rules allow
  • Licence or registration numbers customers expect in your industry, such as an FSSAI licence for food businesses or RERA numbers for real estate projects; check the current display rules for your sector
  • Certifications such as ISO, and authorised dealer or partner status from brands you work with
  • Memberships of industry associations or chambers of commerce
  • Years in business and number of projects, but only numbers you can back up

Make credentials verifiable. A registration number that a visitor can check with the issuing body is far more convincing than a badge image anyone could copy.

Clear policies and honest terms

Policies feel boring, but visitors look for them as soon as money is involved.

  • A privacy policy explaining what you collect through forms, analytics and chat; see privacy policy and cookie basics
  • Terms of service or terms of sale
  • Refund, cancellation and return policies in plain language
  • Delivery timelines and shipping charges, if you sell products
  • How warranties, AMCs or guarantees work, including what they don't cover

If you sell online, India's consumer protection rules for e-commerce generally expect sellers to show details such as their legal name, address, customer care contact and grievance process. Rules change, so confirm the current requirements with your CA or lawyer.

Security and a website that works

  • HTTPS on every page, with no "Not secure" warning in the browser; see fixing SSL certificate warnings
  • Payments through a recognised payment gateway, and UPI details that show your business name rather than a personal name
  • Forms that work, confirm the submission and lead to a thank-you page
  • No broken links, missing images or pages that have said "coming soon" for months
  • Pages that load quickly on mobile data
  • No spammy pop-ups, strange redirects or unfamiliar links, which can be signs of a hacked site

Small details that quietly break trust

  • A footer copyright year that's several years old
  • Last Diwali's offer banner still on the homepage
  • Spelling mistakes, placeholder text or unfinished pages
  • Claims like "No. 1 in India" or "100% guaranteed" without any proof
  • Fake urgency, such as countdown timers that reset on every visit
  • Different phone numbers or addresses on different pages and listings
  • Social media icons that lead to accounts with no recent posts
  • A blog whose latest post is years old

None of these is dramatic on its own, but together they make visitors wonder whether the business is still running.

Check your own site in 15 minutes

  1. Open your website on your phone, on mobile data, as if you'd never heard of your business
  2. Within a few seconds, can you tell who runs it, where they are and how to contact them?
  3. Find one review, one real photo and one credential without searching hard
  4. Submit the form and tap the WhatsApp button, then check that the enquiry actually arrives
  5. Look for the policies a customer would want to read before paying
  6. Ask a recent customer what, if anything, made them hesitate

Fix the gaps on your homepage, contact page and main service pages first, because those are the pages most visitors see before deciding whether to enquire.
