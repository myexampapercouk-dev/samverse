Websites for Event Organisers: Event Pages, Selling Tickets on WooCommerce or a Ticketing Platform, QR Check-in, GST on Tickets, Recurring Events and Sponsors

How event organisers sell tickets from their own site: event pages, WooCommerce vs ticketing platforms, QR check-in, GST, recurring events, sponsors.

Key takeaways
  • Keep event information on your own website even if a ticketing platform handles payment, because those pages keep their search value from one edition to the next.
  • Selling through WooCommerce gives you the buyer relationship and lower platform dependence, but you then own capacity limits, ticket delivery, check-in and sale-day load.
  • Plan the gate before the sale: how QR codes are scanned, what happens offline, and how duplicates and transfers are handled.

An event organiser's website needs a permanent page for each event with every practical detail in plain text, a ticketing method that suits the size of the event, a check-in process worked out before the first ticket is sold, and terms that cover refunds, transfers and changes. Whether tickets are sold through WooCommerce on your own site or through a ticketing platform, the information should live on your site, so each edition builds on the last.

The page that sells the tickets is effectively a landing page: one event, one action, tracking on the button, fast on a phone. If you sell through your own store, the setup sits on the WooCommerce side of what I build. This guide is about public, paid events such as concerts, conferences, comedy nights, workshops, runs and expos.

Planners organising private weddings and parties for clients have different needs, covered in websites for event and wedding planners.

Choose how tickets will be sold

This decision shapes the rest of the build, so make it first. Platform terms and fees change, so check current details with any provider before committing.

Whichever you pick, do not let the platform listing replace your own event page. A platform page often disappears when the sale ends; yours keeps its links and search visibility for next year.

What an event page needs

Think of each event page as the answer to every message you would otherwise get on WhatsApp the week before. Work through it in this order:

  1. The essentials, above the fold: event name, date, start time, gates-open time, venue and city, and a "Book tickets" button that stays reachable on mobile
  2. Ticket tiers: each tier's price including fees, what it includes, and how many remain if you choose to show it
  3. Who is performing or speaking: short bios and photos you have rights to use
  4. Schedule: by day, stage or track for longer events, with time zones for online sessions
  5. Venue and access: map, entry gates, parking, nearest metro or cab drop point, step-free access, bag policy and re-entry rules
  6. Rules: age limits, ID requirements, prohibited items
  7. Terms and FAQs: refunds, transfers, postponement and cancellation, linked from checkout too

Keep this as real text, not only on a poster image, so people using screen readers and search engines can read it. Event structured data can help search engines show the date and venue; check it with the tools described in testing structured data and rich results.

Selling tickets on WooCommerce: how it fits together

If you go the own-site route, a typical setup looks like this. Details depend on the extension you choose.

  • Tiers as variations or separate products: early bird, regular, VIP, student, group, each with its own price and sale dates
  • Capacity as stock: each tier has a limit, so the store stops selling when it is reached and you never oversell
  • Attendee fields: one name per ticket where you need named entry, collected at checkout
  • Ticket delivery: a PDF or email ticket with a unique QR code or ID, sent automatically once payment succeeds
  • Payments: UPI, cards and net banking through an Indian gateway; the comparison in payment gateways in India compared helps you choose
  • Coupons: sponsor codes, alumni discounts and group offers with usage limits

Test the full journey on a phone before announcing: a successful payment, a failed one, a cancelled one, and the emails each produces. A buyer whose money left their account but who received no ticket will message you, so the failure email should tell them exactly what to do.

GST and fees on tickets

Tax treatment of event tickets in India depends on the type of event, the ticket price and your registration, and the rules have changed over time. I am not the right person to tell you the rate, so ask your CA before the first sale. On the website side, what matters is that:

  • The price a buyer sees on the event page is the price they pay, or any fee and tax is shown clearly before checkout
  • Invoices or receipts show the details your CA asks for
  • Platform or convenience fees are explained, not added silently at the last step

WooCommerce can be configured with tax classes and invoice plugins once your CA has confirmed the treatment; see GST invoices in WooCommerce.

QR check-in on the day

A QR code on a ticket is only useful if the gate can scan it quickly. Plan the gate before tickets go on sale:

  1. Choose the scanning method: an app that comes with your ticketing extension or platform, or a searchable attendee list on staff phones as a fallback
  2. Decide whether a ticket can be scanned once only, and what staff do when a code shows as already used
  3. Test scanning on the actual phones your staff will use, in poor light
  4. Plan for weak mobile signal at the venue; check whether the app can work offline and sync later
  5. Set up a separate desk for problems: lost tickets, name changes, payment disputes
  6. Print a backup attendee list sorted by name and booking reference

For the basics of generating and placing QR codes, QR codes for business websites is a useful companion.

Recurring events and editions

Monthly comedy nights, annual conferences and weekly workshops need a structure that does not throw away last year's work.

  • A permanent series page such as /pune-comedy-night/ that describes the event in general and lists upcoming dates
  • A page per edition or date linked from the series page, with its own tickets
  • Past editions kept and marked clearly as finished, with photos, highlights and a link to the next one, rather than deleted
  • A "notify me" form on the series page, so fans hear when the next date opens

For weekly or monthly events, a calendar view or simple list of dates is easier for visitors than a separate menu item for each.

Sponsors and exhibitors

The website can sell sponsorships as well as tickets:

  • A sponsors page with logos grouped by tier, used only with permission
  • A "Sponsor us" page describing your audience, previous editions and packages, with an enquiry form
  • For expos, stall sizes, floor plans and an exhibitor booking or enquiry form
  • Audience figures only where you can back them up, such as ticket sales from your own records

If you also hire out staging, furniture or equipment, that side of the business is covered in websites for event rental businesses.

Sale-day readiness

The moment tickets open is when the site is under most pressure. A checklist for the week before:

  • Event pages cached and images compressed; cart and checkout excluded from cache
  • Hosting checked for how many buyers it can handle at once, and upgraded temporarily if needed
  • Unneeded plugins, sliders and third-party scripts removed from ticket pages
  • Exact sale time announced, and no website changes made on the day
  • A fallback ready: a short notice and backup ticket link if checkout fails
  • Someone watching orders and the gateway dashboard for the first hour

More on keeping a store fast under load is in WooCommerce speed optimisation.

Venues that sell time slots every day rather than one-off events, such as escape rooms and gaming zones, need a different setup; see websites for escape rooms and gaming zones.

FAQs

Can I sell event tickets on WooCommerce?

Yes. WooCommerce with a ticketing or events extension can sell tiered tickets, limit capacity, collect attendee names and email tickets with QR codes. You take responsibility for hosting, updates and check-in, so it suits organisers who want to own the customer relationship.

Should I use a ticketing platform instead of my own site?

For a very large one-off launch, a platform's ready-made infrastructure can be the safer choice. For recurring events, workshops and conferences, selling on your own site often makes more sense. Many organisers do both.

How do I show GST on event tickets?

Ask your CA how GST applies to your type of event and ticket price, then show the final price, or the tax, clearly before checkout. Your store can then be set up to match.

What happens to the event page after the event?

Keep it. Mark it as finished, add photos and a recap, and link to the next edition. Deleting it throws away links and search visibility you will want next year.

How do I stop people using the same ticket twice?

Use unique QR codes and a scanning app that marks each code as used. Staff should know what to do when a code shows as already scanned, usually sending the visitor to a help desk with the booking details.
