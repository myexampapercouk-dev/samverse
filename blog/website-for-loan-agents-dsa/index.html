Websites for Loan Agents and DSAs: Building Trust Without Overpromising

What a loan agent or DSA website needs: the lenders you work with, loan types explained, document checklists, eligibility forms and careful, honest wording.

Key takeaways
  • Name lenders only with permission and accurately, and say clearly that rates and approval are decided by the lender.
  • Explain loan types and document checklists in plain language, and keep enquiry forms short with no PAN, Aadhaar or OTPs.
  • Avoid promises like guaranteed approval or lowest rates, and check with lenders what RBI rules and their policies allow you to advertise.

Borrowers are cautious, and with good reason. Fake loan offers, fraud calls and pushy agents have made many people suspicious of anyone who says they can "get you a loan". If you're a loan agent, a direct selling agent (DSA) or an advisor who helps people choose between lenders, your website has two jobs: explain clearly how you help, and show that you're the kind of professional who doesn't overpromise. This guide covers what to include and what to leave out. It isn't legal or compliance advice, so check the specifics with the lenders you work with and, where needed, a compliance professional.

What borrowers want to know

  • Who you are: your real name, office address, photo and how long you've been doing this work
  • Which lenders you work with, and in what capacity
  • Which loans you help with, and which you don't
  • What it costs them: whether you charge borrowers anything, or are paid by the lender
  • What happens next: documents, steps and who makes the final decision

The last point matters most. Say plainly that interest rates, fees, loan amounts and approval are decided by the lender, based on its own assessment. You help with choosing, paperwork and follow-up; you don't make the decision.

Name your lenders only with permission, and accurately

A list of the banks and NBFCs you work with builds confidence, but only if every name on it is true and current:

  • List a lender only if you have a current agreement and it allows you to mention the relationship publicly
  • Use logos only with written permission and in line with the lender's brand guidelines; many lenders have rules on how partners may present them
  • Describe your role in the terms your agreement uses, such as DSA, channel partner or connector, and never suggest you are the lender, a branch or an employee
  • Remove a lender from the site as soon as the arrangement ends

A borrower may check with the lender whether you're an authorised partner, so what your website says should match what the lender would confirm.

Explain each loan type in plain language

Create a page for each loan you actually help with, such as home loans, loans against property, business loans, personal loans, vehicle loans or education loans. Each page can follow the same structure:

SectionWhat to include
Who it suitsSalaried people, the self-employed or business owners, and typical uses
What lenders generally look atIncome, credit history, existing EMIs and, for secured loans, the property or asset
The processEnquiry, documents, application, lender checks, decision and disbursal
Your roleWhat you do at each step, and what the lender does

Don't publish interest rates or "starting from" figures unless the lender has approved them and you can keep them current; rates change and depend on each borrower's profile. If you add an EMI calculator, label it as an illustration only and let users enter their own rate. Short, accurate answers to questions like "what is a loan against property?" also attract searches; see FAQ sections that help customers and SEO.

Document checklists people can actually use

A clear checklist saves hours of back-and-forth on WhatsApp. Split it by loan type and by applicant type, because a salaried person and a business owner need different papers. Commonly requested items include identity and address proof, income proof such as salary slips or income tax returns, bank statements, business registration documents for business loans, and property papers for secured loans.

Add a note that each lender sets its own requirements and may ask for more. Offer the checklist as a printable page, with a WhatsApp button for questions. Don't ask people to upload documents through an ordinary website contact form; collect them later through a secure process agreed with the lender.

Eligibility enquiry forms that collect less

Call your form an "eligibility enquiry" or "request a call back", not "instant approval". Keep it short:

  1. Name, mobile number and city
  2. Loan type and approximate amount
  3. Employment type: salaried, self-employed or business
  4. Best time to call

You don't need PAN, Aadhaar, bank account numbers or OTPs at this stage, so don't ask for them. A simple step-by-step layout can help; see multi-step forms for lead qualification. Under the form, say how you'll use the details, that you'll share them with a lender only when the borrower agrees, and link to your privacy policy. India's data protection law applies to this kind of personal data; see the DPDP Act and your website.

Wording to avoid, and rules to check

Lenders regulated by the RBI generally remain responsible for how their agents deal with customers, and RBI rules cover areas such as fair practices, disclosures and digital lending. Lenders often add their own marketing rules for partners. Before you publish, check with each lender, and a compliance professional if needed, what you may advertise and how. As a general rule, avoid:

  • "Guaranteed approval", "100% approval" or "loans for everyone"
  • "Lowest interest rates" or "best rates in the market"
  • "No documents needed" or "credit score doesn't matter"
  • Specific approval or disbursal times you can't control
  • Testimonials that suggest an outcome is certain

If it's true for your business, add a line saying what you will never do, such as ask for an OTP or an upfront fee to "release" a loan. It reassures genuine borrowers and helps protect them from fraudsters using your name. If you run ads, check each platform's financial services policies too, as some require advertiser verification.

Keep financial data safe, and get found locally

  • Use HTTPS, send enquiries to a secure CRM or inbox rather than a widely shared spreadsheet, and limit who can see them
  • Delete enquiries you no longer need, and never sell or pass on leads without consent
  • Complete your Google Business Profile with your real office address and hours
  • Target searches like "home loan agent in {city}" and "business loan consultant in {area}" with genuinely useful loan pages
  • Keep the site fast and simple on mobile, with tap-to-call and WhatsApp easy to find
