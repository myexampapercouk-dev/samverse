Websites for Specialist Doctors in India: Credentials, Procedures, Hospitals and Second Opinions

What a website for specialist doctors in India needs: verifiable credentials, procedure pages, hospital and insurance details, second opinions and booking.

Key takeaways
  • Patients and their families compare specialists on specific experience, so show full degrees, registration, fellowships and the conditions you actually treat.
  • Give each major procedure or condition its own plain-language page covering who it is for, what happens, recovery and where it is done.
  • Make consultation, second-opinion and appointment requests easy on mobile, and keep claims factual and in line with current medical advertising rules.

A website for a specialist doctor in India has one job: help a worried patient, or their family, decide that you are the right doctor for their specific problem and book a consultation. That means verifiable credentials, clear pages for the conditions and procedures you handle, the hospital where you operate, and an easy way to book or ask for a second opinion.

Patients rarely find a specialist by chance. A family doctor has suggested an orthopaedic surgeon, a check-up has flagged something for a cardiologist, or a pregnancy needs a gynaecologist the family can trust. By the time they reach your site, they are comparing you with two or three other names, and often a son or daughter in another city is doing the research for a parent. For timings, maps and the other basics every practice needs, start with the clinic website checklist.

What patients check before choosing a specialist

  • Is this doctor experienced in my specific problem, not just the broad speciality?
  • Do they perform the procedure I have been told I may need?
  • Which hospital would I be admitted to, and does it accept my insurance or cashless scheme?
  • Can I get a second opinion before deciding on surgery?
  • When and where can I see the doctor, and how do I book?

Your site should answer each of these within a click or two of the homepage, and on a phone, because that is where most of this research happens.

Credentials that are specific and verifiable

Specialists have years of training behind them, and patients want to see it laid out clearly:

  • Degrees in full, such as MS (Orthopaedics), DM (Cardiology), MD or MS (Obstetrics and Gynaecology) or DNB, with the institution
  • Medical council registration number, so patients and referring doctors can verify it
  • Fellowships and special training, for example in joint replacement, interventional cardiology or high-risk pregnancy
  • Memberships of professional societies you genuinely belong to
  • Hospital affiliations and any teaching or academic roles

Keep it factual. A clear list of qualifications reads as more trustworthy than adjectives like "best" or "top". Medical advertising in India is governed by professional conduct rules, so avoid claims of guaranteed results, comparisons with other doctors and patient-facing superlatives, and check the current rules with your medical council or association if you are unsure.

The pulmonologist website in Agra I built follows this pattern: a doctor profile covering education, a past academic role and memberships of national and international chest societies, placed where patients look before they book.

Pages for conditions and procedures

A single "Services" list with twenty items helps nobody. Give the main conditions and procedures their own sections or pages, written for patients rather than colleagues:

  • What the condition or procedure is, in plain words
  • Who it is usually suitable for, and when other options are tried first
  • What happens before, during and after, including tests and hospital stay
  • Typical recovery steps and follow-up visits
  • Where it is done: clinic, day-care unit or partner hospital

These pages also help search. A family searching for "knee replacement surgeon in Pune" or "bronchoscopy in Agra" is more likely to land on a focused page than a generic homepage. The pulmonology site, for example, has dedicated sections for bronchoscopy, diseases and diagnostics, explaining tests such as pulmonary function tests, polysomnography and the six-minute walk test in patient-friendly language.

Hospitals, insurance and practical details

Many specialists consult in their own clinic and operate or admit at one or more hospitals. Make this clear:

  • A list of where you consult and on which days, with addresses and maps
  • The hospitals where procedures are done
  • A note on insurance: which hospitals offer cashless treatment, and that patients should confirm with the hospital desk and their insurer
  • What to bring to a first visit: previous reports, scans, prescriptions and referral letters

Families travelling from smaller towns appreciate directions, parking notes and nearby landmarks. These details reduce phone calls asking the same questions.

Second opinions and online consultations

Patients told they need surgery often want another view first. If you offer second opinions, say so clearly and explain how it works: which reports to send, whether it is an in-person or video consultation, and how long a reply usually takes. A simple form where patients can describe their situation and request a call can be set up, with a clear note not to use it for emergencies.

If you consult online, explain how booking, payment and the call itself work. Keep any report uploads private and limited to what you need, and ask a lawyer about current data protection obligations for health information.

Booking, calls and WhatsApp

A specialist site should make the next step obvious on every page. The dental clinic website in Agra uses a "Book Now" button beside a short menu, a dedicated appointment page, a contact form, and click-to-chat WhatsApp and click-to-call buttons, so a patient on any page ends next to a way to get in touch. The same approach works for any speciality.

  • A visible "Book appointment" button in the header
  • Click-to-call and WhatsApp for families who prefer to talk
  • A short form asking for name, phone, concern and preferred location
  • A confirmation message so patients know the request arrived

For tips on chat buttons, read adding WhatsApp to a business website.

Local search for specialists

Patients usually search with a city or area. Keep your Google Business Profile complete for each clinic, with the correct speciality category, timings and photos, and respond to reviews politely without sharing any patient details. On the site, mention each consulting location by name and keep phone numbers identical everywhere. If you consult in several cities, give each location its own section with timings and a map.

Answer the questions patients ask at every visit

Every specialist hears the same questions again and again: is this procedure painful, how long will I be in hospital, when can I return to work, can I travel after it, do I need to stop my regular medicines. Writing short, factual answers to these on your site helps patients arrive better prepared and gives families something to read together before deciding.

An FAQ section on each procedure page works well, and a general FAQ covering booking, reports and parking handles the practical questions. The dental clinic site, for example, includes an FAQ answering common questions about pain, booking and location. Keep answers general, avoid promising outcomes, and add a line asking patients to discuss their own case at consultation. A site search, like the one on the pulmonology site, helps patients find the right answer quickly once you have many pages. For writing answers that also help search, see FAQ pages for SEO.

Speed, privacy and upkeep

Many patients and their parents browse on mid-range phones and patchy mobile data, so keep pages light, compress photos and avoid heavy sliders. Use HTTPS throughout, keep forms short, and do not ask for more health information than you need to arrange a call. Finally, keep the site current: a wrong timing or an old hospital address costs trust quickly, so update locations and days whenever they change.

FAQs

What should a website for specialist doctors include?

Full credentials and registration, pages for the main conditions and procedures, consulting locations and hospital affiliations, second-opinion details, and easy booking with call and WhatsApp options.

Can doctors in India show patient testimonials on their website?

Rules on medical advertising and testimonials can change, so check the current guidance from your medical council or association before publishing them. Factual information about qualifications and services is the safer foundation.

Should a specialist publish consultation fees?

It is your choice. Some doctors list consultation fees to reduce calls; others explain what a first visit includes and share fees on booking. Procedure costs vary by case and hospital, so explain what affects them instead.

Is WordPress suitable for a doctor's website?

Yes. WordPress lets you update timings, locations and condition pages yourself, and appointment forms, WhatsApp buttons and site search can be added as needed.

How many pages does a specialist website need?

Start with home, about the doctor, the main conditions and procedures, locations and booking. Add pages over time for the questions patients ask most often at consultations.
