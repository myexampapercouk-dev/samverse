Monthly SEO Reports: What to Track, Where to Find It and How to Spot Vanity Metrics

What an SEO report should track monthly for an Indian small business: enquiries, Search Console clicks and queries, local visibility, and vanity metrics.

Key takeaways
  • A useful SEO report starts with enquiries and sales from organic search, then explains them with Search Console clicks, queries and landing pages.
  • Compare the same period year on year where seasons matter, and look at trends over three months rather than reacting to one month.
  • Keyword ranking lists, authority scores and raw traffic totals are context at best; ask what changed, why, and what happens next month.

An SEO report should tell you what to track in one sentence: is Google search bringing more of the right customers to your business? So the numbers that matter most are enquiries and sales from organic search, followed by Search Console clicks, the queries behind them and the pages they land on. Everything else is supporting detail.

Many reports miss this. They are long PDFs full of charts, keyword lists and scores that look impressive but say little about enquiries. This guide covers what a small business in India should track each month, where to find each number, and how to read a report without being misled. For analytics beyond SEO, see the website metrics that actually matter.

SEO reporting: what to track first

Traffic only matters if some of it turns into business. The first lines of any SEO report should be:

  • Organic enquiries: form submissions, click-to-call taps, WhatsApp clicks and bookings from visitors who arrived through Google search
  • Organic sales: for online stores on WooCommerce or Shopify, orders and revenue from organic search
  • The pages that produced them: where those visitors first landed on your site

In Google Analytics 4 these come from key events (what GA4 used to call conversions), filtered to the Organic Search channel. If calls, WhatsApp clicks and form submissions are not tracked yet, fix that first; see GA4 events explained. In India a lot of enquiries happen on WhatsApp or by phone, so a site that only tracks form submissions will under-count what SEO is doing.

It also helps to ask new customers how they found you, because some people search, then save your number and call days later. A simple "How did you hear about us?" field on the form, or a question at the first call, fills that gap.

Search Console: clicks, impressions and queries

Google Search Console shows how your site performs in Google search itself. Each month, note:

  • Clicks: visits from Google search, the most direct measure of SEO traffic
  • Impressions: how often your pages appeared in results, an early sign of growing visibility
  • Average position: useful as a trend, but averaged across every query, so treat it with care
  • Top queries: the actual searches people used, and whether they match what you sell
  • Top pages: which pages earn the clicks

Filter by country too. If you serve customers in India, a rise in impressions from other countries can look like growth while doing nothing for your enquiries. The country filter in the Performance report shows India-only clicks and positions in a few seconds. If any of these reports are unfamiliar, read Google Search Console reports explained.

Local visibility for Indian businesses

If most of your customers come from one city or a few localities, your Google Business Profile matters as much as the website. Its performance view shows calls, direction requests and website clicks, and which searches found you. Add these to the monthly report, along with new reviews and how quickly they were answered.

It is also worth checking a few important searches by hand, such as "dentist in Kothrud" or "CA firm in Andheri", from a phone in the area. Results change with location, so a ranking tool set to "India" may not reflect what a customer two streets away sees. For a full checklist, see the local SEO guide for small businesses in India.

Compare fairly: trends and seasons

One month on its own tells you little. Compare:

  • The last three months against the three before for a smoother trend
  • The same month last year if your business is seasonal: wedding season, Diwali, admissions, monsoon, exam results or summer holidays can all change search demand
  • Like with like: a 31-day month against a 28-day month, or a month with a long festival break, will move numbers on their own

New sites need patience. In the first few months impressions usually move before clicks, and clicks before enquiries. If you are at that stage, how long SEO takes sets realistic expectations.

Vanity metrics to question

Some numbers appear in nearly every report but rarely change a decision:

  • Long keyword ranking lists: ranking for hundreds of terms nobody searches, or that bring the wrong visitors, is not progress
  • Third-party authority scores: these are tool estimates, not something Google uses, and they move for reasons unrelated to your business
  • Total traffic without context: a blog post read by students abroad can inflate traffic without producing one enquiry
  • Backlink counts: a handful of relevant links from Indian business sites is worth more than a large number of low-quality ones
  • Bounce rate alone: a visitor who reads your phone number and calls may "bounce" and still be your best lead

None of these are useless, but they belong lower in the report, as explanation rather than headline. If a report leads with them and never mentions enquiries, ask why. Some patterns are also warning signs; see SEO red flags.

Work done and next steps

A good report also says what was actually done that month and what comes next. Look for:

  • Pages created or improved, with links so you can read them
  • Technical fixes, such as indexing errors, broken links or speed work
  • Local work: profile updates, photos, posts, review replies
  • What is planned for next month, and why those tasks were chosen

If the work section is vague ("on-page optimisation", "link building") with no specifics, ask for the list. You are paying for actions, and you should be able to see them. If you are comparing how agencies charge for this work, SEO pricing models in India explains the common structures.

A one-page monthly report template

For most small businesses, one page is enough:

  1. Headline: organic enquiries and sales this month, last month and same month last year
  2. Search Console: clicks, impressions, top five queries and top five pages, India only if that is your market
  3. Local: profile calls, direction requests, website clicks and new reviews
  4. Issues: indexing, speed or tracking problems found and fixed
  5. Work done: a short list with links
  6. Next month: two to five planned tasks and the reason for each

You can build this yourself in a shared spreadsheet or a free Looker Studio dashboard connected to GA4 and Search Console. The point is consistency: the same numbers, read the same way, every month.

Online stores should add a few lines of their own. For a WooCommerce or Shopify shop selling across India, note organic orders and revenue, which product and category pages earned them, and whether COD or prepaid orders dominate from search. If a category page gains impressions but few clicks, its title and description may need work. If product pages get clicks but no orders, the problem may be price, delivery details or page speed rather than SEO. Writing one short sentence of interpretation under each number, rather than leaving charts to speak for themselves, is what turns a report into a decision.

FAQs

What should an SEO report track each month?

Start with organic enquiries and sales, then Search Console clicks, impressions, top queries and top pages. Add Google Business Profile actions if you serve a local area, plus a list of work done and planned.

How often should I get an SEO report?

Monthly is enough for most small businesses. SEO changes slowly, so weekly reports mostly show noise. Look at three-month trends before making big decisions.

Are keyword rankings still worth tracking?

A short list of important searches is worth checking, ideally from your own area on a phone. A long list of rankings is less useful than clicks and enquiries from Search Console and GA4.

Why do Search Console and GA4 show different numbers?

They measure different things. Search Console counts clicks from Google search results, while GA4 counts sessions on your site, which depend on the tracking code loading and on cookie consent. Use each for its own job rather than expecting them to match.

Can I make my own SEO report for free?

Yes. GA4, Search Console and Google Business Profile are free, and a spreadsheet or Looker Studio dashboard can pull the main numbers together. Setting up tracking correctly is the part that usually needs help.
