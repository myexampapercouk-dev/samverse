Key takeaways
  • Most small business WordPress sites in India cost roughly ₹15,000–₹40,000 to build, plus yearly domain and hosting.
  • Unique layouts, custom design, features like payments or bookings, and content work drive the build price more than the number of pages.
  • Budget for yearly costs too (domain, hosting renewals, licences and maintenance), and send a clear brief to get an accurate fixed quote.

"How much will my website cost?" is the first question almost every client asks, and the honest answer is: it depends on what the website needs to do. A simple five-page business site and a WooCommerce store with hundreds of products are very different projects.

This guide breaks down every cost involved in a WordPress website in India, both the one-time build and the yearly running costs, so you can budget properly and compare quotes with confidence.

The short answer

For most small businesses in India, a professional WordPress website costs between ₹15,000 and ₹40,000, plus a few thousand rupees a year for domain and hosting. Online stores and larger sites cost more. Focus on value, not the lowest price: a fast, mobile-friendly website that brings in enquiries pays for itself quickly.

The rest of this guide explains where that money goes, what moves a project up or down the range, and what you'll keep paying every year.

Is WordPress free? WordPress pricing and plans explained

The WordPress software itself (from WordPress.org) is free and open source: there's no licence fee to use it, however big your site gets. What you pay for is everything around it:

  • A domain name (your web address), renewed every year
  • Hosting (the server your site lives on), usually paid yearly
  • Design and development, the time someone spends building the site
  • Optional premium themes and plugins, many with yearly licences
  • Ongoing maintenance, whether you do it yourself or pay someone

Don't confuse this with WordPress.com, a hosted service with its own paid plans. Most business sites built by developers in India use the free WordPress.org software on their own hosting. If these terms are new, see domain, hosting and SSL explained.

The one-time and yearly costs

Every website has two kinds of cost: what you pay once to build it, and what you pay every year to keep it running.

ItemTypical costHow often
Domain name (.com / .in)₹500 – ₹1,200Yearly
Hosting (shared / managed)₹2,500 – ₹15,000Yearly
Premium theme or Elementor Pro₹0 – ₹6,000Yearly (optional)
Design & development₹10,000 – ₹1,00,000+One-time
Maintenance & updates₹1,500 – ₹6,000Monthly (optional)

WordPress itself is free. Most of your budget goes into design and development, meaning the time a developer spends planning, building, testing and launching your site.

What the one-time cost covers

  • Planning the pages, structure and features with you
  • Design, either adapting a theme or building from a custom design
  • Building and testing every page on phones, tablets and desktops
  • Setting up forms, WhatsApp buttons, maps, payments or bookings
  • Basic SEO setup, speed optimisation and security hardening
  • Launch: connecting the domain, SSL, backups and a short handover

Ask what's included in each quote, because two quotes can cover very different amounts of this list.

What you pay every year

The yearly cost of a WordPress website is mostly your domain and hosting renewals, plus any premium licences and maintenance you choose. Many hosts include a free SSL certificate, and business email may be included or charged separately depending on the plan. Two things catch people out: hosting that was discounted in the first year renews at a higher price, and premium plugins stop getting updates if the licence lapses. Typical price ranges by type of website

These are typical freelance market ranges in India. Agencies usually charge more; very cheap offers often cut corners on speed, security or mobile design.

  • Landing page (1 page): ₹5,000 – ₹15,000. Ideal for ad campaigns or a single product or service.
  • Small business website (5–8 pages): ₹15,000 – ₹40,000. Home, about, services, gallery, contact, with a mobile-friendly design.
  • Larger business or B2B website: ₹35,000 – ₹80,000. Product catalogues, many service pages, brochures and quote forms.
  • WooCommerce online store: ₹30,000 – ₹90,000+. Products, payment gateway, shipping, order emails and coupons.
  • Custom features: booking systems, memberships, multilingual sites or custom integrations add to the cost based on complexity.

Examples: where different sites usually land

Ranges are easier to use with examples. These show where projects tend to fall within the ranges above; they aren't quotes:

  • A consultant or CA firm with five pages, a contact form and a WhatsApp button, using a good theme and their own text: towards the lower end of the small business range.
  • A clinic with a page per treatment, doctor profiles and online appointment requests: higher in the small business range, because booking needs setting up and testing.
  • A manufacturer with a product catalogue, downloadable brochures and quote forms: the larger business range; the number of templates and how much product data needs entering matter most.
  • A clothing store with sizes and colours, cash on delivery, shipping zones and GST invoices: the WooCommerce range, moving higher with many variations, custom features or a large product upload.
  • A single landing page for an ad campaign: the landing page range, depending on design work and tracking needs (see landing page cost factors).
  • A redesign of an existing site: similar to a new build of the same size; sometimes less if content is reused, sometimes more if many old URLs need redirecting.

What makes a website cost more?

1. Number of pages and templates

Each unique page layout takes design and build time. Twenty service pages sharing one template cost far less than twenty completely different designs.

2. Custom design vs a ready-made theme

Adapting a good theme is quicker and cheaper. A fully custom design built from Figma takes longer but gives you a unique look.

3. Features and integrations

Online payments, appointment booking, CRM integration, multiple languages and product filters all add development and testing time.

4. Content

If you provide finished text and good photos, the project moves faster. Content writing, photo editing or product uploads are usually charged separately.

5. Speed and SEO work

A properly optimised site with caching, compressed images, schema markup and clean structure takes more effort than a basic build, but it pays back in better rankings and more enquiries.

6. Who builds it

Freelancers, small studios and larger agencies have different overheads, processes and levels of support, and price accordingly. See freelancer vs agency for the trade-offs.

7. Deadline and support

A rushed deadline means rearranging other work, and some quotes include post-launch support or training while others don't. Both affect the price.

Hidden costs to watch for

  • Hosting renewals: the first year is often discounted, and renewal can cost 2–3× more.
  • Premium plugin licences: some features need paid plugins with yearly renewals.
  • Changes after launch: ask what is included and what is charged extra.
  • No maintenance: skipping updates is the most common reason WordPress sites get hacked, and cleanup costs far more than prevention.

For ongoing costs after launch, see website maintenance cost in India.

Why quotes vary so much

It's normal to receive quotes that are far apart for what sounds like the same website. Usually they aren't for the same thing: one includes content entry, speed work, SEO setup and a month of support; another is a theme installed with your logo. Very cheap websites often skip the work you don't see, such as backups, security, mobile testing and clean code, and those gaps cost more to fix later. See the real risks of very cheap websites.

How to get an accurate quote

To get a fixed, accurate quote from any developer, share:

  1. The pages you need (a simple list is fine)
  2. 2–3 websites you like the look of
  3. Features you need: forms, payments, booking, WhatsApp, blog
  4. Whether you have content and photos ready
  5. Your deadline and budget range

A clear brief gets you a clear price, and avoids surprises halfway through the project.

How to keep the cost down without cutting corners

  • Start with the pages you really need and add more later
  • Use a well-supported theme instead of a fully custom design if a unique look isn't essential
  • Have your text and photos ready before the project starts
  • Add advanced features in a second phase, once the site is bringing in enquiries
  • Choose hosting on its renewal price and support, not just the first-year offer
  • Avoid paying for premium plugins that duplicate features you already have

FAQs

How much does a WordPress domain cost in India?

A .com or .in domain usually costs about ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year, depending on the registrar and any first-year offer. Check the renewal price, and register the domain in your business's name so you always control it.

What do WordPress plans and pricing pages refer to?

Plans and pricing pages usually belong to WordPress.com, the hosted service. A business site built on the free WordPress.org software does not need those plans: you pay for a domain, hosting and the build instead, as in the tables above.

How much does a WordPress website cost in India?

It depends on the type of site. Most small business websites fall in the small business range shown above; landing pages cost less, and larger business sites and WooCommerce stores cost more. A clear brief gets you an exact figure.

What is the yearly cost of a WordPress website?

At minimum, your domain and hosting renewals every year. On top of that, budget for any premium theme or plugin licences and, if you want someone to handle updates and backups, a maintenance plan. The table above shows typical ranges for each.

Is WordPress free to use?

Yes, the WordPress.org software is free. You pay for the domain, hosting, the developer's time to build the site and any premium add-ons you choose.

Does WooCommerce cost extra?

The WooCommerce plugin is free, but a store takes more work to build than a brochure site: products, payments, shipping, taxes and order emails all need setting up and testing. Payment gateways charge transaction fees and some extensions have yearly licences, so check current terms with each provider.

Is maintenance really necessary?

It's optional, but someone needs to update WordPress, plugins and themes, take backups and check the site regularly. If you don't do it yourself, a maintenance plan usually costs far less than cleaning up a hacked or broken site.

Are freelancers cheaper than agencies?

Usually, because freelancers have lower overheads, while agencies can offer bigger teams and more formal processes. What matters most is relevant experience, a clear scope and support after launch.
