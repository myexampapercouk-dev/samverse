Website Microcopy That Converts: Buttons, Forms and Error Messages

How to write website microcopy that gets people to act: button labels, form labels and hints, error messages, confirmations and empty states, with examples.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

  2. Form labels and hints
  3. Error messages that help people fix the problem
  4. Confirmation messages: close the loop

Key takeaways
  • Button labels should start with a verb and say what the visitor gets, such as "Get my free quote" instead of "Submit".
  • Error messages should explain how to fix the problem next to the field, without clearing what the visitor has typed.
  • Confirmations and empty states should say what happens next or point somewhere useful, and every promise must be true.

Microcopy is the small text that guides people through your website: button labels, form labels and hints, error messages, confirmation messages and the note that appears when a search finds nothing. It's easy to ignore because each piece is only a few words, but these words sit exactly where a visitor decides whether to act. Here's how to write each type, with before-and-after examples you can adapt.

Button labels: say what happens next

A button should tell visitors what they get when they click. Generic labels like "Submit" or "Click here" make people guess, and guessing creates hesitation.

BeforeAfter
SubmitGet my free quote
Click hereSee the 3BHK floor plans
Learn moreSee what's included
Contact usBook a site visit
SendSend my enquiry
  • Start with a verb and name the outcome
  • Use the same label for the same action across the whole site
  • Keep one main button per section; secondary actions can be plain links
  • Make sure the label makes sense on its own, which also helps screen reader users; see website accessibility basics

Form labels and hints

Labels tell people what to enter; hints tell them how, or why you need it. Many form problems come from missing hints.

  • Keep labels visible. Placeholder text inside the box disappears as soon as someone starts typing, so don't use it as the only label.
  • Explain sensitive fields. A short line under the phone field, such as "We'll only use this to reply to your enquiry", answers the obvious worry. Only say it if it's true.
  • Show the format. "Pin code (6 digits)" or "Preferred date, for example 15 March".
  • Mark optional fields. "Company name (optional)" removes doubt faster than a row of asterisks.
BeforeAfter
Phone*Mobile number (for a WhatsApp reply or call back)
MessageWhat do you need help with? A sentence or two is enough.
BudgetRough budget (optional, helps us suggest the right option)

If a form still gets few submissions, check its length and layout too; see why your contact form isn't getting enquiries.

Error messages that help people fix the problem

A good error message says what went wrong and how to fix it, in plain words, right next to the field concerned. It never blames the visitor.

BeforeAfter
Invalid inputPlease enter a 10-digit mobile number
Error: field requiredPlease tell us your city so we can check we cover it
Payment failedYour payment didn't go through. Please try again, or choose another method such as UPI.
  • Show errors when someone leaves a field or presses the button, not while they are still typing
  • Keep everything they have already entered; never clear the whole form
  • Accept common formats instead of rejecting them, such as spaces in phone numbers or a +91 at the start
  • Don't rely on red colour alone; always include the text

Confirmation messages: close the loop

After someone sends a form or places an order, they want to know it worked and what happens next. A bare "Your message has been sent" leaves them wondering.

BeforeAfter
Thank you. Your message has been sent.Thanks, we've got your enquiry. We reply within one working day, usually on WhatsApp. If it's urgent, call us on [your number].
Order received.Your order is confirmed. We've emailed your receipt, and you'll get a tracking link when it ships.

Promise only a reply time you can keep. A dedicated page also lets you track conversions properly; see thank-you pages for website forms.

Empty states and "no results"

An empty state is what people see when there's nothing to show: an empty cart, a search with no matches, a filter that returns nothing. Left blank, it feels like a dead end. Use it to point somewhere useful.

BeforeAfter
No products found.Nothing matches "steel almirah". Try a shorter search or browse all storage furniture. Still can't find it? WhatsApp us a photo.
Your cart is empty.Your cart is empty. Have a look at this week's best-sellers.
No posts found.No articles here yet. Here are our most-read guides.

Reassurance right next to the decision

Short lines placed beside a button answer the doubt that stops people clicking:

  • "No spam. We'll only contact you about this enquiry."
  • "Free, no-obligation quote."
  • "Takes about a minute."
  • "Pay by UPI, card or net banking."
  • "Free cancellation up to 24 hours before."

Every one of these must be true. A reassurance you don't honour does more damage than saying nothing, and anything about payments, refunds or cancellations must match your actual policies.

Where to change microcopy in WordPress, and how to test it

  • Form text: button labels, field labels, hints, error and success messages are usually set per form in your form plugin
  • WooCommerce and theme text: labels such as "Add to cart" or "No results found" can often be changed in theme or builder settings, with a translation plugin, or with a small code snippet, without editing core files
  • Consistency: keep a simple list of your standard labels and messages so every new page uses the same wording

To see whether a change helps, track form submissions and button clicks before and after, or run an A/B test on a busy page. Once a month, fill in your own forms on a phone, and ask someone who has never used your site to try one while you watch without helping.
