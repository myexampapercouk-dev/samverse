Website Microcopy That Converts: Buttons, Form Labels and Errors, Empty States, Pricing Notes, Reassurance Near Buttons, 404 Pages and Writing for Indian Visitors

How to write website microcopy that helps people act: buttons, form labels, error messages, empty states, pricing notes, 404s and Indian-English choices.

Key takeaways
  • Write microcopy for the moment the visitor is in: deciding, filling in, making a mistake, waiting, finding nothing or paying.
  • Every reassurance near a button must be literally true, and anything about price, refunds or reply times must match what actually happens.
  • For Indian audiences, plain Indian English usually works best, with familiar terms such as UPI, pin code and WhatsApp, and Hinglish only where it matches how you already talk to customers.

Microcopy is the short text that sits where people make decisions: button labels, form labels and hints, error messages, confirmation messages, empty search results, notes next to prices, and the 404 page. Good microcopy tells the visitor what will happen next, removes the specific doubt that stops them, and never promises anything untrue. The quickest way to write it is to think about the moment the visitor is in, rather than the type of element.

On a landing page, where one button carries the whole page, these few words matter more than anywhere else on a site. The examples below are invented to show the difference in wording; they are not taken from any client site, and they come with no promised uplift. Test changes on your own pages.

Moment 1: Deciding whether to click

A button label should name what the visitor gets, not the mechanical action. Start with a verb and finish with the outcome.

Interior design studioSubmitGet my free design consultation
Coaching instituteRegisterBook a free demo class
B2B manufacturerContactRequest a quote with drawings
ClinicClick hereBook an appointment
Online storeProceedContinue to payment

Reassurance right beside the button

The line under or beside a button answers the doubt that stops the click. Pick the one doubt that matters most on that page:

  • Cost worry: "Free, no obligation to book."
  • Spam worry: "We will only call or WhatsApp you about this enquiry."
  • Effort worry: "Five short questions."
  • Payment worry: "Pay by UPI, card or net banking."
  • Commitment worry: "Cancel free up to 24 hours before your slot."

Each must be literally true. If you sometimes follow up by email, do not say you will only call. If the cancellation window differs by service, say so or leave it out.

Moment 2: Filling in a form

Labels say what to enter. Hints say how, or why you are asking. Most form friction comes from a missing hint, not a missing field.

  • Keep labels outside the box. Placeholder text vanishes as soon as typing starts, which leaves people unsure what a field was for.
  • Mark the optional fields rather than starring the required ones; "Company (optional)" is clearer than a row of asterisks.
  • Explain sensitive fields in one line. Next to a phone field: "For a call back about your quote." Next to budget: "A rough range helps us suggest the right option."
  • Show the format. "Pin code (6 digits)", "Date of event, for example 14 February".

Here is one form, before and after:

Before: Name* / Email* / Phone* / Message* / Submit
After: Your name / Mobile number (we will reply on WhatsApp or call) / Email (optional) / What do you need help with? A sentence is enough. / Send my enquiry

If a form still gets few submissions, the problem may be its length or placement rather than its words; why your contact form is not getting enquiries covers those checks.

Moment 3: Getting something wrong

An error message should explain the problem and the remedy, placed beside the field concerned, in a calm tone. It should never sound like the visitor is at fault.

BeforeAfter
Invalid inputPlease enter a 10-digit mobile number
Required fieldPlease add your pin code so we can check delivery
File too largeThis file is over 10 MB. Try a PDF, or send it to us on WhatsApp
Transaction failedThe payment did not go through and you have not been charged by us. Try again or choose another method

The behaviour around the message matters as much as the words:

  • Validate when the visitor leaves a field or presses the button, not on every keystroke
  • Keep everything already entered when an error appears
  • Accept common formats: spaces in phone numbers, a leading +91 or 0, pin codes with a space
  • Show the error in text as well as colour, so it works for people with colour blindness; see website accessibility basics

One caution on the payment example above: only say "you have not been charged" if your gateway setup guarantees it. If a failed payment can still leave money pending, say what happens instead, such as how and when it is returned.

Moment 4: Waiting and confirming

After a click, the visitor wants to know it worked and what comes next. Silence breeds duplicate submissions and anxious calls.

  • While processing: change the button to "Sending..." and disable it, so nobody clicks twice
  • On success: say what was received, who will reply, how and roughly when, and what to do if it is urgent
  • After a purchase: confirm the order, where the receipt went and when the next update will arrive
Before: Thank you for your submission.
After: Got it. We have your enquiry about a kitchen for a 2BHK flat. We reply on WhatsApp within one working day. Need us sooner? Call the number at the top of this page.

Promise only a reply time you meet on a busy week. A separate thank-you page also lets you track conversions cleanly; see thank-you pages for website forms.

Moment 5: Finding nothing

Empty states (an empty cart, a search with no results, a filter that matches nothing) are dead ends unless the text points somewhere.

  • Search: "Nothing found for 'teak sofa'. Try a shorter word, or browse all sofas. Can't find it? Send us a photo on WhatsApp."
  • Filters: "No products match all of these filters. Remove one to see more."
  • Cart: "Your cart is empty. Here is what customers in your city ordered most this month." (Only if you actually show that list.)

The 404 page

A 404 means a link is broken or a page has moved. Do not blame the visitor or use a joke that hides the way out. Say plainly that the page is not here, offer a search box, and link to the most useful pages. Helpful 404 pages has more examples.

Before: 404 Error. Page not found.
After: This page has moved or no longer exists. Search below, or go to our services, prices or contact page.

Moment 6: Looking at a price

Notes beside prices prevent the surprise that kills a checkout or a call:

  • "Price includes GST" or "plus GST", whichever is true
  • "Starting from" only when the starting price is genuinely available to most buyers
  • "Delivery charges shown at checkout before you pay"
  • "Installation included within city limits"
  • "Final quote after a free site visit"

On product pages, these notes belong next to the price and the buy button, not at the bottom of the description; WooCommerce product page optimisation covers the layout.

Writing for Indian visitors

Most Indian business sites are written in English, but which English matters.

  • Use the words your customers use: "mobile number" rather than "cell", "pin code" rather than "postal code", "flat" or "BHK" where it fits, "UPI" without explanation
  • Prefer short, plain sentences to formal phrasing like "Kindly do the needful"; it reads more clearly to everyone, including visitors whose first language is not English
  • Hinglish works in some places: a playful brand talking to young customers on Instagram might use "Order karo" on a button, but a clinic or a manufacturer usually should not. Match the tone you already use on calls and WhatsApp
  • If you run Hindi or regional language pages, translate the microcopy too: buttons, errors and confirmations are often left in English by mistake; see multilingual WordPress websites
  • Number formats: write ₹1,50,000 or ₹1.5 lakh consistently, the way your customers read prices

A quick microcopy audit

  1. List every button on your top five pages and rewrite any that do not name an outcome
  2. Fill in every form on a phone, making deliberate mistakes, and note each unclear message
  3. Search your site for something it does not have, and read the result
  4. Visit a made-up URL to see your 404 page
  5. Check every reassurance line against your actual policies
  6. Write the agreed labels and messages into a short list, so new pages use the same words

To see whether a change helped, track button clicks and form submissions as events before and after, or run an A/B test on a page with enough traffic. Watching one person use a form without helping them is often the most revealing test of all.

FAQs

What is website microcopy?

The short functional text on a site: button labels, form labels and hints, error and success messages, empty states, notes beside prices and the 404 page.

Does button text really affect conversions?

It can, because a clear label removes hesitation at the point of action. The effect varies by site, so test changes on your own pages rather than relying on general claims.

Should my website use Hinglish?

Only if it matches how your brand already speaks to customers. For professional services, healthcare and B2B, plain Indian English is usually the safer choice.

Where do I change form and error messages in WordPress?

Most form plugins let you edit labels, hints, errors and success messages per form. Theme, WooCommerce and builder text can often be changed in settings or with a translation tool, without editing core files.

How long should a reassurance line be?

One short line, addressing one doubt. If it needs a paragraph, it belongs in the FAQs or terms, linked from the line.
