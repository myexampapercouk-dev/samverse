Websites for Wedding and Event Decorators: Portfolios, Packages and Bookings

A website for wedding decorators in India should let a couple see your best work by function, understand roughly how your packages work and check whether you are free on their date, all from a phone in a few minutes. Instagram shows your style; the website closes the booking.

This guide covers the pages, portfolio structure, enquiry flow, speed and SEO that a wedding or event decorator needs, with notes on what to avoid.

Why a website still matters when Instagram works

Most decorators get discovered on Instagram or through venue and planner referrals. But when a family is close to deciding, they want a place that answers practical questions: which cities you work in, whether you handle multi-day functions, what is included, how to reach you and whether you are available. An Instagram grid does not answer those well, and it does not show up when someone searches "wedding decorators in Pune" or "haldi decoration near me".

A website also works for planners and venues who refer you, because they can share one link that shows your range and contact details professionally.

Build a portfolio couples can browse by function

Indian weddings are a series of functions, and families often book decor for some but not all. Organise your gallery to match:

  • By function: haldi, mehendi, sangeet, wedding mandap, reception, engagement, baby shower if you do them.
  • By style: floral, minimal, royal, pastel, traditional, contemporary.
  • By scale: intimate home functions versus large venue events.

Each project page should have a short story: the couple's brief, the venue, the colour palette, the flowers or props used and a few photos from different angles. Captions like "Pastel floral mandap at a lawn venue in Pune" help Google understand the image and help couples imagine their own event.

Always credit the photographer where you use their images and get permission from couples before publishing photos with recognisable guests. If you work closely with photographers, the guide for photographer websites shows how they present work, which can help you coordinate.

Packages and how pricing works

Couples want an idea of budget before they call, but decor costs vary hugely with flowers, scale, venue and season. You do not have to publish prices, but explain what affects cost: real versus artificial flowers, stage size, number of functions, lighting, transport and the time needed for setup and dismantling. If you do have entry packages, such as a home haldi setup, you can show starting points with clear inclusions.

The article on showing prices on your website explains when published prices help and when they scare off the right customers.

Make "check your date" the main action

Availability is the first filter. A short form that asks the date, venue, city, functions and rough guest count tells you instantly whether the enquiry is a fit. Keep it to five or six fields. Add a WhatsApp button for couples who prefer to send a reference image and ask quickly; see adding WhatsApp to a business website.

Reply fast. Families often contact several decorators at once, and the first clear response with a few relevant photos often wins. Set up instant notifications for every enquiry, and keep a saved message with your process and next steps.

If you get many enquiries outside your budget range, a multi-step form that first asks the functions and budget band can sort them politely. The guide to multi-step forms explains the approach.

Keep a heavy gallery fast

Decor sites are photo-heavy, and slow galleries lose mobile visitors. A few practical steps:

  1. Resize photos before uploading; a full camera file is far larger than any screen needs.
  2. Use modern formats such as WebP and compress images. The image compression plugins comparison covers good options for WordPress.
  3. Lazy load images below the first screen, so the page appears quickly.
  4. Avoid autoplay background videos on the home page; link to a reel instead.
  5. Use a lightweight gallery rather than a heavy slider plugin.

Local SEO and partnerships

Couples search with a city and often a function: "mandap decoration Pune", "haldi decor Mumbai". Mention the cities and venues you genuinely work at on the relevant project pages, keep your Google Business Profile updated with recent event photos, and ask happy families for reviews after the wedding.

Venues, planners and photographers are your biggest referral sources. A "Venues we have worked at" page and links to partner sites (where they agree) help with both trust and visibility. The guides for wedding planners and wedding venues and banquet halls show what your partners need from their own sites.

Explain your process from first call to dismantling

Families who have never booked a decorator before do not know what to expect, and a clear process page reduces back-and-forth on the phone. Describe each stage in a sentence or two:

  1. First conversation: date, venue, functions, guest count and the look the couple has in mind.
  2. Mood board and proposal: reference images, colour palette and a written list of what is included.
  3. Venue visit: checking stage size, entry points, power supply and the venue's rules on timings, fire safety and fixing items to walls.
  4. Confirmation and advance: how the booking is confirmed and what the payment stages are.
  5. Setup day: how early your team arrives, who the family contacts on the day and how last-minute changes are handled.
  6. Dismantling: when it happens and whether any items, such as flowers, can be kept by the family.

Only describe steps you actually follow. A process page that matches reality builds trust; one that promises more than you deliver creates disputes.

Destination and multi-city weddings

If you travel for destination weddings in places such as Udaipur, Jaipur, Goa or Kerala, give this its own page. Explain how travel, accommodation for your team and local sourcing of flowers are handled, and show any destination events you have genuinely done. Couples planning a destination wedding search well in advance and compare carefully, so a detailed page is worth the effort, and it gives planners in other cities a reason to recommend you. If you only work in your own city, say so clearly to save everyone time.

Common mistakes

  • Uploading hundreds of photos without any order or captions.
  • Using stock decor images that are not your work.
  • Hiding contact details behind a long form or a single email address.
  • No mention of cities served, which brings enquiries you cannot take.
  • Writing every page in vague phrases such as "dream wedding decor" instead of naming the functions, styles and cities you actually cover.
  • Forgetting a privacy note on the form, even though you collect names, phone numbers and wedding dates; say briefly how you use them.
  • Letting the site go stale while Instagram is updated weekly; add new projects every season.

FAQs

What should a website for wedding decorators in India include?

A portfolio organised by function and style, project pages with captions, a page explaining packages or what drives cost, the cities and venues you serve, your process, and a short date-check form with WhatsApp.

Should I put my Instagram feed on my website?

A small feed or link is fine, but do not rely on it. Embedded feeds can slow the page and are not indexed well. Add your best work as proper project pages.

How do I stop my decor gallery from loading slowly?

Resize and compress images, use WebP, lazy load images below the first screen and avoid heavy sliders or autoplay videos.

Can couples book and pay an advance on the website?

This can be set up with a booking form and a payment link or gateway such as Razorpay, though most decorators confirm details on a call before taking an advance.

Do I need a separate page for each function?

It helps. Couples search for haldi, mehendi or mandap decor separately, and a dedicated page with relevant photos answers that search better than one mixed gallery.
