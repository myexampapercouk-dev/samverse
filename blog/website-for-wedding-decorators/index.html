Websites for Wedding and Event Decorators: Portfolios, Packages and Bookings

How wedding and event decorators can use a website to show mandap, stage, floral and lighting work, explain packages and booking terms, and win more enquiries.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Organise your portfolio by function, décor element and venue, and publish client and venue photos only with permission.
  • Describe packages by scope and by what changes the quote, and explain in plain language how dates are held and advances work.
  • Give planners and venues their own page, and use a short enquiry form plus WhatsApp to capture date enquiries.

Couples and families rarely hire a decorator on words alone. They want to see mandaps, stages, floral work and lighting that look like what they have in mind, at a venue like theirs, and then they want to know whether you're free on their date. A good decorator website answers those questions quickly and turns a scroll through your work into an enquiry. If you plan whole events rather than decorate them, see websites for event and wedding planners.

What clients look for before they contact you

Most people comparing decorators are trying to answer a short list of questions:

  • Have you done work in the style and scale they want, whether a grand mandap, a floral haldi set-up or a minimal reception stage?
  • Do you work in their city, and have you worked at their venue or one like it?
  • What does a package actually include, and what costs extra?
  • Are you available on their date, and how do they lock it in?
  • Can they trust you to set up on time and coordinate with the venue and planner?

Every section of your website should help answer one of these.

A portfolio organised by event type and venue

A single gallery of hundreds of photos makes visitors work too hard. Organise your work the way clients think about their events:

  • By function: mandap, haldi, mehendi, sangeet, reception, engagement, puja and housewarming
  • By element: mandaps, stage backdrops, entrances and pathways, floral installations, ceiling work, table centrepieces and lighting
  • By event type: weddings, birthdays and baby showers, corporate events and product launches
  • By venue type: banquet halls, lawns, hotels, farmhouses and functions at home

Treat your best events as short project stories: the function, the theme, the main elements, and photos of the set-up in both daylight and evening lighting, since lighting changes how décor looks. Credit the photographer whenever you use their images.

Ask clients for permission before publishing photos from their wedding, and favour shots where the décor is the focus rather than the guests. Check with venues before naming them on your site. Pages about decorating at a particular venue can help with venue-name searches, but only create them where you have real work and photos there; otherwise they read as thin, copy-paste pages.

Themes and packages described by scope

Many decorators avoid putting packages online because every event is different. You don't need to publish a rate card, but describing what each package covers helps clients shortlist you and makes the first call more productive. Whether to show starting prices is a business decision; see should you show prices on your website.

Package elementWhat to describe
Mandap or stageStructure, size options, fabric draping, seating for the couple and family
FloralsFresh, artificial or a mix, and which areas are covered
LightingFairy lights, uplighting, chandeliers and focus lights for photography
Entrance and pathwaysGates, arches, welcome boards and aisle décor
Add-onsPhoto booths, name boards, car décor, ceiling work and effects the venue allows

Also explain what changes the quote: venue size and ceiling height, fresh versus artificial flowers, the number of functions, set-up time, travel and any venue rules. Theme pages such as pastel floral, traditional South Indian, royal or minimal give clients the words to describe what they want.

Site visits, mood boards and your design process

Clients feel more confident when they can see how a booking turns into a finished set-up. Lay out your process in plain steps:

  1. Enquiry and call: date, venue, functions, guest count and the look they're after
  2. Site visit: measurements, power points, set-up and teardown timings, and the venue's rules on fixing, open flames or special effects
  3. Mood board: colour palette, reference images, and sketches or 3D views if you offer them
  4. Revisions and final quote: every agreed element listed in writing
  5. Set-up day: who from your team is on site and how you coordinate with the planner and venue

Invite clients to share inspiration photos, outfit colours and the venue name in the enquiry form, so you're prepared for the first call.

Booking dates and advance terms, explained simply

Popular wedding dates are in demand, so many clients want to know how to secure their date before every detail is final. Explain your terms in general language on a booking or FAQ page:

  • How a date is held, for example only once the quote is accepted and an advance is paid
  • When the balance is due and which payment methods you accept, such as UPI or bank transfer
  • What happens if the date changes, the event is postponed or the scope grows
  • Your cancellation terms, written clearly rather than buried in small print

Keep the full details in your written quotation or agreement, and have your terms reviewed by a lawyer or your CA rather than copying them from another business. A "check availability" form asking for the date and city gives clients a clear first step.

Working with planners and venues

A lot of décor work comes through wedding planners and venues rather than directly from couples. Give these partners a page of their own that covers:

  • The types and scale of events you handle, and how many you can take on in one day
  • Your team, inventory and transport, so they know you can deliver on time
  • How you coordinate on the day: a single point of contact, set-up windows and teardown
  • A downloadable portfolio PDF they can share with their own clients

Mention venues you regularly work at only with their agreement. Many venues keep a list of decorators they recommend, so it helps to understand what they look for; see websites for wedding venues and banquet halls.

Enquiries, local search and speed

Keep the enquiry form short: event type, date, city or venue, functions, approximate guest count and an optional budget range, with a way to attach inspiration images. Add a WhatsApp button, because many families prefer to send photos and voice notes there, and reply quickly during the wedding season.

  • Target searches like "wedding decorator in {city}", "mandap decoration {city}" and "haldi decoration ideas"
  • Keep your Google Business Profile updated with recent event photos, and ask happy clients for reviews
  • Reuse your best Instagram reels and photos on the website, where clients can see them organised by function
  • Compress images and use gallery thumbnails so photo-heavy pages stay fast on mobile; see image optimization for WordPress
