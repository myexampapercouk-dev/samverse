Websites for Colleges and Universities: Programme Pages, Admission Funnels, Approvals, Placements and Many Audiences

Plan a college or university website: programme pages, an admissions funnel, approvals stated accurately, verifiable placement data and pages for each audience.

Key takeaways
  • Give every programme its own page with eligibility, duration, curriculum, fees or fee structure, careers and an enquiry button, because programme pages match what students actually search for.
  • Build admissions as a funnel, from programme page to enquiry, counsellor follow-up, campus visit, application and fee payment, with dates staff can update in one place.
  • State approvals, affiliations, accreditations and placement figures only exactly as held and verifiable, and keep mandatory disclosure, anti-ragging and grievance pages current.

A college or university website serves more audiences than almost any other: prospective students and their parents, current students, faculty and job applicants, alumni, recruiters and the regulators and accreditation teams who check what you publish. Admissions should still drive the structure. That means a page for every programme, an admissions funnel that moves enquiries quickly to a counsellor, approvals and accreditations stated exactly as held, placement data only where you can verify it, and a content system that lets each department keep its own pages current.

My websites for schools, colleges and coaching institutes cover the pieces that matter at admission time: course pages, admission enquiry forms with WhatsApp follow-up, results, faculty profiles, a notice board staff can update, galleries, a downloadable prospectus, optional online fee payment and local SEO. This guide covers what changes for degree colleges, autonomous institutions and universities. For schools and junior colleges, see what parents and students look for in school websites.

Put admissions and programmes first in the main menu, and give the other audiences a clear secondary menu or quick links, so a prospective student never has to dig past circulars to find the BBA page.

Programme pages: the most important pages on the site

Students search for programmes, not institutions: "BBA college in Pune", "BSc data science colleges in Nashik", "MBA with hostel near me". Each programme, and each specialisation that has its own intake, deserves its own page:

  • A plain summary of who the programme is for and what it leads to
  • Eligibility, duration, intake and mode (full-time, part-time, online)
  • The affiliating university or awarding body, stated exactly
  • Curriculum by semester or year, with electives, projects and internships
  • Fees, or a fee structure that explains what is included, scholarships and instalments
  • Careers and further study routes, without promising outcomes
  • Faculty who teach it, labs and facilities used
  • Admission steps, key dates and an enquiry or "Download brochure" button
  • A short FAQ answering the questions counsellors hear every day

Keep programme pages at stable addresses, such as /programmes/bba/, and update them each year rather than creating new pages, so they keep their search history.

An admissions funnel, not just a form

Admissions is a sequence, and the website should support every step:

  1. Discovery: a programme page found on Google, social media or an ad
  2. Enquiry: a short form or WhatsApp message, or a brochure download
  3. Counselling: a call or chat from a counsellor, ideally the same day
  4. Campus visit: a visit booking form, or a virtual tour for families further away
  5. Application: an online form or a link to your admission portal
  6. Payment and documents: application or admission fees through a payment gateway, with receipts emailed automatically

The enquiry form should ask only what the counsellor needs: name, phone, programme of interest, current qualification or year of passing, city and a "how did you hear about us?" question. A two-step form can collect more detail without putting people off; see multi-step forms for lead qualification. Send enquiries to the admissions team and a CRM or Google Sheet, so nothing is lost in one person's inbox. Keep important dates, such as application deadlines, entrance tests and counselling rounds, in one place staff can update, and show them on every relevant page. Admission season campaigns deserve their own landing pages so ad spend can be tracked by programme.

Approvals, affiliations and accreditations: state them exactly

Students and parents check approvals carefully, and regulators take misleading claims seriously. Keep the wording precise:

  • Name the affiliating university, and whether you are an affiliated, autonomous or other type of institution, exactly as your status stands
  • Mention a UGC recognition, AICTE approval, NAAC grade, NBA accreditation or any other status only if your institution or that specific programme genuinely holds it, with the validity period
  • Never imply an approval through wording such as "recognised" or through a regulator's logo when the status is different or has lapsed; check whether you are allowed to use a regulator's logo at all
  • Link to the regulator's or university's own website or listing, so visitors can verify the status themselves
  • Quote rankings only with the ranking body, the year and the category

Regulators and affiliating universities may also require certain information on your website, such as mandatory disclosures, committee details, grievance redressal, anti-ragging measures and an internal complaints committee. Requirements differ by regulator and type of institution and change over time, so check the current rules and keep these pages updated with dates.

Placements and outcomes: only what you can verify

Placement claims are where college websites most often overreach. Publish only what you can support with records:

  • Placement data by programme and batch year, with how the figures were calculated
  • Recruiter names and logos only with permission, and only companies that actually recruited your students
  • Student placement stories with written consent, naming the programme and year
  • No "100% placement" or "highest package" headlines without the full context behind them

Faculty, departments and research

Each department needs its own page with its programmes, faculty, labs, research areas and achievements. Faculty profiles should list qualifications, specialisations, publications and responsibilities, with each person approving their own profile. Research centres, journals and conferences give the site depth that search engines and serious applicants both value.

Campus life, hostel and location

  • Real photos and short videos of classrooms, labs, library, hostels, sports and events, compressed so they load on mobile data
  • Hostel details: room types, facilities, mess, rules, safety and how to apply
  • Transport routes, the nearest railway station and bus stand, and directions
  • Clubs, festivals, NSS or NCC units and student committees
  • Student photos only with consent, and a quick way to remove any on request

Notices and content that stays current

An outdated website does real damage: last year's fees, a deadline that has passed, a circular nobody removed. Large institutions solve this with clear ownership. Give each department or office its own editor login with access only to its pages, and an administrator who approves major changes; WordPress user roles explained shows how. Notices get categories, such as examinations, admissions and events, with old notices moving to an archive automatically. When notices are PDFs, type the key points on the page too, and use real text in prospectus PDFs so they can be read and found; see SEO for PDFs and brochures.

Regional languages

Many parents read Marathi, Hindi or another regional language more comfortably than English. Admissions, fees, contact and key notices are usually enough to translate first, each on its own pages. Our Temples, a directory I built, shows temple names and sections such as history and festivals in Hindi and Telugu as well as English, which is the same approach a college can use.

Speed, accessibility and search

  • Avoid full-screen homepage sliders and autoplay videos that slow the site on budget phones
  • Make forms, menus and PDFs usable with screen readers and keyboards; see website accessibility basics
  • Use programme and city names naturally in page titles and headings
  • Complete a Google Business Profile for each campus, with real photos and accurate hours
  • Add CollegeOrUniversity structured data for the institution, and FAQ sections on programme and admission pages

Common mistakes on college websites

  • One "Courses" page listing programme names with no detail
  • Approval logos and wording that overstate the institution's actual status
  • Placement headlines with no batch, programme or basis
  • Admission dates and fees from last year still live
  • Enquiries going to an inbox nobody checks during the admission rush
  • Important information only in scanned PDFs

FAQs

What pages should a college website have?

At minimum: programme pages, admissions, fees or fee structure, approvals and affiliation, faculty and departments, placements, campus life and hostel, notices, mandatory disclosures and contact. Larger institutions add research, alumni and careers pages.

How should we show approvals and accreditations?

State each one exactly as held, with the body's name, the scope and the validity period, and link to the body's own listing so visitors can check it. Never imply a status you do not have.

Can students apply and pay fees on the website?

Yes. Enquiry and application forms can feed your admissions team, and a payment gateway such as Razorpay can collect application or admission fees with automatic receipts.

Can each department update its own pages?

Yes. WordPress user roles can give each department an editor login limited to its own pages and notices, with an administrator overseeing the rest.

Should we publish placement statistics?

Only if you can support them with records. Show them by programme and batch, explain how they were calculated, and use recruiter names only with permission.
