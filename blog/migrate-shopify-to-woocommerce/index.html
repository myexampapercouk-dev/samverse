How to Move From Shopify to WooCommerce, Step by Step: Products, Customers, Orders, Redirects, Payments and Cutover

Key takeaways
  • Export everything from Shopify first, rebuild the store on WooCommerce staging, and check products, variants, customers and orders in samples before going further.
  • Map every Shopify URL, especially /products/ and /collections/, to its WooCommerce equivalent with 301 redirects, or match the old URL bases in WooCommerce permalinks.
  • Set up and test an Indian payment gateway, shipping, COD and GST invoices on staging, then switch in a quiet period and keep Shopify until everything is verified.

To move from Shopify to WooCommerce, export your products, customers and orders from Shopify, build the new store on WooCommerce in a staging copy, import and check the data, and set up payments, shipping and taxes for India. Then map every old Shopify URL, especially /products/ and /collections/ addresses, to its new page with 301 redirects, test real orders end to end, and switch the domain during a quiet sales period, keeping Shopify running until you have verified everything.

Moving a store from Shopify is something I handle as part of WooCommerce development: products, categories and images are moved across, along with customers and past orders where Shopify's exports allow, and old URLs are redirected to protect your search visibility. The domain switch and redirects follow my website migration process, so the old store stays live until the new one is tested.

Why stores move, and what doesn't come with you

Stores usually move to cut monthly subscription and app costs, to own their hosting and data, to build features such as dealer pricing or complex product options, or to run a proper content and SEO strategy on WordPress. What doesn't move is the design: Shopify themes and apps don't run on WordPress, so the store is rebuilt. It can be matched closely, but most owners use the rebuild to fix layouts that weren't working.

Step 1: Export and audit everything on Shopify

Before touching WooCommerce, take a full copy of what you have, because closing a Shopify store later limits your access to it.

  • Products: the product CSV export from the Products screen, which includes handles, descriptions, variants, SKUs, prices, inventory, image URLs and SEO titles and descriptions.
  • Customers: the customer CSV export, with names, emails, addresses and marketing consent.
  • Orders: the order export, for records and accounting.
  • Pages, blog posts and policies: copy them or export them with a migration tool.
  • Reviews, gift cards and discount codes: these usually live in apps or settings, so check what each app can export.
  • Every URL: save the sitemap at https://your-store.com/sitemap.xml and export your top pages from Search Console. This list becomes your redirect map.

Also list the apps you rely on, such as reviews, WhatsApp, filters, pin code checks or invoices, and find the WooCommerce plugin or setting that replaces each one.

What transfers, and how

DataHow it movesWatch out for
Products and variantsWooCommerce's product CSV importer, after reshaping the Shopify file, or a migration toolShopify puts each variant and extra image on its own row; options become WooCommerce attributes and variations
PricesSame importWhere a compare-at price is set, it becomes the regular price and Shopify's price becomes the sale price
ImagesThe importer downloads them from Shopify's image URLsDo this before closing Shopify, then compress them for speed
CategoriesCollections become product categories; tags can become tagsSmart collections built on rules need rebuilding as categories
CustomersA customer import plugin or migration toolPasswords can't be moved, so customers set a new one
OrdersA migration tool or order import pluginThe core importer handles products only; check order numbers don't clash with new orders
ReviewsExport from the reviews app, import with a review pluginKeep verified-buyer status where possible
SEO titles and descriptionsMapped into your SEO plugin's fields during importCheck a sample of products afterwards

Paid migration tools can move products, customers and orders in one pass, and suit large catalogues. Whichever route you use, check samples by hand: a simple product, a product with several variants, a product on sale, a customer with several addresses and an old order. Planning attributes before the import keeps filters clean; see WooCommerce product variations.

Step 2: Build the WooCommerce store on staging

  1. Set up WordPress and WooCommerce on good hosting, on a staging address that is blocked from search engines.
  2. Choose a fast, WooCommerce-ready theme and build the shop, product, cart and checkout pages for phones first.
  3. Decide your permalinks before importing anything (see the URL section below).
  4. Import products, then customers and orders, and check samples.
  5. Recreate pages, policies, blog posts, menus, coupons and email templates.
  6. Install only the plugins that replace apps you actually used.

Step 3: Map Shopify URLs and set up 301 redirects

This is the step that protects your rankings and links. Shopify uses fixed URL patterns, and WooCommerce's defaults differ:

Shopify URLWooCommerce defaultWhat to do
/products/blue-kurta/product/blue-kurta/Set the product base to /products/ so URLs match, or redirect each one
/collections/kurtas/product-category/kurtas/Set the category base to "collections", or redirect each one
/collections/kurtas/products/blue-kurtaNo equivalentOne pattern redirect to the product URL
/collections/all/shop/Redirect to the shop page
/pages/about-us/about-us/Redirect each page
/blogs/news/post-nameDepends on your permalink settingRedirect each post, or match the structure
/policies/refund-policyYour returns pageRedirect to the matching policy page

Under Settings > Permalinks, WooCommerce lets you set a custom product base and a product category base. Using /products/ and "collections" keeps most URLs the same apart from the trailing slash, which WordPress handles itself, so far fewer redirects are needed. Product handles must match the old Shopify handles exactly for this to work.

For the nested collection URLs, a pattern rule in a redirect plugin covers every product at once. In the Redirection plugin, for example, tick the regex option and use:

Source:  ^/collections/[^/]+/products/([^/?]+)
Target:  https://example.com/products/$1/

Redirect everything else to its closest match, never all to the homepage, and use permanent 301 redirects; 301 vs 302 redirects explains why and how to avoid chains. Test the full list of old URLs once the new store is live.

Step 4: Payments, shipping and GST for India

  • Payment gateway. Install your gateway's WooCommerce plugin, such as Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree or PhonePe. If you already use one with Shopify, ask whether the account can be used on the new website or needs the new site added for approval, and set up webhooks for the new address. Payment gateways in India compared lists what to check.
  • Test mode first. Run successful, failed and cancelled payments, and check that paid orders are marked paid even if the shopper closes the browser.
  • Shipping and COD. Recreate zones and rates, connect your courier or aggregator, and set COD rules by order value or pin code; see WooCommerce shipping setup for India.
  • GST. Set tax classes for each GST rate and an invoice plugin with your GSTIN, HSN codes and invoice numbering. Continue invoice numbering in the way your CA advises; WooCommerce GST setup covers the steps.

Step 5: Test before you switch

  • Place prepaid and COD test orders on a phone, and check order emails, invoices and stock levels.
  • Log in as an imported customer after resetting the password, and check their past orders show.
  • Check every product type: simple, variable, out of stock and on sale.
  • Compare prices, SKUs and stock for your best sellers against Shopify.
  • Check titles, descriptions and Product schema on key pages.
  • Test speed on mobile data, and check the redirect list on staging if your setup allows it.

The WooCommerce store launch checklist has the full pre-launch list.

Step 6: Cutover day

  1. Pick a quiet sales period and tell your team. Pause product edits on Shopify.
  2. Run a final sync of new orders, customers and stock changes since the first import.
  3. Point the domain's DNS to the new host, keeping email records unchanged, and activate SSL.
  4. Switch the gateway to live keys and place a real low-value order.
  5. Turn on redirects, remove the staging noindex setting, and submit the new sitemap in Search Console.
  6. Email customers that the store has moved and how to set a new password.

Keep the Shopify store open, password-protected, for a week or two in case anything was missed, and download any remaining exports before you close it.

After the move

For the first weeks, check Search Console for 404 errors and fix them with redirects, compare traffic and orders for your top products with the weeks before the move, and watch the gateway dashboard for failed payments. Expect some ranking movement while Google recrawls the store. The WooCommerce SEO guide covers what to improve once things settle.

FAQs

Can I migrate from Shopify to WooCommerce without losing SEO?

You can protect most of it by keeping URLs where possible, redirecting every changed URL to its closest match with a 301, carrying over titles and descriptions, and monitoring Search Console after the switch. Some movement while Google recrawls is normal.

Will my customers' passwords transfer to WooCommerce?

No. Passwords are stored in a form that can't be moved between platforms, so customer accounts come across without them and customers set a new password with a reset link.

Can I move my old Shopify orders to WooCommerce?

Yes, with a migration tool or order import plugin. WooCommerce's built-in importer handles products only, so orders and customers need one of those.

Can I keep the same /products/ URLs?

Mostly. Set the product base to /products/ and the category base to "collections" in Settings > Permalinks, and keep the same handles. Nested collection URLs and pages still need redirects.

Should I cancel Shopify straight after the switch?

No. Keep it for a week or two, password-protected, so you can check anything missed, and export everything you need before closing it.
