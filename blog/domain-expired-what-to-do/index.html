Domain Name Expired? How to Get It Back, What Breaks in the Meantime and How to Stop It Happening Again

Domain name expired? How to get it back in the grace or redemption period, what breaks for your website and email, and how to stop it happening again.

Key takeaways
  • An expired domain is rarely lost straight away: most registrars give a grace period to renew at the normal price, then a redemption period where the owner can restore it for an extra fee.
  • When a domain expires, the website and the email on that domain stop working, and SSL, payment callbacks and password-reset emails can fail with them.
  • Turn on auto-renew, keep the registrar account in your business name with a recovery email on a different domain, and check that renewals actually went through.

If your domain name has expired, you can usually still get it back. Most registrars give a grace period after the expiry date when you can renew at the normal price, followed by a redemption period when the original owner can restore it for an extra fee. Only after that is the domain deleted and released for anyone to register. Log in to your registrar today, renew it, and then check that your website and email come back.

An expired domain is one of the most avoidable outages a business website can have. The uptime monitoring in my WordPress maintenance plans flags a site that stops loading quickly, but renewing is down to whoever controls the registrar account, so it's worth knowing exactly where your domain sits and who can log in.

Exact periods and fees depend on the domain extension (.com, .in, .co.in and so on) and on your registrar, so treat the timings below as typical, and check your registrar's own policy for your domain.

Some registrars also put valuable expired domains up for auction during the grace or redemption period, so don't assume you have the whole period shown.

What breaks when a domain expires

  • The website stops loading, or shows the registrar's parking page with ads.
  • Email on the domain stops arriving, even if your Google Workspace, Zoho Mail or hosting email subscription is paid up, because the domain's DNS records no longer point to it. Senders get bounces.
  • SSL certificates can fail to renew, because renewal checks the domain.
  • Online payments and integrations: payment gateway callbacks, WooCommerce order emails, forms and anything that calls your site can fail.
  • Ads and listings: Google Ads may disapprove ads with a broken landing page, and your Google Business Profile links to a dead site.
  • Google rankings: a short outage usually recovers once the site is back, but a site that stays down for long can start dropping out of results.
  • Account security: if someone else registers the domain, they can receive email sent to it, including password-reset emails for any account that used your old business address.

How to get an expired domain name back, step by step

  1. Find out where it's registered and its status. Search for the domain on a WHOIS or RDAP lookup, such as ICANN's lookup tool. It shows the registrar, the expiry date and status codes.
  2. Log in to the registrar (GoDaddy, Hostinger, BigRock, Namecheap or whoever it is) and renew. If it's in redemption, look for a restore option and expect a restoration fee.
  3. Can't log in? Use the account recovery process. If the recovery email is on the expired domain itself, you won't receive the reset email, so contact the registrar's support and be ready to prove ownership with invoices, business documents or ID.
  4. Registered by a developer or agency? Contact them urgently and ask them to renew, then move the domain into an account in your business name. See regaining access from an old developer if they don't respond.
  5. Check the DNS after renewing. Some registrars switch an expired domain's nameservers to a parking page. If the site doesn't return within a few hours, compare the nameservers with what your hosting or email provider expects and set them back.
  6. Test everything: the website over HTTPS, sending and receiving email, contact forms, and a test payment if you sell online.
  7. Turn on auto-renew before you close the tab.

Thinking of moving the domain to a better registrar once it's back? Renew first, and note that registrars may lock transfers for a period after some changes; see how to transfer a domain to another registrar.

What the status codes in a WHOIS lookup mean

  • autoRenewPeriod: just expired and in the grace period. Renew now.
  • redemptionPeriod: past the grace period but restorable by the owner for a fee.
  • pendingDelete: too late to restore. It will be released in a few days.
  • clientHold or serverHold: the domain is registered but switched off, which can happen at expiry, for unverified contact details or for abuse.

If someone else has registered your domain

Once a domain is released, anyone can register it. Valuable or older domains are often picked up within seconds by drop-catching services, sometimes to sell back to the previous owner.

  • Check auctions first. If the domain hasn't been released yet, it may be listed in your registrar's expired-domain auction, and a backorder service may be able to catch it when it drops.
  • Contact the new owner through the WHOIS contact or the parking page. If you agree a price, use an escrow service rather than paying directly.
  • Trademark disputes: if you hold a trademark and the new owner is using the domain in bad faith, a dispute may be possible under the UDRP for .com and similar domains or the INDRP for .in domains. Take legal advice first; these processes cost money and need evidence.
  • Move to a new domain as a last resort. You can't redirect a domain you don't control, so update your Google Business Profile, social profiles, directories, printed material and every account that used the old email address.

Beware of fake renewal and expiry notices

Many "your domain is expiring" emails and letters come from unrelated companies hoping you'll pay them or transfer the domain. Some offer "SEO submissions" or directory listings dressed up as renewals. Only ever renew by logging in to your actual registrar directly. Domain and SEO scam emails shows the common versions.

How to make sure it never happens again

  • Turn on auto-renew and keep a valid payment method on the account.
  • Check that renewals actually go through. In India, saved cards for recurring payments may need fresh authorisation, and auto-renewals can fail quietly. Look for the renewal invoice each year.
  • Use a recovery email on a different domain, such as a Gmail address, so an expired domain can't lock you out of the account that renews it.
  • Register the domain in your business name and your own account, not your developer's. See the website ownership checklist.
  • Renew for several years at a time for your main domain. A .com or .in typically costs roughly ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year, so it's a small cost against the risk.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for the registrar account.
  • Keep a renewals list of domain, hosting, email and licences with dates and owners; the renewals checklist has a template.

Expired domain or something else?

Not every "site down" is an expired domain. If WHOIS shows the domain is active and paid up, the problem is more likely hosting, DNS or the site itself; see what to do when your website is down.

Domain expired on a .in or .co.in address?

Indian .in, .co.in and other .in domains are run by the .IN registry, but you renew them through the registrar you bought from, exactly like a .com. The grace and redemption periods for .in names are not always the same length as for .com, and registrars set their own restore fees, so check the expiry notice or your registrar's help pages rather than assuming the dates match a .com you also own.

A few India-specific points make recovery smoother:

  • Find the right account first. Many Indian businesses had the domain bought by a past developer, a cousin or a hosting reseller. Search your email for the registrar's name and the word "invoice" to find the account it sits in.
  • Pay in the account that holds the domain. Paying a reseller's invoice by UPI or bank transfer does not renew anything until they actually renew it with the registrar. Ask for the updated expiry date as proof.
  • Check the GST invoice details. If the renewal is billed to your business, make sure the invoice shows your business name and GSTIN, which also helps prove ownership later.
  • Update the registrant email. If expiry reminders went to an old staff address, change the contact to a role address such as an admin mailbox you control.

Once the domain is renewed, test the website, your business email and any WhatsApp or payment links that use the domain. Payment gateways and email services sometimes need DNS records re-checked if the nameservers were changed to a parking page during the expiry.

FAQs

How can I get my expired domain name back?

Log in to your registrar and renew it. During the grace period this is usually at the normal price; during the redemption period you pay a restoration fee as well. If you can't access the account, contact the registrar's support with proof of ownership.

How long after a domain expires can I still renew it?

It depends on the extension and registrar. For .com domains, the grace period is often around 30 days and the redemption period roughly another 30, but some registrars offer less. Check your registrar's renewal policy and act as soon as you notice.

Can someone else buy my domain after it expires?

Not during the grace and redemption periods, when it's still reserved for you, although some registrars auction expired domains during that time. Once it's deleted and released, anyone can register it.

Will my business email stop working if my domain expires?

Usually, yes. Email depends on the domain's DNS records, so it stops arriving even if your email subscription is paid. It comes back once the domain is renewed and DNS resolves again, though emails sent during the outage may have bounced.

Will an expired domain hurt my Google rankings?

A short outage of a day or two usually recovers once the site is back. Longer outages can cause pages to drop from results, and losing the domain to someone else means losing the rankings and links it built.

What is a domain redemption period?

It's a window after the grace period when the domain has been removed from normal renewal but the original owner can still restore it, usually for a restoration fee on top of the renewal price. After it ends, the domain is deleted and released.
