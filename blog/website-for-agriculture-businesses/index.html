Websites for Agriculture and Agri-Input Businesses: Crop-Wise Product Pages, Dealer Locators, Marathi and Hindi Content, WhatsApp and Label-Safe Claims

Plan an agriculture business website: crop-wise product pages, a dealer locator, Marathi or Hindi content, WhatsApp enquiries and label-safe product claims.

Key takeaways
  • Build product pages around crops, seasons and regions, so a farmer or retailer can see what suits their field before they call.
  • Make the dealer locator and WhatsApp the main routes to buy, and put key pages in Marathi, Hindi or whichever languages your buyers actually read.
  • Keep dosage and performance wording inside the approved label and current seed, fertiliser and pesticide rules, and never promise yields.

An agriculture business website serves people who rarely buy online but often check online first: a farmer comparing seed varieties before sowing, a krishi kendra owner deciding which brand to stock, an FPO looking for a buyer or an input supplier, a distributor asking about territory. The site that earns their call shows products by crop and season, points to the nearest dealer, speaks the farmer's language and keeps every claim inside the label.

Seed, fertiliser, micronutrient and equipment companies mostly sell through dealers, so their sites are structured much like my websites for manufacturers and B2B suppliers: a product catalogue with downloadable leaflets, enquiry forms for dealers and bulk buyers, and SEO for product and crop searches. Smaller dealers and FPOs need less catalogue and more of the local side, which a compact WordPress website with WhatsApp and a map handles well.

Who visits an agri-input website

VisitorWhat they wantWhere the site should send them

A homepage that greets all five with a slogan and a tractor photo helps none of them. Give each one a clear first link.

Product pages: one variety or formulation at a time

A catalogue entry reading "Hybrid tomato seeds" with one photo answers nothing. A seed variety page, for example, should cover:

  • Crop and variety name, the type (hybrid or open-pollinated) and the segment, such as fresh market or processing
  • Seasons and regions it is recommended for, such as kharif or rabi, and the states or zones where it has been tested
  • Key characteristics as your breeders describe them: plant habit, maturity range, fruit or grain traits, and tolerance claims only where your trial data supports them
  • Sowing guidance: seed rate, spacing and nursery notes, matching the packet
  • Pack sizes and a leaflet to download
  • Where to buy: a link to the dealer locator, filtered by district

A fertiliser or crop nutrition page swaps varieties for composition: nutrient content as printed on the label, the crops and stages it is recommended for, how and when to apply it, compatibility with other inputs, and storage. For machinery and irrigation equipment, specifications, power needs, spares and service matter more; websites for tractor and farm equipment dealers covers that side.

Crop-wise guides, organised by the season

Farmers think in crops and seasons, not in your product categories. A page for each major crop in your market, such as soybean, cotton, onion, sugarcane or pomegranate in Maharashtra, can walk through the crop's stages and link the relevant products at each one:

StageWhat the farmer is decidingWhat the page offers
Before sowingVariety, seed treatment, basal nutritionVariety pages, and seed treatment guidance as per label
Early growthNutrition, weeds, early pestsA nutrition schedule and photos to help identify common problems
Flowering and fruitingMicronutrients, disease and pest pressureProducts whose labels cover the crop, and when to consult a local expert
Harvest and afterTiming, storage, sellingStorage advice, and market or buyback information if you offer it

Publish each guide before the season starts, and keep it as one page you update every year instead of a fresh post each season. Use photos from real fields in your region, credited with the farmer's permission, and short videos, since many farmers come across a company on YouTube first. Agronomic advice should come from your agronomists, carry their names, and send farmers to your field staff or local agriculture officers when a problem needs diagnosing on the spot.

A dealer locator farmers can use from the field

For most farmers, the website's job ends with "where can I buy this near me?". A dealer locator has to work on a basic phone with a weak signal:

  1. Choose state, then district, then taluka, from simple dropdowns rather than a heavy map
  2. See each dealer's shop name, village or town and phone number, with a tap-to-call button
  3. Filter by product only if your sales team can keep stock information current
  4. Open the shop's location in Google Maps with one tap

Let the sales team update the list from the dashboard or a spreadsheet, and remove dealers who stop stocking you; a locator full of closed shops sends farmers to someone else's counter. Keep these pages light, and test them on the handsets and networks your buyers really use, as website speed on Indian mobile networks describes.

Marathi, Hindi and English: which pages to translate first

Many farmers read comfortably in their own language and less so in English, while distributors and institutional buyers may prefer English. You need not translate everything on day one. A sensible order:

  1. Crop guides and product pages for the crops you sell most in that language's region
  2. The dealer locator's labels and instructions
  3. The contact and WhatsApp pages
  4. Company and dealer pages later, if dealers ask for them

Give each language its own pages and URLs so Google can show the right version, and have translations written or checked by a fluent speaker who knows farming terms rather than an automatic tool. Our Temples, a directory I built, runs Telugu content alongside English, and multilingual WordPress websites explains the setup. Marathi and Hindi share the Devanagari script, so pick a font that renders conjuncts cleanly and loads quickly; Hindi and Devanagari fonts compares the options.

WhatsApp, missed calls and photo queries

Farmers who contact you will mostly call or send a WhatsApp message, often with a photo of a leaf or a fruit. Make WhatsApp the main button on every product and crop page, with a pre-filled message naming that page, and say what happens next: who replies, in which languages, and during which hours. If your team answers photo queries about crop problems, be careful about prescribing products from a photo alone, and suggest a field visit, the nearest dealer or an agriculture officer when a picture is not enough. Adding WhatsApp to your website covers buttons, pre-filled messages and tracking. If you also run a toll-free or missed-call number, show it beside the WhatsApp button with honest hours.

Regulated products: wording that stays inside the label

Seeds, fertilisers and pesticides are regulated in India, with licences for making and selling them and rules on labels, quality and claims. The details are spread across several laws and orders and have been revised over time, so confirm current requirements with your regulatory team or licensing authority. On the website, a few habits keep you on safe ground:

  • Recommend a crop protection product only for the crops and pests on its approved label, at the label dose
  • Take dosage and safety instructions from the label word for word, and link to the label or leaflet
  • Avoid yield promises such as "doubles your yield" or "assured income"; describe what the product does, and show trial results only with the location, season and comparison made
  • Call a product "organic" or "natural" only if that is accurate and you hold whatever certification the claim needs
  • List your dealer or manufacturing licences only as they actually stand

FPOs and producer companies

Farmer producer organisations use a website differently. Buyers, banks and scheme officials want the FPO's registration, its members and area, the crops and quantities it can supply, grading and storage facilities, and contacts for the board and CEO. Members want notices about input purchases, meetings and payments. A simple site with a crop availability page, a buyer enquiry form and a notices section covers both. If the FPO starts selling packed produce to households, that becomes a retail site with its own needs; websites for organic food stores covers that move.

Dealer and distributor enquiries: a separate path

For many agri-input companies, the most valuable enquiry is not from a farmer but from a shop owner or distributor who wants to stock your products. Treat this as its own journey.

  • A dedicated "become a dealer" page. Explain which districts or states you are appointing dealers in, what range they would stock and what support you give, such as field demos, point-of-sale material or training.
  • A short qualifying form. Ask for the shop or firm name, town and district, current brands handled and whether they hold the licences needed to sell your category. Keep it to a few fields so it works on a phone.
  • Routing by territory. Send each enquiry to the right area sales manager by district, by email and WhatsApp, so it is answered quickly.
  • Downloadable material. A product catalogue and price-list request option (not public prices if you do not publish them) helps dealers make a case to their own buyers.

Track these enquiries separately from farmer queries, so you can see which districts produce dealer interest and plan field visits around them.

FAQs

Do farmers really use agriculture websites?

Many search on their phones, often after a YouTube video or a WhatsApp forward, before visiting a dealer. The website is where those links lead, so it has to load fast and answer in their language.

Should we put dosage information on product pages?

Yes, provided it is taken from the approved label, names the crops it applies to, and links to the label or leaflet.

Which language should we add first?

The language of the region where most of your sales happen. Start with crop guides, product pages and the dealer locator in that language.

Can our sales team update the dealer list?

Yes. Dealers can be managed from the dashboard or updated from a spreadsheet, so new shops appear and closed ones disappear without a developer.

Can an FPO take buyer enquiries through its website?

Yes. A crop availability page and an enquiry form asking for the crop, quantity, grade and delivery location can send enquiries straight to the FPO's office.

Should an agriculture company publish product prices online?

That depends on how you sell. Many input companies sell through dealers and leave retail pricing to them, so the site shows products and a dealer locator instead. If you sell directly, show clear prices with GST and delivery terms.
