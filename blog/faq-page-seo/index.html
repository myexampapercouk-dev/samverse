FAQ SEO: How to Write FAQ Pages and FAQs for SEO on Service, Sales and Local Pages

Key takeaways
  • FAQs help SEO when they answer real customer questions directly, in the words people search, on the page where the question comes up.
  • Put most FAQs on the service, product or sales page they belong to; keep a separate FAQ page for general questions about ordering, delivery and policies.
  • On a sales page, answer the objections that stop people buying, keep each answer short and link to the detail.

FAQ SEO means writing FAQs that answer the real questions customers ask, in the words they search, on the page where the doubt comes up. To write an FAQ page, collect the questions people ask you on calls, WhatsApp and email, answer each one directly in the first sentence, and group them under clear topics. A good FAQ removes a doubt that stops someone enquiring or buying; a bad one repeats the sales pitch as a question nobody asked.

Are FAQs good for SEO? Yes, but indirectly. FAQs are not a ranking factor on their own; they help when each one answers a question people really search, in plain words, on the page where that doubt comes up. That makes the page match more searches, gives Google and AI assistants a clean answer to quote, and turns more visitors into enquiries. Filler FAQs written only for keywords do nothing.

How to write an FAQ for a sales page

An FAQ on a sales page handles the last objections before someone pays or enquires, so write it from the doubts buyers actually raise, not from features you want to repeat.

  1. List every objection you hear. Go through recent enquiries, chats and calls where the buyer hesitated or went quiet, and write each doubt in their words.
  2. Keep the ones that block a decision. Fit, what is included, timing, payment, risk and support usually matter most.
  3. Turn each doubt into the question the buyer would type or say, not a heading in your own jargon.
  4. Answer in one to three sentences, starting with yes, no or the plain fact, then the condition or detail.
  5. Order them the way doubts arise: who it is for, what you get, what happens after paying, payment options, refunds or cancellation, and who to contact.
  6. Place the block near the price or the main button, so doubts are answered where they arise, and end with the next step.
  7. Revisit it monthly. If the same question keeps arriving by WhatsApp, the page hasn't answered it clearly enough.

Short example for a website package sales page:

Q: What if my content isn't ready yet?

"You can still start. We build the layout with placeholder text, and you send final text and photos before launch; the timeline starts from when content arrives."

Five to eight questions is usually enough. Don't promise results you can't keep, and never use the FAQ to bury important terms. One gap most guides miss: if your FAQ sits in an accordion, make sure the answers are in the page HTML and not fetched only when someone clicks, or search engines may never see them. How to write an FAQ page, step by step

  1. List the questions you already get. Go through the last few months of enquiries, sales calls and support messages and write each question in the customer's own words.
  2. Add search questions. Check Search Console queries, People Also Ask and autocomplete for the questions people type before they find you.
  3. Cut the ones that don't matter. Keep questions that affect a decision: price drivers, timelines, delivery, refunds, what is included, who it is for.
  4. Group by topic. Ordering, payments, delivery, services, aftercare. Put the most asked group first.
  5. Answer first, then explain. Start each answer with yes, no, a time frame or a clear statement, then add the detail.
  6. Link to the deeper page. If a service or policy page covers it fully, link there rather than repeating everything.
  7. Make it easy to scan. Questions as headings, short answers, and a contact link at the end for anything not covered.
  8. Review it regularly. Update answers when prices, policies or processes change, and add new questions as they come in.

FAQs for SEO: how they help

Well-chosen FAQs help in four practical ways:

  • They cover more of what people search. Specific questions match specific, longer searches; see long-tail keywords explained.
  • Clear question-and-answer formatting is easy to quote. It can help a page get picked for featured snippets and People Also Ask, though there's no guarantee; see how to win featured snippets.
  • AI assistants can understand and cite them easily; see AI search optimisation.
  • They help visitors decide. A page that answers the last few doubts usually turns more visitors into enquiries.

Should you create a separate FAQ page or put FAQs on each page?

Both have a place, but FAQs on the page they relate to are usually more useful for SEO, because each answer sits next to the content it supports and matches the searches that page already targets. A standalone FAQ page works best as a hub for general questions:

  • Service and product pages: questions about that specific service or product, such as sizing, compatibility, delivery time or what's in the box. This is the most valuable placement.
  • Sales and landing pages: the objections that stop people buying, placed near the price or call to action.
  • Location pages: questions about that area, timings and access.
  • A main FAQ page: general questions about payments, delivery, cancellations and how you work, with links to the relevant service pages.

Avoid pasting the same FAQ block onto every page. Tailor the questions to each page, and keep shared policies in one place.

How to pick the right questions

Search Console

In Google Search Console, open the Performance report, add a Query filter and choose Custom (regex). A pattern like this pulls out question-style searches:

^(how|what|why|when|which|where|who|can|does|should)

It will catch a little noise, but you'll see the questions people already find your site for. Filter by page to see which questions each page attracts. Questions with impressions but few clicks are good candidates; see finding content ideas in Search Console.

People Also Ask and autocomplete

Search for your main service, with and without your city, and note the questions in Google's "People also ask" box and the autocomplete suggestions. Keep only the ones your customers would genuinely ask. See keyword research for small businesses.

Sales calls, WhatsApp and email

This is the best source. Keep a running list of questions people ask before they buy, and the doubts that come up just before they go quiet. Their words are the words your customers search with.

Reviews

Your reviews, and your competitors' reviews, show what customers worry about: hidden charges, delays, parking, aftercare. Each worry is a question you can answer before it's asked.

Then filter. Keep questions that real people ask, that matter to the decision and that you can answer honestly. Drop vanity questions like "Why are we the best?", or rewrite them as real questions, such as "How are you different from a cheaper option?"

How to write a good FAQ

  • Phrase the question as the customer would. "Do you charge for a visit?" rather than "Inspection fee policy".
  • Be specific. If the answer is "it depends", say what it depends on.
  • One question per item, two to four sentences each, linking to a full page when more detail exists.
  • Stay consistent with your prices, policies and Google Business Profile, and don't force the same keyword into every question.

Example: weak vs better

For a plumbing business:

Q: Do you charge for a visit?

Weak: "Please contact us for details."

Better: "Yes, there's a fixed visit charge. If you go ahead with the repair, we adjust it against the final bill, and we always tell you the price before starting work."

The better answer removes a real worry and sets expectations. Use your own policy, of course; the point is the shape of the answer.

FAQPage schema: what it does now

FAQPage schema is structured data that labels questions and answers on a page. It used to make expandable FAQ dropdowns appear under many sites' search results. That changed in August 2023, when Google limited FAQ rich results to well-known, authoritative government and health websites. For almost every business site, adding FAQ schema will not produce those dropdowns.

It can still be worth adding, as long as you keep expectations realistic:

  • It describes your content clearly to machines, which may help some search features and tools understand it
  • It is not a ranking factor and won't lift a page by itself
  • The marked-up questions and answers must be visible on the page, word for word
  • If the same FAQ appears on several pages, Google's guidelines say to mark up only one instance
  • Google's guidelines also say FAQ markup shouldn't be used for advertising, which is another reason not to expect much from it on sales pages

On WordPress, many SEO plugins and FAQ blocks add the markup for you. Check the output with the Rich Results Test or Schema Markup Validator; see testing structured data and schema markup explained.

FAQ pages for SEO: a quick checklist

  • Each question uses the searcher's own phrasing, ideally taken from Search Console or your enquiries.
  • The first sentence of each answer answers it; detail follows.
  • Every answer is visible on the page, not hidden behind scripts that load later.
  • No question repeats across many pages with the same answer.
  • Answers link to the service, pricing or policy page with the full detail.
  • Questions are reviewed when prices, delivery times or policies change.

Answer-first writing also suits people who ask their phones questions out loud; see voice search for local businesses.

To check your FAQs are found, open Performance > Queries in Search Console for matching question searches, and confirm the page is indexed with URL Inspection.

After adding FAQ markup, check it with Google's tool; see how to use the Rich Results Test.

FAQs

Should I add FAQs to product pages for SEO?

Yes, if they answer questions shoppers actually have about that product, such as size, fit, compatibility or delivery. Write them for each product or product type rather than repeating one store-wide block, and keep shipping and returns details on a single policy page that the answers link to.

How many FAQs should a page have?

Usually four to eight on a service or sales page. A main FAQ page can have more if it's grouped into clear sections. Include as many as there are genuine, useful questions, and no more.

Is FAQ schema still worth adding?

It's optional. Since 2023 it won't produce FAQ rich results for most business sites, but valid, accurate markup does no harm and describes your content clearly. Don't add it expecting more clicks.

How do you write a good FAQ?

Use a question your customers actually ask, written in their words, and answer it in the first sentence. Then add the detail, conditions or next step, and link to a fuller page if one exists. Keep it short enough to read on a phone.

How do I write an FAQ page?

Collect real customer questions, group them by topic, answer each directly in the first sentence, link to deeper pages and add a contact option at the end. Review the page whenever your prices, policies or processes change.

Are FAQs good for SEO?

Yes, when they answer genuine questions clearly. They help a page match more of the ways people search and give search engines and AI assistants direct answers to quote. Filler FAQs written only for keywords add nothing.

How do you write an FAQ for a sales page?

List the objections people raise before buying (price, timing, fit, risk, what happens next), answer each in one or two plain sentences, and place the FAQ just above or below the main call to action.
