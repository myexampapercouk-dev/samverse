A/B Testing Landing Pages: A Practical Guide for Small Businesses

Landing page A/B testing for small businesses: what to test first, how much traffic you need, WordPress tools and how to read the results honestly.

Key takeaways
  • A/B testing only gives trustworthy answers with enough conversions, so low-traffic sites should test big changes such as the offer or form, not button colours.
  • Set up conversion tracking first, change one main thing per test, run it for full weeks and decide the success measure before you start.
  • When traffic is too low for testing, use heatmaps, recordings, customer questions and before-and-after comparisons to improve the page instead.

Landing page A/B testing means showing two versions of a page to different visitors at the same time and measuring which one produces more enquiries, calls or sales. For a small business it works best when you test big changes, such as the headline offer, the form length or the page layout, because small sites rarely get enough conversions to detect the effect of a new button colour. Before you test anything, make sure conversions are tracked accurately; a test is only as good as its measurement.

This guide explains what to test first, how much traffic you need, how to run tests on WordPress and what to do if your traffic is too low for testing at all.

What landing page A/B testing actually is

In an A/B test, version A (the current page, or "control") and version B (the "variant") run side by side. A tool splits visitors randomly, usually half and half, and records which version each person saw and whether they converted. Because both versions run in the same period, seasonal changes, ad changes and news events affect both equally, which makes the comparison fair.

This is different from changing a page and comparing this month with last month. Before-and-after comparisons are still useful, but many other things change at the same time, so they give weaker evidence. If you are new to the idea of improving conversions, conversion rate optimisation basics is a good starting point.

Is your landing page ready for a test?

Check these before spending time on testing:

  • Conversion tracking works. Form submissions, calls and WhatsApp clicks are recorded as key events. See GA4 events explained.
  • The page has steady traffic from the same source, for example a Google Ads campaign that isn't changing every week.
  • The basics are right. Fixing obvious problems first, such as a slow page, a broken form or an offer that doesn't match the ad, beats testing. Landing page mistakes that waste your Google Ads budget lists the common ones.
  • You have a clear question you want answered, written down as a hypothesis.

How much traffic do you need?

This is where many small businesses go wrong. The number that matters is conversions, not visits. A page with a few hundred visitors a month and a handful of enquiries can't reliably tell you that one version is a few percent better; random variation is bigger than the difference you are looking for.

Practical rules of thumb:

  • Use an online sample size calculator with your current conversion rate and the smallest improvement you care about. It tells you roughly how many visitors each version needs.
  • The smaller the change you want to detect, the more traffic you need. Bold changes produce bigger differences, which need fewer visitors to spot.
  • Run tests for at least one full week, and ideally two or more, so weekdays and weekends are both covered.
  • Don't stop the test the moment one version pulls ahead. Early leads often disappear.

If the calculator says a test would need many months, choose a bolder change or use the low-traffic methods later in this guide.

Sometimes the bigger question is the page format itself. Before testing headlines, check whether the offer suits a short opt-in page or a full page; see squeeze page vs landing page.

What to test first

Test the elements that change a visitor's decision, not just the decoration. Roughly in order of likely impact:

ElementExample testWhy it matters
Offer"Free site visit" vs "Get a quote in 24 hours"The offer is the main reason people act
HeadlineProblem-focused vs outcome-focusedDecides whether visitors keep reading
FormShort form vs multi-step formChanges both the number and quality of leads
ProofReviews near the top vs further downReduces doubt before the form
Contact methodForm only vs form plus WhatsApp or call buttonSome audiences prefer to talk
Page lengthShort page vs detailed page with FAQsDepends on how considered the purchase is

Button colours and small wording changes come last. They rarely make a difference big enough for a small site to measure.

Write a hypothesis before you start

A simple format keeps tests honest: "Because [what we observed], we think [change] will [effect] for [audience], measured by [metric]." For example: "Because visitors ask about price on calls, we think adding a 'what affects the cost' section will increase quote requests from Google Ads visitors, measured by form submissions."

Decide the main metric and how long the test will run before launching. Changing the success measure after seeing the results is one of the easiest ways to fool yourself.

Tools for A/B testing on WordPress

Google Optimize was shut down in 2023, so you will need another approach. Common options include:

  • Landing page builders with built-in testing, where the landing page is hosted on the builder's platform.
  • WordPress A/B testing plugins that split traffic between two pages or page variations on your own site.
  • Dedicated testing services that change page content with a script; these are flexible but can cause a brief flicker and slow the page if set up poorly.
  • Split by ad: in Google Ads, you can send two ads, or an experiment, to two different landing page URLs and compare conversions in Ads.

Whatever you use, mark the variant page noindex or use a canonical tag pointing to the original, so search engines don't see two near-identical pages. Check the tool's current pricing and its effect on page speed before committing.

Reading the results honestly

When the test ends:

  • Look at the confidence or probability figure your tool reports, but also at the actual number of conversions. A "winner" based on a handful of enquiries is not reliable.
  • Check lead quality, not just quantity. A shorter form may bring more enquiries that are less likely to buy. Ask whoever handles leads.
  • Check results by device. A change can help on desktop and hurt on mobile.
  • Write down what you tested, the result and what you learned, even if there was no clear winner. A "no difference" result still tells you that element isn't what's holding the page back.

Common A/B testing mistakes

  • Testing several big changes in one variant and then not knowing which one made the difference. If you must change a lot at once, treat it as a redesign test and follow up with smaller tests.
  • Changing ads, budgets or targeting mid-test, which changes who arrives on the page.
  • Running overlapping tests on the same page and the same visitors.
  • Ignoring speed. If the variant loads more slowly because of a heavier image or extra script, you are partly testing page speed rather than the content.
  • Forgetting to switch the winner on properly, leaving a testing script running on the page for months after the test has finished.
  • Testing during an unusual period, such as a festival sale or a one-off promotion, and assuming the result applies all year.

What to do when traffic is too low to test

Most small business landing pages don't have enough conversions for frequent A/B tests. That doesn't mean you can't improve them:

  • Watch heatmaps and session recordings to see where people stop scrolling or get stuck.
  • Read sales calls, WhatsApp chats and form messages for repeated questions, then answer them on the page.
  • Ask five people who match your customer profile to use the page and say what's unclear.
  • Make one well-reasoned change at a time and compare a few weeks before and after, keeping a note of other changes (ad budget, season) in the same period.
  • Send people to a proper thank-you page so every conversion is measured reliably.

FAQs

What is landing page A/B testing?

It is showing two versions of a landing page to randomly split visitors at the same time and comparing which version gets more conversions, such as enquiries or sales.

How long should an A/B test run?

At least one full week, usually two or more, and until each version has enough conversions for the result to be reliable. Don't stop as soon as one version leads.

Can a small business with low traffic do A/B testing?

Only for bold changes, and even then slowly. With low traffic, use heatmaps, customer questions and careful before-and-after comparisons instead.

What should I A/B test first on a landing page?

The offer, headline and form usually matter most. Test those before colours, images or button wording.

Does A/B testing hurt SEO?

Not if done properly: run tests only as long as needed, use canonical tags or noindex on variant URLs, and don't show search engines different content from visitors.
