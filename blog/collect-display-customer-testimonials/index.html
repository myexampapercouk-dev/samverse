How to Collect and Display Customer Testimonials: Asking, Permission, Written, Video and WhatsApp Formats, Placement and the Honesty Rules

How to collect customer testimonials with permission, use written, video and WhatsApp formats, place them where visitors decide, and keep them honest.

Key takeaways
  • Ask happy customers soon after a good outcome, with two or three specific questions, and get written permission for the exact wording, name and photo you will publish.
  • Show testimonials word for word next to the decisions they support, such as service pages, forms and landing pages, rather than only on a testimonials page.
  • Never invent, buy or reword testimonials to change their meaning, and do not mark them up as review stars, which Google does not show for reviews of your own business.

To collect testimonials, ask happy customers soon after a good outcome, make it easy with two or three specific questions on WhatsApp or email, offer to tidy their words into a draft they approve, and get written permission for exactly what you will publish. Display them word for word, with a name and a specific detail, next to the decisions they support, and never invent, buy or reword them to change the meaning.

Testimonials are one of the things I ask clients to gather before a website redesign starts, because proof needs space planned into service pages and beside forms, and real quotes fit a layout better than placeholder text. The same applies to campaign pages built through landing page design, where visitors from ads usually know nothing about you.

This guide covers testimonials you collect directly. For reviews on your Google Business Profile, see how to get more Google reviews and how to show Google reviews on your website.

Most businesses benefit from all three: testimonials on key pages, a link to your Google reviews, and a few detailed case studies.

When and how to ask

Ask at the high point: just after a delivery, a launch, a successful treatment or a problem solved, or when the customer thanks you. The person who served them should ask, because they have the relationship. Make it a fixed step in your handover or follow-up routine rather than something the owner remembers occasionally.

Keep the request short and give people an easy way to say no:

Hi [Name], thank you again for choosing us for [the job]. Would you be happy to share two or three lines about your experience for our website? It helps people who are deciding. If it's easier, just answer these on WhatsApp: what were you looking for, why did you choose us, and how did it go? I'll send you the final wording to approve before anything goes up.

Many customers have already written something useful, such as a thank-you message on WhatsApp or a line in an email. You can ask whether you may use those words, and show them the exact version you plan to publish.

Questions that get specific answers

"Great service!" convinces nobody. These questions draw out the details that persuade:

  1. What problem were you trying to solve before you contacted us?
  2. What nearly stopped you from going ahead?
  3. Why did you choose us over the other options?
  4. What was the experience like, including anything that surprised you?
  5. What is different now?

The second question is the most useful. An answer such as "I was worried the work would overrun, but it was finished when promised" answers the exact doubt your next customer has. If you help shape the answers into a short quote, keep the customer's own words and meaning, and get their approval for the final version.

Getting permission properly

Agree in writing, even if it is a WhatsApp reply, on:

  • The exact wording you will publish, including any shortened version.
  • How they are named: full name, first name and city, or name, role and company.
  • Photo and logo: whether you may show their face, their company logo or photos of the work.
  • Where it appears: your website, social media or ads. Ask separately for ads, as some people are happy with one and not the other.
  • Withdrawal: that they can ask you to remove it later, and that you will.

Keep each approval in a folder alongside the quote. B2B clients sometimes need internal approval before being named, so ask early. Doctors, lawyers, CAs and other regulated professionals should check their professional body's current rules on testimonials before collecting any, and never reveal a patient's or client's situation without explicit consent. For anything involving children, ask a parent.

Formats: written, video, WhatsApp screenshots and photos

Written quotes

The workhorse. Use a short pull quote with the full text available, the person's name and detail, and a photo or company logo if they agreed. Specific beats long: one sentence about a real result outweighs a paragraph of general praise.

Video

A customer talking on camera is hard to fake and easy to believe, and a phone is enough to record it. Add captions and a short written summary beside each video, and load the player only when someone taps play so the page stays fast. Video testimonials for your website covers recording, editing and hosting.

WhatsApp screenshots

Screenshots of happy WhatsApp messages are popular in India because they feel unpolished and real. Use them only with the sender's consent, crop or blur phone numbers, profile photos and anything else in the chat, and never edit the text. Add the same words as real text beside the image, so they can be read on small screens, by screen readers and by search engines. Never use made-up chats from screenshot generator apps; it is the same as writing fake reviews.

Photos of the customer or the finished work

A photo of the customer with the finished kitchen, the installed system or the delivered product adds weight to a quote. Ask before taking or using it, and check backgrounds for house numbers, documents or anything private.

Where to place testimonials

  • Service pages: testimonials about that specific service, not a general mix.
  • Beside forms, booking buttons and prices: reassurance at the moment someone decides.
  • The homepage: two or three strong ones near the main call to action.
  • Landing pages for ads: essential, because ad visitors haven't checked your reviews yet.
  • A testimonials page: useful as an archive, but never the only place they appear.

Tie each testimonial to what was done. On Dr. Sunaina Dental Care, a dental clinic site I built, each testimonial names the treatment the patient had, such as a root canal or braces, together with the patient's name and occupation, and every inner page ends with testimonials and a contact form. On Vansh Group, an electronics manufacturer, the testimonial carousel gives each person's name, job title and company, which matters to B2B buyers. Prefer a simple grid to an auto-rotating carousel where you can, as moving text is hard to read. The website trust signals checklist covers the other proof visitors look for.

The honesty rules

  • Never invent testimonials, or present staff, family or friends as customers. Don't use stock photos as customer faces.
  • Don't change the meaning. Trimming is fine, marked with an ellipsis; joining half-sentences into a claim the customer never made is not.
  • Don't pay for praise. If a customer received something in return, such as a discount or a free service, say so beside the testimonial.
  • Don't present the exceptional as typical. If a result depended on unusual circumstances, say so, or choose another quote.
  • Keep them current. Remove testimonials when a customer withdraws consent, or when they describe a service you no longer offer.

India's consumer protection rules and advertising guidelines generally treat fake or misleading reviews and endorsements as unfair, and professions have their own rules on top. The details change, so if you are unsure about a particular use, check with your lawyer.

Schema markup: why testimonials won't give you stars

Many business owners hope that marking up testimonials will put gold stars beside their listing in Google. At the time of writing, Google doesn't show review stars for reviews a business publishes about itself under its LocalBusiness or Organization markup, and its guidelines say review markup shouldn't be used for reviews copied from other sites. Some testimonial plugins add review markup automatically; check what yours outputs and switch it off if it applies to your own business. Markup that breaks the guidelines is ignored at best, and can lead to a manual action. Treat testimonials as proof for visitors, not a search trick; your stars already appear where they matter, on your Google Business Profile.

FAQs

How do I ask a customer for a testimonial?

Soon after a good outcome, personally, on WhatsApp or email. Ask two or three specific questions, offer to tidy their answer into a short quote, and send them the final wording to approve.

Do I need permission to use a customer testimonial?

Yes. Get written permission, even a WhatsApp reply, covering the wording, how they are named, any photo or logo and where it will appear, and remove it if they later ask.

Can I use WhatsApp messages as testimonials?

Yes, with the sender's consent. Blur phone numbers and profile photos, never edit the text, and add the words as real text beside the screenshot.

Can I edit a testimonial?

You can shorten it and fix obvious typos, as long as the meaning stays the same and the customer approves the final version. Never add claims they didn't make.

Should I add review schema to my testimonials?

Not to get stars. Google doesn't show review stars for testimonials a business publishes about itself, so the markup gains nothing and can break its guidelines.
