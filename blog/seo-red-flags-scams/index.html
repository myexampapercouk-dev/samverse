SEO Scams and Red Flags: How to Avoid Bad SEO Services and Choose a Provider (Including What a Fair Monthly Retainer Covers)

SEO scams and red flags: guaranteed rankings, cheap link packages, locked accounts. How to vet an SEO agency and what a fair monthly SEO retainer includes.

Key takeaways
  • Guaranteed rankings, bulk backlink packages, secret methods and providers who keep your accounts in their name are the clearest signs of a bad SEO service.
  • A fair monthly retainer names the work it covers, starts with an audit, reports Search Console and enquiry data, and can be stopped on reasonable notice.
  • You should always own your website, domain, Search Console, Analytics and Google Business Profile, so you can change provider without losing anything.

The clearest signs of a bad SEO service are guaranteed rankings, packages selling hundreds of backlinks, "secret" methods they won't explain, keeping your Google accounts in their name, reports full of vanity metrics, and long contracts with no clear deliverables. A good SEO provider explains what they'll do and why, starts with an audit of your site, measures results in Search Console clicks and real enquiries, and makes sure you own every account. If a pitch sounds too good to be true, it is.

Below are the red flags in detail, the scams aimed at small businesses in India, what a fair monthly SEO retainer should include, and the questions to ask before you sign. I offer WordPress SEO services myself, so use these questions on me too.

SEO red flags to watch for

  1. Guaranteed rankings. Nobody controls Google's results, and Google's own guidance on hiring an SEO warns that no one can guarantee a top ranking. Guarantees usually mean obscure keywords nobody searches, or a refund clause you'll never satisfy.
  2. "Hundreds of backlinks" packages. Cheap links in bulk come from spam directories, comment spam or networks of fake blogs. At best they're ignored; at worst they lead to a manual action against your site.
  3. Secret or proprietary methods. Real SEO isn't secret. A provider should be able to explain every task in plain language.
  4. They own your accounts. Search Console, Analytics, Google Business Profile and your website should be in your business's name, with the provider added as a user.
  5. Reports full of vanity metrics: rankings for phrases nobody searches, "backlinks built" and traffic graphs with no link to enquiries or sales.
  6. Long lock-in contracts with cancellation fees and no defined work.
  7. Cold emails and calls about "critical errors" or your site "not being on page one".
  8. Fake reviews or review schemes for your Google profile, which risk your listing.
  9. Mass-produced pages, such as hundreds of city pages with the same text and a different place name; see local landing pages without doorway pages.
  10. Instant results promised. Real SEO takes weeks for technical fixes and months for competitive searches; see how long SEO takes.

Common SEO scams aimed at small businesses

  • "We're a Google Partner, so we can get you ranked." The Google Partner badge relates to Google Ads. Google has no paid SEO partners and no way to buy organic rankings.
  • "Your Google listing will be removed unless you verify with us." Calls like this push paid "verification" for a Business Profile, which is free to manage yourself.
  • "Submission to thousands of search engines and directories." Google finds sites by crawling and through your sitemap; mass directory submissions do nothing useful.
  • Fake traffic. Bot visits that make your Analytics graph rise while enquiries stay flat. Check engagement and key events, not just visitor numbers.
  • Hostage accounts. The agency registers your domain, Business Profile or Analytics in its own name and won't hand them over when you leave.
  • Hidden links. Links to the agency's other clients quietly added to your footer or pages, using your site to boost theirs.

Scam emails about domain renewals and "SEO for your domain" invoices are a separate problem, covered in how to spot domain renewal and SEO scam emails.

What a fair SEO monthly retainer includes

A retainer isn't a red flag in itself. Sites that add pages often, online stores and businesses in competitive markets benefit from ongoing work. The question is what you get each month. Here's how a fair retainer compares with one to avoid:

A fair SEO retainerA retainer to avoid
How it startsAn audit with prioritised findings, and a plan for the first three monthsStraight into "link building" with no look at your site
Monthly workNamed tasks: technical fixes, on-page work on specific pages, content briefs, internal links, Business Profile updates, Search Console monitoringVague "SEO optimisation" or "promotion"
LinksEarned through useful content, partners, suppliers and local organisationsA fixed number of backlinks a month
ReportingSearch Console clicks and impressions for target pages, enquiries from GA4, and the work doneRanking screenshots and "backlinks built"
ContractMonth to month or a short notice periodA 12-month lock-in with an exit fee
OwnershipEverything in your name, with the provider as a userAccounts and content held by the agency

Prices vary with the size of the site, the competition and how much work is included, so compare what each retainer delivers rather than the headline fee. How SEO services are priced in India explains retainers, audits, project fees and "performance" deals side by side.

Do you need a retainer at all?

Not always. Many small business sites are best served by a one-time audit and fixes, followed by a check-up after a few months. A retainer makes more sense when the site changes often, competitors are active, or nobody in the business has time to monitor Search Console.

Green flags: signs of a good SEO provider

  • They ask about your business, customers and the enquiries you want before quoting
  • They explain what they'll do, in what order, and why
  • They set realistic timelines and don't promise positions
  • They fix technical problems and improve real pages, not just "build links"
  • Monthly reports use Search Console and Analytics data and show the work done; see what to track in a monthly SEO report
  • You keep ownership of everything, and they say so in writing

Questions to ask before hiring an SEO provider

  1. What will you do in the first 90 days? A good answer names an audit, specific fixes and the pages they'll work on.
  2. How do you get links? Listen for content, partnerships and outreach, not packages; see ethical link building.
  3. How will you measure success? Clicks, enquiries and sales, not just rankings.
  4. Can I see examples of similar work, with real Search Console data shared with the client's permission?
  5. Who owns the accounts, content and any links built? The answer should be you.
  6. What happens if I stop? You should keep everything, with a short notice period.
  7. Who will actually do the work? A named person, not an unnamed team you never speak to.

How to check a provider's work

  • Look at their own website: is it fast, clear and found for what they do?
  • Read reviews on Google and elsewhere, and look for detail rather than one-line praise.
  • After they start, watch the Links report in Search Console for sudden batches of spammy links.
  • Check Security & Manual Actions in Search Console every month.
  • Compare enquiries in GA4 with the months before they started, allowing for seasonality.

If you've already been burned

  1. Check Search Console for manual actions and security issues.
  2. Review your backlinks. If there's a manual action for unnatural links, remove what you can and use the disavow tool for the rest; see backlink audits and disavow. Without a manual action, Google usually ignores spammy links.
  3. Remove hidden links and doorway pages the provider added.
  4. Take back your accounts: domain, hosting, website, Search Console, Analytics and Business Profile; see regaining control of your website.
  5. Remove fake reviews they posted, and start collecting genuine ones.

FAQs

How do I know if an SEO company is a scam?

Watch for guaranteed rankings, bulk backlink packages, secret methods, refusal to give you account ownership, reports without enquiry data and long lock-in contracts. Cold emails claiming your site has "critical errors" are another warning sign.

What should a monthly SEO retainer include?

Named monthly tasks such as technical fixes, on-page improvements to specific pages, content guidance, internal linking, Business Profile updates and Search Console monitoring, plus a report showing clicks, enquiries and the work done. It should start with an audit and be cancellable on reasonable notice.

What are SEO retainer services?

An SEO retainer is an ongoing arrangement where you pay a fixed monthly fee for a defined amount of SEO work, rather than paying per project. It suits sites that change often or compete hard for searches. The agreement should list the monthly tasks, the reporting and the notice period, so you can judge whether it's worth continuing.

Can an SEO company guarantee first page on Google?

No. Google decides rankings, and its own guidance warns against anyone promising a top position. A provider can commit to work and reporting, not to positions.

Is buying backlinks a scam?

Buying links to manipulate rankings is against Google's spam policies, and cheap bulk packages are mostly worthless. At best Google ignores them; at worst they lead to a manual action. Links earned through useful content and real relationships are what work.

Should I sign a 12-month SEO contract?

Be cautious. SEO takes months, so some commitment is reasonable, but a long lock-in with an exit fee and no defined work protects the agency, not you. Prefer month-to-month terms or a short notice period with clear monthly deliverables.
