A Sample Content Calendar for Small Businesses: Blog Posts, Case Studies and Google Posts

A monthly content routine for small businesses: turn customer questions into blog posts, case studies and Google Business Profile posts, then review results.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Build a question bank from real customer questions so every blog post, FAQ and Google post answers something people actually ask.
  • Choose a publishing level you can keep up for a year, and mix blog posts, case studies and Google Business Profile posts that point back to your website.
  • Spend 20 minutes a month in Search Console spotting rising pages, low-click queries and missing topics to decide what to write next.

Most small business owners don't need a complicated content strategy. They need a routine that fits around running the business. This article gives you a ready-made monthly rhythm you can copy, mixing website articles, case studies and Google Business Profile posts, with one short review each month to see what's working.

If you want the planning steps first, such as mapping topics to your services, see how to plan a website content calendar. This article is the practical template that sits on top of that plan.

Start with a question bank, not a blank page

The best topics come from your customers, not from brainstorming. Keep one shared Google Sheet or note called "Questions" and add to it whenever someone asks something on a call, on WhatsApp or at the counter. Write the question in the customer's own words, and note which service it relates to.

A few weeks of this gives you more topics than you can write. For example:

  • A dental clinic: "Does a root canal hurt?" or "How many visits does it take?"
  • A CA firm: "Which documents do I need for GST registration?"
  • An interior designer: "How long does a 2BHK interior take?"

Each good question can become a blog post, an FAQ on a service page, a Google Business Profile post and a few social posts.

How often to publish, realistically

Pick a level you can keep up for a whole year. A steady, modest routine beats an ambitious one that stops after six weeks.

LevelWebsiteGoogle Business ProfileBest for
LightOne article a monthA post or photo update every week or twoOwners doing it all themselves
SteadyTwo articles a month, plus a case study every couple of monthsA post every weekBusinesses with one person who can give it a few hours
ActiveWeekly articles or page updates, plus regular case studiesWeekly posts and regular photosCompetitive services and multiple locations

If you're unsure, start light. You can always step up once the habit sticks.

The mix: blog posts, case studies and Google posts

Each format does a different job:

  • Blog posts answer the questions people type into Google and link readers to your service pages.
  • Case studies are proof. They show real work, the problem, what you did and the outcome, which helps people who are close to deciding. See how to write case studies.
  • Google Business Profile posts keep your profile looking active to people who find you on Maps and in local results. They're short and can link to your new article or case study. See how to use Google Business Profile posts.

Google posts don't replace website content. Think of them as signposts pointing people to the fuller story on your website.

A sample month

Here's what the "Steady" level can look like in practice:

WeekWebsiteGoogle Business ProfileSocial and WhatsApp
1Publish an article answering your most common questionA post linking to the articleShare the main tip as a short post or status
2Add an FAQ or fresh photos to a service pagePhotos of recent workBehind-the-scenes photo or short video
3Publish a second article, or a case study in alternate monthsA post about the project, with a photoBefore-and-after post linking to the case study
4Review results and pick next month's topicsAn offer or event post, if you have oneReshare the month's most useful tip

Around festivals and peak seasons, swap in timely topics a few weeks early, so the content is live before customers start searching.

Reuse every piece on social media

Writing is the slow part, so get more out of each piece. One article can become:

  • Three or four short tips for Instagram, Facebook or LinkedIn
  • A carousel that walks through the main steps
  • A WhatsApp status, or a message to customers who have opted in to updates
  • A short video of you answering the question in a minute

Always link back to the full article on your website, where visitors can see your services and contact you.

Review what works in Search Console

Set aside 20 minutes in the last week of each month. In Google Search Console, open the Performance report and look at:

  1. Which pages gained impressions and clicks compared with the previous period. Write more on those topics.
  2. Queries with plenty of impressions but few clicks. A clearer page title and description can help.
  3. Questions you don't have a page for yet. Add them to your question bank.
  4. Articles with almost no impressions after a few months. Improve them, merge them into a stronger page, or leave the topic.

Also check which pages lead to enquiries, calls and WhatsApp messages, not just visits. For more ways to dig into the data, see finding content ideas in Search Console.

Keep the system simple

A spreadsheet is enough. Use one row per piece, with columns for topic, the customer question it answers, related service, format, owner, due date, status, published link, and whether it has gone out on Google and social media.

If writing is the bottleneck, record a voice note answering the question as you would to a customer, then turn it into an article or ask someone to edit it. Batch the work: one afternoon to draft two pieces is often easier than squeezing writing into every week.
