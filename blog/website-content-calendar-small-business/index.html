A Realistic Content Calendar for Small Business Websites in India: Cadence, Festivals, Repurposing and a Template

A content calendar a small Indian business can keep: how often to publish, festival hooks, repurposing, refreshing old posts and a ready-to-copy template.

Key takeaways
  • Choose a publishing rhythm you can keep for a full year, usually one or two useful pieces a month, rather than an ambitious plan that stops after six weeks.
  • Plan festival and seasonal content four to eight weeks ahead, because people search before the occasion, and check the dates each year since many festivals move.
  • Give every month one refresh slot for an older post, and turn each new piece into Google Business Profile posts, social posts and WhatsApp updates.

A content calendar for a small business website is a simple monthly plan of what you will publish, when, and where it will be reused. For most owners in India a realistic version is one or two useful articles or case studies a month, one older page refreshed, a weekly Google Business Profile post, and festival or seasonal topics scheduled four to eight weeks before the date. Keep it in a spreadsheet, review it once a month in Search Console, and choose a pace you can sustain for a year rather than one that looks impressive in January.

The calendar is the routine; the website is where the routine pays off. A site with clean page titles, internal links and Search Console set up gives each new article a fair chance in Google, which is the part I handle in WordPress SEO services. I advise on which topics to write, but the knowledge in each piece has to come from you, because it is your customers asking the questions.

If you want the planning method first, such as mapping topics to services, read how to plan a website content calendar. This article is the practical, filled-in version: cadence, a year of Indian seasonal hooks, a template you can copy and a monthly routine.

Step one: build a question bank before you build a calendar

A blank calendar invites guesswork. A question bank fills it with topics customers have already asked. Keep a single shared sheet or note and add to it every time someone asks something on a call, at the counter or on WhatsApp. Write the question in their words and note the service it relates to.

  • A dental clinic: "Does a root canal hurt?" or "How many visits does an implant take?"
  • A CA firm: "Which documents do I need for GST registration?"
  • An interior designer: "How long does a 2BHK interior take?"
  • An AC repair business: "Should I service my AC before summer or when it stops cooling?"

After a month or two you will usually have more questions than you can write about. The calendar then becomes a matter of choosing which question goes in which week, not inventing topics.

How often to publish: pick a level you can keep

Consistency matters more than volume. Google does not reward a burst of ten posts followed by silence, and neither do customers who land on a blog whose latest article is two years old.

LevelWebsiteGoogle Business ProfileWho it suits
LightOne article a month, plus one refresh every two monthsA post or new photos every week or twoOwners doing everything themselves
SteadyTwo pieces a month (articles or case studies), plus one refresh a monthA post every weekBusinesses with one person who can give it a few hours a month
ActiveWeekly articles or page improvements, regular case studiesWeekly posts and regular photosCompetitive services, several branches or an online store

If you are unsure, start at Light for three months. Moving up once the habit is in place is much easier than recovering from an abandoned plan.

Festival and seasonal hooks for an Indian business

Seasonal content works because people search before the occasion: a Diwali gifting guide is useful in September and October, not on Diwali morning. Many Indian festivals follow the lunar calendar, so their dates shift each year. Check the dates every January and plan the content four to eight weeks ahead. Not every festival fits every business. A B2B manufacturer may care more about trade exhibitions, the financial year and budget cycles than about Holi. Pick the hooks your customers actually act on, and skip the rest.

Repurpose every piece at least three ways

Writing is the slow part, so get more from each piece. One article can become:

  • Three or four short tips for Instagram, Facebook or LinkedIn
  • A carousel walking through the main steps
  • A WhatsApp status, or a broadcast to customers who have opted in to updates
  • A one-minute video of you answering the question
  • A short answer added to the FAQ section of the matching service page

Always link back to the full article on your website, where visitors can see your services and contact you. Repurposing website content for social media goes further, including which formats suit which platform.

Updating old posts is part of the calendar

An article published two years ago with outdated prices, screenshots or rules can hurt more than help. Treat refreshes as calendar items, not as something you will get round to:

  1. In Search Console, find posts with impressions but few clicks, or posts that have slipped.
  2. Check facts that change: rules, fees, product names, your own services and contact details.
  3. Answer the questions people now search for, which the Queries tab for that page often shows.
  4. Improve the title and description if they do not match those queries.
  5. Add links to newer articles and service pages.
  6. Change the "updated" date only when the content has genuinely changed.

The full method is in how to update old blog posts.

The 30-minute monthly review

Block half an hour in the last week of each month. In Search Console's Performance report, compare the last 28 days with the period before and note:

  • Pages that gained impressions or clicks: write more around those topics
  • Queries with impressions but few clicks: a clearer title or description may help
  • Questions you have no page for: add them to the question bank
  • Posts with almost no impressions after several months: improve, merge or retire them

Also check which pages lead to calls, WhatsApp messages and form enquiries, not only visits. Finding content ideas in Search Console shows how to dig deeper.

Tools: keep them simple

Tools change and most have free tiers that come and go, so choose by habit rather than features. Google Sheets or Excel handles everything above. Trello, Notion or a similar board helps if two or three people share the work. WordPress itself can schedule posts: set a future date in the editor and the post publishes on time. Editorial calendar plugins exist too, but check they are maintained before adding another plugin to your site.

If writing is the bottleneck, record a voice note answering the question as you would to a customer, then turn it into an article or ask someone to edit it. AI tools can help with outlines and editing, but the facts and examples should be yours; see using AI tools for website content responsibly.

Checklist: is your calendar realistic?

  • Every item answers a real customer question
  • Every item links to a service page
  • Festival topics are scheduled four to eight weeks ahead, with dates checked for this year
  • At least one refresh slot each month or two
  • Each piece has a named owner and due date
  • Reuse is planned, not improvised
  • A monthly review is booked in your diary

FAQs

How often should a small business publish blog posts?

One useful article a month is a sensible start for most small businesses, and two a month if someone has a few hours to give. A steady rhythm kept for a year usually does more than a burst of posts that stops.

How far ahead should I plan festival content?

Plan four to eight weeks ahead and publish at least a few weeks before the date, because people research early. Check dates every year, because festivals set by the lunar calendar fall on different dates each year.

Do Google Business Profile posts replace blog posts?

No. They are short and help your profile look active in Maps and local results, but they don't build lasting pages on your website. Use them to point people to your articles and case studies.

What should a content calendar template include?

Publish week, customer question, content type, the service page it supports, the seasonal hook, owner and due date, status, reuse plan, and the live link with a date to review it again.

Is it worth updating old blog posts instead of writing new ones?

Often, yes. An older post that already has impressions can improve with corrected facts, better answers and a clearer title. Give refreshes a regular slot in the calendar alongside new pieces.
