Fixed Price vs Hourly Billing for Website Projects: Which Is Better, and Which Suits Your Project?

Key takeaways
  • Fixed price suits most website projects with a clear scope, such as a new site, a redesign or a landing page, because you know the total before work starts.
  • Hourly billing suits work nobody can scope in advance, such as diagnosing a slow or broken site, but always agree an estimate and a cap.
  • A common, fair setup is a fixed quote for the build and a monthly plan or fixed-price batches for changes after launch, all written into the agreement.

For most website projects, fixed price is the better choice: a new business website, a redesign, a landing page or a clearly defined feature can be scoped upfront, so you know the total cost before work starts. Hourly billing makes more sense when nobody can know the work in advance, such as finding out why a site is slow or broken, or an open-ended stream of changes. Ongoing work after launch usually fits a monthly plan.

How fixed-price website projects work

You send a brief, the developer asks questions, and you receive a quote for a defined scope: the pages, features, number of revision rounds, who supplies content, and what happens at launch. Work starts once the quote is accepted and the advance is paid.

The advantages: you know the cost upfront, it's easy to budget and compare quotes, and if the work takes longer than the developer expected, that's their problem, not yours.

The disadvantages: it needs a clear scope, changes cost extra, and developers may add a buffer to cover uncertainty. Most arguments on fixed-price projects come from a vague brief, where each side remembers the scope differently.

How hourly billing works

You pay an agreed rate for the time actually spent, usually tracked with a timer or a timesheet and reported weekly or at the end of the job. Freelance platforms often support both models: hourly contracts with time tracking, and fixed-price contracts paid by milestone.

The advantages: it's flexible when requirements are unclear or keep changing, you pay only for work done, and the developer doesn't need to pad the price for unknowns.

The disadvantages: the total is uncertain, it relies on trust and honest time reporting, and you can end up watching the clock instead of the result.

WordPress developer hourly rates in India

Hourly rates vary widely, and there's no standard figure worth quoting. The main factors are experience, specialism (a WooCommerce or custom plugin specialist charges more than a general site builder), freelancer or agency, urgency, and whether the client is in India or abroad. For a small business website, you'll often be offered a per-project price instead; hourly rates come up more on freelance platforms, with international clients and for open-ended agency work.

When you compare hourly quotes, compare the likely total, not the rate. A lower rate with slower work, or with time spent learning on your project, can cost more than a higher rate from someone who has done the same job many times.

Monthly plans and retainers

A fixed monthly fee covers either a set of services, such as updates, backups, security checks and small content changes, or a block of hours. Freelance WordPress maintenance plans for small and medium sites typically cost about ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month; see website maintenance cost in India for what drives the price.

Retainers work well for agencies that send a developer a steady flow of work, as long as everyone agrees how requests are queued and what happens to unused hours; see agency development retainers.

How to avoid scope creep on a fixed-price project

  • Write the scope down: every page, feature, integration and number of revision rounds
  • Agree who supplies content and photos, and by when, because waiting for content is a common reason projects slip
  • Agree how changes are handled: a short written quote for anything outside the scope, approved before work starts
  • Batch small change requests instead of sending them one at a time
  • Keep decisions in one place, such as an email thread or a shared document, not scattered across calls and WhatsApp

How to avoid surprise bills on hourly work

  • Ask for an estimate and a cap before work begins: "up to six hours, check with me before going further"
  • Ask for a time log with short notes on what each block of time was spent on
  • Agree the minimum billing unit and whether calls and emails count
  • Ask to be told as soon as the estimate looks likely to be exceeded, not after

Payment terms and milestones

Fixed-price projects in India usually start with an advance, with the rest paid at milestones or before launch. Tie each payment to something you can see, such as an approved design or a working staging site, rather than to dates. Ask whether the quote includes GST if the developer is registered, and make sure the domain, hosting and admin access are in your name by the end. FAQs

Is fixed price or hourly better for a website project?

Fixed price, for most projects with a clear scope, because you know the cost upfront and the developer carries the risk of overruns. Hourly suits investigations and work whose scope can't be known until it starts.

What is the hourly rate of a WordPress developer in India?

It varies too much for a single figure to be useful: experience, specialism, urgency and freelancer or agency all change it. Compare likely totals rather than rates, and for a defined project, ask for a fixed quote instead.

Why do fixed-price website quotes vary so much?

Because quotes often cover different things: custom design or a template, who writes the content, how many revisions, testing, training and support. Send everyone the same brief; see how to compare website quotes.

What if I want changes after the fixed price is agreed?

Small corrections within the agreed scope are part of the job. New pages or features are quoted separately and approved before work starts, so the original price stays fixed and you decide on each addition.

Should I pay the full amount upfront?

For a website build, usually not. An advance followed by payments at milestones, with the balance before launch, protects both sides. Small fixed-price jobs are sometimes paid in full upfront because the amount is small.

Can a project be partly fixed and partly hourly?

Yes. A fixed price for the defined build plus a capped hourly allowance for a part nobody can scope yet, such as a third-party integration, is a sensible middle ground. Write both into the agreement.
