Solar Lead Generation: How Rooftop Solar Companies Can Get More Enquiries Online, Qualify Them and Turn Them Into Site Surveys

Solar lead generation for installers: ad landing pages, forms asking roof type and bill range, WhatsApp follow-up, call tracking and local SEO.

Key takeaways
  • Match each lead source to the right page: search ads and Google Maps bring buyers who are ready to compare, while social ads and articles bring people who need more convincing.
  • Qualify with a few questions that change your proposal, such as property type, PIN code, roof type and bill range, and judge campaigns by surveys booked rather than form fills.
  • Speed of follow-up decides many solar sales, so reply instantly, call as soon as you can, use WhatsApp for documents and track every lead through to survey and order.

Solar lead generation works when three things line up: the right channels bring people who are actually considering solar, a focused page and a short qualifying form turn them into enquiries your team can price, and fast follow-up turns those enquiries into site surveys before a competitor gets there. Most installers who complain about lead quality have a problem in one of those three places, not a shortage of traffic.

I build websites for solar and power companies and landing pages for ad campaigns, with quote forms, WhatsApp and conversion tracking set up. The solar company website guide covers the pages on the main site. This guide covers the pipeline around it: where leads come from, how to qualify them and what to do in the hours after one arrives.

Landing pages built for solar campaigns

Sending ad clicks to the homepage asks a homeowner to find their own way through commercial projects and battery products. Give each campaign a page about one audience and one offer:

  • Homes: the subsidy process as you handle it, what a survey involves, photos of installations on similar homes in that city, and a survey booking form.
  • Housing societies: what solar can power in common areas, how committee approval works and a request for a presentation to the committee.
  • Businesses: capacity examples from your own projects, the assumptions behind any payback estimate, monitoring and AMC, and a site assessment request.

Keep claims checkable. "Free electricity", "zero bill" or a fixed savings figure in an ad sets up a disappointed customer, and ad platforms review misleading claims too. Show the process, real projects and an honest estimate instead, and keep the page light enough to load quickly on mobile data, because every visitor who leaves while it loads has already cost you the click.

Qualifying forms: what to ask

A solar lead your team can't price is just a phone number. A few well-chosen questions let sales prepare before the first call and spot enquiries you can't serve:

QuestionWhy it mattersHow to ask it
Property typeDecides the proposal and who signs offButtons: independent house, flat in a society, shop or office, factory
PIN codeConfirms you serve the area and which DISCOM appliesA short text field
Monthly bill rangeA first guide to system sizeA dropdown of ranges your team chooses, with "not sure"
Roof typeAffects the mounting structure and the surveyRCC, metal sheet, tiled, or "no roof access"
Do you own the roof?Tenants and flat owners usually need permissionYes, no, or society-owned
Subsidy interestTells sales which process to explainYes, no or not sure
Best time to callRaises the chance of reaching themMorning, afternoon or evening

On a phone, split these into two or three short steps, with property type and PIN code first and contact details last; multi-step forms explains why that order helps. Ask for an electricity bill or roof photos later, on WhatsApp, rather than on a public form. For Meta instant forms, keep to two or three qualifying questions, because every extra field there costs completions.

A follow-up playbook for the first days

Solar buyers often contact several installers, and the first company to have a useful conversation is at an advantage. A simple sequence:

  1. Instantly: an automatic WhatsApp or email reply confirming the request, naming who will call and when, within your real working hours.
  2. As soon as possible: the first call, with the form answers in front of the caller, so the conversation starts with their roof and bill rather than "how can I help?".
  3. If they don't answer: a WhatsApp message with your name, a link to projects in their area and a request for a convenient time.
  4. Before the survey: ask for a recent bill and roof photos on WhatsApp, and confirm the date and surveyor.
  5. After the survey: send the proposal with its assumptions stated, then follow up on agreed dates rather than every day.

Give every lead one owner and a status (new, contacted, survey booked, proposal sent, won, lost with a reason), in a shared sheet or CRM. If you message customers through the WhatsApp Business Platform, follow-ups sent long after the customer's last message generally need pre-approved templates under Meta's current rules, so prepare them in advance. How to follow up website leads covers alerts and auto-replies. On a busy day, call first the leads whose answers fit your best customers: own roof, a bill range worth a system, and a PIN code you serve.

Tracking calls, chats and real outcomes

Form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and calls should all be counted as conversions in GA4 and your ad accounts, and every ad link should carry UTM tags. Calls need extra care, because a tap on the call button doesn't tell you whether the call connected; call tracking for small businesses explains tracking numbers per source and how to use them without upsetting your Google listing.

The figure that matters is not cost per lead but cost per survey booked and per order. Record which source each surveyed and signed customer came from, review it monthly, and move budget toward the campaigns that produce surveys. Google Ads can also accept offline conversions, so the platform learns from sales rather than form fills once you have enough of them.

Local SEO for steady leads without ads

  • Google Business Profile: the closest category Google offers, your service areas, photos of real installations and a link to your survey booking page.
  • Reviews: ask every customer after handover and reply to each one; genuine reviews mentioning the work done carry weight with the next buyer.
  • City or district pages: only where you have genuinely installed, each with local projects, the DISCOM and anything specific to that area; SEO for service-area businesses covers how to do this without thin copies.
  • Articles: the subsidy process in your state, net metering with your DISCOM, monsoon performance and cleaning, each linking to the calculator or survey booking.

Write FAQs in the words customers use, including the localities and districts you cover, and answer the questions your sales team hears on every call: how long installation takes, what happens on cloudy days, who handles the subsidy paperwork and what maintenance involves.

FAQs

What is the best way to get solar leads?

For most installers, a mix: Google search ads and a strong Google Business Profile for buyers who are ready now, articles and city pages that keep bringing researchers, and referrals from happy customers. Whatever the channel, a focused landing page and fast follow-up decide how many become surveys.

Are bought or shared solar leads worth it?

They can be, but shared leads are often sold to several installers at once, so the fastest caller usually wins. Test a small batch, track how many become surveys and orders, and compare that with your own campaigns.

What should a solar lead form ask?

Property type, PIN code, a monthly bill range, roof type, whether they own the roof and a good time to call. Ask for bills and photos later on WhatsApp.

How quickly should we contact a solar lead?

As soon as you reasonably can during working hours, with an instant automatic reply in the meantime. Buyers comparing installers tend to go with the first one who gives them useful answers.

Google Ads or Facebook ads for solar?

Google search ads reach people already looking for an installer, so leads tend to be closer to buying. Facebook and Instagram can reach more people at a lower cost per lead, but they need stronger qualification. Many installers test both and judge them by surveys booked.

If your leads are plentiful but rarely turn into surveys, the fix is usually the page, the form and the follow-up.
