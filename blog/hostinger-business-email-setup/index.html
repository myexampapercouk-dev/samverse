How to Set Up Business Email on Hostinger With Your Own Domain: Mailboxes, DNS Records, Phone Apps and WordPress Forms

Key takeaways
  • Create the mailbox in hPanel, then make sure MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records point at Hostinger, copying the exact values hPanel shows for your domain.
  • Use IMAP, not POP, on phones and Outlook so every device sees the same mailbox, and keep one SPF record that lists every service sending mail for your domain.
  • Send WordPress form emails through your Hostinger mailbox with an SMTP plugin, using the same address as the sender, then test that enquiries reach the inbox, not spam.

To set up business email on Hostinger, create the mailbox in hPanel's Emails section, make sure your domain's MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records point at Hostinger, then sign in through webmail or add the account to your phone or Outlook using IMAP and SMTP. If your website runs on WordPress, send its form emails through that mailbox with an SMTP plugin, so enquiries reach your inbox rather than spam.

An address like info@yourbusiness.in looks more trustworthy than a Gmail address, but only if mail actually arrives. On my WordPress website development projects, forms are tested before launch to make sure their emails arrive, and a mailbox with missing DNS records is one of the common reasons they don't. Hostinger changes its plans, limits and menu names from time to time, so treat the labels below as a guide at the time of writing and check hPanel for what your plan includes.

Before you start

  • A domain you control, registered with Hostinger or elsewhere.
  • An email plan. Some Hostinger hosting plans include mailboxes, sometimes for a limited period; others need a separate email plan. hPanel shows what you have.
  • Where your DNS is managed. If the domain uses Hostinger's nameservers, hPanel can usually add the email records for you. If DNS sits with another registrar or Cloudflare, you add them there by hand.
  • The addresses you need. Decide who needs a real mailbox and which addresses can simply forward to someone.

Step 1: Create your mailboxes in hPanel

  1. Log in to hPanel and open Emails, then choose your domain. If the domain isn't listed yet, hPanel prompts you to connect it.
  2. Create the mailbox, such as name@yourbusiness.in, with a long, unique password.
  3. Add more mailboxes for people who need their own inbox and sent folder.
  4. For shared addresses such as info@ or sales@, consider a forwarder or alias that delivers to a real person's mailbox, rather than a shared login.

Give each person their own mailbox. Shared passwords are hard to change when someone leaves, and you lose track of who sent what.

Step 2: Add the MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records

These DNS records decide whether your email is delivered, and whether other servers trust it:

  • MX tells other mail servers where to deliver mail for your domain.
  • SPF is a TXT record listing which services may send mail as your domain.
  • DKIM publishes a key that lets receivers check your messages were signed by your provider and not altered.
  • DMARC tells receivers what to do with mail that fails those checks, and where to send reports.

If your domain uses Hostinger's nameservers, hPanel normally offers to set these up automatically. Otherwise, open hPanel's email DNS or configuration section, copy each record exactly, and add it where your DNS is managed. The values below are examples of the format only; use the exact values hPanel shows for your domain:

Type    Name                  Value (example only)                         Priority
MX      @                     mx1.hostinger.com                            5
MX      @                     mx2.hostinger.com                            10
TXT     @                     v=spf1 include:_spf.mail.hostinger.com ~all
CNAME   selector._domainkey   the DKIM target hPanel gives you
TXT     _dmarc                v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.com

Three mistakes cause most problems:

  • Two SPF records. A domain must have only one. If your website, a newsletter tool or Google Workspace also sends mail, merge their include entries into a single SPF record.
  • Old MX records left behind from a previous provider, so some mail goes to the old server.
  • Skipping DMARC. Start with p=none to collect reports, then tighten it once you are sure every legitimate sender passes.

DNS changes can take from minutes to a few hours to spread, occasionally longer. SPF, DKIM and DMARC explained covers what each record does in more detail.

Step 3: Check that mail flows both ways

  1. Send a message from your new address to a personal Gmail account, and reply to it.
  2. In Gmail, open the message, choose Show original from the three-dot menu and check that SPF, DKIM and DMARC all say PASS.
  3. Send to an Outlook or Yahoo address too, since each provider filters differently.
  4. If anything fails or lands in spam, recheck the records in Step 2 before changing anything else.

Step 4: Webmail, phones and Outlook

You can read mail in Hostinger's webmail straight away; at the time of writing it opens from hPanel or at mail.hostinger.com. For phones and desktop apps, use IMAP, which keeps the mailbox on the server so your phone, laptop and webmail all show the same messages. POP downloads mail to one device and is best avoided.

SettingTypical Hostinger value (confirm in hPanel)
Incoming server (IMAP)imap.hostinger.com, port 993, SSL/TLS
Outgoing server (SMTP)smtp.hostinger.com, port 465, SSL/TLS
UsernameYour full email address
PasswordThe mailbox password
  • Gmail app on Android or iPhone: add an account, choose Other, enter your address, pick IMAP, then enter the servers above.
  • iPhone Mail: in Settings, add a mail account under Other, choose IMAP and enter the same details.
  • Outlook on a computer: add an account, open the advanced options to set it up manually, choose IMAP and enter the servers and ports.

Google has been phasing out Gmail on the web's option to fetch mail from other accounts, so don't build your setup around it. Use the Gmail app over IMAP, or forward mail if you only need to read it.

Step 5: Send WordPress form emails through your mailbox

By default WordPress sends mail through the server's PHP mail function, without proper authentication, and those messages often vanish or land in spam. Routing them through your Hostinger mailbox over SMTP fixes most of this:

  1. Create a mailbox for the website, such as website@yourbusiness.in, or use an existing one.
  2. Install one SMTP plugin, such as WP Mail SMTP, FluentSMTP or Post SMTP.
  3. Enter smtp.hostinger.com, port 465 with SSL, authentication on, the full email address as the username and the mailbox password.
  4. Set the From address to that same mailbox. Mail sent from one address but authenticated as another is a common reason for rejection.
  5. In your form plugin, put the visitor's email in the Reply-To field, not the From field, so you can reply directly.
  6. Send the plugin's test email, then submit your real contact form and check the inbox.

Mailboxes have sending limits, so a busy store sending order emails may need a transactional email service instead. Fixing WordPress emails with SMTP covers that choice, and why contact forms stop getting enquiries covers the problems that aren't about email.

Switching providers or moving hosts

Email and website hosting are separate services, and most email disasters happen when one is changed without thinking about the other.

  • Moving your email to Hostinger: create the mailboxes first, copy old mail across (with an import tool if hPanel offers one, or by adding both accounts to a desktop mail app and moving folders), then switch the MX and SPF records.
  • Moving your website to Hostinger: note every existing DNS record before changing nameservers, or email hosted elsewhere stops arriving; see migrating a WordPress site to Hostinger.
  • Leaving Hostinger hosting but keeping the email: check whether your mailboxes are part of the hosting plan before cancelling it.

Keeping email records intact during a move is part of my WordPress migration service, and mailboxes are set up before the MX records change.

Good habits for business email

  • A strong, unique password for every mailbox, stored in a password manager.
  • Forwarders for shared addresses, so nobody shares a login.
  • Delete or reset the mailboxes of people who leave, after forwarding or exporting what you need.
  • Track the email plan's renewal date, which may differ from the hosting and domain renewals.
  • If customers report receiving spam "from you", act quickly; see hosting accounts sending spam emails.

FAQs

Is Hostinger business email free?

Some Hostinger hosting plans have included mailboxes, sometimes free for a limited period, while others need a paid email plan. Offers change often, so check what hPanel lists for your plan and when it renews.

What are Hostinger's IMAP and SMTP settings?

At the time of writing, IMAP is imap.hostinger.com on port 993 and SMTP is smtp.hostinger.com on port 465, both with SSL, using your full email address and mailbox password. Confirm them in hPanel's email configuration section.

Why are my Hostinger emails going to spam?

Usually a missing or duplicate SPF record, DKIM not set up, or no DMARC record. New domains also have little sending reputation, so avoid bulk mailing from a new mailbox. Check the Show original view in Gmail to see which test fails.

Can I use my Hostinger email in the Gmail app?

Yes. Add it as an "Other" account over IMAP in the Gmail app on Android or iPhone, and you can read and send as your business address alongside your personal Gmail.

Can my website be on Hostinger and my email on Google Workspace?

Yes. Point the MX records at Google, keep the website's A record pointing at Hostinger, and include both services in your single SPF record if the website sends mail too.
