WordPress vs Google Sites: Is a Free Website Good Enough for Your Business? Features, SEO, Custom Domains, Forms and Cost Compared

Key takeaways
  • Google Sites is free, simple and reliable, which makes it fine for event pages, internal sites, quick tests and stopgaps.
  • It has no blog, payments or bookings and very little SEO control, so it struggles once your website needs to bring in enquiries from Google.
  • If you start on Google Sites, connect your own domain from day one, so you can move to WordPress later without losing your address.

Google Sites is good enough when you need a simple, free page quickly: an event, an internal team site, a test of a business idea or a stopgap while a proper website is built. It isn't built to be a business website that wins enquiries from Google, because it has no blog, no payments or bookings, basic forms and very little SEO control. WordPress costs money to build and run, but gives you full control over design, content, SEO and features, and it grows with the business.

If your website needs to bring in customers rather than just confirm you exist, that's what WordPress website development is for: service pages, forms, WhatsApp, SEO foundations and a site you can edit yourself after launch. What Google Sites does well

Google Sites is a simple drag-and-drop page builder that comes free with a Google account. Its strengths are real:

  • No cost for the builder or hosting, and no servers to manage
  • Easy editing, with several people able to work on the same site, much like a Google Doc
  • Google integrations: embed Docs, Sheets, Slides, Forms, Maps, Calendar and YouTube videos
  • Mobile layouts handled automatically
  • Security and uptime handled by Google

That makes it good for internal team sites, event and workshop pages, project pages, school or class information and quick one-page summaries. It was never designed to be a full business website platform, and that's where the gaps appear.

Where Google Sites falls short for a business

  • Design: a handful of themes and layouts, so your site looks like many others, with little control over fonts, spacing or the header.
  • No blog: there are no posts, categories or feeds, which makes regular content hard.
  • No payments or bookings: you can link to a payment page from your gateway or show a UPI QR code, but there's no cart, checkout or booking calendar.
  • Basic forms: usually an embedded Google Form, which looks different from your site and can't easily feed your CRM or send a WhatsApp alert.
  • Limited tracking: you can connect Google Analytics, but tracking calls, WhatsApp clicks and form submissions properly is hard.
  • No plugins: anything Google hasn't built in has to be embedded from another service.

Free plans on other website builders share similar limits, and usually add the platform's branding or a subdomain on top.

Custom domain, email and looking professional

A new Google Sites site lives at an address like sites.google.com/view/yourbusiness. On a visiting card or a quotation, that looks temporary, and customers comparing you with two or three competitors notice. B2B buyers, investors and job applicants do too.

Google Sites does support custom domains. At the time of writing, Google's instructions map an address such as www.yourbusiness.com to the site after you verify the domain, with the bare domain forwarded at your registrar; follow Google's current help pages, as the steps change. Use business email on the same domain rather than a free address; business email options compares the choices.

There's also platform risk. Free products change or disappear. Google switched off the free websites it used to offer through Business Profiles in 2024, and businesses relying on them had to find another home quickly. Owning your domain is what lets you move without starting from zero.

SEO: can a Google Sites page rank?

Yes, Google can index a Google Sites page, and it may show up when someone searches your exact business name. Being a Google product gives it no ranking advantage, though.

Ranking for searches like "physiotherapist in Indore" or "packaging supplier in Ahmedabad" usually needs more than Google Sites offers:

  • Separate, detailed pages for each service and area, with control over each page's title and description
  • Clean, readable URLs you choose, and redirects when they change
  • Structured data (schema) telling Google your address, hours and services
  • A blog or resources section that answers customer questions
  • Fast, mobile-friendly pages you can tune, and an XML sitemap you control

For local businesses, your Google Business Profile often matters more than your website for "near me" searches, and it's free whichever platform you choose. Start with the Google Business Profile checklist.

What drives the cost of each

Google Sites costs nothing beyond the domain if you connect one. The real cost is your time, and what the limits cost you in missed enquiries.

With WordPress, you pay for a domain and hosting every year, the build, any premium plugin licences, and maintenance, whether you do it yourself or pay someone. The build is the biggest part, and it moves with the number of pages, the design, features such as payments or bookings, and how much content help you need. When Google Sites is genuinely enough

  • You're testing a business idea and need something live this week
  • You need a simple page for an event, a workshop, a class or an internal team
  • You're a small local shop that gets customers through walk-ins, Google Maps and WhatsApp
  • You need a stopgap while a proper website is being built

If you go with Google Sites, connect your own domain from day one, register that domain in your own account, and put your phone number and WhatsApp link on every page. For shops, see does a local shop need a website?

When it's time for WordPress

  • Your website should bring in enquiries, not just confirm you exist
  • You need service pages, location pages or a blog to rank on Google
  • You want online payments, appointment booking or a product catalogue
  • Forms should reach your CRM, a Google Sheet or WhatsApp automatically, with tracking
  • You sell to businesses, or need to look credible to investors and partners
  • You want the design to look like your brand, not a template

A WordPress site doesn't have to start big. Five or six well-written pages can launch first, with more added as the business grows.

Moving from Google Sites to WordPress later

There's no direct export from Google Sites to WordPress, so content is copied across and pages are rebuilt, usually with better structure along the way. The steps:

  1. List every page and its current address.
  2. Collect the content: copy the text, and gather the original image files rather than saving the resized versions from the pages.
  3. Build and test the new site on a staging link, with improved page titles, descriptions and service pages.
  4. Point your domain to the new hosting, removing the Google Sites mapping. If the domain stays the same, visitors and Google arrive at the new site.
  5. Redirect old page addresses that change to their new equivalents, so links and rankings carry across; redesigning without losing rankings covers the details.
  6. Update everywhere the site is linked, starting with your Google Business Profile, then submit the new sitemap in Search Console.

If the old site only lived on a sites.google.com address, you can't redirect those URLs. Instead, add a clear "we've moved" link to the new site and keep it up for a while. That's one more reason to use your own domain from the start.

FAQs

Is Google Sites free for businesses?

Yes, it's free with a Google account, and also included in Google Workspace. A custom domain is a separate cost, paid to your domain registrar.

Can I use my own domain with Google Sites?

Yes. You verify that you own the domain and map it to the site, following Google's current instructions. It's worth doing from the start, because it makes a later move much easier.

Is Google Sites good for SEO?

It can be indexed and may rank for your business name, but it offers very little control over titles, descriptions, URLs and structured data, and has no blog. For competitive local or service searches, it's usually at a disadvantage.

Can I sell products or take payments on Google Sites?

Not directly. You can link to a payment page from your gateway, embed a form or show a UPI QR code, but there's no cart, checkout or order management.

Can I move my Google Sites website to WordPress?

Yes, by rebuilding it. Content is copied across, your domain is pointed to the new hosting, and old addresses are redirected where they change.
