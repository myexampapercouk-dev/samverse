Google Business Profile Checklist: Setup, Optimisation and Monthly Management

A Google Business Profile checklist for service businesses: setup, categories, services, photos, reviews and a monthly management routine to rank in Maps.

Key takeaways
  • Get the foundations exactly right first: your real business name, the most specific primary category, an accurate address or service area, hours and a link to the most relevant page.
  • Reviews are the biggest ongoing factor, so ask every happy customer with your direct review link, reply to every review and never buy or incentivise them.
  • Manage the profile like a routine: weekly review replies, monthly photos, posts and detail checks, and a quarterly look at categories, services and performance.

To optimise a Google Business Profile, claim and verify it in your business's own Google account, use your real business name, choose the most specific primary category, set an accurate address or service area, hours, phone and website link, add every service with a description, upload real photos, collect and reply to reviews, and keep it updated every month. Your website should show exactly the same name, address and phone number.

Below is the full checklist, from setup to a weekly and monthly management routine. Google My Business was renamed Google Business Profile, so older guides using that name cover the same thing. 1. Claim, verify and secure the profile

  • Search for your business on Google or Maps first. If a profile already exists, claim it rather than creating a duplicate.
  • Verify it. Google chooses the method, often a short video showing your signage, workplace and proof of management. Have the business name visible, and film in one take.
  • Own it from the business's Google account, not a staff member's or agency's personal account. Add others as managers from the profile's settings under people and access.
  • Remove people who shouldn't have access, such as former employees and previous agencies.
  • Report duplicates for the same location, which split reviews and confuse Google.

2. Business information: get this exactly right

  • Business name: your real trading name, as on your signage and website. Adding keywords or the city ("Sharma Dental Clinic Best Dentist Pune") breaks Google's guidelines and risks suspension.
  • Primary category: the most important choice on the profile. Pick the most specific match for your main service, for example "Orthodontist" rather than "Dentist" if that's your focus, or "Air conditioning repair service" rather than "Contractor". Look at which categories the top-ranking competitors use.
  • Secondary categories: add other genuine categories, but only ones you really offer.
  • Address or service area: show your address if customers visit you. If you visit customers, as a plumber or pest control company does, set service areas and hide the address. Virtual offices and addresses you don't staff aren't allowed; see SEO for service-area businesses.
  • Phone: a number someone answers during your listed hours, ideally a local or mobile number rather than a call-centre line.
  • Website: link to the most relevant page, which for a single-location business is usually the homepage, and for branches the branch's own page. Adding UTM tags, such as ?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp, lets you see profile visits separately in Analytics.
  • Hours: regular hours, special hours for festivals and holidays, and "more hours" for things like appointment or delivery times.

3. Services, products, description and attributes

  • Services: add every service you offer, using the suggested ones where they fit and custom ones where they don't, each with a short description in plain language.
  • Products: shops and stores can add products or product categories with photos and prices.
  • Business description: up to 750 characters on what you do, who you help, where and what makes you different. Write it for people; don't repeat keywords or include links or offers.
  • Attributes: fill in the ones that apply, such as wheelchair accessible, online appointments, women-led or languages spoken. They vary by category.
  • Booking or appointment link: if you take bookings online, add the link so customers can book straight from the profile.

4. Photos and videos

  • Logo and a cover photo that represent the business well
  • Exterior photos from the street, so people recognise you when they arrive
  • Interior, team and at-work photos
  • Finished work, products or before-and-after results where appropriate for your industry
  • A few new real photos every month; stock images add nothing

5. Reviews: the biggest ongoing factor

  1. Ask every happy customer, ideally right after a good experience, while they're still with you or on the same day.
  2. Make it one tap. Copy your review link from the profile's "ask for reviews" option and share it on WhatsApp, in invoices and emails, or as a QR code at the counter.
  3. Reply to every review, positive and negative, politely and without sharing customers' private details. For difficult ones, see handling negative reviews professionally.
  4. Never buy reviews, offer discounts for them or ask only happy customers through a filter. All of these break Google's policies, and fake reviews can be removed or the profile restricted.

Templates and ethical ways to ask are in how to get more Google reviews.

6. Posts and updates

  • Posts: share updates, offers, events or recent work with a photo and a button. A short weekly or fortnightly post keeps the profile looking active; see Google Business Profile posts and offers.
  • Questions: Google has been changing how questions and answers appear on profiles, so check what yours currently shows and make sure the information customers see is accurate.
  • Messages: Google ended the built-in chat on Business Profiles in 2024, so make sure your phone number and website contact options are what customers use.

7. Connect it to your website

Your profile and website support each other. Google compares them, and customers often check both.

  • The same business name, address and phone number on both, written the same way
  • A contact page with your hours, address and an embedded map
  • A page on the website for each main service listed on the profile
  • LocalBusiness schema markup with your name, address, phone, hours and profile link
  • A separate page for each branch, linked from its own profile; see websites for businesses with multiple branches
  • Consistent details on directories like Justdial and industry listings; see business directories and citations in India

Google Business Profile management checklist

Once it's set up, a profile needs a little attention regularly rather than a big effort once a year:

WhenTasks
WeeklyReply to new reviews; publish a post; check for suggested edits from Google or the public
MonthlyAdd new photos; check hours, phone and website link; add special hours for upcoming holidays; look at the Performance report
QuarterlyReview categories, services and description; check who has access; compare your profile with the top competitors
After any changeUpdate the profile and website together when you move, change number, add a service or change hours

In the Performance report, watch calls, direction requests, website clicks and the searches that found you. If people find you for services you don't list, add them; if a service gets no visibility, improve its page on your website.

Mistakes that get profiles suspended

  • Keywords or city names added to the business name
  • A virtual office, co-working desk you don't staff, or residential address shown when customers don't visit
  • Several profiles for the same business at the same address
  • Fake, bought or incentivised reviews, including reviews from staff and family
  • Frequent changes to the name, address or category in a short time

If your profile is suspended, fix the cause first, then request reinstatement through Google's appeal process with evidence such as signage photos, a utility bill or a business registration document.

FAQs

Is Google Business Profile the same as Google My Business?

Yes. Google My Business was renamed Google Business Profile, and the profile is now managed directly from Google Search and Maps. Any Google My Business checklist covers the same profile, though some menus have moved.

How do I optimise my Google Business Profile?

Use your real name, the most specific primary category and accurate details, list every service with a description, add real photos, collect and reply to reviews, post regularly and link to a website that shows the same details. Then keep it updated with a simple weekly and monthly routine.

How often should I update my Google Business Profile?

Reply to reviews as they come in, post weekly or fortnightly, add photos and check details monthly, and review categories and services every quarter. Always update hours before festivals and holidays.

Why is my business not showing on Google Maps?

Common reasons are an unverified or suspended profile, a category that doesn't match what people search, a service area set too wide or too narrow, a duplicate profile, or strong competitors closer to the searcher. Distance from the searcher matters a lot in local results, so you may show for nearby searches only.

Can I have a Google Business Profile without a shop or office?

Yes, if you serve customers at their location. Set it up as a service-area business, list the areas you cover and hide your address. You still need a real address for verification, but it won't be shown publicly.

Should my developer or agency own my Google Business Profile?

No. The business should be the primary owner, with developers and agencies added as managers. That way you keep the profile and its reviews if you change agency, and you can remove access at any time.
