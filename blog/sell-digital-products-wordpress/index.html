How to Sell Digital Products on WordPress: E-books, Courses, Templates and Software, With File Protection, Licence Keys, GST and Refunds

Sell e-books, courses, templates and software on WordPress: WooCommerce downloads vs dedicated plugins, file protection, licence keys, GST and refunds.

Key takeaways
  • WooCommerce downloadable products suit a few files or a mix of digital and physical products, while dedicated download plugins suit software with licence keys and LMS plugins suit courses.
  • Serve files through the store rather than public links, require login where it makes sense, set download limits, and make sure delivery emails reach the inbox.
  • Digital products can be taxed and refunded differently from physical goods, so agree GST treatment with your CA and publish a clear refund policy before launch.

To sell digital products on WordPress, use WooCommerce's virtual and downloadable products if you sell a handful of files or mix digital and physical products, a dedicated downloads plugin if you mainly sell software with licence keys, or an LMS plugin if you sell courses. Then protect the files, deliver them by email and in the customer's account, connect an Indian payment gateway, check GST treatment with your CA and publish a clear refund policy.

As part of WooCommerce development, I can set up downloadable products, secure file delivery, the payment gateway and branded order emails, tested with test orders before launch. Each product also needs a page that persuades people to buy, which is where landing page design comes in.

Selling ongoing access rather than one-off files? See membership websites on WordPress, or selling subscriptions with WooCommerce for monthly or yearly billing.

What you can sell

  • E-books and guides: PDFs, workbooks and recipe books.
  • Online courses: recorded lessons, test series and cohort programmes.
  • Templates and printables: design templates, spreadsheets, planners and worksheets.
  • Creative assets: photo presets, stock photos, fonts and music.
  • Software: plugins, themes, desktop tools or scripts with licence keys.

Setting up a downloadable product in WooCommerce

  1. Go to Products > Add New and enter the name, description and images.
  2. In the Product data box, keep Simple product and tick both Virtual and Downloadable. Virtual removes shipping; Downloadable adds the file fields.
  3. Under Downloadable files, click Add File, give the file a clear name and choose or upload the file.
  4. Set a Download limit and Download expiry, or leave them blank for unlimited.
  5. Set the price and publish, then place a test order.

When a digital-only order is paid, WooCommerce normally marks it complete and the customer gets their download links straight away. Many stores also trim checkout fields for digital-only orders, but keep the details your invoices need, such as the billing state, which decides the GST place of supply.

Protecting your files

WooCommerce's file settings live under WooCommerce > Settings > Products > Downloadable products:

  • File download method: choose a method that serves the file through your store, such as Force downloads or X-Accel-Redirect/X-Sendfile if your host supports it. Avoid redirect-only mode, which exposes the file's real address.
  • Access restriction: require login for downloads if buyers create accounts, and grant access only after payment.
  • Unique file names: the option to append a unique string to file names makes the real file harder to guess.
  • Approved download directories: files should come only from the folders listed there.

Uploaded product files go into a protected folder. On some server setups, such as Nginx, that protection needs a server rule, so ask your host or developer to confirm a direct file address doesn't open. Large files and course videos don't belong on basic shared hosting; use cloud storage or a video host with privacy and domain restrictions.

Licence keys for software, plugins and themes

If you sell software, a licence key controls who gets updates and support. A typical setup:

  • A unique key is generated for each order and shown in the order email and the customer's account.
  • Each key activates on an agreed number of sites or devices.
  • Keys can expire after a set period, often a year, and renewing restores updates and support.
  • Your software checks the key with your site before it updates.

Easy Digital Downloads offers licensing as an add-on, and licence manager plugins exist for WooCommerce. Yearly renewals work best with recurring billing or renewal reminders, which the subscriptions guide linked above covers, including how recurring payments work in India.

Delivery emails and the customer account

  • Deliver in two places: the completed order email carries the download links, and buyers with accounts also find them under My Account > Downloads.
  • Say what happens next on the thank-you page: where the file is, the format, and how to open it on a phone.
  • Check deliverability: if order emails land in spam, buyers assume they've been cheated. Set up SMTP and your domain's email records; see SPF, DKIM and DMARC explained.
  • Handle lost links quickly: from the order screen you can resend the order email to the customer, or regenerate download permissions after replacing a file.
  • Test on a phone: offer PDFs directly where you can, and explain how to open ZIP files when you can't.

Payments, GST and invoices

  • Payments: an Indian gateway gives local buyers UPI, cards and net banking, and international cards or PayPal can be added if you sell abroad.
  • GST caution: digital products can be taxed differently from physical goods. Some may count as what GST calls online information and database access or retrieval (OIDAR) services, sales to overseas buyers follow their own rules, and registration requirements can differ. Agree the treatment with your CA before launch, not after.
  • Invoices: business buyers often need a proper invoice with their GSTIN, so set up an invoice plugin from day one; GST invoices in WooCommerce shows the setup.

Refund policy for digital products

A downloaded file can't be returned, so many sellers don't offer refunds once a file is downloaded, with sensible exceptions:

  • A duplicate purchase.
  • A faulty or wrong file that can't be fixed quickly.
  • For courses, a short refund window before much of the content has been accessed.
  • For software, a product that doesn't work as described after support has tried to help.

State the policy on the product page and at checkout. Payment gateways generally expect a visible refund policy too, and consumer protection rules still apply. Returns and refunds for WooCommerce stores covers how to issue a refund in WooCommerce.

Sales pages that sell digital products

  • One page per product: who it's for, what's inside, the format, file size or course length, and what buyers get straight after paying.
  • Samples: a free chapter, preview lesson or template screenshots.
  • Honest reviews from real buyers, added as they arrive.
  • An FAQ covering access, devices, updates and refunds.
  • A free lead magnet to build an email list for launches and new products.
  • Fast loading on mobile, with the price and buy button easy to find.

For paid ads, a focused page with one product and one action usually works better than sending people to the shop page.

Piracy: what you can and can't control

Any file a buyer can download can be shared. You can make sharing less convenient and buying more attractive:

  • Stamp PDFs with the buyer's name or email; plugins can do this automatically.
  • Keep courses behind a login rather than offering video downloads.
  • Use licence keys for software, tied to updates and support.
  • Add value pirated copies lack: updates, support, community access or certificates.

Don't let anti-piracy measures annoy paying customers; overly strict limits hurt genuine buyers more than pirates.

Launch checklist

  1. Test orders in the gateway's test mode, then one real low-value order.
  2. Download links work from the email and from My Account, on a phone and a computer.
  3. A direct file address doesn't open without going through the store.
  4. Invoices, the refund policy and contact details are in place.
  5. Analytics records purchases, so you know which pages and campaigns sell.

Pricing, bundles and coupons for digital products

Digital products have no stock and no shipping, so the structure of your offer does more work than it would in a physical store. A few patterns that WooCommerce handles without custom code:

  • Tiers by licence, not by file. A template sold as "personal use" and "client use", or a plugin sold for one site and for several sites, is usually one variable product with a licence attribute. Each variation can deliver the same file but carry a different licence key limit and a different terms page.
  • Bundles. A set of e-books or a template pack can be a grouped product, or a bundle product from a bundle plugin, so the buyer gets every file in one order and one email.
  • Launch and festival coupons. WooCommerce coupons can be limited by date, by product, by first order or by number of uses, which keeps a Diwali or launch-week offer from being shared forever.
  • Free products as lead magnets. A free checklist sold at zero through the normal checkout gives you a customer account and an email address with consent, which a plain download link does not.

For Indian buyers, check how the checkout looks on a phone with UPI as the first option. Many digital purchases are impulse buys from Instagram or WhatsApp, and a long checkout asking for a shipping address loses them. Turn off shipping fields for virtual products, keep billing fields to what your GST invoice needs, and test a real UPI payment before launch. The guide to accepting online payments on WordPress in India covers gateway choice and KYC.

If you also sell internationally, decide early whether you will show prices in INR only or in other currencies too, because currency switching, international cards and tax rules for overseas buyers each add setup and testing. Starting with INR and adding currencies once foreign orders actually arrive is often the calmer route.

Upsells that don't feel pushy

Digital buyers are often open to one sensible add-on: the workbook that goes with an e-book, the extended licence for a template, or a one-to-one call after a course. Offer it on the product page or as a single order bump at checkout, not as a chain of pop-ups. Track how many orders take the add-on before adding more.

FAQs

Can I sell digital products with WooCommerce for free?

WooCommerce itself is free, and downloadable products are built in. You still pay for hosting, the gateway's transaction charges and any premium plugins you add, such as licensing or PDF stamping.

Should I use WooCommerce or Easy Digital Downloads?

WooCommerce suits stores that sell physical products too, or want its wider range of extensions. Easy Digital Downloads suits sites that only sell files or software. Both work; choose by the features you need.

How do I stop people sharing download links?

Require login for downloads, set download limits and an expiry, and serve files through the store rather than a public address. You can't stop sharing entirely, but you can make it inconvenient.

Do I need GST registration to sell e-books or courses?

It depends on your turnover, who you sell to and how your products are classified, and the rules for digital products can differ from physical goods. Ask your CA before you launch.

Can I sell online courses through WooCommerce?

Yes. Several LMS plugins integrate with WooCommerce, so the course is sold as a product and access is granted after payment.

Should I sell digital products on my own WordPress site or on a marketplace?

A marketplace brings buyers but takes a cut and keeps the customer relationship. Your own site keeps the customer list and the margin but needs you to bring traffic. Many sellers use both, with the full range and best offers on their own site.
