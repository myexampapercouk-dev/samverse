Websites for Signage, LED Board and Branding Companies

What signage, LED board and branding companies need on their website: a strong project portfolio, sign type pages, detailed quote forms and B2B rollout pages.

Key takeaways
  • Organise your portfolio by sign type and sector, with day and night photos of real installations.
  • Quote forms should capture size, indoor or outdoor, lit or non-lit, location and an artwork upload, with WhatsApp for site photos.
  • Pages showing rollout capacity, brand consistency and after-sales support help win retail chains and corporate clients.

A café owner opening next month needs a lit board over the shopfront. A retail brand needs the same signage made and installed across twenty outlets in different cities. A hospital wants wayfinding signs for a new block. Each of them will look at your website before asking for a quote, and each wants to know the same things: have you made signs like this before, will they look good and last, and how quickly can you deliver and install?

This guide is for companies that design, fabricate and install signage: shop boards, LED and acrylic letters, glow signs, neon, digital displays, wayfinding and in-store branding. If your main work is printed products and packaging, see websites for printing and packaging companies instead.

What signage buyers want to know

  • Proof of work: real signs you have made, ideally for businesses like theirs
  • Options: the sign types, materials and lighting you offer, and what suits their budget
  • Durability: how your signs cope with sun, dust and monsoon rain
  • End-to-end service: whether you handle design, fabrication and installation, or only part of it
  • Timelines and coverage: how long a typical job takes and which cities you install in
  • After-sales: who fixes it when a letter stops glowing

Answer these clearly and visitors won't need to call three competitors just to compare the basics.

A portfolio that sells the work

Signage is visual, so your portfolio is the most important part of the site. Organise it so buyers can find relevant examples quickly:

  • Filter projects by sign type (ACP boards, LED letters, neon, wayfinding) and by sector (retail, restaurants, clinics, offices, real estate)
  • For each project, note what was made, the materials, approximate size, lighting and the city
  • Show lit signs in both daytime and night-time photos, because customers will see them both ways
  • Include before-and-after shots of shopfronts where you can
  • Name clients or show their logos only with permission

For bigger jobs such as multi-outlet rollouts or large facades, write short case studies: the brief, the challenge (a tight deadline, difficult access or strict brand guidelines), what you delivered and how installation went. See how to write case studies for your website.

Pages for each sign type

People search for specific kinds of signage, so give each major type its own page:

Sign typeWhat buyers usually ask
ACP boards with 3D lettersFinish options, lit or non-lit, how well they last outdoors
LED and acrylic lettersFront-lit or back-lit, letter sizes, power use, warranty on LEDs
Glow sign boards and light boxesBudget options, replacing the printed face later
LED neon signsCustom text and logos, colours, indoor use
Digital LED displays and video wallsScreen size, viewing distance, how content is updated
Wayfinding and safety signsStandard designs, quantities, installation across floors
Office and in-store brandingReception logos, wall graphics, window films, display stands

Each page should show real photos, the materials and lighting options you offer, typical uses, what affects the price (size, material, lighting, installation height and access) and a clear quote button. Short guides such as "ACP or acrylic signage?" or "front-lit vs back-lit letters" help buyers decide and bring in extra search traffic.

Quote forms that capture the right details

Signage prices depend heavily on specifications, so a well-planned quote form saves both sides time. Ask for:

  1. Sign type, with a "not sure, please suggest" option
  2. Approximate width and height
  3. Indoor or outdoor, lit or non-lit
  4. Logo and artwork upload (AI, CDR, PDF or a clear image)
  5. Installation address, floor or height, and whether an old sign needs removing
  6. Quantity or number of locations
  7. Deadline, such as a store opening date

Add a WhatsApp button so customers can send photos of the shopfront or wall, which often tells you more than the form does; see adding WhatsApp to your website. For larger projects, offer a site visit for measurement. Rather than listing fixed prices, explain what drives the cost so enquiries arrive with realistic expectations.

Pages for brands and multi-location clients

Retail chains, franchises, banks, hospitals, builders and corporate offices bring repeat, higher-value work. They want to see that you can deliver consistently at scale:

  • Rollout capability: how you handle surveys, production and installation across many sites, and which cities you cover directly or through installation partners
  • Brand consistency: matching colours, fonts and specifications to brand guidelines at every outlet
  • Project management: a single point of contact, progress updates and photos after each installation
  • Vendor paperwork: GST details, a downloadable company profile and references
  • Sector pages: retail, hospitality, healthcare, real estate or corporate offices, only where you have real projects to show

If you run Google Ads for searches like "LED sign board maker", send that traffic to a focused landing page with relevant portfolio examples and a short quote form, not to your homepage.

Trust signals that matter

  • Photos or a short video of your workshop, machines and team, showing what you make in-house
  • Clear warranty terms for LED modules, power supplies and installation, including what isn't covered
  • Safety practices for working at height and handling electrical connections
  • Repair, relighting and maintenance services for existing signs, including ones made by others
  • Genuine Google reviews, years in business and your workshop address

Outdoor signs and hoardings may need approval from the local municipal body, and the rules differ from city to city. If you help clients with this, say so, but check current local rules and never promise approvals.

Local SEO and speed

Most signage work comes from your own city and nearby towns, through searches like "sign board maker in {city}", "LED sign board {city}", "ACP signage {area}" and "neon sign shop near me". To show up:

  • Keep your Google Business Profile updated with photos of recent installations
  • Ask happy clients for a review once their sign is up and working
  • Create city pages only where you genuinely install, with local projects on each
  • Add FAQs about materials, lighting, timelines and maintenance

Portfolio-heavy sites get slow quickly. Compress photos, use thumbnails that open into larger images, avoid autoplaying videos and heavy sliders, and test the site on a phone over mobile data.
