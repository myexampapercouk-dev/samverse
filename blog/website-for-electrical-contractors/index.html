Websites for Electrical Contractors: Winning Commercial and Industrial Work

What commercial and industrial electrical contractors need on their website: service pages, licences, project portfolio, safety, RFQ enquiries and AMC plans.

Key takeaways
  • Give each type of electrical work its own service page and state your licence class, number and validity exactly.
  • Project pages with real scope, scale, constraints and photos prove capacity better than claims about quality.
  • An RFQ form with BOQ and drawing uploads, a safety page and a clear AMC offer turn visits into business.

When a factory is expanding, a warehouse needs a new substation or a hospital is looking for a maintenance partner, the people choosing an electrical contractor rarely pick from a phone directory. Plant heads, project consultants, EPC companies and purchase teams search, shortlist and then check your website before they send an RFQ. This guide is for contractors doing commercial and industrial work; if you mainly handle household repairs, see websites for home services instead.

What buyers check before shortlisting you

Commercial buyers are cautious because a bad electrical contractor means shutdowns, safety risks and failed inspections. They look for:

  • Scope: the type of work you do and the voltage levels you handle, LT only or HT as well
  • Licences: the contractor licence you hold and its class or grade
  • Similar projects: work for plants, buildings or clients like theirs
  • Capacity: team size, supervisors, engineers, tools and testing equipment
  • Safety: how you work on live sites and in running plants
  • After-handover support: whether you can maintain what you install

Service pages, one per type of work

A single "Our Services" page listing everything tells a buyer very little. Give each major service its own page, for example:

  • HT and LT electrical installations
  • Substations and transformer installation
  • LT panels, switchgear and power distribution
  • Cabling, cable trays and industrial wiring
  • Factory, warehouse and outdoor lighting
  • Earthing and lightning protection
  • Testing and commissioning

Each page should explain the scope, the kind of clients you do it for, the brands and standards you usually work with, related projects and a clear enquiry button. Only list services your own team actually delivers. It also helps to add short "industries served" pages (manufacturing, warehouses, hospitals, commercial buildings, housing societies) that link back to the relevant services.

Licences and credentials, stated precisely

This is where many contractor websites are vague, and where buyers are most careful. Be specific and honest:

  • Contractor licence: the licence issued by your state's licensing board or electrical inspectorate, with its class or grade, number and validity. Names and grades vary from state to state, so use the exact wording on your certificate.
  • Key people: supervisors and engineers with their certificates of competency, where you hold them
  • Government enlistment: registration with departments such as CPWD or the state PWD, only if you are enlisted, and in which class
  • ISO and other certifications: only if current, with the scope they cover
  • Manufacturer authorisations: only if you genuinely hold them, and use brand logos only with permission

Never show lapsed certificates, and set a reminder to update the page when licences are renewed. Tender teams often cross-check.

A project portfolio that proves capacity

Your projects do more selling than any paragraph about quality. For each key project, include:

DetailWhy it matters
Client type and locationBuyers look for work similar to theirs; name the client only with permission
Scope and scaleActual quantities, such as substation rating, number of panels or length of cabling
Duration and constraintsShutdown windows, working inside a running plant, tight handover dates
PhotosReal photos of panels, cable trays and substations, not stock images

Let visitors filter projects by industry and service. A few detailed write-ups beat fifty photos with no context; see how to write case studies.

Give safety its own page

Many companies now ask about safety during vendor registration, and their EHS teams read this page. Describe what you actually do: permit-to-work procedures, lockout and tagout, PPE, toolbox talks, safety training and calibrated testing instruments. Photos of your team in proper PPE on real sites support the words. Don't claim an accident-free record unless you track it properly and can back it up.

Make tender and RFQ enquiries easy

A generic "name, email, message" form doesn't suit this work. Build an RFQ form that asks for:

  1. Project type and site location
  2. Scope and approximate connected load
  3. Expected start date and timeline
  4. Uploads for the BOQ, drawings or single line diagram
  5. Tender reference number, if there is one
  6. Name, company, role, phone and email

Splitting this across two or three steps keeps it manageable; see multi-step forms. Send enquiries straight to your estimation team, send the visitor an automatic acknowledgement and add spam protection, as upload forms attract junk. Also offer a downloadable company profile PDF with your licences, key projects, team and equipment, since consultants often need one for pre-qualification.

Sell AMC and maintenance

Annual maintenance contracts bring steady income and keep you close to clients for future projects. Give AMC its own page covering:

  • What's maintained: substations, transformers, panels, DG set changeover, earthing
  • Visit frequency and the checks done on each visit
  • Breakdown support and response times you can genuinely commit to
  • Reports the client receives after each visit

Add a short AMC quote form asking for the equipment list, connected load and location.

Local SEO and speed

  • Target searches like "industrial electrical contractor in {city}", "HT electrical contractor {city}" and "substation installation contractor"
  • Mention the industrial areas you serve naturally on service and project pages
  • Keep your Google Business Profile complete, with the most accurate category, real project photos and reviews from clients
  • Compress project photos so pages load quickly for site engineers checking on mobile data
