Websites for Coworking Spaces: Plans Compared, Tour and Day Pass Booking, Meeting Rooms, Virtual Offices and Centre Pages

Plan a coworking space website: hot desk, dedicated desk, cabin and virtual office plans, tours, day passes, meeting room booking, amenities and centres.

Key takeaways
  • Compare every plan in one table, covering who it suits, what is included, access and the minimum term, so visitors sort themselves before they call.
  • Give each thing people book its own short flow: a tour, a day pass, a meeting room slot and a virtual office enquiry each need different questions.
  • Describe virtual office documents without promising GST or company registration approval, and give each centre its own page with real photos and exact amenities.

People shortlist coworking spaces on their phones in a few minutes: price, distance, whether the internet and air conditioning can be trusted, and whether the place feels right in the photos. A coworking website wins them by setting plans side by side, letting them book a tour, a day pass or a meeting room without phoning, and being exact about amenities and documents.

Most of this is a well-planned WordPress website: plan and centre pages your team can edit, forms and WhatsApp, payment links, and SEO basics in place at launch. Campaigns for a new centre or a first-month offer are better served by separate landing pages whose headline matches each ad.

Plans compared in one table

Visitors arrive knowing roughly what they need, but every space names its plans differently. One comparison table settles most questions before the tour. Here is a starting layout; replace the entries with your own terms:

Below the table, answer the questions it raises: whether prices include GST, the security deposit and notice period, what happens if a team grows mid-contract, and whether hot desk members are sure of a seat on busy days. Publishing prices is a business decision. "From" prices for each plan filter out poor fits, and showing prices on your website weighs up both sides.

A tour request that gets people through the door

For desks and cabins, the tour is where the sale happens. Keep the tour form quick and specific:

  1. Name, phone number and company (optional)
  2. Centre, if you have more than one
  3. Plan of interest and number of seats
  4. Expected move-in month
  5. Preferred tour day and time, or a video walk-through on WhatsApp

Send a confirmation with the address, a map pin, parking and entry instructions, and the name of the person who will meet them. If someone asks for an eight-seat cabin and none is free, say so and offer a waitlist, instead of letting them find out halfway through the tour.

Larger teams and company enquiries

A company moving twenty people is a different buyer from a freelancer. Its admin or HR team wants a floor plan, the largest block of seats you can offer together, whether the space can carry its branding, how its own IT equipment and internet can be set up, how access cards are issued, and whether invoices carry its GSTIN correctly. Give these enquiries their own short page and form with seat count, move-in date and a contact person, and route them to whoever handles larger deals, because they rarely book a casual tour first.

Day passes and meeting rooms: book and pay without calling

Day passes and meeting rooms are bought at short notice, often the same morning, so they should not depend on someone picking up the phone.

Day passes

One page with the price, what is included, the hours, what to bring (an ID for the front desk) and a booking form with online payment or a payment link is enough. Say whether day pass users may use phone booths and meeting rooms, and what those cost.

Meeting and conference rooms

For each room, give the seating capacity, layout, screen or projector, video-call setup, whiteboard, and whether tea and coffee are included. Show hourly and half-day rates or how to ask for them, and whether non-members can book. A calendar showing free slots saves the most back-and-forth; if you already use coworking management software with a booking widget, it can be embedded rather than replaced. Online booking on your website compares the options, and accepting online payments in India covers taking payment as people book.

Virtual offices: describe what you provide, not what an officer will decide

Virtual office plans are often bought so a business can show an address for GST registration, company incorporation or a bank account. Whether a particular address is accepted is up to the authority concerned, and the documents asked for have changed over time and can differ between offices. So the page should describe what you supply, and stop there:

  • The documents included, such as a rent or service agreement, a no-objection letter from the property owner and a recent utility bill, as you actually provide them
  • Whether the address may be used for GST registration, as a company's registered office, or only for correspondence
  • How mail and couriers are received, and how members are told
  • Whether officials may visit to verify the address, and how you handle it
  • Meeting room hours included, if any

Avoid phrases such as "guaranteed GST approval" or "registration in [number] days". A line like "we provide the documents commonly requested for GST registration; approval rests with the tax department" is accurate and still persuasive. Suggest that buyers confirm current requirements with their CA.

Amenities, stated the way members check them

"High-speed internet" and "premium amenities" appear on every coworking site. Members test the specifics in their first week, so state them precisely:

  • Internet: how many connections, from how many providers, and whether there is a backup line
  • Power: whether backup covers the whole floor, air conditioning included, or only lights and sockets
  • Hours: when the space is staffed, when the air conditioning runs, and whether members can get in at night or on Sundays
  • Parking: car and two-wheeler spaces, and whether they are free
  • Calls: the number of phone booths, and whether video calls are fine at desks
  • Pantry: what is included, such as tea, coffee or filtered water
  • Access: lift, step-free entry and accessible toilets
  • Printing, lockers and storage: included or charged

Precise claims survive a member's first month; vague ones turn into complaints and one-star reviews.

One page per centre

If you run more than one centre, give each its own page with the address, a map pin, the nearest metro or bus stop, real photos of that floor, its plans and prices, its amenities (which often differ), its meeting rooms and its own Google Business Profile. Use the names people search, such as "coworking space in Baner" or "office space near Hinjewadi Phase 1", only where the centre really is. Websites for businesses with multiple branches covers location pages and profiles.

Photos matter more here than on most business sites. Shoot each centre in daylight on an ordinary working day, include the busy hot desk area as well as the empty boardroom, and add a short walk-through video. If your space is quieter and rents seats to students preparing for exams, that is a different audience; websites for self-study centres and libraries covers it.

Community and events, without the stock photos

Community is a real reason people pick one space over another, but "vibrant community" means nothing without evidence. An events page listing upcoming talks, workshops or networking evenings, with photos from past ones, shows it. A member directory, with each business's consent, helps members find each other and gives visitors a sense of who works there. Keep it current: an events calendar that stopped last year signals the opposite of community.

FAQs

Should a coworking space show prices on its website?

"From" prices for each plan, with GST and deposit terms stated, filter out poor fits and speed up decisions. Cabins and custom team spaces can stay on request.

Can members book meeting rooms on the website?

Yes. A booking calendar with free slots and online payment can be added, or the booking widget from your existing coworking software can be embedded.

Can a virtual office be used for GST registration?

Many are bought for that purpose, but the tax department decides whether an address is accepted. Describe the documents you provide, and advise buyers to check current requirements with their CA.

Does each centre need its own page?

Yes. Each centre has its own address, photos, amenities and searches, and its page should match its Google Business Profile.

Can we sell day passes online?

Yes. A day pass page with a booking form and online payment lets people buy in the morning and walk in the same day.
