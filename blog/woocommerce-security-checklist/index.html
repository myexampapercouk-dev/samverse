WooCommerce Security Checklist: Payments, Checkout, Card-Testing Bots, Staff Accounts and Customer Data

A WooCommerce security checklist: admin roles, payment gateways, PCI basics, card skimmers, card-testing bots, fake orders and protecting customer data.

Key takeaways
  • Start with general WordPress hardening, then add the store layer: a checkout where card details are entered with the gateway, not on your server, and two-factor login for every admin and shop manager.
  • Card-testing bots, skimmers and fake orders target the checkout, so add bot protection there, verify every payment in the gateway dashboard and check the checkout page for scripts you did not add.
  • Every plugin that touches checkout, orders or payments runs on your most sensitive page, so keep that list short, buy only from official sources and update with a test order afterwards.

To secure a WooCommerce store, first apply the general WordPress hardening every site needs, then add the store-specific layer: let your payment gateway handle card details so they never touch your server, give staff their own Shop Manager accounts with two-factor login, keep WooCommerce and every payment-related plugin updated, protect the checkout from card-testing bots, and check payments in the gateway dashboard before you ship. A store holds customers' names, phone numbers, addresses and order histories, and sits in the path of their payments, so it is a bigger target than an ordinary business site.

Admin, Shop Manager and customer accounts

  • One account per person. Never share a login among staff; you lose any record of who did what.
  • Use the Shop Manager role for staff who process orders and edit products. It can't install plugins or switch the theme. Keep Administrator for the one or two people who really need it; see WordPress user roles explained.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication for every administrator and shop manager.
  • Remove accounts for former staff, freelancers and agencies as soon as their work ends.
  • Let customers check out as guests if accounts don't add much for your shoppers. Fewer accounts means fewer passwords to attack. The options are under WooCommerce > Settings > Accounts & Privacy.
  • Protect registration with bot protection if customers can create accounts; see stopping spam registrations.

Protect the accounts around the store too: hosting, domain registrar, the payment gateway dashboard and the email inbox that receives password resets. If any of those is taken over, so is the store.

Payments: keep card details off your server

The safest store never lets a card number reach its own server. Most Indian gateways' WooCommerce plugins either send the customer to the gateway's own payment page or open the gateway's payment window on your checkout, so card details are entered with the gateway. Check how yours works, and avoid any plugin that posts raw card fields to your site. UPI, net banking and wallets avoid card numbers entirely.

  • Never store card numbers, CVVs or UPI PINs, and never ask customers to send them by WhatsApp, email or phone
  • Keep gateway API keys and webhook secrets private, and regenerate them if a developer leaves or you suspect a leak
  • Set up the gateway's webhook with its secret, so order statuses are updated by verified messages from the gateway
  • Make sure the live site uses live keys and test mode is off, and delete gateway plugins you no longer use
  • Confirm payment in the gateway dashboard before shipping, never from a customer's screenshot

Choosing or switching gateway? See how to compare payment gateways in India.

PCI DSS basics for small stores

PCI DSS is the card industry's security standard, and it applies to anyone who accepts card payments. How much of it you have to deal with depends on where card details are entered. In India, RBI rules at the time of writing also stop merchants from storing customers' card details; saved cards work through tokens handled by the card networks and gateways. Your gateway's onboarding documents explain what is expected of you, so ask them if you're unsure.

Checkout typeDo card details touch your server?What you still have to protect
Redirect to the gateway's payment pageNoYour checkout page, so nobody can change where it sends customers
Gateway's payment window or card fields on your checkoutNo, the fields are served by the gatewayEvery script on the checkout page, because a skimmer can add a fake form
Card fields posted to your own serverYesFar more; not a sensible choice for a small store
UPI, net banking, walletsNo card dataPayment verification and order status updates

With a hosted or embedded checkout, your compliance work is usually much lighter, but not zero. At the time of writing, recent versions of PCI DSS also expect merchants to keep control of the scripts on payment pages, because of skimming.

Card-testing attacks

Card testing is when criminals use your checkout to find out which stolen card numbers still work. You'll see a burst of small failed orders in a short time, often guest orders with random names and email addresses, many of them within minutes. Even failed attempts can cost you gateway fees, chargebacks and, in bad cases, a suspended merchant account.

  • Add bot protection such as Cloudflare Turnstile or reCAPTCHA to the checkout, and test that real customers can still pay easily
  • Rate-limit checkout requests at your firewall. The classic checkout submits to /?wc-ajax=checkout and the block checkout to /wp-json/wc/store/v1/checkout, which is where a rule needs to go; see WordPress firewalls explained
  • Turn on any fraud and card-testing protection your gateway offers
  • If an attack is happening now, temporarily turn off card payments or guest checkout, block the worst IP ranges and tell your gateway

Fake orders, COD abuse and order spam

Not every threat is technical. Fake and fraudulent orders cost Indian stores money in shipping, return-to-origin charges and stock held for nothing.

  • Watch for mismatched names and addresses, unusually large quantities, several orders from one phone number to different addresses, and urgent requests to change the delivery address
  • Confirm higher-value or first-time cash on delivery orders by phone or WhatsApp before dispatch, and consider a maximum COD order value
  • Be wary of "payment done" screenshots and refund requests to a different account; check the gateway dashboard every time

The COD side is covered in detail in cash on delivery without losing money.

Plugins that touch payments, checkout and orders

Every plugin that adds checkout fields, upsells, abandoned-cart emails, GST invoices, order exports or SMS and WhatsApp notifications runs on your most sensitive pages or handles customer data. Treat them more strictly than ordinary plugins.

  • Keep the list short; each one is more code on the payment page
  • Choose actively maintained plugins with recent updates, and buy premium ones only from the official marketplace or the developer, with the licence active so updates keep arriving
  • Never install nulled (pirated) plugins or themes on a store
  • Check what each plugin sends to outside services, and which API keys or tokens it stores
  • Don't leave order or customer export files in the uploads folder, where anyone with the address could download them
  • Update them on staging first, then place a test order on the live site

Watch the checkout for skimmers

Card skimmers are scripts injected into a hacked store's checkout that copy what customers type, sometimes by showing a fake card form even when your real gateway is hosted. Signs include an extra payment form, customers reporting card fraud after buying from you, and unfamiliar external scripts in the checkout page's source. Place a test order every month, look at the checkout as a logged-out customer, and turn on file-change alerts. If you suspect one, read WooCommerce card skimming malware straight away.

Protect customer data

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets duties for businesses that collect personal data, and its rules are being phased in, so check current requirements with a lawyer. Good habits help either way:

  • Collect only the details you need to fulfil orders
  • Set personal data retention for inactive accounts and old orders under WooCommerce > Settings > Accounts & Privacy
  • Limit who can view and export orders and customer lists, and don't share exports in WhatsApp groups or open drives
  • Store backups securely, since they contain customer data too

FAQs

Is WooCommerce secure?

Yes, when it's maintained. WooCommerce is actively developed and security fixes are released regularly. Most store compromises come from outdated plugins, weak or shared logins, nulled software and poor hosting.

Does a WooCommerce store need to be PCI compliant?

Any business that accepts card payments falls under PCI DSS, but with a checkout where the gateway handles card details, your part is usually much smaller. Your gateway will tell you what it needs from you.

How do I stop card-testing attacks on WooCommerce?

Add bot protection to the checkout, rate-limit checkout requests at your firewall, and turn on your gateway's fraud tools. During an active attack, pause card payments or guest checkout and contact your gateway.

Can I store customers' card details for faster checkout?

Not on your own site. Saved cards should work through your gateway's tokenisation, so the card number never sits in your database.

Which security plugin is best for a WooCommerce store?

Use one reputable security plugin, such as Wordfence or Solid Security, ideally alongside a cloud firewall. Running two security plugins often causes conflicts and slows the checkout.

How do I know if my checkout has been skimmed?

Customers reporting card fraud after buying from you is the most common sign. Also look for unexpected payment forms and unfamiliar external scripts on the checkout page. Act immediately: take card payments offline, tell your gateway and have the store cleaned.
