Website for Book Publishers and Independent Presses: Catalogue, Submissions and Sales

Planning a website for book publishers in India? What to include: catalogue, author pages, manuscript submissions, and selling on WooCommerce or Shopify.

Key takeaways
  • A publisher website needs a searchable catalogue, clear author pages and a submissions process that sets expectations, before it needs anything fancy.
  • Selling direct works on WooCommerce or Shopify with UPI, cards and COD, but shipping weights, ISBN fields and pre-orders need planning up front.
  • Each book page is a small SEO landing page: unique description, author bio, sample pages and structured data help readers find it on Google.

A good website for book publishers in India does three jobs: it shows the full catalogue in a way readers can browse, it tells authors how to submit work, and it lets readers buy books directly or find them at a retailer. Everything else, from blog posts to events, builds on those three.

This guide is for independent presses, regional-language publishers, academic and children's publishers, and self-publishing imprints. It covers what to include, how to sell on WooCommerce or Shopify, and the mistakes that make publisher sites hard to use.

What a website for book publishers in India should include

Readers, authors, booksellers, librarians and the press all visit a publisher's site, and each wants something different. A clear menu that serves all of them matters more than a striking home page.

VisitorWhat they look forPages that serve them
ReadersNew releases, genres, prices, where to buyCatalogue, book pages, shop
AuthorsWhether you accept their genre, how to submit, response timesSubmissions page, FAQs
Booksellers and librariesISBNs, trade terms, bulk orders, catalogue PDFTrade page, downloadable catalogue
Media and festivalsAuthor bios, photos, review copiesPress kit, author pages
Rights buyersTranslation and rights availabilityRights page or contact

Most small presses can start with the catalogue, book pages, author pages, a submissions page, an about page and a contact page. A news or blog section can follow once there is a rhythm of releases and events.

The catalogue and book pages

The catalogue is the heart of the site. Readers should be able to filter by genre or series, language, age group (for children's books), format and new or forthcoming titles. On WordPress this is usually built with custom post types and taxonomies, or with WooCommerce product categories and attributes if books are sold on the site.

Each book page should include:

  • A high-quality cover image, with alt text that names the title and author.
  • Title, subtitle, author, translator or illustrator, and series.
  • A unique description written for readers, not copied from the back cover alone.
  • Format details: paperback, hardback, ebook, audiobook, page count, dimensions and language.
  • ISBN for each format, publication date and price.
  • A sample chapter or "look inside" pages, where rights allow.
  • Review quotes you have permission to use, with the source named.
  • Buy buttons: your own shop and, if you choose, links to other retailers.

Avoid publishing a book page with only a cover and a price. Thin pages are hard to rank and give readers no reason to buy from you rather than elsewhere. The guide to schema markup explains how structured data such as Book and Product can help search engines understand these pages.

Author pages and the submissions process

Author pages link every book by that author, a short bio, a photo, and links to interviews or events. They help readers who search for an author's name and give the press a ready media kit.

The submissions page saves a lot of email. State clearly:

  1. Which genres and languages you accept, and which you do not.
  2. Whether you take unagented submissions, and in which months.
  3. What to send: synopsis, sample chapters, author bio, word count.
  4. The file format and size limits.
  5. How long a response usually takes, and whether you reply to every submission.
  6. Whether you charge any fee. If you run a paid self-publishing service, label it plainly and keep it separate from traditional submissions.

A form with file upload is usually better than an email address, because every submission arrives with the same fields. A multi-step form can ask the genre question first and politely turn away work you do not publish. Store uploaded manuscripts securely, limit who can access them, and follow current Indian data protection rules for personal details; check with your lawyer if you are unsure what applies.

Selling books online: WooCommerce or Shopify

Many presses rely on large marketplaces, but selling direct gives you the customer relationship, better margins on each order, and a place to sell bundles, signed copies and pre-orders. Both main platforms can do this well.

Shipping, COD, GST and pre-orders

Books are heavy for their price, so shipping needs thought. Set weights accurately for each format, decide whether to offer free shipping above a basket value, and check what your courier or aggregator charges for different zones. The guide to WooCommerce shipping setup in India covers zones, rates and courier integrations.

Cash on delivery can lift orders from first-time buyers but brings returns-to-origin risk; many stores limit COD by pin code or basket size. GST treatment for printed books, ebooks and shipping charges can differ, so ask your CA to confirm the rates and invoice format before going live rather than guessing.

Pre-orders work well for launches. Both platforms can take a pre-order with a clear dispatch date shown on the product page and in the order email. Be honest about dates and update buyers if printing slips.

Bulk orders from schools, colleges, libraries and corporate buyers often follow a different path from retail orders. A simple trade enquiry form that asks for the titles, quantities, delivery city and billing details lets you quote properly and issue a GST invoice in the buyer's business name. Keep this separate from the retail checkout so trade buyers do not get stuck behind a consumer cart with fixed prices and standard shipping.

SEO for publisher websites

Readers often search for a specific title, an author, or a type of book in a language, such as Marathi children's picture books. Each of these is a chance to rank.

  • Write unique descriptions for every book page and every category page.
  • Give author pages proper bios rather than one line.
  • Use readable URLs that include the title, and keep them stable after launch.
  • Compress cover images and sample pages so mobile pages load quickly.
  • Publish articles around your list: reading guides, interviews and book club notes.
  • Keep out-of-print titles live with a note, rather than deleting pages that have earned links.

If you publish catalogues as PDFs, the guide to SEO for PDFs and brochures explains how to make them findable without replacing proper web pages.

Common mistakes on publisher websites

  • Catalogue only as a PDF. Readers on mobile struggle with it and search engines see one document instead of many book pages.
  • No clear way to buy. If a book is only sold elsewhere, say so with a link instead of leaving readers to guess.
  • Submissions by email only. Inboxes fill with incomplete submissions and nobody can track replies.
  • Outdated forthcoming titles. A "coming soon" book that came out two years ago makes the press look inactive.
  • Slow pages full of large covers. Optimise images and test on a normal mobile connection.

FAQs

What should a website for book publishers in India include?

A searchable catalogue, a detailed page for each book, author pages, a clear submissions page, a trade or bulk order page for booksellers, and a way to buy directly or through linked retailers.

Should a publisher sell books on its own website?

It helps if you want the customer relationship, can pack and ship orders, and want to offer bundles, signed copies or pre-orders. Many presses do both: sell direct and link to marketplaces.

Is WooCommerce or Shopify better for a book publisher?

WooCommerce suits a large backlist with lots of content around it. Shopify suits a smaller list where a hosted, low-maintenance store matters more. Both support Indian payments and COD.

Can I sell ebooks on my publisher website?

Yes. WooCommerce supports downloadable products, and Shopify can sell downloads through apps. Decide on file formats and any download limits, and check your rights for each title first.

How should a publisher handle manuscript submissions online?

Use a form with file upload, state which genres you accept and how long replies take, store files securely and limit access to the editorial team.
