Website for Homestays and B&Bs: Winning Direct Bookings Alongside OTAs, Room Pages, House Rules, Directions, Booking Options and Local Experiences

Plan a homestay or B&B website that wins direct bookings: room pages, house rules, directions, booking or enquiry options, calendar sync and local guides.

Key takeaways
  • Keep your OTA listings; the website is there to convert guests who find you on a platform and then search your name, plus repeat guests and referrals.
  • Answer the questions guests otherwise message about: each room in detail, house rules, the exact route in, meals and what is nearby.
  • Choose the simplest booking method you can run reliably, from WhatsApp enquiries to a booking plugin with calendar sync or a full channel manager.

A homestay or B&B website earns its keep by turning guests who already know your name into direct bookings: people who saw you on a booking platform and then searched for you, past guests coming back, and friends they sent your way. It does that by answering every question a guest would otherwise ask on WhatsApp, and by making the booking step simple and trustworthy.

The build has a lot in common with the hotel and homestay websites I make: room pages, a booking enquiry or engine, galleries, policies and local SEO. A homestay is smaller and more personal, though, so the hosts, the house and the surroundings carry more of the story than amenities lists do.

Where direct bookings actually come from

Online travel platforms are good at reaching people who have never heard of you. Many of those guests then look you up before booking, to see more photos, check the location or find a phone number. That search is your opening. If they find a clear site with the same rooms and a simple way to book, some will book with you instead.

Room pages: one per room, not one for the whole house

Small properties often put every room on a single page with a few photos. Guests then ask which room has the balcony, whether the twin beds can be joined, and whether the bathroom is attached. Give each room its own page with:

  • Bed type and size, maximum guests, and whether an extra mattress can be added and at what charge
  • Attached or shared bathroom, hot water arrangement, and whether there is a geyser or solar heater
  • View, floor, and whether stairs are involved, which matters for older guests
  • Heating, fan or air conditioning, and any hours the power backup does not cover
  • The rate, stating clearly whether taxes and meals are included
  • Eight to twelve photos taken in daylight, including the bathroom

Use the same room names as your platform listings, so a guest comparing the two knows they are looking at the same room. Preparing photos for a website explains how to shoot and size them without slowing the site.

House rules, written as a page of their own

House rules protect your home and set expectations, and guests read them more carefully than you might think. Put them on a page linked from every room and from the booking form, written in a friendly but plain tone:

  • Check-in and check-out times, and what happens if a guest arrives late at night
  • ID required at check-in, and who must show it
  • Your policy on unmarried couples, visitors, pets, children, smoking and alcohol, stated without judgement
  • Quiet hours, especially if your family lives on the property
  • Kitchen access, if any, and meal timings
  • The cancellation policy, with dates and amounts expressed simply

A guest who disagrees with a rule is better off finding out before booking than at your gate.

Getting there, in more detail than a map pin

Many homestays sit off the main road: up a hill, down a lane, past a village. A map pin alone often leaves guests calling from the last junction. A "Getting here" page should cover:

  1. The nearest airport, railway station and bus stand, with rough travel time by road
  2. The last stretch: road condition, whether a small car can manage it, where to park if vehicles cannot reach the door
  3. Landmarks at each turn, with a photo of the final turning if it is easy to miss
  4. Seasonal notes, such as monsoon landslides, winter snow or a stream that rises after rain
  5. Whether you arrange pick-ups, and from where
  6. Mobile network coverage at the property, and whether there is Wi-Fi

Embed a map as well, set to load only when the visitor scrolls down to it, so the rest of the page stays quick on a weak connection.

Taking bookings: pick the method you can run

There is no single right booking setup. The best one is the one you will keep up to date.

Enquiry form and WhatsApp

The guest sends dates and number of guests; you reply with availability and a payment link for an advance. This suits homestays with two or three rooms where the host manages everything by phone. Putting WhatsApp on a business website describes the options.

A booking plugin with calendar sync

A WordPress booking plugin shows live availability and takes payment. Many can import and export calendars with booking platforms using iCal. iCal syncs at intervals rather than instantly, so a booking on one platform may take a while to block the dates elsewhere. With a few rooms on one or two platforms this is often manageable; check how often your plugin and each platform refresh before relying on it.

Booking engine with a channel manager

Once the same rooms are sold on several platforms, a channel manager that keeps rates and availability in step, with its own booking engine embedded on your site, is the safer option. You pay the provider; I connect it to the site. Booking engines and channel managers compares the setups.

Whichever route you choose, advances can be paid by UPI, card or net banking through a payment gateway. Accepting online payments on WordPress in India explains the choices.

A reason to book direct

Guests need a small reason to leave the platform they trust. Offer something you can sustain: a welcome meal, free pick-up from the bus stand, early check-in when the room is free, or a flexible cancellation window. Show it beside the booking button, not buried on an offers page.

Local experiences: the content platforms cannot match

A listing on a platform shows rooms and a review score. Your own site can show why someone should stay in your valley rather than the next one. Write short pages on things you actually help guests do:

  • Walks and treks from the door, with distance, difficulty and the best time of day
  • Food: what is cooked at home, local dishes, and whether guests can join in the kitchen
  • Nearby villages, markets, temples, waterfalls or tea estates, with travel time
  • Seasonal events: harvest, festivals, bird migration, snowfall
  • Guides or drivers you trust, if you work with any

These pages help guests plan, and they give search engines more reasons to show your site to people searching for the area. If your stay is on a working farm, websites for farm stays and agritourism covers the farm activities side.

Registration and guest data

Many Indian states run homestay or bed and breakfast schemes through their tourism departments, with registration, classification or approval. The rules differ by state and change over time, so check what applies to your property with your state tourism department. If you are registered, show the registration on the site.

Guest registration and police intimation rules may also apply, particularly for foreign guests. Collect ID documents at check-in rather than through a website form, and keep the booking form to name, phone, dates and guests. A short privacy note and consent line on the form fits what India's data protection law expects; DPDP Act basics for websites covers the essentials.

Running seasonal campaigns

If you advertise a monsoon or winter package, send the ad to a page about that package, not the homepage. A focused landing page with the dates, inclusions, price and one enquiry button turns more of those clicks into conversations.

FAQs

Does a small homestay need its own website?

If guests search your name, yes. A website with room pages, rules, directions and a direct enquiry route gives those searchers somewhere to book without a platform in between.

Can the website calendar sync with booking platforms?

Many booking plugins support iCal import and export. Syncing happens at intervals, so check the refresh timing, and consider a channel manager if you sell on several platforms.

Should I show the same rates as on the platforms?

Check each platform's current terms on rate parity before deciding. Many hosts keep the rate the same and add a direct-booking perk instead.

Can guests pay an advance on the website?

Yes. Through a payment gateway, guests can pay a deposit with UPI, a card or net banking, either on the booking page or through a payment link sent after confirming availability.

Do I need state registration to run a homestay?

It depends on your state and your property. Check the current scheme with your state tourism department, and show your registration on the site if you have one.
