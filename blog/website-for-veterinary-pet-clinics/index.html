Websites for Veterinary and Pet Clinics: Emergency Hours, Species Pages, Vaccination Schedules, Appointment Booking and Vet Credentials

Plan a vet or pet clinic website: exact emergency hours, species you treat, vaccination schedules, booking, vet credentials, boarding and grooming.

Key takeaways
  • Put emergency information first and make it exact: the hours you really take emergencies, the number to call, and where owners should go when you are closed.
  • Give each species you treat its own section, say honestly which animals you refer elsewhere, and let your vets set the vaccination schedule shown on the site.
  • State each vet's degrees, university, year and council registration precisely, and check the licensing rules before selling medicines through the website.

A veterinary clinic's website is visited by owners in three very different states: panicking because a dog has eaten something it should not, planning a routine vaccination, or researching vets before bringing home a new kitten. The best vet sites serve all three without making any of them hunt, with emergency details in plain view, booking that takes a minute, and vets whose qualifications are stated as precisely as a doctor's would be.

Structurally, a vet site is close to the clinic websites I build for doctors: service pages, profiles with qualifications and registration, an appointment enquiry form, one-tap call and WhatsApp, timings, a map and local SEO. The differences are species, the emergency question, preventive care that repeats every year, and the extra services many clinics now run alongside the consulting room, such as boarding, grooming and a pet shop. When you advertise something specific, such as a new branch opening, send those clicks to a separate landing page built around that one offer.

Three visitors, three different needs

Plan the site around the state the owner is in when they arrive:

VisitorWhat they need within secondsWhere it belongs
Owner with a sick or injured petWhether you are open now, the number to call, and directionsA strip at the top of every page, and a dedicated emergency page
Owner of a registered pet with a vaccination or follow-up dueA quick way to book a date and sessionThe main booking button, repeated on service and species pages
New or prospective owner comparing clinicsWho the vets are, which animals you treat, facilities, and what a first visit costsVet profiles, species pages, a facilities page and clear fees

Emergencies: say exactly what you offer

"24x7 emergency care" on a clinic that closes at nine at night is the fastest way to lose an owner's trust permanently. Be exact instead:

  • The hours you take emergencies, including Sundays and public holidays, and whether a vet is on site or on call
  • A separate emergency number if you have one, shown as a tap-to-call button on phones
  • What to do before setting off: call ahead so the team can prepare, and bring the packet or plant if the pet has eaten something
  • Where to go when you are closed, naming another clinic or hospital only with its agreement
  • Whether you can arrange transport or an ambulance, if you genuinely can

An emergency page can also list signs that mean "come in now", such as breathing difficulty, repeated vomiting, suspected poisoning, a road accident, collapse or heatstroke. Have your vets write and approve that list, keep it short, and avoid home treatment advice that could delay a visit.

Species pages, and honesty about referrals

Owners of anything other than a dog often wonder whether a clinic will see their animal at all. Give each group you treat its own section: dogs, cats, birds, rabbits and guinea pigs, turtles and other reptiles, and farm animals if you visit dairies or farms. For each, list the common services, any vet with a particular interest in that species, and the equipment or handling arrangements you have, such as separate waiting areas for cats or bird-safe cages.

If you refer exotic species or complex surgery elsewhere, say so. "We see rabbits and guinea pigs for routine care and refer reptiles to a colleague with the right facilities" saves a distressed owner a wasted trip and is the kind of honesty they remember.

Vaccination and preventive care

Preventive care brings owners back every year, so it deserves more than a line on the services page. Build a page for each species that explains, in plain words:

  1. Which vaccinations your vets recommend as core, and which depend on lifestyle, such as boarding or travel
  2. When the first course usually starts and when boosters fall due, shown as a chart your vets fill in, with a note that the vet sets the schedule for each pet after an examination
  3. Deworming and tick and flea prevention, with the reasons they matter in Indian conditions
  4. What owners receive: a vaccination card or certificate, and a reminder before the next dose

Some municipal corporations ask for proof of rabies vaccination when owners register or license a pet dog, so a short note pointing owners to your local body's current rules is a useful extra. Reminders can be sent on WhatsApp or SMS, but only to owners who have agreed to receive them, and with an easy way to stop.

Appointment booking that tells the vet what is coming

A booking form for a vet needs a few fields a doctor's form does not. Each one helps the clinic plan the visit:

FieldWhy it helps
Pet's name, species and breedLets the team allot the right vet and room
Age, and weight if knownPuppies, kittens and older pets may need longer slots
Reason: vaccination, illness, follow-up, surgery consultation, groomingRoutine visits and sick pets can be kept apart
First visit or already registeredNew patients may need extra time to set up a record
Preferred date and sessionThe clinic receives a real request, not just "please call"
Owner's phone and WhatsApp consentConfirmation and reminders go to the right number

On the Dr. Nitish Gupta site, the booking form offers the clinic's actual morning and evening sessions in a dropdown, so requests arrive with a date and session attached; the same idea suits a vet clinic. Ask owners to send photos of a wound or rash on WhatsApp rather than uploading them to a form. If you want owners to pick confirmed slots, online appointment booking compares the tools.

Vet profiles, stated precisely

Owners choose a person as much as a clinic. For each vet, give:

  • Degrees with the university and year, for example B.V.Sc. and A.H. and, where held, an M.V.Sc. with its subject
  • Registration with the state veterinary council or the Veterinary Council of India, as applies to them, with the number if they are happy to show it
  • Areas of particular interest, such as surgery, dermatology, imaging or small mammals
  • The days and sessions they consult
  • A real photo, ideally with an animal

Use "specialist" only where a vet holds a postgraduate qualification in that field, and avoid "best vet in the city". Plain, checkable facts persuade more than adjectives.

Surgery, diagnostics and the facility

A facilities page answers questions owners are too worried to ask on the phone. List the diagnostics you offer in-house, such as X-ray, ultrasound and blood tests, and which samples go to an outside laboratory. For surgery, especially spaying and neutering, explain what happens on the day: fasting instructions as your vet gives them, drop-off and pick-up times, whether pets stay overnight, and the aftercare sheet you send home. Photograph the consultation rooms, the operating theatre and any in-patient ward, clean and in daylight.

Pharmacy and shop: check the rules before you sell

Many clinics sell food, accessories and medicines. Food, toys and accessories can be listed or sold online like any other product. Medicines are different: selling them generally needs a drug licence, and prescription-only medicines should not be sold through an open online cart. Check the current requirements with your state's drug control department before adding anything beyond a "medicines dispensed after consultation" note. If the shop grows into a business of its own, websites for pet shops and grooming services covers catalogue and delivery questions.

Boarding and grooming under the clinic's name

Owners often prefer boarding at a clinic because a vet is nearby, so say exactly how that works: whether a vet checks boarders daily, what happens if a boarder falls ill, the vaccinations you require, and what owners should bring. Grooming needs its own page with services, the time each takes, and how anxious or older pets are handled. Websites for pet boarding and daycare goes into kennel photos, daily routines and booking.

Content pet owners actually search for

Seasonal content brings owners to the site before they need a vet, and Indian seasons give it a natural calendar:

SeasonUseful topics
SummerHeatstroke signs, safer walking times, water and cooling for pets left at home
MonsoonTicks and tick fever, skin and ear infections, drying paws and coats
Festival seasonFirecracker noise and anxiety, sweets and foods that are dangerous for pets
WinterOlder pets and stiff joints, bedding, short-coated breeds
Any timeThe first week with a puppy or kitten, travelling with pets, what a health certificate involves

Have a vet review each article, show their name and the review date, and end each piece by suggesting a visit for anything specific. Short videos of a vet explaining tick checks or nail trimming work well, as long as the animals in them are clearly comfortable.

Finally, keep your Google Business Profile hours identical to the website, including emergency hours, because an owner searching "vet near me" late at night will trust whichever one they see first. The Google Business Profile checklist covers categories, photos and hours.

FAQs

Should a vet clinic say it offers 24-hour emergency care?

Only if a vet is genuinely available round the clock. Otherwise state your exact emergency hours and tell owners where to go when you are closed.

Can pet owners book vet appointments on the website?

Yes. A form can ask for the pet's details, the reason for the visit and a preferred date and session, and confirm on WhatsApp, or a booking tool can show free slots.

Should we publish a vaccination schedule?

Publish a chart your vets have approved, explaining core and optional vaccines and when boosters usually fall due, with a clear note that each pet's schedule is set after examination.

Can we sell pet medicines on our website?

Food and accessories, yes. Medicines generally need a drug licence, and prescription-only medicines should not go in an open cart. Check the current rules with your state's drug control department first.

Do we need separate pages for boarding and grooming?

Yes. Owners compare them separately, and each needs its own details on routine, requirements, photos and booking.
