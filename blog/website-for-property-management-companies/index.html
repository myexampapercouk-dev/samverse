Websites for Property Management Companies: Separate Owner and Tenant Journeys, Service Packages, Rent and Maintenance, NRI Owners and Reporting

Plan a property management website: separate owner and tenant journeys, clear packages, rent and maintenance requests, NRI owners and honest reporting.

Key takeaways
  • Give owners and tenants separate paths from the homepage, because one is choosing whom to trust with an asset and the other wants a home or a repair.
  • Compare packages in one table, explain the owner journey from first call to first rent, and show a sample of the monthly report owners will receive.
  • Speak directly to NRI owners, and describe agreements, police verification and tax points in general terms, pointing owners to their lawyer or CA for specifics.

A property management company's website has two audiences who want opposite things. Owners, many of them living in another city or country, are deciding whom to trust with a valuable asset and a monthly income; tenants want a decent flat at a fair rent and repairs that actually get done. The websites that work give each group its own path, explain packages and reporting precisely, and treat NRI owners as the distinct audience they are.

Much of the build overlaps with the real estate websites I design: listings you manage from the dashboard with photos, rent and an available or let status, enquiry forms, WhatsApp on every page, and maps of the localities you cover. Around that sits a service business site, built along the lines of my WordPress website development work, with package pages, an owner enquiry form, a maintenance request form and content for owners.

Two front doors on the homepage

Put the choice at the top of the homepage in plain words: "I own a property" and "I am looking to rent" or "I am a current tenant". Everything after that click should speak to one audience only.

  • The owner path leads to packages, how you find and screen tenants, how rent reaches the owner, maintenance handling, sample reports, NRI services and an enquiry form
  • The tenant path leads to available rentals, how applications work, what documents are needed after a viewing, the maintenance request form and move-out rules

Mixing the two is a common mistake. A tenant reading about your management fee, or an owner scrolling past twenty rental listings to find your services, leaves with the wrong impression.

Packages owners can compare at a glance

Owners shopping for a manager compare scope before price. One table, adapted to your own services, saves a dozen phone calls:

PackageWhat you handleWhat stays with the ownerHow it is usually charged
Tenant placementListing, viewings, screening, agreement coordination, move-in inspectionRent collection, repairs and renewalsA one-time fee when a tenant moves in
Rent and maintenanceRent follow-up, routine repairs through your vendors, tenant queriesMajor repairs approval, tax and legal mattersA monthly fee or a share of the rent
Full managementEverything above, plus periodic inspections, renewals, move-outs and deposit settlementApprovals above an agreed spending limitA monthly fee or a share of the rent, plus placement
Vacant property careRegular visits, cleaning, airing, bill payments, photos after each visitDecisions on letting or sellingA fixed monthly or per-visit fee

Under the table, answer the questions it raises: the spending limit for repairs you can approve without asking, how vendor bills are shown, what happens if the tenant stops paying, how long the management agreement runs and how either side can end it. Whether to publish the actual fees is a business decision; showing prices on your website weighs it up.

The owner journey, from first call to first rent

Owners who have never used a manager want to know what happens next. Set it out as numbered steps:

  1. The owner submits the enquiry form or messages on WhatsApp
  2. You visit the property, or the owner's representative shows it on a video call
  3. You prepare a condition report with dated photos of every room, fittings and meter readings
  4. You advise on rent based on comparable homes nearby, and on any repairs that would help it let
  5. The property is listed on your site and wherever else you advertise
  6. Applicants are screened, and the owner approves the tenant
  7. The agreement is prepared and signed, and the tenant moves in after a joint inspection
  8. Rent arrives, and the first monthly statement follows

What the owner enquiry form should ask

Ask for the property's city and locality, type and size, whether it is furnished, whether it is vacant or tenanted, the owner's country and time zone, the package they are interested in and the best way to reach them. Leave out ownership documents, PAN and bank details; those are collected after the owner signs up, through a secure channel. The DPDP Act and your website explains why collecting less at the enquiry stage is the safer habit.

NRI owners: the questions they ask from abroad

Owners living overseas are often the most valuable clients and the most anxious. A dedicated page should answer what they ask on the first call:

  • Communication: who their single point of contact is, which hours that person is reachable in their time zone, and how quickly messages are answered
  • Seeing the property: video walk-throughs on request and photo reports after every inspection
  • Money: how rent reaches them, into which kind of account as advised by their bank or CA, and how expenses are deducted and shown
  • Tax: Indian tax rules generally require a tenant paying rent to a non-resident owner to deduct tax at source, which many tenants do not know; say that you explain this to tenants and coordinate with the owner's CA, without giving tax advice on the site
  • Authority: whether you need a power of attorney or a narrower letter of authority for particular tasks, and that its scope should be drafted by the owner's lawyer
  • Bills and dues: property tax, society maintenance and utility bills paid on their behalf, with receipts

The tenant side: listings, applications and repairs

Rental listings should carry the details tenants filter on: rent, deposit, monthly society maintenance and who pays it, available-from date, furnishing, floor, parking, and any rules the housing society sets. Describe the home, not the tenant you want. Avoid wording that excludes people by religion, caste, community or food habits; if a society has its own rules, state them as the society's rules and keep your listing neutral. Mark let properties as let promptly, because stale listings make the whole site look neglected.

Applications should start with a viewing request, not a document upload. Ask for documents only after a viewing, and only those the owner and the law require.

Each request should create a reference number and an acknowledgement, and land in a sheet or system your team actually checks; connecting forms to a CRM or Google Sheets shows how. Publish your emergency number for genuine emergencies such as leaks, electrical faults and lockouts.

Agreements, verification and deposits, in general terms

Owners expect a manager to know the paperwork, but the rules vary by state and change, so your website should describe your process rather than state the law:

  • Rental agreement formats, registration and stamp duty differ between states; in some states, such as Maharashtra, leave and licence agreements are commonly registered online. Say that you coordinate the agreement as your state requires
  • The Model Tenancy Act is a central model law that states may adopt in their own form, so avoid quoting it as if it applied everywhere
  • Many city police forces ask owners to register tenant details, often online; say that you help complete it
  • Explain how the deposit is held, what deductions can be made at move-out, and how disputes are handled

Add a line suggesting owners take legal advice on their specific agreement.

Reporting owners can trust

A sample report, with names and addresses removed, is the strongest proof a property manager can publish. It shows exactly what the owner will receive:

SectionWhat it shows
RentAmount due, amount received, date received, any arrears
ExpensesEach repair or bill, with the vendor invoice attached
Net amountWhat was transferred to the owner and when
RequestsMaintenance requests raised, closed and pending
InspectionDate, findings and photos, for the months with a visit
UpcomingAgreement renewal date, deposit or rent revision, planned work

If you use property management software with an owner login, link to it from the site; if you send statements by email, say so. Either is fine, as long as the description matches what owners actually get.

Locality pages, only where you manage homes

Owners search for "property management in Kharadi" or "rental management for NRIs in Whitefield". Write a locality page only where you genuinely manage properties, with local rent trends in words, typical property types, the societies you know and response arrangements for that area; pages that only swap the locality name are what Google calls doorway pages, and the guide to local landing pages shows how to avoid them. If owners also ask you to sell, or tenants to help them buy, websites for real estate agents and brokers covers the brokerage side, and websites for housing societies covers managing a whole complex.

FAQs

Should a property management website show its fees?

It should at least show how each package is charged and what it includes. Exact figures are a business decision, but owners trust managers whose structure is clear.

Can tenants raise maintenance requests on the website?

Yes. A short form with category, urgency and availability, plus photos on WhatsApp, can create a reference number and alert your team.

What should a page for NRI owners include?

A named contact and hours in their time zone, video inspections, how rent and expenses are handled, and general notes on tax deduction and authority documents, with advice to consult their CA and lawyer.

Should rental listings be on our own website?

Yes, alongside portals. Your own listings carry your contact details, link to your other homes and show owners that you market properties actively.

Can owners log in to see reports?

If you use property management software with an owner portal, the site can link to it. Otherwise, emailed monthly statements work well when they are consistent.
