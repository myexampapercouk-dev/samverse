Websites for Sports Goods and Bicycle Stores: Catalogue, Fitting and Servicing

How sports goods and bicycle stores can build a website with a clear catalogue, size and fitting guides, servicing bookings, team orders and local SEO.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways
  • Organise the catalogue by sport, player and level, and be honest about stock if it isn't synced with your billing.
  • Fitting guides based on each brand's size chart, plus servicing bookings, reduce returns and bring customers back.
  • A team and school order page, clear returns rules and a strong Google Business Profile win local business.

Sports goods shops and bicycle stores sell products where fit really matters. A cricket bat that's too heavy, running shoes half a size off or a cycle frame that's too big for the rider all lead to unhappy customers and returns. Most buyers now research online before they visit or order, so your website needs to help them choose the right item, check you have it, and book the fitting or servicing that keeps them coming back.

This guide is for shops that sell sports equipment, fitness gear and bicycles. If you run coaching programmes rather than a store, see websites for sports academies.

What sports and cycling customers look for

  • Range and availability: do you stock the brand, model and size they want, and is it in stock today?
  • Help choosing: which bat, racquet, shoe or cycle suits their age, level and budget
  • Prices or price ranges: enough to know whether a visit is worth it
  • After-sales service: cycle servicing, racquet stringing, repairs and spares
  • Easy contact: a WhatsApp button, phone number, store timings and a map
  • Delivery or pickup: whether you deliver to their area, and whether bicycles arrive assembled

Parents and first-time buyers need plain-language guidance more than long lists of specifications.

A catalogue organised the way people shop

Customers rarely think in your stock categories. Organise the catalogue around how they search:

  • By sport: cricket, football, badminton, tennis, table tennis, swimming, fitness and cycling
  • By player: kids, juniors, adults and women's ranges
  • By level: beginner, club and advanced, so a new player isn't overwhelmed by professional gear
  • For cycles: kids' cycles, city and commuter bikes, mountain bikes, road bikes, hybrids and e-bikes, plus helmets, lights, locks and other accessories

Use filters that match real decisions: size, weight, material, brand and price band. Products sold in several sizes or colours should be one product with options rather than separate listings; see WooCommerce product variations.

If your online stock isn't synced with your billing software, be honest about it. A "check availability on WhatsApp" button beats taking an order for an item that sold out in the shop yesterday.

Size and fitting guides that prevent wrong purchases

Good fitting information is the most useful content a sports store can publish, and it cuts returns. Sizing differs between brands, so use each manufacturer's own size chart on the product page rather than inventing a universal one.

ProductWhat customers need to knowWhat to add on the page
BicyclesFrame or wheel size for the rider's heightThe brand's size chart and a "book a test ride or fitting" button
Kids' cyclesWheel size by age and height, and whether training wheels are includedGuidance for parents and an invitation to bring the child in
Cricket batsBat size and weight for the player's height and strengthSize and weight on every listing, and whether knocking-in is offered
RacquetsWeight, balance and grip sizeSpecs in plain words, plus stringing options
Shoes and clothingUK, US or EU sizing and how the brand fitsThe brand's chart, how to measure, and the size exchange policy
Helmets and protective gearHead or body measurementsA simple how-to-measure guide

Short guides such as "how to choose your first cricket bat" or "which cycle for a seven-year-old" also answer the questions people type into Google, and bring in visitors who are close to buying.

Servicing, repairs and bookings

Servicing brings customers back long after the sale, and it's often what sets a local shop apart from online marketplaces. Give each service its own page or section:

  • Cycle servicing packages, with exactly what each one includes (brakes, gears, chain, wheel truing and so on)
  • Puncture repairs, spare parts and upgrades
  • Racquet stringing, with string types and tension options
  • Bat knocking-in, grip replacement and other equipment care
  • Assembly of cycles and home fitness equipment, if you offer it

A short booking form should ask for the item, the problem, a preferred date and whether pickup and drop is needed. Send a confirmation and a reminder; see online appointment booking for the options. Show prices or "starting from" guidance if you're comfortable doing so, and explain that parts are charged separately.

If treadmills, home gyms and commercial fitness setups are the main part of your business, see websites for gym and fitness equipment sellers.

Team, school and club orders

Schools, academies, corporate sports days and local clubs buy in bulk, and a single team order can be worth a lot of walk-in sales. Make these orders easy to place:

  • A dedicated page for schools, academies and clubs explaining what you supply
  • A quote form that captures the sport, quantities, a size breakdown (or an uploaded size sheet), names, numbers and logos for printing, and the deadline
  • Clear information on customisation, minimum order quantities and typical lead times
  • Photos of team kits you've supplied, shared with the team's permission
  • GST invoices and purchase order handling mentioned upfront, since institutions need them

Selling online: delivery, cycles and returns

If you sell online, plan for the awkward items:

  • Bicycles: explain whether they're delivered fully assembled locally or shipped partly assembled further away, and what the customer needs to fit themselves
  • Click and collect: many buyers prefer to pay online and pick up, especially for cycles that need a final fitting
  • Delivery areas: a pincode check for bulky items you only deliver locally
  • Returns: state clearly what can't be returned once used, such as worn shoes, knocked-in bats or strung racquets, and how size exchanges work
  • Payments: UPI, cards and COD with sensible limits; if your payment gateway offers EMI on premium cycles, describe it exactly as the gateway's terms allow

Local SEO, trust and speed

Much of your business comes from people nearby searching "cycle shop near me", "bicycle repair in {area}", "cricket kit shop in {city}" or "badminton racquet stringing near me". To show up:

  • Complete your Google Business Profile with the right categories, opening hours and real photos of the store and workshop, and ask happy customers for reviews
  • Mention the areas you serve and your workshop services on the website, in natural language
  • Only describe yourself as an authorised dealer for brands where that's true, and check before using brand logos
  • Share community activity, such as group rides, tournaments you sponsor or school events, which builds local trust

The local SEO guide for Indian small businesses covers the basics step by step. Keep the site fast too: sports catalogues are image-heavy and most visitors browse on mobile data, so compress photos and avoid heavy sliders.
