How to Prepare Photos for Your Business Website: What to Shoot, Phone Tips, Sizes, Aspect Ratios, Formats, File Names, Alt Text and Consent

Key takeaways
  • Shoot real photos in soft natural light at the shape they will be used, landscape for banners and square for products, and take more than you think you need.
  • Resize each photo to roughly the largest size it is shown at, save it as WebP or a compressed JPEG, and give it a descriptive file name and alt text.
  • Get consent from anyone recognisable, then send full-quality files to your developer in folders by page, with a short sheet of captions and notes.

To prepare photos for your website, shoot real photos in soft natural light at the shape they'll be used (landscape for banners, square for products), then resize each one to roughly the largest size it's shown at: about 1600 to 2000 pixels wide for full-width banners and about 1200 pixels for images within the page. Save them as WebP or a well-compressed JPEG, name each file descriptively, write alt text, get consent from anyone recognisable, and send them to your developer organised by page.

Every site I build through WordPress website development includes optimized images: resized, compressed and served in modern formats, tested on real phones before launch. Photography itself isn't included, so this guide helps you take and organise photos that work. If your current site is already slow because of heavy images, image compression, resizing and WebP conversion are part of WordPress speed optimization.

Phone photography tips

A recent phone is enough for most business websites if you follow a few habits:

  • Use soft natural light. Face a window or shoot outdoors in shade or on an overcast day. Avoid harsh midday sun and mixed coloured lights indoors.
  • Clean the lens. A smudged lens is the most common cause of hazy phone photos.
  • Hold it steady, with both hands or leaning on something, or use a small tripod.
  • Turn on the grid in your camera settings to keep lines straight and horizons level.
  • Tap to focus on the subject, then slide the exposure down slightly if bright areas look washed out.
  • Walk closer instead of zooming, unless your phone has a dedicated zoom lens.
  • Clear the clutter: cables, boxes, paperwork and anything with customer details on it.
  • Leave space around the subject, so the image can be cropped for phones and desktops, and text can sit beside it.
  • Take several shots of each scene, in landscape and portrait, and pick the best later.

Product photos at home or in the shop

Use a plain white or neutral background, such as a sheet of card curving up behind the product, with window light from one side and a white card on the other to soften shadows. Keep the same distance, angle and background for every product so category pages look tidy, and add a detail shot and an in-use or scale shot for each.

People and team photos

Natural shots of people at work feel more trustworthy than stiff poses. For team portraits, use the same background and framing for everyone, so the page looks consistent and new staff can be added later in the same style.

Sizes and aspect ratios

Upload photos at roughly the largest size they'll be displayed, allowing a little extra for sharp high-density screens:

UseTypical shapeUpload width
Full-width bannerWide landscape, such as 16:9 or widerAbout 1600 to 2000 px
Image within the pageLandscape, such as 3:2 or 4:3About 1200 px
Cards, team photos, thumbnailsSquare or 4:5About 600 to 800 px
Product imagesSquare, all the same ratioAbout 1000 to 1200 px
Blog featured and social sharing imageLandscape, roughly 1.91:1At least 1200 px

Banners are usually cropped differently on phones, often to a taller shape, so keep the important part of the photo near the centre. Your developer can tell you the exact sizes your theme uses.

Formats: WebP, JPEG, PNG and others

FormatUse it forNotes
WebPMost photos and graphics on the siteUsually much smaller than JPEG at similar quality
JPEGPhotos, and the files you send your developerFine when compressed; a plugin can create WebP copies
PNGScreenshots and graphics that need transparencyVery large for photos, so avoid it for them
SVGLogos and iconsSharp at any size; ask your designer for it
HEICiPhone originalsConvert to JPEG before sending, or set the camera to Most Compatible

On an iPhone, the Most Compatible option is under Settings > Camera > Formats, and it saves new photos as JPEG.

Compression without losing quality

Compression shrinks the file by removing detail the eye barely notices. Photos straight from a phone are often several megabytes each, far more than a web page needs. A few rules:

  • Resize first, then compress; a smaller image needs less compression to look good.
  • Check the result at full size on a phone and a laptop, looking at faces, text and edges.
  • Use one tool, such as a free online compressor before upload, or one optimization plugin on the site, not several stacked together.
  • Strip location data from photos taken at home or at a client's premises; many compression tools do this automatically.

The image optimization guide linked above includes a simple weight budget for banners, content images and thumbnails.

Light editing, honestly

Straighten, crop, and adjust brightness and colour so photos look like the real thing on a good day. Keep a consistent look across the site, with similar warmth, brightness and framing. Avoid heavy filters, and never edit product colours or before-and-after results in a way that misleads; customers notice, and with products it leads to returns.

File names and alt text

Rename each file before uploading, in lowercase with hyphens, describing what it shows: teak-dining-table-front.webp rather than IMG_2045.jpg. Add a location only when it's true and relevant, such as clinic-reception-pune.webp.

Alt text describes the image for people using screen readers and for search engines:

Weak alt textBetter alt text
image1Technician servicing a split AC unit on a balcony
dining table furniture best price buySix-seater teak dining table in a bright dining room
teamThe clinic's three doctors at the reception desk

Purely decorative images can have empty alt text. Image SEO goes further into file names, captions and Google Images.

Consent and rights

  • People: get consent from anyone recognisable, in writing where you can, and especially from customers, patients and parents of children. Data protection rules may treat identifiable photos as personal data, so ask a lawyer if you're unsure.
  • Staff: agree what happens to photos of employees who later leave.
  • Client premises and projects: ask before showing a client's home, office or work.
  • Photographers: confirm in writing that you can use their photos on your website and social media, and whether a credit is required.
  • Photos found online: images from Google, Instagram or another website aren't yours to use without permission, and stock images need a licence that covers your use.

Organising files to send to your developer

Send full-quality files through a shared folder rather than WhatsApp, which compresses photos unless you send them as documents. A simple structure makes the build faster:

website-photos/
  01-home/
    hero-team-at-work.jpg
  02-about/
    owner-portrait.jpg
    team-group.jpg
  03-services/
    ac-repair/
      technician-servicing-split-ac.jpg
  04-products/
    teak-dining-table-front.jpg
    teak-dining-table-detail.jpg
  photo-notes (spreadsheet)

In the notes, list each file's page, a caption if it needs one, who is in it, whether consent is confirmed and any photographer credit. Include photos in your website brief from the start, so layouts are planned around what you actually have.

When to hire a photographer

For hotels, restaurants, interiors, real estate, fashion, jewellery and large product catalogues, visuals often decide the sale, so professional photos are usually worth it. A half-day shoot planned around your shot list can cover the whole site, your Google profile and social media.

Photos for online stores: product shots that sell

Product photos on a WooCommerce or Shopify store have extra jobs to do. They must look consistent across the whole catalogue, load fast on a product grid and answer questions that a customer would otherwise ask on WhatsApp.

Keep every product shot consistent

Use the same background, the same aspect ratio (square is the simplest choice for most stores) and roughly the same framing for every product. A mixed grid of different backgrounds and crops looks untidy and makes products harder to compare.

Shoot the views customers ask about

  • Front, back and side views, plus a close-up of the material, stitching or finish.
  • A photo that shows scale: the product in a hand, on a table, or worn by a person.
  • Labels, ingredient lists or size charts where they matter, shot flat and readable.
  • Each colour or variant photographed separately, so the image changes when a customer picks a variant.

Show true colour

Wrong colour is a common reason for returns, especially for clothing, sarees and home decor. Shoot in daylight or under neutral lights, avoid heavy filters, and compare the photo on a phone screen with the real product before uploading.

Plan for marketplace and social use

If you also sell on marketplaces or run ads, check their image rules before the shoot. Keeping a plain-background master image for each product saves reshooting later.

FAQs

What size should photos be for a website?

About 1600 to 2000 pixels wide for full-width banners, about 1200 pixels for images within the page, and smaller for cards and thumbnails. Product photos work well square, at about 1000 to 1200 pixels.

Should I use WebP or JPEG?

WebP for the website, because files are usually much smaller. Sending JPEGs to your developer is fine; a plugin can convert them to WebP on upload.

Are phone photos good enough for a business website?

For most businesses, yes, if they're well lit, steady, uncluttered and consistent. Real phone photos usually build more trust than polished stock images.

Can I send website photos on WhatsApp?

Only as documents, because normal WhatsApp photos are compressed. A shared folder is easier for more than a few files.

Do I need permission to show customers or staff?

Yes. Get consent from anyone recognisable before publishing, and keep a record of it, especially for customers, patients and children.

What size should product photos be for Shopify or WooCommerce?

Square images of around 2000 pixels on each side work well on both platforms, allowing zoom while the platform creates smaller versions for grids. Compress them before upload and keep the same ratio across the catalogue.
