How to Build a Product Catalogue Website for an Industrial Company: Categories, Spec Tables, Datasheets and RFQs

Structure an industrial product catalogue website: categories, spec tables, datasheet PDFs, RFQ baskets, WooCommerce catalogue mode, filters and schema.

Key takeaways
  • Plan the category tree before any design: product type first, then family and model, with applications and industries as a second way in, named in the terms buyers search.
  • Every product or family page needs a real text spec table, drawings, a downloadable datasheet and a Request a Quote button that carries the product name into the form.
  • WooCommerce works well as a catalogue without prices, giving you attributes for filters, bulk CSV import and a quote basket, with Product and Breadcrumb schema for search.

To build an industrial product catalogue website, plan the category structure first (product type, then family, then model), give every product family its own page with a text specification table, drawings, a downloadable datasheet and a Request a Quote button, and let buyers filter by the specifications they actually use, such as capacity, size or material. For most manufacturers, a catalogue without prices, where buyers add products to an enquiry list and send one RFQ, works better than a shop. On WordPress, WooCommerce in catalogue mode handles this well.

Plan the category structure first

The category tree decides how easily buyers find products and which searches you can rank for. Design it in a spreadsheet before anyone opens a design tool:

  • Product type first, then family, then model: for example Pumps, then Centrifugal Pumps, then End Suction Pumps.
  • Applications and industries as a second route, such as "Pumps for water treatment" or "Products for the pharma industry", linking to the same product pages rather than duplicating them.
  • Names buyers search for, not internal codes. Check your enquiry emails and Search Console for the words buyers use, including common alternatives such as "SS" and "stainless steel".
  • Two or three levels deep for most catalogues. Deeper trees make buyers click more and spread thin content across too many pages.

India Automation Hub, an industrial automation news portal I built, shows the same principle on the content side: its subjects are grouped into six families, such as Control Systems and Automation with PLC, DCS, SCADA and HMI beneath it, and each category has its own archive page showing how many articles it holds. Grouping things the way engineers think about their work is exactly what a product catalogue needs.

Family pages or one page per model?

If models differ only by size, rating or capacity, one product family page with a full specification table usually works better than dozens of near-identical pages. Thin, repetitive pages are hard to rank and harder to keep updated. Give a model its own page when buyers search for it by model number, or when it has distinct applications, drawings and certifications.

What goes on each product page

  1. A clear name with model numbers and a one-line summary of what it's for
  2. Photos and dimension drawings, with descriptive alt text
  3. A specification table as real text: capacity, dimensions, materials, ratings, tolerances and standards
  4. Applications and industries served
  5. Downloads: datasheet, manual and, where buyers need them, CAD files
  6. Certifications that apply to this product, with validity dates
  7. Request a Quote or Add to Enquiry List, plus WhatsApp for quick questions
  8. Related products and accessories

Specification tables buyers and Google can read

Engineers want to compare and copy specifications, so never put them only in an image or a PDF. Use a proper table, keep units consistent across the catalogue (and give both metric and imperial if export buyers expect them), and name standards precisely. In WooCommerce, enter the specifications buyers filter by as global attributes under Products > Attributes, such as material, capacity or voltage; the same data then powers both the spec table and the filters. Everything else can go in the description or a custom field.

Datasheets, drawings and catalogue PDFs

Buyers still forward PDFs internally, because the person who finds your site often isn't the one who approves the supplier. Vansh Group, an electronics OEM whose website I built, offers its company profile as a PDF download and links to two product catalogues, one for LED fixtures and one for complete LED lights made as an OEM. To make PDFs work for search too:

  • Use descriptive file names, such as model-x200-centrifugal-pump-datasheet.pdf
  • Export with real, selectable text, and set the document title
  • Link each PDF from its product page, and don't hide it behind a form
  • Put your phone number, WhatsApp and website inside the file
  • Compress files so they download on mobile data

Enquiry baskets and RFQ forms

An RFQ form should give your sales team enough to quote without days of back-and-forth:

  • The product name and model filled in automatically from the page
  • An enquiry basket for buyers who need several products in one quote
  • Quantity with units, delivery city or country, and the requirement in the buyer's words
  • Uploads for drawings and specifications in the formats your team opens
  • Company name and contact details last

On Vansh Group's site, the enquiry form has a service dropdown (product, PCB, part or tool development), so every enquiry arrives tagged with the kind of work needed and can go straight to the right person. RFQ forms for B2B websites covers routing and follow-up.

Setting up WooCommerce as a catalogue without prices

  1. Leave the price empty on products you don't sell online. WooCommerce doesn't show an Add to cart button on a product without a price.
  2. Or turn off purchasing entirely with a small code snippet or a catalogue mode plugin, which also lets you hide prices you've entered for internal use.
  3. Add a request-a-quote plugin for the quote button and enquiry basket, and set where enquiries are emailed.
  4. Remove the cart and checkout pages from menus if nothing is sold online.
  5. Set up attributes for the specifications you filter by.
  6. Import products from a spreadsheet with the built-in CSV importer under Products > Import, which is how large catalogues launch quickly.
  7. Choose short permalinks under Settings > Permalinks, so product and category addresses stay readable.

For developers, the snippet that makes every product non-purchasable is one line, added in a site-specific plugin or a code snippets plugin rather than the theme:

add_filter( 'woocommerce_is_purchasable', '__return_false' );

If you later want to sell spares online alongside the catalogue, the same setup grows into a store; see WooCommerce development.

Filters and search

Once a category holds more than a page or two of products, filters by capacity, material, size or application save buyers time. Build them from attributes so they stay accurate as products change; WooCommerce product filters compares the options. Site search matters just as much: engineers often type a model number, so use a search that includes SKUs and attributes and tolerates partial numbers.

SEO for an industrial catalogue

  • A unique title and description for every category and product page, using specific terms such as "SS 304 storage tank 5000 litre manufacturer".
  • A short introduction on category pages explaining the range, applications and how to choose, so they're more than a grid of thumbnails.
  • Schema markup: Product markup with name, image, description, brand and model or SKU, BreadcrumbList for the category path, and Organization for the company. Without a price, review or rating, products won't qualify for Google's product rich results, but the markup still helps search engines understand the page; see schema markup explained.
  • Internal links between categories, products, accessories and related articles.
  • Redirects for discontinued products to the replacement or the category, rather than leaving 404 pages.

Keeping the catalogue up to date

A catalogue goes stale quickly when only the developer can edit it. Keep a master spreadsheet of products and specifications, decide who in your team updates the site, and train them to add products, change specs and upload datasheets themselves. Review certifications and datasheets at least yearly.

FAQs

How do I create a product catalogue website for my company?

Plan categories in a spreadsheet, gather specifications, photos and datasheets, and build templates for category and product pages with a quote form. On WordPress, WooCommerce without prices or custom post types with fields both work; import products from a spreadsheet if the range is large.

Can WooCommerce be used as a catalogue without prices?

Yes. Leave prices empty, or turn off purchasing with a snippet or catalogue mode plugin, and add a request-a-quote plugin. You keep WooCommerce's attributes, filters, CSV import and product management without a cart.

Should my catalogue be a PDF or web pages?

Both. Web pages for each product family are what buyers find on Google and can enquire from. The PDF is the printable companion buyers forward internally, linked from the relevant pages.

How should industrial product categories be organised?

By product type first, then family and model, using the names buyers search for. Add applications and industries as a second route that links to the same product pages, and keep the tree two or three levels deep.

Do product pages without prices get rich results in Google?

Generally not product rich results, which need a price, review or rating. Product, Breadcrumb and Organization markup are still worth adding, because they help search engines understand your pages.
