How to Set Up Online Donations on a Temple, Trust or NGO Website: Gateways, UPI, Receipts and Monthly Giving

Set up online donations for a temple, trust or NGO website in India: Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU or Instamojo onboarding, UPI QR, receipts and monthly giving.

Key takeaways
  • Make a donation form connected to a payment gateway the main route, with a UPI QR code for people at the temple and bank details for large donors.
  • Gateways activate only after checking your registration, PAN, a bank account in the organisation's name and the policy pages on your website, so prepare these first.
  • Test every amount, payment method and receipt email in test mode, then make a small real donation before announcing the page.

To accept online donations on a temple, trust or NGO website: gather your registration documents and a bank account in the organisation's name, apply to a payment gateway such as Razorpay, Cashfree, PayU or Instamojo, add the policy pages gateways check, build a donation form with suggested amounts and purposes, and send an automatic acknowledgement after each payment. Add a UPI QR code for devotees at the temple, test everything in the gateway's test mode, then make a small real donation before you announce it.

This is the hands-on setup, step by step. If you'd like it done for you as part of a complete site, with timings, events, donations and receipts, see my websites for temples, trusts and NGOs. For the tax side in more depth, 80G receipts, FCRA and payment setup covers what your CA will ask about.

Step 1: Get your documents ready

Gateways verify the organisation before activating payments, and gathering papers often takes longer than building the page. Typically they ask for:

  • The registration certificate: trust deed, society registration or Section 8 company certificate
  • The organisation's PAN
  • A current account in the organisation's name, usually shown with a cancelled cheque
  • ID and address proof of the authorised signatory, such as a trustee or secretary
  • 12A and 80G certificates, if you hold them
  • A board or trust resolution authorising the signatory, for some gateways

Step 2: Choose a payment gateway

All four gateways below are widely used in India and support UPI, cards and net banking. Their policies for trusts, religious organisations and NGOs, their fees and their features change, so check the current details with each before applying:

  • Razorpay: a common choice for NGOs, with no-code Payment Pages and Payment Buttons, subscriptions for recurring payments and WordPress integrations.
  • Cashfree: payment forms, links and subscriptions, with integrations for common form and e-commerce plugins.
  • PayU: a long-established gateway with payment links and WordPress plugins.
  • Instamojo: quick to start with payment links and simple pages, popular with smaller organisations.

When comparing, ask: do you onboard our type of organisation, what are the fees for UPI and cards, how soon do funds settle, do you support UPI AutoPay for monthly giving, and can international payments be switched off or settled separately? How to choose a payment gateway in India goes through these questions in detail.

Step 3: Add the pages gateways check

Gateway reviewers visit your website before approving it. Make sure it has:

  • An About page with the organisation's registered name, registration details and activities
  • A Contact page with a phone number, email and address
  • Terms and conditions, and a privacy policy explaining what donor data you collect and why; see privacy policy basics for Indian websites
  • A refund and cancellation policy, for example that donations are non-refundable except for duplicate or mistaken payments reported within a stated period
  • A donation page that explains clearly what donations support

Step 4: Build the donation form

A good form is short and asks for more only when needed:

  1. Amount: four or five suggested amounts plus a custom amount
  2. Purpose: general donation, annadanam, gaushala, renovation or a named programme
  3. Frequency: one-time or monthly, if you offer recurring giving
  4. Name, email and mobile number
  5. "I want an 80G receipt" tick box, shown only if you hold 80G, which reveals PAN and address fields
  6. Optional dedication: in memory of, or on behalf of, someone
  7. Citizenship declaration, if you don't hold FCRA registration
  8. Consent line linking to your privacy policy

Three ways to build it on WordPress

  • The gateway's hosted page or button, such as a Razorpay Payment Page, linked or embedded from your donation page. Quickest, with receipts handled by the gateway, but less control over design and data.
  • A form plugin with a gateway integration. Some form plugins connect to Razorpay or Cashfree directly; others need an add-on. This keeps the form on your site and stores entries in WordPress. WordPress form plugins compared covers the main options.
  • WooCommerce with a donation product, useful if you also sell prasad, books or seva bookings, using the gateway's WooCommerce plugin.

Step 5: Add a UPI QR code the right way

Use a merchant QR in the organisation's name from your bank's merchant app or your gateway, not a trustee's personal UPI ID. Show the UPI ID as text below it. On a phone, a donor can't scan their own screen, so add a "Pay with UPI app" button that opens their UPI app directly. The link looks like this, with your own UPI ID and name:

upi://pay?pa=yourtrust@bankname&pn=Your%20Temple%20Trust&cu=INR&tn=Donation

Some UPI apps limit payments from links to personal UPI IDs, so a merchant ID works more reliably. Because a QR payment tells you almost nothing about the donor, add a short "Paid by UPI? Tell us for your receipt" form asking for the name, amount, date and transaction reference.

Step 6: Send receipts automatically

Every online donation should trigger an acknowledgement email straight away, with a PDF receipt if your setup supports it. Include:

  • The organisation's registered name, address and PAN
  • A receipt number, date, amount, payment method and transaction ID
  • The donor's name, and PAN and address if they asked for an 80G receipt
  • The purpose of the donation
  • Your 80G registration number and validity, only if you hold it

For 80G, the website receipt is an acknowledgement. The certificate donors use for their deduction, Form 10BE, is generated after the organisation files its statement of donations, Form 10BD, so say this on the receipt and confirm the process and dates with your CA. Follow the receipt with a thank-you page and a personal note for larger gifts; see thank-you pages that do more.

Foreign donations need care. An organisation with FCRA registration must receive foreign contributions in its designated FCRA account at SBI's New Delhi Main Branch, which usually means a separate gateway setup for international payments. Without FCRA, switch off international cards and keep the citizenship declaration on the form. Take professional advice before accepting money from abroad.

Step 7: Offer monthly giving

Regular small donations are steadier than one-off appeals. Gateway subscription features can collect them by UPI AutoPay, card mandates or eNACH bank mandates. The donor approves the mandate once in their UPI or banking app, and later payments are taken automatically. Keep it donor-friendly: send a receipt for every payment, a reminder before the charge where the gateway supports it, and a simple way to cancel.

Step 8: Test before you go live

  1. Switch the gateway to test mode and enter its test keys on the site.
  2. Try each suggested amount and a custom amount, with UPI, card and net banking test details from the gateway's documentation.
  3. Try a failed payment and check the donor sees a clear message.
  4. Check the receipt email arrives, isn't in spam and shows the right details, with and without the 80G option.
  5. Check the entry appears in the gateway dashboard and in WordPress.
  6. Test a monthly donation mandate, then cancel it.
  7. Switch to live keys and make a small real donation, then refund it if you wish.
  8. Test the whole flow on a basic Android phone over mobile data.

After launch

  • Export donations every month and match them against gateway settlements in the bank account.
  • Keep donor data private and limited to the people who need it.
  • Plan appeals around festivals, publishing pages well ahead; see planning for festival peaks.
  • Link the donation page from your Google Business Profile, WhatsApp channel and social pages.

FAQs

How can a temple accept online donations?

Register with a payment gateway using the trust's documents and bank account, add a donation form to the website with suggested amounts and purposes, send automatic receipts, and display a merchant UPI QR code for devotees at the temple.

Can a trust or NGO use Razorpay for donations?

Many trusts and NGOs do. Razorpay, like Cashfree, PayU and Instamojo, reviews the organisation's registration, PAN, bank account and website before activating payments. Check its current requirements for your type of organisation before applying.

Is a UPI QR code enough for online donations?

It works for small offerings, but you don't get the donor's name, email or purpose, can't send automatic receipts and can't offer monthly giving. Use it alongside a gateway form, not instead of one.

Do we need 80G registration to accept online donations?

No. Any registered organisation can accept donations. 80G only decides whether donors can claim a tax deduction, so mention it, and collect PAN and address, only if you hold a valid 80G registration.

Can we accept donations from abroad?

Only with FCRA registration, and foreign contributions must be received in the designated FCRA account. Without it, block international payments and ask donors to confirm they're Indian citizens. Confirm with your CA.
