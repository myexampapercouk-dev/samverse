Featured Snippets and People Also Ask: How Small Businesses Can Win Them

How to win featured snippets and People Also Ask boxes: answer-first paragraphs, lists, tables and question headings, with tips for Indian businesses.

Key takeaways
  • Featured snippets usually go to pages that already rank on page one and answer the question directly in the first lines under a matching heading.
  • Format answers the way Google shows them: a 40 to 60 word paragraph, a numbered list for steps, or a table for comparisons.
  • Target long-tail questions your Indian customers actually ask, such as costs, timelines and how-tos with a city or local angle.

To win featured snippets, first rank on page one for the query, then answer the exact question directly under a heading that matches it: a short paragraph for definitions, a numbered list for steps, or a table for comparisons. Google picks the clearest answer from pages it already trusts, so snippet work is about formatting good answers, not tricks.

This guide explains how to win featured snippets and People Also Ask boxes, with examples suited to small businesses in India.

What featured snippets and People Also Ask are

A featured snippet is the highlighted answer box that can appear near the top of Google results, quoting a passage, list or table from one page with a link. People Also Ask is the expandable list of related questions, each opening to a short answer from a web page.

Search results are changing, and AI-generated summaries now appear for some queries alongside or instead of these boxes. The same principles still help: clear, well-structured answers from a trusted page are what any of these features can draw on.

ParagraphWhat is, why does, how much doesA 40 to 60 word direct answer
ListHow to, steps, types of, best waysNumbered or bulleted list under a heading
TableCompare, vs, prices by type, specsAn HTML table with clear column headings

How to win featured snippets: step by step

  1. Find queries where you already rank. In Google Search Console, open the Performance report and look for question-style queries where your page sits in the top ten or close to it. These are the realistic targets.
  2. Check what Google shows now. Search the query and note whether there is a snippet, which format it uses, and what the current answer says.
  3. Add a heading that matches the question. Use the searcher's wording, such as "How much does a website cost in India?".
  4. Answer immediately below it. Write a direct answer in the first sentence or two, then expand with detail.
  5. Match the format. If Google shows a list, give a clean numbered list; if it shows a table, give a table.
  6. Keep it accurate and current. Update answers when facts change, and avoid made-up numbers.
  7. Wait and re-check. Changes can take weeks to show as Google recrawls the page.

Writing answer-first paragraphs

The most common mistake is burying the answer. Many pages open with background, history or a sales pitch before getting to the point. Google, and readers on mobile, want the answer first.

A good snippet paragraph:

  • Restates the question in the first sentence.
  • Gives the direct answer, including a clear "it depends on" if that is the honest answer, with the main factors.
  • Stays roughly 40 to 60 words.
  • Avoids pronouns that depend on earlier text, so it makes sense on its own.

For example, a dental clinic page might use the heading "How long does a root canal take?" followed by a short paragraph explaining the usual number of visits and what affects it, written and checked by the dentist.

Lists and tables that Google can lift

For process queries, use real HTML lists, with each step starting with a verb and kept to one line where possible. Use headings for long processes and a summary list at the top. For comparisons, build a proper table rather than an image of a table: Google cannot quote text inside an image.

In WordPress, the block editor's List and Table blocks produce clean HTML. Some page builders wrap content in complex markup or render tables as images; check the page source if you are unsure.

People Also Ask and FAQ sections

People Also Ask boxes reveal what else searchers want to know. Note the questions shown for your main topics and answer the relevant ones on your pages, either in the body or in an FAQ section with each question as a heading.

FAQ schema markup used to produce extra rich results for many sites, but Google has since limited FAQ rich results to a narrow set of sites. FAQ sections are still useful for readers and can still be quoted in People Also Ask. The guide to FAQ pages for SEO covers how to write them, and schema markup explained covers which structured data is worth adding.

Picking questions Indian customers actually ask

Global questions like "what is SEO" are dominated by huge sites. Small businesses do better with specific, local and commercial questions:

  • Cost questions with India context: how much does a modular kitchen cost in Pune, what is the fee for a GST registration consultant.
  • Process questions: how to apply for admission to a coaching batch, how to book a site visit.
  • Comparison questions: UPVC vs aluminium windows, Shopify vs WooCommerce for a small store in India.
  • Local questions: best time to paint a house exterior in Mumbai, documents needed for a property registration in your state.

Answer only what you genuinely know, and for legal, medical or financial topics, keep answers general and suggest speaking to a professional. The guide to voice search and local SEO shows how spoken questions overlap with snippets, and the local SEO guide for India covers the wider picture.

Example: rewriting a section to target a snippet

Imagine a web design firm with a page that ranks on page one for "how long does it take to build a website". The current section opens with two paragraphs about the company's experience before mentioning timelines at the end. To target the snippet, the firm would add a heading that matches the question, then open with a direct answer: a sentence that says a small business site usually takes a few weeks, followed by the factors that change it, such as the number of pages, content readiness, custom features and feedback rounds.

Below that, a numbered list of the main stages, from planning to launch, gives Google a list format to use if it prefers one. A short table comparing a basic brochure site, a larger business site and an online store, with the factors that affect each, covers comparison queries. None of this needs invented numbers; ranges and honest "it depends on" answers are fine when they explain what it depends on.

Technical basics that snippets depend on

A page must be indexed and ranking to win a snippet. Check that it appears in Search Console without indexing errors, loads quickly on mobile, and is not blocked by a noindex tag. The guide to Search Console page indexing errors helps if pages are missing. Avoid the nosnippet or max-snippet settings unless you deliberately want to limit how Google quotes your page.

Measuring snippet results

There is no separate featured snippet report in Search Console, so track progress indirectly. Note the queries you targeted and their average position before you edit the page. After a few weeks, check whether position, impressions and clicks for those queries have changed, and search the query yourself, ideally in a private window, to see whether your page now appears in the snippet or People Also Ask.

Snippets can come and go as Google tests answers, so treat a win as a sign the answer is clear rather than a permanent position. If you lose a snippet, compare your answer with the new one: it may be shorter, more direct, more current or better formatted. Small edits often win it back.

Does a featured snippet bring more clicks?

Not always. For simple facts, people may read the answer and not click. For questions that need more detail, such as costs that depend on several factors or step-by-step processes, a snippet can bring interested visitors. That is why commercial and how-to questions are usually better targets than one-line facts. Make sure the page they land on continues the answer and offers a clear next step, such as a related service or a contact option.

FAQs

How do I win featured snippets on Google?

Rank on page one for the query, add a heading that matches the question, answer it directly in 40 to 60 words or with a list or table, and keep the answer accurate and up to date.

Do I need schema markup for featured snippets?

No. Featured snippets come from the page content itself. Schema helps Google understand pages and enables some rich results, but it is not required for a snippet.

How long should a featured snippet answer be?

For paragraph snippets, about 40 to 60 words works well. Lists and tables can be longer, but each item should be short and clear.

Can a small business website win featured snippets?

Yes, especially for specific, local or commercial questions where large sites do not give a clear answer. Start with queries where you already rank on page one.

Why did I lose my featured snippet?

Google may have found a clearer or more recent answer, changed the format it shows, or your ranking may have dropped. Recheck the result and improve your answer.
