Online Ordering on Your Restaurant Website: WhatsApp Orders, WooCommerce Menus, Add-ons, Pickup, Delivery Areas and UPI

Set up online ordering on your restaurant website: WhatsApp orders, a WooCommerce menu with add-ons, table bookings, pickup, delivery areas and UPI.

Key takeaways
  • Choose the ordering route your team can actually run: WhatsApp orders for small menus and regulars, a WooCommerce menu with online payment once manual replies start slipping.
  • Plan portions, add-ons, delivery areas, ordering hours and payment before anything is built, because these choices decide how every dish and checkout is set up.
  • Make sure every order is seen and confirmed quickly, by email, app alerts or WhatsApp, and give regulars a simple reason to order direct instead of through an app.

Online ordering on your restaurant website means customers choose dishes from your own menu and send the order straight to you, with no platform commission. There are three practical routes: a WhatsApp ordering button for small menus, a WooCommerce ordering system with add-ons, delivery areas and UPI payments for higher volumes, or a booking or ordering tool you already pay for, linked from the site. The right choice depends less on technology than on who in your team will answer each order, and how quickly.

Direct ordering is one of the things I set up as part of restaurant website design: WhatsApp ordering for simple needs, or a full WooCommerce ordering system with online payments and delivery areas, with ordering that pauses outside your opening hours. This guide explains how each piece works, so you can decide what your restaurant needs before anyone builds anything.

Many restaurants start with WhatsApp and move to online ordering once replies start slipping on a Saturday night. Building the menu as proper web pages from day one makes that move easy.

WhatsApp ordering, done properly

The simplest version is an "Order on WhatsApp" button on every menu page that opens a chat with a message already typed. A WhatsApp click-to-chat link looks like this, with your number in international format and the message encoded:

https://wa.me/919800000000?text=Hi%2C%20I%20would%20like%20to%20place%20an%20order

A step up is a small cart: the customer taps the dishes and quantities they want on your menu page, adds an address, and the site sends the whole list to WhatsApp as one message. Every order then arrives in the same format.

What WhatsApp ordering cannot do is take payment, check your delivery area or stop orders when the kitchen is closed. Your staff confirm the order, the total and the delivery time, then send a UPI payment link or QR code, or collect cash. That works at a few orders an hour and struggles at peak times, so show your ordering hours and delivery area on the page. Adding WhatsApp to your business website covers buttons, pre-filled messages and tracking clicks.

A WooCommerce menu with online payment

WooCommerce is the free e-commerce plugin for WordPress, and it adapts well to food ordering. Each dish becomes a product, each menu section becomes a category, and the cart and checkout handle the rest.

Dishes, sections and photos

Keep menu sections in the order diners expect: starters, mains, breads, rice, desserts and drinks. Each dish needs a name, a price, a short description, a veg or non-veg marker and a photo where you have a good one. On Southern Flavours, the website I built for an Indian restaurant in Solihull, UK, the WooCommerce shop lists more than 100 dishes in 13 categories, from dosa, biryani and breads to starters, mains, salads and desserts. Each dish shows its price, a quantity control and an Add to Cart button, and a cart summary in the header keeps the running total in view.

Modifiers and add-ons

Restaurant orders are rarely just "one biryani". Plan the choices before building, because they decide how each dish is set up:

WooCommerce handles sizes on its own through variations. Required and optional add-ons need an add-ons extension or plugin, and there are several reputable ones, free and paid.

A checkout built for food

The default WooCommerce checkout asks for things a food order doesn't need, such as a company name and a second address line on a pickup order. Trim it to name, phone, delivery address with a landmark, delivery or pickup, a preferred time and notes. Make the phone number required, because that is how your rider will find the customer.

Pickup, delivery areas and minimum orders

Delivery is where most restaurant ordering systems go wrong: orders arrive from addresses you can't reach, or the fee doesn't cover the trip. The shipping settings under WooCommerce > Settings > Shipping cover most of it:

  • Local pickup. Offer it as a choice at checkout, with clear pickup instructions.
  • Delivery zones by PIN code. A shipping zone can be limited to a list of postcodes, a wildcard such as 4110* or a numeric range such as 411001...411062, so only addresses inside your area can check out with delivery.
  • Delivery fees. A flat fee per zone, a higher fee for a further zone, or free delivery above an order value using the free shipping method's minimum amount.
  • Minimum order value. WooCommerce has no built-in minimum for every checkout, so this needs a small plugin or code snippet.
  • Time slots and preparation time. A delivery-time plugin can offer "as soon as possible" or a later slot, so an office lunch order can be placed in the morning.

Decide who delivers: your own staff, a hyperlocal delivery partner booked per order, or pickup only to begin with. Show the delivery area on the menu page too, so nobody fills a cart they can't order.

Ordering hours, busy evenings and sold-out dishes

  • Follow your hours. A store-hours plugin can pause checkout outside kitchen hours and on holidays, with a message such as "Ordering opens at 12 noon".
  • A manual switch. On a packed evening, the manager should be able to pause online orders from the dashboard in seconds.
  • Sold out. Mark a dish out of stock and it can't be ordered. Staff can do this from the WooCommerce mobile app without opening a laptop.

Payments: UPI, cards and cash

For an Indian restaurant, the payment gateway matters more than the plugin. Gateways such as Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree and PhonePe give customers UPI, cards, net banking and wallets in one checkout, and the money settles to your bank account. You apply in your business name and pass the gateway's KYC checks, so start early. WooCommerce adds no transaction fee of its own; you pay the gateway's charges.

  • Test in test mode first: a successful payment, a failed one and a cancelled one, checking the order emails for each.
  • Set up webhooks, so an order is marked paid even when the customer closes the browser before returning to your site.
  • Cash on delivery or pay at pickup can stay available, limited to smaller orders if refused deliveries worry you.
  • Refunds for a cancelled order can be issued from the order screen, through the gateway.

Show taxes, packing and delivery charges before payment, and put your FSSAI licence or registration number in the footer and on ordering pages. FSSAI expects food businesses to show it to customers, including when food is sold online; your FSSAI consultant can confirm what applies to your licence type. Making sure every order is seen

An order that sits unnoticed for twenty minutes is worse than no order at all. Decide where orders arrive and who watches for them:

  • Email to an inbox someone checks during service, not only the owner's personal address.
  • The WooCommerce mobile app, which can notify the manager's phone of new orders.
  • WhatsApp or SMS alerts to the kitchen or counter number, through a notification plugin or service; WooCommerce order notifications by email, SMS and WhatsApp explains the options.
  • A kitchen printer or POS, where your printer or POS provider supports a WooCommerce connection. This is separate work, so check compatibility first.

Table reservations alongside ordering

Dine-in restaurants usually need bookings as much as orders. For most, a short reservation form is enough: party size, date, time, name and phone, with a confirmation message on screen and an alert to whoever confirms bookings. The Southern Flavours form asks only for the number of people, from one to eight, and a date, then shows "Reservation received! We'll confirm shortly." on the same screen, so the guest knows the request has gone through.

A form like that still needs someone to confirm each booking by phone or WhatsApp. If you take many bookings, a booking tool with time slots and table capacity can confirm automatically, and one you already use can be linked or embedded rather than replaced. Online booking for your website compares forms, booking plugins and hosted tools.

What to have ready before you start

  1. Your full menu in a spreadsheet: sections, dishes, prices, veg or non-veg, and every option and add-on with its price
  2. Kitchen hours for each session, holidays and last-order times
  3. Your delivery area as PIN codes or localities, delivery fees and any minimum order
  4. Payment gateway KYC documents, and your GSTIN if you are registered
  5. Your FSSAI number, logo and real photos of your dishes
  6. The name of the person who will watch for orders during service

FAQs

Can customers order food directly from my restaurant website?

Yes. A WhatsApp button that opens a chat with a pre-filled message suits small menus, and a WooCommerce ordering system takes complete orders with add-ons, delivery areas, pickup and online payment.

Can customers pay by UPI on my website?

Yes, through a payment gateway such as Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree or PhonePe, which also offer cards and net banking. You apply in your business name, and the money settles to your bank account.

Can I offer half and full portions, spice levels and extras?

Yes. Portion sizes are set up as WooCommerce variations with their own prices, and spice levels, extras and dietary versions as add-ons, some required and some optional.

Should I leave Zomato and Swiggy once I have my own ordering?

Usually not. The apps bring people who have never heard of you, while your own site and WhatsApp suit regulars, dine-in bookings and larger orders. Most restaurants keep both and give regulars a reason to order direct.
