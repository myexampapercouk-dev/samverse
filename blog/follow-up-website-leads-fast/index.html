How to Follow Up Website Leads: Alerts, Auto-Replies and a Simple Sequence

How to follow up website enquiries properly: instant alerts, an honest auto-reply, one owner per lead, a simple follow-up sequence and out-of-hours handling.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 6 min read
On this page

  2. Send an auto-reply that sets honest expectations
  3. Give every lead one owner
  4. A simple follow-up sequence
  5. Enquiries outside business hours

Key takeaways
  • Send form alerts to a shared address and route them by service or branch, with entries saved as a backup.
  • Give every lead one named owner and follow a short, planned sequence of calls and messages before closing it politely.
  • Track status and the reason each lead was lost, and handle out-of-hours enquiries with clear hours, away messages and a morning queue.

Plenty of good website leads go cold not because the business was rude or expensive, but because the alert went to the wrong inbox, nobody knew whose job it was, or one missed call was the only attempt anyone made. For why speed matters and how to set a response target, read how to respond to website enquiries fast. This guide covers the system behind it: alerts, auto-replies, ownership, a follow-up sequence and tracking, so no lead depends on one person's memory.

Set up alerts that reach a person, not just an inbox

Follow-up starts with an alert someone actually sees. On WordPress, most of this lives in your form plugin and email settings.

  • Email to a shared address. Send notifications to an address such as enquiries@yourdomain that two or more people receive on their phones, not one person's personal inbox.
  • Route by answer. Most popular form plugins can send different notifications based on what the visitor chose, so a "Commercial" enquiry goes to your commercial team and a "Nashik" enquiry to your Nashik branch. Some keep this for paid plans.
  • Useful subject lines. Put the service and area in the subject, such as "New enquiry: AC installation, Wakad", so leads can be judged at a glance.
  • Phone notifications. If you use a CRM, its mobile app can usually push an alert for new leads. Automated WhatsApp alerts generally need the WhatsApp Business Platform through a provider, so check costs and setup before relying on them.
  • Reliable delivery. Send notifications through SMTP so they don't vanish into spam, and keep entries saved in WordPress or a sheet as a backup; see connecting WordPress forms to a CRM or Google Sheets.

Test the whole chain after every plugin update or form change: submit a test enquiry and check that every alert arrives where it should.

Send an auto-reply that sets honest expectations

Most form plugins can send an automatic confirmation to the email address the visitor enters. Keep it short and specific:

  • Thank them by name and repeat what they asked about, so they know the right details reached you
  • Say when you'll reply, in terms you can keep, such as "within two working hours" or "by 11am the next working day"
  • Say how you'll contact them and from which number, so they don't ignore an unknown caller
  • Give a way to reach you sooner if it's urgent, such as a WhatsApp link or phone number
  • Point to one useful page while they wait, like your FAQs or a guide to pricing

Avoid sales pitches, long brochures and anything that adds them to a newsletter without a separate opt-in. The thank-you page shown after submission should say the same thing, because some people never open the email.

Give every lead one owner

Shared inboxes are good for alerts but bad for responsibility. Each lead needs one named person who owns it until it's won, lost or closed, not just until the first reply.

  • Decide the rule in advance: by service, by city or branch, by language, or in turn (round robin) if everyone on the team does the same work
  • Mark ownership visibly: an "Owner" column in your sheet or the owner field in your CRM, filled in as soon as someone picks a lead up, so two people don't call the same customer
  • Plan cover: a named backup for leave, site visits and busy days, and reassign open leads when someone is away
  • Prioritise fairly: when enquiries pile up, a quick score helps decide who to call first; see simple lead scoring for small businesses

A simple follow-up sequence

Many people miss the first call because they're driving, in a meeting or wary of unknown numbers. A short, planned sequence gives every lead a fair chance without pestering anyone. Here's an illustrative example; stretch the gaps for B2B work or bigger purchases.

WhenWhat to do
First replyCall. If there's no answer, send a short WhatsApp message or email saying who you are, what they asked about and when you'll try again.
Next working dayTry again at a different time of day, and on a different channel if the first one didn't work.
Two or three days laterSend something genuinely useful: an answer to their question, a relevant project example, or a request for the one detail you need to quote.
About a week laterA polite last message: "Shall I close this for now? Reply any time if you'd like to pick it up again."

After sending a quote, follow up a few days later to ask whether they have questions. Don't chase so often that it feels like pressure, and if someone says they're not interested, thank them and stop. Note every attempt against the lead, so anyone picking it up can see what has happened.

Enquiries outside business hours

Evening and weekend enquiries are common, especially from people who research after work. You don't need to answer the phone at midnight, but you do need a plan:

  • Show your hours clearly next to the form, phone number and WhatsApp button, and repeat them in the auto-reply
  • Set a WhatsApp Business away message that says when you'll reply
  • Let people choose a callback time, such as "tomorrow morning" or "after 6pm"
  • Clear the overnight queue first each working day, before new work starts
  • Treat urgent services differently. If you offer emergency work, such as breakdown repairs, show an on-call number rather than a form that waits until morning
  • Announce closures. During Diwali or other holidays, add a short notice to the site and update the auto-reply and away message

Track what happens to every lead

Following up properly is much easier when every lead has a status. Whether you use a Google Sheet or a CRM, a few columns are enough to start:

  • Received: date, time, source and the page they enquired from
  • Owner and the time of first contact
  • Status: New, Contacted, Quote sent, Won, Lost or Not a fit
  • Next action and date, so nothing depends on memory
  • Reason lost: price, timing, went elsewhere, no response or outside your area

Once a month, look for patterns. Are many leads marked "no response" after a single attempt? Do enquiries from one page or campaign keep ending as "not a fit"? The answers tell you whether to fix your follow-up routine, your form questions or your marketing.
