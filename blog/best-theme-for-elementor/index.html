The Best Theme for Elementor: Hello Elementor, Astra, GeneratePress, Kadence and Blocksy Compared

The best theme for Elementor depends on who builds your header, footer and shop pages. Hello Elementor, Astra, GeneratePress, Kadence and Blocksy compared.

Key takeaways
  • The best theme for Elementor is a lightweight one that matches who draws the header, footer, blog and shop templates: the theme, or Elementor Pro's Theme Builder.
  • With Elementor Pro designing everything, Hello Elementor keeps things minimal; with free Elementor, Astra, GeneratePress, Kadence or Blocksy handle the templates Elementor can't.
  • Avoid multipurpose themes that bundle other builders and many required plugins, use a child theme only for custom code, and switch themes on a staging copy first.

The best theme for Elementor is a lightweight, well-maintained one that matches how your site is built. If you have Elementor Pro and design the header, footer, blog templates and shop pages in its Theme Builder, Hello Elementor is usually the simplest choice, because it adds almost nothing of its own. If you use free Elementor, a theme such as Astra, GeneratePress, Kadence or Blocksy is better, because it handles the header, footer and blog layouts that free Elementor can't design.

When I review a site for Elementor development work, the theme is one of the first things I check, alongside the Elementor and Pro versions and the add-ons, because it decides where headers, footers and templates live and how much code loads on every page. The client sites in my case studies are all built with Elementor, on themes from Hello Elementor and Astra to OceanWP and specialised directory and magazine themes. Features move between free and paid versions, so treat the details as correct at the time of writing.

Why the theme still matters with Elementor

Elementor designs the content area of your pages. The theme still provides the frame around it: the base CSS loaded on every page, the header and footer unless Elementor Pro replaces them, blog and archive pages, the 404 page, WooCommerce styling and the default fonts and widths. A heavy theme adds weight to every page and can clash with Elementor's styles; a lean one stays out of the way. Most of an Elementor site's weight usually comes from how pages are built, add-on packs and images, so a light theme helps but won't rescue a slow build; see why Elementor sites get slow.

The key question: who builds the header, footer and templates?

Free Elementor designs pages. Elementor Pro's Theme Builder adds headers, footers, single post templates, archives, the 404 page and WooCommerce product, shop, cart and checkout layouts. That splits sites into two camps:

  • Elementor Pro with Theme Builder: Elementor draws everything, so the theme's own header builder and layout options go unused. A minimal theme such as Hello Elementor avoids paying for features twice in page weight.
  • Free Elementor: the theme draws the header, footer, blog and archive pages, so you want a theme with a good header builder and sensible blog layouts.

Theme by theme

Hello Elementor

Made by Elementor as a near-blank canvas. It loads very little and has almost no settings of its own, which is ideal when Elementor Pro's Theme Builder handles every template. On free Elementor it can feel bare, because anything beyond the basic header and footer settings needs Pro. Studio Agama Interiors is an example I built with Elementor Pro on Hello Elementor.

Astra

A lightweight theme with a free header and footer builder, extensive Customizer options and a large library of Elementor starter templates. It works well with free Elementor and with Pro, and its WooCommerce integration is strong. Sahni Power Solutions, a generator rental site, runs Elementor on Astra.

GeneratePress

Known for clean code and stability. The free theme is very minimal; the premium plugin adds modules such as hook-based Elements and a site library. It suits developers who want control and few surprises.

Kadence

Has a capable free header and footer builder and a strong block editor focus through its own blocks plugin. A good fit if some pages are built in Elementor and others, such as blog posts, in the block editor.

Blocksy

A modern theme with a free header builder and good-looking defaults. Many of its WooCommerce extras and advanced features sit in the paid version, so check what you need before building around it.

Which theme for your situation

  1. Elementor Pro, designing every template yourself: Hello Elementor.
  2. Free Elementor, a business site with a blog: Astra, Kadence or Blocksy, for their free header builders and blog layouts.
  3. A WooCommerce store without Pro's WooCommerce Builder: Astra or Kadence, which style shop, product and cart pages well out of the box.
  4. A developer-led build with custom code: GeneratePress or Hello Elementor, with a child theme.
  5. Some pages in Elementor, some in the block editor: Kadence or Blocksy. If you're still choosing between builders, see Elementor vs Gutenberg.
  6. A directory, magazine or other specialised site: a dedicated theme can be justified when it provides the listings, search or layouts you need. How to choose a WordPress theme covers what to check.

Themes to avoid with Elementor

  • Multipurpose "mega themes" that bundle another page builder such as WPBakery, several sliders and a long list of required plugins. They add weight and can conflict with Elementor's styles.
  • Themes with heavy demo imports that install dozens of pages, widgets and plugins you'll never use.
  • Themes without recent updates, or with no stated compatibility with current Elementor and WordPress versions.
  • Nulled copies of premium themes, a common source of malware; see the risks of nulled themes and plugins.

Do you need a child theme?

Elementor stores your designs in the database, not in theme files, so a theme update won't wipe your Elementor pages. You need a child theme only if you add custom PHP in functions.php, override template files or add custom CSS to theme files. Hello Elementor has an official child theme for exactly this. WordPress child themes explained shows how to create one, with code.

Switching themes on an existing Elementor site

Your Elementor page content survives a theme switch, but anything the old theme controlled does not: its header and footer, Customizer settings, menu locations, sidebar widgets, blog layouts, container widths and default fonts. To switch safely:

  1. Take a full backup and work on a staging copy; staging sites explained covers the setup.
  2. Note the old theme's settings: logo, colours, fonts, widths, menus and widgets.
  3. Install and activate the new theme on staging, then set colours and fonts in Elementor's Site Settings so they no longer depend on the theme.
  4. Rebuild the header and footer in the new theme's builder or Elementor Pro's Theme Builder.
  5. Check every page type at desktop, tablet and mobile sizes: home, a service page, a blog post, the blog archive, the contact form and, for stores, product, cart and checkout.
  6. Push it live, clear every cache and recheck forms and speed.

FAQs

What is the best free theme for Elementor?

With free Elementor, Astra, Kadence, GeneratePress and Blocksy are all solid, lightweight choices; pick the one whose free header builder and blog layouts suit your design. Hello Elementor is free too, but it's at its best with Elementor Pro.

Is Hello Elementor better than Astra?

Neither is better in general. Hello Elementor suits sites where Elementor Pro designs every template. Astra suits sites that use free Elementor, need a header builder or want stronger WooCommerce styling without Pro's WooCommerce Builder.

Does Elementor work with any theme?

It works with most well-coded themes, but some multipurpose themes clash with its styles or add a second builder. A theme that offers full-width page layouts and per-page title controls makes building much easier.

Will changing my theme break my Elementor pages?

The page content stays, but headers, footers, blog layouts and anything set in the old theme's Customizer will change. Test the switch on a staging copy before doing it on the live site.

Do I need a premium theme with Elementor?

Usually not. The free versions of the themes above cover most business sites. Paid add-ons are worth it only for specific features, and any licence should be bought in your business name.
