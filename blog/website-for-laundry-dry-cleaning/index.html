Websites for Laundry and Dry Cleaning Services: Item Price Lists, Pickup Booking, Express Orders, Monthly Plans and WhatsApp Updates

Plan a laundry and dry cleaning website: per-kg and per-piece prices, pickup booking, express service, monthly plans, WhatsApp updates and bulk clients.

Key takeaways
  • Publish a real price list as a web page, priced the way each item is actually charged: per kg for wash and fold, per piece for dry cleaning, per pair or per size for shoes, curtains and carpets.
  • Keep the pickup form short, check the area first, cap bookings per slot, and send order updates on WhatsApp from pickup count to delivery and payment.
  • Give monthly plans and bulk clients such as hostels, salons and hotels their own pages, and write a plain damage and missing-item policy backed by inspection at pickup.

A laundry or dry cleaning website should let someone check what you charge for their clothes, book a pickup from their phone without calling, and know when the order will come back. Monthly plans, hotel contracts and local search all build on those three jobs, so they deserve most of the effort.

A laundry site of this kind is a standard WordPress website development project for me: your own design, booking forms and WhatsApp buttons, on-page SEO, and pages your counter staff can edit themselves when rates change. If you mainly want ads to fill pickup slots in one part of the city, a single focused landing page with conversion tracking can come first.

Start with the rate card, because that is what people look for

Behind most laundry searches sits one question: what will this cost me? A photo of the counter rate card answers it badly. It is hard to read on a phone, Google cannot read it, and it is out of date the day a price changes. A price list page fixes all three, as long as each item is priced the way you actually charge for it.

Keeping the list current

Build the list as an ordinary table your staff can edit, not a PDF or an image. Group it the way customers think (everyday wear, ethnic and bridal, home textiles, shoes), use "from" prices only where the price really varies, and add a "prices last updated" line at the bottom. For a laundry, publishing prices is almost always right; the guide on showing prices on a business website deals with the exceptions.

A pickup form with as few questions as possible

Customers booking a pickup are often doing it between other chores. Every extra field loses a few of them. Ask only what your rider and your counter need, in this order:

  1. Area or pin code first, so someone outside your delivery zone finds out before typing an address
  2. Pickup date and slot, with full slots and holidays already greyed out
  3. Address details riders actually use: tower, flat number, landmark, and whether to collect from the security desk
  4. What is going: a wash and fold bag, dry cleaning items, or both, with a rough count only
  5. Standard or express, with the extra charge for express shown right there
  6. A WhatsApp number for updates

Do not ask customers to count every shirt. Your rider or counter staff count at pickup and send the tally, which is more accurate anyway. Set a limit on bookings per slot so riders are not sent to twenty flats in the same hour, and let staff close a slot from the dashboard when a rider calls in sick.

The form can send the booking to your email and a shared sheet, or open WhatsApp with the details already typed. Many laundries do both: the form for people who like forms, a WhatsApp button for everyone else. Express service you can actually deliver

Same-day and next-day service sells, especially to people packing for a trip or a wedding. It also creates the angriest customers when it slips. Treat express as a separate service with its own rules rather than a promise on the homepage:

  • A cut-off time for express pickups, after which the form offers the next available slot
  • A list of what is excluded, such as bridal wear, carpets, heavy curtains and anything needing stain treatment
  • The surcharge, shown before the customer confirms
  • A daily express limit, so a busy morning does not push regular orders back

Be honest about the monsoon. Drying takes longer in humid weeks, and a note on the express page saying so does more for trust than a promise broken in July.

Monthly plans for families, flatmates and hostels

Regular customers are the steadiest part of a laundry business, and a plan turns them into predictable income. Typical buyers are families with weekly loads, working professionals sharing a flat, students, and older parents whose children arrange the service for them from another city.

Decide these points before building the plan page, because customers will ask about each one:

  • What the plan counts: kilograms a month, pickups a month or pieces a month
  • Unused allowance: whether it carries over or lapses at month end
  • Extra usage: the rate once the allowance is used up
  • Fixed pickup days, such as every Tuesday and Friday, which also makes your routes easier
  • Pausing for travel or exams without cancelling
  • Payment: a monthly UPI payment link to start with; automatic renewals once the plan is settled

Automatic monthly billing is possible on WordPress, but it brings mandates, failed payments and renewal reminders with it. Starting with a prepaid plan and a payment link each month is simpler and lets you adjust the plan before you automate it. Selling subscriptions with WooCommerce covers the automated version when you are ready.

Hostels and PGs deserve their own section. Some owners want one invoice for the building; others let residents pay individually, with a weekly collection day and name tags on every bag. Say which you offer. Laundry is one of the amenities PG owners list on their own sites, as websites for hostels and PGs shows, so a page aimed at owners can bring steady contracts.

WhatsApp updates from pickup to payment

Once clothes leave the house, customers want to know where they are. A short message at each stage answers that before anyone has to ask:

  1. Picked up: order number, item count and the expected delivery day
  2. Inspected: stains that may not come out, missing buttons or existing damage, with a photo
  3. Ready: delivery slot options, or a reminder if it is a counter pickup
  4. Delivered: the bill and a UPI payment link

The inspection message is the one that saves arguments. Something like this, sent before cleaning starts, puts both sides on record:

Order 1042 collected: 6 shirts, 2 trousers, 1 silk saree for dry cleaning. There is an old stain near the saree border, shown in the photo; we will treat it but cannot promise it will come out fully. Expected back on Thursday evening. Reply here to change the slot.

A "track my order" page on the website can be as simple as a form that takes the order number and opens a WhatsApp chat. If you already use laundry management software with its own customer page, the website should link to it rather than rebuild it. For bulk messages and templates, check the current WhatsApp Business rules on opt-in before you start.

Garment care, damage and missing items

Every laundry eventually loses a sock or fades a kurta. The policy page is where trust is either built or lost, so write it plainly and put a link to it beside the booking button. Cover:

  • How items are checked and counted at pickup, and that the count is sent to the customer
  • What you do with clothes whose care label says not to dry clean or wash
  • Colour bleeding, shrinkage and delicate embellishments, and when you will ask before cleaning
  • How and within what period a customer can report a problem
  • How compensation is worked out, if you offer it, in plain terms
  • What happens to items left uncollected for a long time

Words like "eco-friendly" or "hygienic" need something behind them, such as the detergent or solvent you use and how you separate loads. Consumer protection rules apply to service businesses too, so have the final policy read by someone who knows them; website terms and conditions explains why a copied template rarely fits.

Hotels, salons, gyms and clinics: a separate door for bulk work

A guesthouse, salon, gym or clinic is buying reliability, not a good price on one shirt. Give them their own page covering:

  • What you handle: bed linen, towels, uniforms, aprons, table linen, spa sheets
  • Collection frequency and the turnaround you commit to in the agreement
  • How items are counted, tagged and returned, and how shortages are reported
  • Monthly invoicing with GST details, and whether you sign a written agreement

The quote form should ask the type of business, the approximate number of pieces per week, the item types, the current collection frequency and the location. That is enough to price a trial week. Residential cleaners face a similar split between households and businesses; websites for cleaning services deals with it from their side.

Showing up for "laundry near me"

Laundry searches are local and often specific: "dry cleaners in Kothrud", "saree dry cleaning near me", "shoe cleaning in Viman Nagar". A few things help you appear for them:

  • A service area list on the pickup page, naming localities and large housing societies you cover
  • Separate pages for specialist services such as saree and bridal care, shoe cleaning or curtain cleaning, each with its own prices and photos
  • A Google Business Profile set up correctly: shop address shown if customers can walk in, hidden if you only collect and deliver
  • LocalBusiness schema with your hours and areas, using the dry cleaning or laundry type

Area pages only help when each says something different, such as that area's pickup days. SEO for service-area businesses explains how to rank across a city without a shopfront in every part of it.

FAQs

Should my laundry price list be per kg or per piece?

Both, item by item. Everyday wash and fold is usually easier by weight, while dry cleaning, ironing and special items such as sarees and suits are fairer by piece. Label each line so customers know which applies.

Can customers pay online after delivery?

Yes. A UPI or payment gateway link sent with the delivery message lets customers pay from their phone, and the payment is matched to the order number.

Can the website stop taking pickups when a slot is full?

Yes. A booking form with slot limits closes a slot once it fills, and your staff can block dates or slots from the dashboard for holidays or short staffing.

Do I need a laundry app, or is a website enough?

For most local laundries a fast website with a pickup form and WhatsApp updates is enough to start. An app makes sense later, once you have many repeat customers who would genuinely use it.

How do I handle a customer who says something is missing?

The count and photos sent at pickup are your record. Check them against your tags, reply with what you found, and follow the process set out in your policy page.
