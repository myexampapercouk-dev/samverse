How to Find Content Ideas and Pages to Improve in Search Console

Use Search Console's Performance report to find content ideas: queries with impressions but few clicks, near-page-one rankings and questions people ask.

Key takeaways
  • Queries with many impressions but few clicks point to weak titles, mismatched pages or missing content.
  • Pages ranking around positions 8 to 20 are often the quickest wins when you strengthen the existing page.
  • Filter queries for question words to find FAQ entries and new article topics people already search for.

Most business owners open Google Search Console, glance at the clicks graph and close it again. That's a missed chance: the Performance report shows the actual words people typed before your site appeared in Google, which makes it one of the best free sources of content ideas. Here's a working method for turning that data into a short to-do list. For an overview of every report first, read Search Console reports explained.

Set up the report so it's useful

Open Performance, then Search results, and make a few changes before you start digging:

  • Turn on all four metrics: total clicks, total impressions, average CTR and average position. You need all four to spot opportunities.
  • Use a longer date range: the last three months is a good default. A single week is too noisy for a small business site.
  • Switch between tabs: Queries shows what people searched; Pages shows which of your URLs appeared.
  • Export when the list gets long: the export button sends the table to Google Sheets or Excel, where sorting and filtering are easier.

Treat average position as a rough guide, since it's averaged across many searches, locations and devices. Google also leaves out some rare queries for privacy, so the table won't always add up to the graph's totals.

Queries with impressions but few clicks

Sort the Queries table by impressions and look down the CTR column. A query with plenty of impressions and very few clicks is telling you something. Check its average position first:

  • Position well beyond page one: you're appearing, but too far down for people to click. That's a content problem, not a title problem; see the next section.
  • Position on page one but low CTR: searchers see you and choose someone else. Your title and description may be vague or may not match what they want, or the map pack, ads and videos may be pushing you down the screen.
  • The query doesn't match the page: if "AC service charges" shows impressions for your general services page, people want a specific answer that page doesn't give. That may be a new section or page waiting to be written.

For page-one results with weak CTR, rewrite the title and meta description so they're specific and answer the search directly; see how to write meta titles and descriptions. Note the date you made the change, so you can compare fairly later.

Near-page-one queries: your quickest wins

Queries where your average position is roughly 8 to 20 are close to page one but not quite there. Improving the page that already ranks often moves these faster than starting a new page from nothing. Use the filter above the table, or your exported sheet, to show positions in that range, then sort by impressions.

For each promising query, open the page that ranks for it and ask:

  1. Does the page properly answer this query, or only mention it in passing?
  2. Is there a clear heading and section for it, or is it buried in a paragraph?
  3. Is the information current: prices, steps, rules and years?
  4. Do other relevant pages on your site link to it with descriptive link text?
  5. Compared with the pages ranking above you, what's missing: examples, a table, real photos, an FAQ?

Then strengthen the existing page rather than writing a new one on the same topic, which would only compete with it. The full process is in how to update old blog posts.

Questions people are already asking

Question searches are ready-made blog titles and FAQ entries. Add a filter, choose Query, then the Custom (regex) option. A pattern like this shows queries that start with common question words:

^(how|what|why|which|when|can|does|should|kya|kaise|kitna)

Hindi and Hinglish question words often come at the end of a search ("website banane ka kharcha kitna hai"), so try a second filter with just kya|kaise|kitna as well. Then group what you find:

  • Questions your page already answers well: make sure the answer is near the top and easy to spot.
  • Questions answered badly or not at all: add a short section or FAQ to the relevant page; see FAQ sections that help customers and SEO.
  • Questions big enough for their own article: cost, comparison and "how to choose" questions often deserve a dedicated post.

Questions that reveal doubts before buying, such as "how long does it take" or "what's included", are worth answering on your service pages too.

Topics hiding inside your existing pages

In the Pages tab, click one of your main pages, then go back to Queries. Pages often appear for searches they were never written for. Look for patterns:

  • Location searches: impressions for "pest control in Noida" on a page that mentions Noida only once may justify a genuinely useful local section or page.
  • Sub-services: a general "AC services" page showing up for "AC gas refilling" and "split AC installation" may be ready to split into dedicated pages.
  • Prices and comparisons: searches with "price", "charges", "cost" or "vs" show what people want to know before they enquire.
  • Two pages taking turns: if two of your pages appear for the same query and swap places, consider merging them or making each one's focus clearer.

Turn findings into a simple action list

What you seeWhat it usually meansWhat to do
High impressions, low CTR, page oneYour result isn't tempting or doesn't match the searchRewrite the title and description
Position 8–20 for a valuable queryThe page is relevant but not strong enoughImprove the content and internal links
Question queriesPeople want a direct answerAdd an FAQ or section, or write a post
Searches a page wasn't written forDemand for a topic you don't cover properlyAdd a section or create a dedicated page
Falling clicks on a once-strong pageOutdated content or stronger competitorsRefresh and update the page

Pick three to five actions a month and log what you changed and when.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Reacting to a few days of data. Judge changes over several weeks.
  • Comparing different seasons. Festivals, exams, weddings and the monsoon change what people search, so compare with the same period last year where you can.
  • Copying queries word for word into pages. Write naturally for the person searching; awkward phrases pasted from the report read badly.
  • Chasing irrelevant queries. Impressions for things you don't offer aren't opportunities.
  • Overhauling pages that already bring enquiries without a clear reason.
