How to Find Content Ideas and Pages to Improve in Search Console

Use Google Search Console to find content ideas: queries with impressions but few clicks, near page-one rankings, question searches and India-only filters.

Key takeaways
  • The Performance report shows the exact queries you already appear for; impressions with few clicks point to titles, pages or topics worth improving.
  • Filter by country (such as India) and look at positions roughly 5 to 30 first, because those pages are closest to earning real clicks.
  • Turn findings into a short list: improve an existing page when it matches the query, and write a new page only when the intent is genuinely different.

To find content ideas in Google Search Console, open the Performance report, switch on impressions, clicks, CTR and position, and look for queries where your site already appears but gets few clicks. Those queries tell you which pages to improve, which questions to answer, and which new topics your audience is searching for. Filter by country if you sell mainly in India.

This guide walks through the exact steps, the filters that matter, and how to turn the data into a simple monthly content plan for a business website.

Why Search Console beats guessing

Keyword tools estimate what people might search. Search Console shows what Google actually showed your site for, how often, and at what position. For a small business site, that is the most reliable source of content ideas you have, and it is free. If you have not set it up yet, see setting up Analytics and Search Console.

The data has limits. Search Console samples and hides some rare queries for privacy, positions are averages, and the numbers lag by a day or two. Treat it as strong direction, not exact measurement.

Finding content ideas in Google Search Console: step by step

  1. Open Performance, then Search results.
  2. Tick all four metrics: total clicks, total impressions, average CTR and average position.
  3. Set the date range to the last three months, so you have enough data but it is still recent.
  4. Click Queries and sort by impressions, highest first.
  5. Add a Country filter if most of your customers are in India. Global impressions can hide what your actual buyers search for.
  6. Export the table to Google Sheets so you can sort and mark rows.

Then repeat with the Pages tab to see which pages get impressions, and click a page to see the queries it appears for.

Queries with impressions but few clicks

These are your quickest wins when the position is already reasonable. Look at the search result for that query on your phone. Ask: does my title answer what the searcher wants? Does a competitor's snippet promise something more specific, such as a price range, a checklist or a location?

Rewrite the title so the exact phrase appears near the start, and make the description say what the reader gets. Keep titles roughly within 60 characters so they are not cut off. Then give it a few weeks before judging the change. Focus on matching the query word for word rather than writing clever headlines.

Also check whether something else on the results page is taking the clicks: a map pack, a video carousel, shopping results or an AI answer. If so, the fix may be a better Google Business Profile, a video, or schema markup rather than a new title.

Pages close to page one

Pages with an average position from about 5 to 30 are often called striking distance pages. They already rank, so improving them usually pays off faster than writing something new. For each one:

  • List the top queries the page appears for.
  • Check that the main query appears in the title, one H2 and the first paragraph.
  • Add sections that answer related queries you found, such as costs, steps, comparisons or local details.
  • Add a short FAQ with questions phrased the way people search.
  • Link to the page from two or three related posts using natural anchor text; see internal linking explained.
  • Make sure it loads quickly on mobile and has no indexing issues.

Question and long-tail queries

Use a regex filter on queries to find questions. In the query filter, choose Custom (regex) and enter something like ^(how|what|why|which|can|should|is|does), or a word like cost|price|charges. This shows the exact questions people typed.

Group similar questions. If several are variations of one need, answer them on one page. If a question has a clearly different intent, it may deserve its own post. The guide to long-tail keywords and search intent help you decide.

Improve or write new?

A common mistake is writing a new post for every query, which creates several weak pages competing with each other. Use this rule of thumb:

  • Improve the existing page when the query is a variation of what the page already covers.
  • Write a new page when the query needs a different answer: a different service, audience, location or format such as a comparison or price guide.
  • Merge pages when two of your pages appear for the same queries and neither ranks well. Redirect the weaker one to the stronger one.

The guide on duplicate content explains why overlapping pages can hold each other back.

India-specific tips

For businesses selling in India, a few habits help:

  • Compare the India filter with worldwide data. Global impressions on head terms rarely turn into customers; India queries usually show real buying intent.
  • Look for city names, Hinglish phrasing and price words like "charges", "cost" and "rate", and use those exact words on your pages.
  • Check mobile versus desktop with the Device filter. Most Indian searches are on phones, so titles and intros must work on a small screen.
  • Note queries mentioning UPI, COD, GST or Indian platforms; they often signal topics competitors from abroad do not cover.

Spotting pages that are slipping

Content ideas are not only about new topics. Use the Compare option in the date picker to compare the last three months with the previous three, then sort pages by the change in clicks. Pages losing clicks may have outdated information, a competitor with a better answer, or a technical problem such as an indexing issue or a slow template.

For each slipping page, open it and read it as a customer would. Update prices or steps that have changed, add the new questions people now ask, refresh screenshots, and check that internal links still point to live pages. If a query has moved from your page to a different page on your own site, decide which one should rank and make that clear. The Search Console reports guide explains the Pages and indexing reports, and diagnosing a traffic drop covers bigger falls.

Keeping a simple record

A spreadsheet with four columns is enough: the page, the query you targeted, what you changed, and the date. Without it, you will not remember months later which change helped. Note outside events too, such as a site redesign, a hosting move or a Google update, so you do not credit or blame a content change for something else.

A simple monthly routine

  1. Export the last three months of queries and pages, filtered to your main country.
  2. Pick three striking distance pages to improve.
  3. Rewrite one or two titles with high impressions and low CTR.
  4. Choose one genuinely new topic from question queries.
  5. Note the date of each change in a sheet, and compare the same period a month or two later.

Keep these tasks on a simple content calendar so improvements are not forgotten when the business gets busy.

FAQs

How do I find content ideas in Google Search Console?

Open Performance, Search results, enable all four metrics, view Queries for the last three months, and look for queries with high impressions but few clicks, positions near page one, and question-style searches.

What is a good position range to focus on?

Pages averaging roughly 5 to 30 are usually closest to earning more clicks, so they are a sensible place to start.

Should I write a new post for every query?

No. Improve the existing page when the intent matches, and write a new one only when the query needs a genuinely different answer.

Can I filter Search Console to India only?

Yes. Add a Country filter in the Performance report. It is useful when your customers are mainly in India.

How long until changes show in Search Console?

It varies. Give changes a few weeks and compare equal date ranges before deciding whether they worked.
