How to Build an Online Course Website with WordPress in India: LMS, Payments, Video and Student Access

How to build an online course website with WordPress in India: LMS plugins, UPI payments, secure video hosting, GST invoices and student access.

Key takeaways
  • An online course website with WordPress needs four parts working together: an LMS plugin, a payment gateway with UPI, secure video hosting and student accounts.
  • Host videos on a proper video platform rather than your web server, and plan for India-specific needs such as UPI, GST invoices and mobile-first students.
  • Start with one course, a clear sales page and a simple checkout, then add memberships, bundles or certificates once students are enrolling.

To build an online course website with WordPress, you combine four parts: an LMS (learning management system) plugin for lessons and progress, a payment gateway that supports UPI and cards for Indian students, a secure video host for your lectures, and student accounts with login. Put a clear sales page in front of each course and you have a platform you own, can update yourself and can grow over time.

This guide explains how those parts fit together, the India-specific details to plan for, and when WordPress is the right choice compared with a hosted course platform or Shopify.

Choosing an LMS plugin

There are several well-known WordPress LMS plugins, and most can handle courses, lessons, quizzes and certificates. Rather than chasing the longest feature list, decide what you actually need:

  • Course structure: simple lessons, or sections with quizzes and assignments?
  • Drip content: release lessons weekly, or give everything at once?
  • Selling model: one-time purchase per course, bundles, or a monthly membership?
  • Certificates: do students need a downloadable certificate on completion?
  • Live classes: do you need links to live sessions alongside recorded lessons?
  • Payments: does the plugin work with an Indian gateway directly, or through WooCommerce?

Many course sites use WooCommerce for checkout and the LMS for access, which gives you coupons, GST settings and order records in one place. If you plan a subscription model, the guide on a membership website with WordPress explains the extra pieces.

Payments for Indian students: UPI, cards and GST

Most students in India expect to pay by UPI, with cards, net banking and sometimes EMI as alternatives. Razorpay and other Indian gateways offer WordPress and WooCommerce integrations that support these. A few points to plan:

  • Automatic access: a successful payment should enrol the student immediately, without you doing it by hand.
  • Failed payments: UPI payments sometimes stay pending, so the site should handle late confirmations properly.
  • GST invoices: if you are registered for GST, invoices can be generated automatically. Ask your CA how GST applies to your courses, especially for students outside India.
  • International students: check whether your gateway accepts international cards and in which currencies.
  • Refund policy: state it clearly on the sales page and in the checkout terms.

Video hosting and protecting your content

Uploading lecture videos directly to your WordPress media library is one of the most common mistakes. Large video files slow the site, eat hosting space and are easy to download. A better approach is to host videos on a dedicated video platform and embed them in lessons.

When choosing a video host, check that it can restrict playback to your own domain, hide the video from public search, play smoothly on mobile networks and offer adaptive quality for slower connections. No method makes videos impossible to copy, but these settings make casual sharing much harder.

Also think about students on mid-range phones with patchy data. Shorter lessons, downloadable notes and audio-friendly explanations help them keep going.

Sales pages that convert students

A course sales page has to answer the questions a student would ask before paying:

  1. Who is this course for, and who is it not for?
  2. What will I be able to do after finishing it?
  3. What is in the curriculum, and how long is each module?
  4. Who is teaching, and why should I trust them?
  5. Can I watch a free preview lesson?
  6. How long do I get access, and on how many devices?
  7. What is the refund policy?

Genuine reviews from past students help more than anything else on the page. The guide on collecting testimonials covers how to gather them with permission. Avoid income or job promises you cannot back up.

WordPress, Shopify or a hosted course platform?

Hosted course platforms are quick to start with but you work within their design, fees and rules. WordPress gives you more control and ownership, but you need hosting, updates and someone to look after it. Shopify is excellent for physical products and can sell digital products and courses through apps, which suits a brand that already runs a Shopify store and wants to add a course alongside its products.

The student experience after purchase

Getting the payment right is only half the job. Students judge a course by what happens after they pay, and a confusing dashboard leads to support messages and refund requests. Plan these details before launch:

  • Instant welcome: an email and, if you use it, a WhatsApp message with the login link, the course name and how to get help.
  • A simple dashboard: one place listing each enrolled course, progress so far and a clear "continue where you left off" button.
  • Mobile-friendly lessons: readable text, video that fits the screen and buttons large enough to tap.
  • Notes and resources: downloadable PDFs or worksheets for revision, especially for exam preparation courses.
  • Doubt support: a comment section, a community group or scheduled live doubt sessions, whichever you can actually keep up.
  • Completion: a certificate or a short congratulations email with a link to the next course.

Language matters too. If many of your students are more comfortable in Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or another language, consider bilingual course pages or subtitles. WordPress can handle multilingual content, though it adds some work, so start with the language most of your students prefer.

Finally, keep an eye on where students drop off. Most LMS plugins show lesson completion, and a quick look each month tells you which lessons need to be shorter, clearer or split into parts. Small improvements there often matter more than adding new courses.

Performance, security and maintenance

Course sites have logged-in users, payments and private content, so a few basics matter:

  • Good hosting with enough resources for logged-in traffic, which cannot be cached the same way as public pages.
  • HTTPS everywhere, strong admin passwords and two-factor login for admins.
  • Regular backups of both files and the database, especially before plugin updates.
  • Reliable email sending for welcome emails and password resets.
  • Testing the purchase and login flow on a phone after every major update.

A sensible launch plan

  1. Start with one course and one clear sales page.
  2. Set up checkout with UPI and cards, and test real purchases end to end.
  3. Offer a free preview lesson and a simple email sign-up for people not ready to buy.
  4. Collect feedback and reviews from the first batch of students.
  5. Add bundles, certificates, memberships or live classes once you know what students want.

FAQs

How do I build an online course website with WordPress?

Install an LMS plugin for courses and lessons, connect a payment gateway that supports UPI and cards, host videos on a dedicated video platform, and create a sales page for each course with clear curriculum and pricing.

Which payment gateway works for online courses in India?

Indian gateways such as Razorpay offer WordPress and WooCommerce integrations with UPI, cards and net banking. Compare fees, settlement times and international card support before choosing.

Should I upload course videos to WordPress?

No. Host them on a video platform that supports domain restrictions and adaptive streaming, then embed them in lessons. This keeps the site fast and makes casual sharing harder.

Can I sell courses on Shopify instead?

Yes, through course or digital product apps. It suits brands that already sell physical products on Shopify. For a course-first business, WordPress with an LMS usually gives more control.

Do I need to charge GST on online courses?

It depends on your registration, turnover and the type of course. Ask your CA, and once you know the rule, invoices can be set up to apply it automatically.
