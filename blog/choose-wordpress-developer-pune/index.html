Key takeaways
  • Judge Pune developers on live work you open on your own phone, what they personally built, and how clearly they communicate; being nearby is a bonus, not a qualification.
  • Get a written scope with pages, features, revision rounds, exclusions, milestone payments and post-launch support, so quotes can be compared fairly.
  • Make sure the domain, hosting, WordPress admin and Google accounts are in your business name before you pay, and walk away from anyone who won't agree.

To choose a WordPress developer in Pune, shortlist three or four people or studios, open their live websites on your own phone over mobile data, ask what they personally built on each, and get a written scope and fixed quote from each that lists pages, features, revision rounds, exclusions and support after launch. Insist that the domain, hosting, WordPress admin login and Google accounts are in your business name. Being local helps if face-to-face meetings matter to you, but communication, process and the quality of live work matter more.

Step 1: write down what you need

A developer can only give a useful answer to a clear question. Before calling anyone, note:

  • The pages you need and the main action visitors should take: call, WhatsApp, enquiry, booking or purchase
  • Features such as an online store, payments, bookings, or Marathi and Hindi versions
  • Who will write the text and supply photos
  • A rough budget and any fixed date you are working towards, for example a festive season or an exhibition stall

Step 3: check live work properly

Screenshots and mock-ups prove very little. Ask for links to live websites and check them yourself:

  1. Load each site on your own phone over 4G, rather than the office broadband. Do they load quickly and read easily?
  2. Try the buttons a customer would use: call, WhatsApp and directions. Don't submit forms on other businesses' sites, but check they are easy to find.
  3. Ask what they did on each site: design, development, content, or only small changes.
  4. Look for work like yours, such as clinics, manufacturers, restaurants or stores.
  5. Check the sites are still cared for. Broken pages or an expired SSL certificate say something about aftercare.
  6. Read their Google reviews, and ask to speak to a past client if you can.

You can practise on my own portfolio of case studies, which links to live sites such as Dr. Sunaina Dental Care, a dental clinic in Agra, and Vansh Group, an electronics OEM.

Step 4: ask the right questions in the first conversation

  • Who will actually build my website? Some agencies pass work to subcontractors or junior staff. That isn't always bad, but you should know.
  • Who will I talk to after launch?
  • Which theme or page builder will you use, and why for my site?
  • Will I be able to edit text, photos and prices myself, and will you show me how?
  • How do you keep sites fast on mobile networks?
  • What do you need from me, and by when? What happens to the timeline if my content is late?
  • What isn't included? Content writing, photography, premium licences and hosting are common extras.
  • Which SEO basics are included at launch? Titles, a sitemap, Search Console and links to your Google Business Profile.

A good developer will ask you plenty of questions too. Be careful with anyone who quotes before understanding your business.

Step 5: compare proposals like for like

Quotes vary widely because people quote for different things. Compare scope, not just the total. A fair proposal spells out:

  • The pages and features, and the design approach
  • How many revision rounds are included
  • Who supplies content and images
  • A timeline with milestones, and payments linked to them rather than everything upfront
  • What is excluded, and what extras would cost
  • Whether GST is included, if the developer is registered
  • The support period after launch, and maintenance options

Red flags

  • No live sites to show, only screenshots or templates
  • Guaranteed first-page rankings or a fixed number of leads
  • Full payment upfront for a new build
  • Domain or hosting registered in the developer's name "for convenience"
  • No written scope, or a scope with no exclusions
  • Vague answers about who builds the site
  • Nulled premium themes or plugins offered to save money
  • Slow or evasive replies before you have even paid

Plan for after launch

A website needs updates, backups and the occasional fix for years. Before signing, ask how long free fixes last, what counts as a fix rather than a new change, whether there is a monthly maintenance plan and what it covers, and what you receive at handover. For the questions worth asking, see choosing a WordPress maintenance provider. If a developer doesn't offer maintenance, that's fine, as long as you know who will.

FAQs

Should I hire a WordPress developer in Pune or anywhere in India?

Choose on live work, communication and a clear written scope first. A Pune developer is useful if you want in-person meetings or local knowledge, but remote work over WhatsApp and video calls works well for most projects.

How do I check a developer's portfolio?

Open their live sites on your phone over mobile data, try the call and WhatsApp buttons, ask what they personally did on each site, and check the sites are still maintained.

What should a website proposal include?

Pages and features, design approach, revision rounds, who supplies content, milestones and payments, exclusions, GST if applicable, and post-launch support.

Who should own the domain and hosting?

Your business, with your email on the accounts. The developer can be added as a user. You should also have an administrator login to WordPress.

What are the red flags when hiring a web developer?

No live work, guaranteed rankings, full payment upfront, domain or hosting in the developer's name, no written scope and vague answers about who builds the site.
