Websites for Fashion Boutiques: Lookbooks, Size Guides, Variations, Instagram and WhatsApp Selling, COD and Returns

Plan a fashion boutique website: on-model photos, size charts, size and colour variations, lookbooks, Instagram and WhatsApp selling, COD and exchanges.

Key takeaways
  • Answer the fit question on every product page with a size chart in centimetres and inches, garment measurements, fit notes and the model's height and size worn.
  • Set up sizes and colours as proper WooCommerce variations with stock per size, and keep lookbooks and collections linked to products so a styled photo turns into an order.
  • Use Instagram for discovery and WhatsApp for styling questions, but send both to product pages, and keep COD, exchange and returns rules short, clear and the same everywhere.

A boutique website has to do the work of the trial room and the shop assistant. It needs to show how each piece looks on a real person, answer size and fabric questions before they are asked, and make buying by UPI, card or cash on delivery quick on a phone. The boutiques that do well online keep Instagram for discovery and WhatsApp for styling questions, and use their own website to take orders, sizes and payments without a long chain of DMs.

WooCommerce suits boutiques because sizes, colours, stock and Indian payment options all sit on a site you own. This guide covers what is specific to clothing and accessories. If you mainly sell sarees, lehengas or other ethnic wear, read websites for saree and ethnic wear stores alongside it.

Photos that show fit, fabric and true colour

Shoppers cannot touch the fabric or try the piece on, so the photos carry most of the decision.

  • On-model shots from the front, side and back, showing how the piece sits and falls
  • Flat lays or mannequin shots for shape and length, kept consistent across the catalogue
  • Close-ups of the fabric, prints, embroidery, buttons, lining and neckline
  • A short video of the model walking or turning, which shows drape and movement better than any still
  • True colour: the same lighting for every shoot, light editing only, and a check of edited photos against the real piece
  • The model's height and the size worn, written under the photos

Good daylight phone photos on a plain background often work well for a boutique. Before upload, resize and compress them; image optimisation for WordPress explains sizes and formats step by step.

Size guides and fit notes that cut returns

Wrong size is one of the most common reasons clothes come back. A size chart only helps if shoppers can read it on a phone and trust it.

  • Body or garment measurements? Say which one the chart shows. Garment measurements, taken flat, are the most useful for fitted pieces.
  • Centimetres and inches, since many Indian shoppers measure in inches
  • A chart per category, because a kurta, a blazer and a skirt are measured differently; include lengths for kurtas and dresses
  • Fit notes such as "relaxed fit", "runs small, size up" or "stretch fabric", written by someone who has tried the piece on
  • How to measure, with a simple diagram for bust, waist, hip and length
  • Free size and adjustable pieces explained, with the measurements they actually fit
  • Help choosing: a "not sure of your size?" WhatsApp link with the product name pre-filled

Size charts in WooCommerce

Build size charts as text tables, not images, so they stay readable on small screens and can be read by screen readers. One chart per category, shown in a tab or pop-up on every product in that category, is easier to keep accurate than a separate chart typed into each product. A size chart plugin can do this, or a small custom block if you want it to match your theme exactly. If you also offer made-to-measure or alterations, a guided measurement form at checkout works better than asking for measurements over chat.

Sizes, colours and stock as variations

Each size and colour combination should be a proper WooCommerce variation, with its own stock count, SKU and, for colours, its own photos. Shoppers then see straight away which sizes are available instead of messaging to ask.

  • Use global attributes for Size and Colour, so filters work across the whole catalogue
  • Show sold-out sizes greyed out rather than hiding them, so shoppers know the size exists
  • Colour swatches instead of a dropdown, with each swatch switching the main photo
  • One-off pieces, common in boutiques, can be simple products with a stock of one
  • Back-in-stock alerts for popular sizes, sent only to shoppers who ask for them

WooCommerce product variations walks through attributes, bulk editing and swatches. If the same stock is sold in your shop and online, plan how counts stay in sync before launch, so a piece sold at the counter does not stay available on the website.

Lookbooks, collections and occasion pages

People rarely shop for "a top". They shop for a wedding function, a work week, a holiday or a festival. Organise the catalogue that way as well as by category:

  • Lookbooks: styled outfits, each with a "shop the look" list linking to every piece in it
  • Occasion pages: festive wear, wedding guest, workwear, vacation and everyday basics
  • New arrivals and drops: a page that always shows the latest pieces, which you can share on Instagram and WhatsApp
  • Festival and season edits kept at the same address each year and refreshed, so they keep the visibility they have earned
  • Accessories suggested on outfit pages, such as the dupatta, bag or earrings that complete the look

Collection pages need a short, written introduction and descriptive product names such as "hand block printed cotton kurta set" rather than codes, because those words are what people type into Google.

Keeping an image-heavy store fast

A boutique catalogue is mostly photos, so it can easily become slow on mobile data. The common causes are oversized images, autoplaying videos at the top of the page, an Instagram feed widget loading on every page, heavy sliders and product filters that reload the whole page. Practical fixes:

  • Upload images at the size the theme displays, in WebP where possible, with lazy loading below the first screen
  • Show videos on product pages only when tapped, not as background videos
  • Replace a live Instagram feed with a few linked images, or load it only on the homepage
  • Keep the product filter light, and test collection pages on a mid-range phone over mobile data

If your store is already slow, WordPress and WooCommerce speed optimisation finds out whether the cause is hosting, plugins, the theme, images or scripts, and fixes it safely with test orders after each change.

Selling through Instagram and WhatsApp, with the store behind it

  • Link to products, not the homepage: every post, reel and Story about a piece should lead to that product page
  • Product tags on Instagram: a Meta catalogue connected to WooCommerce lets you tag products in posts where Meta's shopping features are available; see connecting WooCommerce to Meta's catalogue
  • A WhatsApp button on product pages that opens a chat with the product name already typed, so staff know what the shopper is asking about
  • Video calls for bridal, occasion or high-value pieces, booked through a short form with the occasion, budget and preferred time
  • Orders agreed on WhatsApp can still go through the store: create the order in WooCommerce and send the customer its payment link, so stock, invoice and order history stay in one place

COD, exchanges and returns for clothing

Cash on delivery still matters to many shoppers, and clothing is returned more often than most products, so set the rules before launch:

  • COD limits: a cap on order value or certain pin codes, any COD fee shown clearly, and COD orders confirmed on WhatsApp before dispatch; see cash on delivery in WooCommerce
  • Exchanges for size are often easier for a boutique than refunds; say how to request one and who pays for the return shipping
  • Conditions: unworn, unwashed, tags attached and within a set number of days
  • Exclusions: custom-stitched or altered pieces, and any categories you exclude for hygiene reasons, listed clearly
  • Refunds or store credit: how and when each is given
  • GST invoices for every order; GST rates on clothing have depended on the item's price band and have changed over time, so set up tax classes as your CA advises

Keep one returns page and link to it from product pages, checkout and order emails. Returns and refunds for WooCommerce stores covers the wording and the process.

Store visits and local search

Many boutique customers still want to try before they buy, and local shoppers search for boutiques by area, such as "designer boutique in Koregaon Park" or "kurta boutique near me".

  • A Google Business Profile with real photos of the store, trial area and current stock, and hours that include festival timings
  • A store page with the address, a landmark, parking and what you offer in person, such as alterations or custom fittings
  • A "reserve to try in store" option for selected pieces, if your staff can manage holds
  • Product structured data so Google can show price and availability alongside your listings

Common mistakes on boutique websites

  • A size chart as a blurry image, or one generic chart for every category
  • Photos edited so heavily that colours look different in person
  • Sizes that look available but are not, because stock is not updated
  • Instagram posts that link only to the homepage
  • Exchange and COD rules that differ between the website, WhatsApp and the shop counter
  • Huge images and widgets that make collection pages slow on mobile

FAQs

Do I need a website if I already sell on Instagram?

Instagram is good for discovery, but a website lets shoppers browse sizes, see stock and pay at any time without waiting for a reply. It also gives you a customer list you own, which matters if your reach on social media drops.

How do I add a size chart to WooCommerce products?

Create one chart per category as a text table and show it in a tab or pop-up on every product in that category. A size chart plugin or a small custom block can do this, and it is easier to keep accurate than a chart typed into each product.

Should a fashion boutique offer cash on delivery?

Often yes, because many shoppers expect it. Limit it by order value or pin code, show any COD fee clearly, and confirm COD orders on WhatsApp before dispatch to reduce refused deliveries.

Can I sell one-off and handmade pieces?

Yes. A unique piece can be a simple product with a stock of one, so it shows as sold once someone buys it. Say clearly in the description that it is a single piece.

How should exchanges for the wrong size work?

State the time limit, the condition the piece must be in, who pays the return shipping and how the replacement is sent. Keep the same rules on the website, in WhatsApp replies and at the shop counter.
