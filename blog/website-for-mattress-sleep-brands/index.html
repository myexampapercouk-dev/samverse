Mattress Brand Website in India: Comparisons, Trials, Sizes and Trust on Shopify or WooCommerce

A mattress brand website in India has to replace the showroom experience: explain how each mattress feels and who it suits, help buyers pick the right size for an Indian bed, and spell out trial, warranty, delivery and EMI terms honestly. Built on Shopify or WooCommerce, the store should make comparing models easy and give enough reassurance for a purchase people make once every several years.

Here is how I plan mattress and sleep product stores, from platform choice to the pages that do the selling.

What a website for mattress brands must answer

  • How firm is it, and will it suit my sleeping position and body weight?
  • What is inside: foam types, springs, latex, coir, and their thickness?
  • Which size fits my bed, including custom sizes?
  • Is there a trial period, and how does a return actually work?
  • What does the warranty cover, and for how long?
  • When will it arrive, and is it delivered rolled or flat?

Most buyers also compare two or three of your own models. Make that comparison effortless.

Product pages that explain feel and fit

  • A firmness scale used consistently across all products, with a sentence on what it means in practice.
  • "Best for" notes: back sleepers, side sleepers, couples, those who prefer a firm or soft feel.
  • A layer diagram showing each layer, its material and thickness.
  • Size and thickness options in inches and centimetres, matching common Indian bed sizes.
  • Cover details: removable or not, washable or not, fabric.
  • Delivery format: roll-packed or flat, and how long to let it expand.

Variants for size and thickness are covered in product variations, including how to keep each size and thickness combination priced correctly.

Photos and video that show what words cannot

A mattress looks much the same as any other in a flat photo. Plan visuals that answer the questions buyers would answer by touch in a showroom:

  • A cut-away or exploded view showing the real layers, matched to the layer diagram.
  • A short video of someone pressing a hand into the surface and lying down, so the give is visible.
  • The mattress on a typical Indian bed, with a person for scale, to show height.
  • Unboxing and expansion for roll-packed models, including how long it takes.
  • Close-ups of the cover fabric, zip and stitching.

Keep videos short and host them on a video platform rather than inside the store, so product pages stay fast on mobile.

Own store, marketplaces and showrooms together

Many sleep brands also sell through marketplaces and dealer showrooms. Your website is where the full range, the detailed comparisons and the direct trial policy live, and where you build a customer list for accessories like pillows and protectors later. Keep model names and specifications consistent across every channel, so a buyer who tried a mattress at a dealer can find exactly the same one online.

A size guide built for Indian beds

Size confusion causes many returns. Indian beds are often made by local carpenters, so "queen" or "king" can mean different dimensions. A good size guide:

  1. Tells the buyer to measure the inside of the bed frame, length and width.
  2. Shows standard sizes in a simple table, in inches and centimetres.
  3. Explains how to choose thickness for a bed with a high or low headboard.
  4. Offers a custom size option, or a WhatsApp link to confirm before ordering.

Add a "Measure your bed" link right next to the size selector, not hidden in a footer.

For custom sizes, decide whether price is calculated from length and width automatically or confirmed manually. Automatic pricing is quicker for the buyer but needs careful rules and testing. Manual confirmation is safer at the start, and also lets your team catch measurement mistakes before production begins.

Comparison pages that actually help

A side-by-side comparison of your own range is one of the most useful pages you can build. Compare firmness, layers, height, trial, warranty and best-suited sleepers, using the same terms as the product pages. Do not compare against named competitors with claims you cannot prove; it creates legal risk and buyers rarely trust it. A short "which mattress is right for me" quiz can guide people who are unsure, as long as it recommends based on clear rules.

Trials, returns and warranty, explained honestly

Trial periods are a strong reason to buy online, but only if the terms are clear before purchase:

  • How long the trial lasts and when it starts.
  • Minimum use period before a return, if any.
  • How pickup works, who pays, and how the refund is made.
  • Areas where trial or pickup is not available.
  • What the warranty covers (for example sagging beyond a stated depth) and what it does not.

Write these in one plain-language policy page and link it from every product. A guide to policy writing is in returns and refunds policy.

Be careful with health and sleep claims

Phrases like "orthopaedic", "cures back pain", "doctor recommended" or "improves sleep quality" need real support. Describe what the mattress is (for example, a firm support layer) rather than what it will do for a medical condition. If you mention a test or certification, make sure the product genuinely holds it and describe it accurately. Suggest that buyers with back problems talk to their doctor. Check current advertising rules before using strong claims.

Reviews and proof buyers believe

Ask for reviews after the customer has slept on the mattress for a few weeks, not on delivery day. Let reviewers mention sleeping position and the model and size bought. Publish the critical reviews too, with a helpful reply. Real photos of the mattress in customers' homes, shared with permission, are more convincing than polished studio shots. See collecting product reviews.

Delivery, payments, EMI and where to try

  • Pin code check for delivery times, trial availability and pickup.
  • UPI, cards and EMI options through an Indian gateway that supports them.
  • COD only with limits, since large items are expensive to return.
  • Assembly or old mattress removal, if you genuinely offer it.
  • A store or experience centre locator if you have physical outlets.
  • GST invoices, with HSN codes confirmed by your CA.

Content and SEO for sleep brands

Head terms like "best mattress" are dominated by large brands and marketplaces. A growing brand does better with specific, helpful content:

  • Guides on choosing firmness by sleeping position.
  • Size guides for Indian beds and custom sizes.
  • Comparisons of materials, such as memory foam vs latex vs spring, written fairly.
  • Care guides: rotating, cleaning and protecting a mattress in humid weather.

Each guide should link to the relevant product or comparison page. Use the Shopify SEO checklist to get the technical basics right.

Budget and build

A mattress store is a small catalogue with a lot of explaining, so the cost sits in product pages, comparison content and integrations rather than in the number of products. FAQs

What should a mattress brand website in India include?

Clear product pages with firmness and layers, a size guide for Indian beds, model comparisons, honest trial and warranty terms, reviews, EMI and delivery information.

Is Shopify good for a mattress brand?

Yes, for most D2C brands. WooCommerce suits brands that need custom-size pricing, configurators or heavy content.

How do I sell custom-size mattresses online?

Use a size input with pricing rules, or a confirm-on-WhatsApp step for unusual sizes. Show how to measure the bed first.

Can I call my mattress orthopaedic?

Only with genuine support for the claim. Describe the construction rather than promising medical benefits, and check current advertising rules.

Should a mattress store offer cash on delivery?

Some do, with limits. Large items are costly to return, so many brands encourage prepaid or EMI and confirm COD orders.

Is a website for mattress brands worth it if we already sell on marketplaces?

Usually yes. Your own site is where you control how each model is explained, collect reviews and direct enquiries, and point showroom and social traffic. Marketplaces can stay as an extra channel.
