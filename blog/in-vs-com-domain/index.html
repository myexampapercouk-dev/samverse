.in vs .com (and .co.in): Which Domain Should an Indian Business Choose?

.in or .com for an Indian business? How audience, trust, Google country signals, cost, .in registration rules, email and owning both should shape your choice.

Key takeaways
  • Choose .in if your customers are mainly in India and .com if you sell abroad or plan to go global; prefer .in over .co.in for a new business, and choose a clean name over an awkward .com.
  • Google treats .in as a signal of relevance to India, while a .com relies on content, addresses, links and your Business Profile now that the Search Console country setting has been retired.
  • Where possible, register both in your own name, use one for your website and email, 301-redirect the other with a working SSL certificate, and protect the unused domain from email spoofing.

You've settled on a business name, and now the registrar is offering .com, .in, .co.in and a dozen newer extensions. Which one should you pick? For most Indian businesses there's no single wrong answer, but the right choice depends on who your customers are and what's actually available. This guide focuses on the extension; for choosing the name itself, see how to choose a domain name for your business.

The short answer

  • Customers mainly in India: .in is a strong, natural choice, and .com works just as well if you can get it
  • Customers abroad, or plans to go global: .com, because it carries no country signal
  • New business choosing today: prefer .in over .co.in; it's shorter and more familiar
  • If both are available: register .in and .com, use one as your main address and redirect the other

Start with your audience

  • Local and national businesses: clinics, coaching institutes, contractors, shops and service companies serving Indian customers. A .in address clearly says "Indian business", which is usually a plus.
  • Exporters and international B2B: overseas buyers are most familiar with .com. A .in address isn't a problem in itself, but it signals a focus on India, which isn't always the message you want; see websites for export businesses.
  • SaaS, IT services and startups with global ambitions: .com is the safer long-term choice, even if your first customers are in India.

Trust and local signals

.com is the default most people type without thinking. Indian customers are also used to seeing .in addresses, so either looks professional. For a local business, .in can even be a small trust signal: it tells visitors at a glance that you're based in India, which matters for things like delivery, pricing in rupees and after-sales service.

But the extension is a small part of trust. Visitors decide far more from your address and phone number, real photos, reviews, GST and registration details where relevant, and a fast, secure site. A .com with no local details won't look more trustworthy than a well-built .in.

The practical risk is habit. If your address is yourbrand.in, some people will type yourbrand.com, especially when writing your email address. If someone else owns that .com, those visitors and emails go to them. Owning both extensions solves this.

Availability and cost

Short, memorable .com names are hard to find because so many are already registered. The same name is often still free as a .in. Before choosing, check what's on the .com version of your name:

  • Parked or unused: manageable, but some customers will land there by mistake
  • For sale: only worth buying if the price makes sense for your business
  • An active business in your field: risky, because customers and emails will go to them, and there may be trademark issues

Don't twist your name into a hyphenated or misspelt .com just to get the extension. A clean .in beats an awkward .com.

On price, standard .in and .com registrations are usually in the same broad range, but first-year offers, renewal prices and whether GST is shown upfront vary by registrar. Discounted first years are common, and renewals are often higher. Names already owned by someone else, or listed as "premium", can cost far more whichever extension you choose. Compare the yearly renewal cost, not the headline offer.

Registering a .in: rules and ownership

The .in registry is run by the National Internet Exchange of India (NIXI), and you buy through accredited registrars. A few points worth knowing, all of which can change, so check your registrar's current rules:

  • Who can register: at the time of writing, you don't need to live in India or have an Indian company to register a plain .in. Some extensions, such as .gov.in and .ac.in, are restricted to eligible organisations.
  • Accurate details: registrant details must be accurate, and registrars may ask you to verify your identity or contact details. False details can put the domain at risk.
  • Register it in the business's name, with an email address you control, in your own registrar account. A domain registered in a developer's or ex-employee's name is a common headache later.
  • Disputes: arguments over who has the right to a .in name go through the .IN Domain Name Dispute Resolution Policy (INDRP), while .com disputes generally use the UDRP. If you're protecting a trademark, ask a lawyer.
  • Privacy: what contact details are shown publicly has differed between .in and .com over the years; ask your registrar.

Once you own a domain, expect official-looking emails offering "renewals", SEO packages or rival registrations of your name in other countries. Most are sales tactics or scams; see domain renewal and SEO scam emails.

SEO: does the extension affect rankings?

A little, in a specific way. Google treats .in as a country-code domain, so it takes it as a signal that the site is especially relevant to users in India. That helps Google understand your audience; it doesn't make you rank higher on its own.

A .com is generic and has no country attached. Search Console used to have an International Targeting report where owners of generic domains could choose a target country. Google retired that report, and its country setting, around 2022, so you can't set a country for a .com there any more. Instead Google works it out from other signals: your content and language, addresses and phone numbers, links from Indian websites, your Google Business Profile and, for multi-country sites, hreflang tags (see hreflang for Indian websites).

  • A .com site can rank perfectly well in India
  • A .in site can rank abroad, but the country signal works against it for international audiences
  • Google treats a few country codes, such as .io and .co, as generic because they're used worldwide; .in isn't one of them
  • For local searches like "CA in Jaipur", your Google Business Profile, reviews and location pages matter far more than the extension; see the local SEO guide for Indian businesses
  • Keywords in the domain carry little weight, so choose for your brand, not for search

What about .co.in and other options?

  • .co.in was common before plain .in registrations became popular. It's longer, and people easily shorten it to .co or .in by mistake. If you've used one for years it's fine to keep; for a new business, .in is simpler.
  • .org.in suits non-profits and trusts; .gov.in and academic extensions like .ac.in are restricted to eligible government and educational institutions.
  • Newer extensions such as .shop or .tech can work for strong brands but are less familiar to many customers.

The brand and email angle

Your domain appears in far more places than your website: email addresses, visiting cards, invoices, vehicle branding and social profiles. Say it out loud over the phone before you commit.

  • Email is where mix-ups hurt most. A customer who types sales@yourbrand.com instead of sales@yourbrand.in sends their enquiry or purchase order to whoever owns the .com. Owning both and forwarding mail from the second domain catches these.
  • Use one domain consistently for email, signatures, print and your Google Business Profile.
  • Protect the domain you don't send from. Scammers can spoof a domain that never sends email. SPF and DMARC records stating that no mail is sent from it make that harder; see SPF, DKIM and DMARC explained.

Buying both, and setting it up properly

  1. Register both in your own account, not your developer's, and turn on auto-renew
  2. Pick one primary domain for your website, business email, visiting cards and Google Business Profile
  3. Redirect the other with a permanent 301 redirect to the same page on your main domain; see 301 vs 302 redirects
  4. Make the redirect work on https too. If someone types https://yourbrand.com, the redirecting domain needs its own SSL certificate, or visitors see a security warning first. Pointing it at your hosting and redirecting there is usually more reliable than basic registrar forwarding.
  5. Don't run two copies of the same website on both extensions; that creates duplicate content
  6. Consider email forwarding on the second domain, so messages sent to the wrong extension still reach you

Every extra domain is one more renewal to track; keep them on the renewal checklist.

Switching extensions later

If you start on .co.in or .in and later move to a .com, it's very doable. Every old URL is 301-redirected to its new address, Search Console's Change of Address tool tells Google about the move, and your email, Google Business Profile, social profiles and printed material are updated. Expect some temporary ranking movement while Google processes the change, and keep the old domain renewed permanently so the redirects keep working. The full process is in how to change your domain name without losing SEO.

FAQs

Is .in good for SEO?

Yes, for businesses targeting India. It tells Google the site is relevant to Indian users, which helps with audience matching, but it won't lift rankings on its own. Content, links and local signals still do most of the work.

Can someone outside India register a .in domain?

At the time of writing, yes: plain .in domains are open to registrants worldwide, though details must be accurate and registrars may ask for verification. Some extensions, such as .gov.in and .ac.in, are restricted.

Is .co.in worse than .in?

Not for SEO; Google treats both as Indian country-code domains. It's just longer and easier to mistype, so for a new business .in is usually the better pick.

I already have a .in. Should I buy the .com too?

If it's available at a normal price, it's usually worth it, mainly to catch mistyped emails and visits. Redirect it to your .in rather than building a second site.

Does a .in look less professional than a .com?

Not to Indian customers, who see .in addresses all the time. Overseas buyers may read it as India-focused, which is only a drawback if you're selling internationally.
