Website Quizzes for Lead Generation: Product Finders, Assessments and Calculator-Style Quizzes That Help Visitors Decide

Plan a lead generation quiz: product finders, assessments and calculator-style quizzes, question design, consent, result pages and WhatsApp follow-up.

Key takeaways
  • A quiz earns leads only when the result is genuinely useful, so show the answer first and make sharing contact details an optional next step with clear consent.
  • Keep it to the few questions that change the result, map answers to results in a simple rules table, and recommend the cheaper option when it fits.
  • Send quiz answers to your CRM or inbox with the result attached, follow up on WhatsApp only with permission, and track starts, drop-offs and completions.

A lead generation quiz is a short set of questions on your website that ends with a personalised answer: the product that fits, a score with next steps, or a rough estimate. It works when visitors face several similar options and want help choosing. The pattern that tends to work best is four to seven easy questions, an honest result shown on screen, then an optional offer to send the full result or talk to someone, with consent asked clearly. Answers and the result then go to your inbox or CRM so your team can follow up with context.

On a WordPress site this can be built with a form plugin, a dedicated quiz plugin or a custom block, depending on how complex the rules are. I build these as part of WordPress website development for service businesses, and as product finders on stores through WooCommerce development.

Three types of quiz, and what each is for

TypeWhat the visitor getsGood examplesWatch out for
Product or service finderA recommended plan, product, package or courseMattresses, internet plans, gym memberships, coaching batches, skincare ranges, interior packagesRecommending the priciest option to everyone
Assessment or readiness checkA score or category with next steps"Is your website ready for ads?", "How export-ready is your business?", a home safety checklistScores that sound scientific but aren't
Calculator-style quizA rough range, quantity or sizeSolar system size, paint quantity, tiffin plan cost, website budget rangeRanges presented as quotes

If your visitors mainly want a number, a straight calculator may suit better than a quiz; website calculators for lead generation covers that format. If you mainly need details to quote, a qualifying form is simpler; see multi-step forms for lead qualification.

When a quiz is worth building

  • You have several options that look alike to an outsider
  • The right choice depends on things visitors know about themselves: budget, usage, space, goals or preferences
  • Customers already ask "which one should I take?" by phone or WhatsApp
  • You can write down the rules your best salesperson uses to recommend

Skip it if you only have two or three options; a comparison table is quicker. Be very careful where a recommendation needs a professional, such as medical treatment, legal matters or financial products. A quiz can help someone decide whom to speak to, but it should not diagnose or advise.

Designing questions people finish

Every question should change the result. If an answer makes no difference, cut it.

  • Few questions: four to seven is often plenty, one per screen on mobile
  • Everyday language: "How does your skin feel by the afternoon?" is easier than "What is your skin type?"
  • Tappable answers with simple icons or images, not typing
  • A "Not sure" option, handled sensibly in the result
  • Visible progress such as "Question 3 of 5", and a back button
  • Easiest question first, sensitive ones (budget, health) later, and only if they matter
  • Local options: Hindi or regional-language versions if your customers prefer them

An example: a mattress finder

  1. Who will sleep on it? (One person, two people, a child)
  2. How do you usually sleep? (Back, side, stomach, it changes)
  3. Do you wake with back or shoulder stiffness? (Often, sometimes, rarely)
  4. Do you feel hot at night? (Yes, no, only in summer)
  5. Which bed size? (Single, double, queen, king, not sure)
  6. Your budget range (three bands plus "show me everything")

Six questions, each of which changes the recommendation. Note what is missing: name and phone number. Those come after the result, if the visitor wants more.

Result pages that build trust

The result is the reason people take the quiz, so it carries most of the weight.

  • Rules your team agrees with. Write a simple table mapping answer combinations to results, and review it when products or prices change.
  • Explain why: "Because you sleep on your side and feel hot at night, we suggest..."
  • Recommend the cheaper option when it genuinely fits. It earns more trust than an upsell.
  • Show an alternative and a link to compare everything, so the quiz never feels like a funnel with one exit.
  • Avoid claims you can't support, especially about health, beauty or money, and suggest a professional for anything persistent or serious.
  • One clear next step: buy, book a visit, or message on WhatsApp with the result attached.

Lead capture and consent

The fairest pattern is to show the result on screen, then offer something extra: the full result by email, a personalised plan on WhatsApp, or a short call to confirm the choice. People who want it share their details willingly. Hiding the result behind a compulsory phone number tends to bring fake numbers and frustrated visitors.

  • Say what they will receive and how you will contact them
  • Ask only for what you need, usually a name and one contact method
  • Keep marketing consent as a separate, unticked checkbox
  • Treat quiz answers as personal data, especially anything about health, finances or children, and mention the quiz in your privacy policy
  • Decide how long you keep answers, and delete them when they are no longer needed

India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act sets general expectations around notice, consent and purpose, and its rules are still being phased in. For a plain-language overview, read India's DPDP Act and your website, and confirm what applies to your business with a lawyer.

Ways to build a quiz on WordPress

ApproachSuitsTrade-offs
Form plugin with conditional logicSimple finders and assessments with a few result pathsQuick to build; complex scoring gets messy
Dedicated quiz or product-recommendation pluginScoring, many results, store product matchingMore features, but check maintenance, script weight and data storage
Custom block or small custom pluginBranded experiences, complex rules, tight speed budgetsMost control and lightest output; needs a developer for changes

Whichever you choose, check that the quiz is actively maintained, loads its scripts only on the page that uses it, runs smoothly on an ordinary Android phone using 4G, and stores answers somewhere you control. WordPress form plugins compared helps if you go the form plugin route.

On a WooCommerce store

  • Map results to categories, tags or attributes rather than single products, so recommendations keep working as your range changes
  • Show live price, stock and an add-to-cart button on the result
  • Hide out-of-stock products, or show the next-best option
  • Offer a complete set where it makes sense using bundles and cross-sells

Follow-up: CRM, email and WhatsApp

A quiz lead arrives with context, so use it. The notification to your team should include every answer and the result, not just a name and number.

  • Send leads to one place: a shared inbox, a Google Sheet or a CRM. Connecting website forms to a CRM covers the options.
  • Reply quickly, with the result in hand: "You chose the medium-firm range; would a store visit on Saturday help?" lands better than a generic call. See following up website leads fast.
  • WhatsApp only with permission, and in line with WhatsApp's own business messaging policies, which change from time to time.
  • Tag by result, so later messages are relevant rather than a blast to everyone.

Measuring whether it works

Using GA4 events, track quiz starts, each question answered, completions, which results appear most, clicks from results to products or WhatsApp, and opt-ins. Then look at outcomes: if one result leads to frequent returns or "not what I expected" feedback, the mapping is probably wrong. If many people choose "Not sure" on one question, rewrite or remove it.

Pre-launch checklist

  • Every question changes the result
  • Result shown before any contact form
  • Rules table reviewed by the people who sell
  • Consent wording and privacy policy updated
  • Answers and result included in lead notifications
  • Tested on a mid-range phone over mobile data
  • Tracking events firing for start, completion and next step

FAQs

How many questions should a lead generation quiz have?

Usually four to seven, one per screen. Keep only questions that change the result; each extra question gives people another reason to leave.

Should I ask for a phone number before showing the result?

Generally no. Show the result first and offer extra value, such as a detailed plan or a call, in exchange for contact details with clear consent. Forced capture tends to bring fake numbers.

Can a quiz recommend WooCommerce products automatically?

Yes. Map answers to categories, tags or attributes, then show matching in-stock products with prices and add-to-cart buttons on the result page.

Do quiz answers count as personal data?

They can, especially when linked to a name or number, or when they touch on health, money or children. Mention the quiz in your privacy policy, collect only what you need and check obligations with a lawyer.

Is a quiz better than a calculator?

It depends on the question. A quiz suits "which one is right for me"; a calculator suits "how much or how many". Some businesses use both on different pages.
