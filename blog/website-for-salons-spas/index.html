Websites for Salons and Spas: Service Menus With Durations, Booking by Stylist, Bridal Packages, Memberships, Real Galleries, Hygiene and Google Reviews

Plan a salon or spa website that gets bookings: service menus with durations, stylist choice, bridal packages, memberships, hygiene and Google reviews.

Key takeaways
  • Write the service menu with durations and starting prices, because clients plan around time as much as cost and your booking system needs both.
  • Let clients choose a stylist or therapist when booking, and give bridal packages and memberships their own pages with clear inclusions and terms.
  • Show your own work with client consent, say plainly how you keep tools and rooms clean, and link the site to your Google Business Profile reviews.

A salon or spa website wins bookings when it lets someone answer three questions on their phone: what do you offer, how long will it take, and can I book with the person I want? Get the service menu, booking and gallery right, add honest pages for bridal work, memberships and hygiene, and the site does what your receptionist does on a busy day.

Most salon sites gain more from a few focused pages than from a big design. The core pages are a standard business build, covered under WordPress website development, but the pages that bring new clients are often campaign pages: a festive offer, a bridal season page, a first-visit package for a new branch. Those work best as landing pages built for one offer, with a booking form and WhatsApp, and tracking that shows which ads actually brought clients in.

Write the service menu before anything else

The menu is the page clients visit most, and it is also the data your booking system runs on. Each service needs a name clients recognise, a duration, a starting price or a note on what decides the price, and the level of staff who performs it. A section of a well-planned menu looks like this:

ServiceDurationPrice shown asBooked with
Haircut and blow-dry, women45 to 60 minutesStarting price, varies by stylist level and hair lengthAny stylist, or a chosen one
Global hair colour2 to 3 hoursStarting price, final after consultation and patch testColour specialist
Keratin or smoothening3 to 4 hoursRange by hair lengthSenior stylist
Classic facial60 minutesFixed priceAny therapist
Swedish massage60 or 90 minutesFixed price per durationTherapist, with a choice of male or female where offered

Durations help clients plan a lunch break or a Saturday morning, and they stop the booking system from double-booking a chair. Group services the way clients look for them: hair, skin, nails, makeup, spa and massage, men's grooming, bridal. If a service needs a patch test or consultation first, say so on the menu, not at the chair.

Price display that does not backfire

"Starting from" prices are honest only if a typical client can actually pay that. If length, density or product choice changes the price a lot, give a range or explain the factors, and say whether prices include GST. Keep the menu on a web page, not a PDF or an Instagram image, so it can be searched, read on a phone and updated in minutes.

Booking with stylist or therapist choice

Regular clients book a person, not a salon. Let them choose a named stylist or "any available", and show only the services that person performs. Good salon booking also handles:

  • Buffer time between services for cleaning and setup
  • Combined bookings, such as a cut and colour with the same stylist, or a pedicure while hair colour develops
  • Deposits for long services and bridal bookings, by UPI or card, with the cancellation terms on the same screen
  • Reminders by WhatsApp or SMS the day before, with a link to reschedule
  • Walk-in information, so people know whether to come without booking

If you already use salon management software for billing and appointments, the site should link to or embed its booking page rather than run a second calendar. If you do not, a WordPress booking plugin can do the job for a single location. Online appointment booking for small businesses compares the routes.

Late arrivals and no-shows

A missed three-hour colour appointment costs a salon a large part of a stylist's day. Publish your policy where clients book: how late a client can be before the service is shortened or moved, how much notice you need for a cancellation, and when a deposit is kept. Clients rarely object to rules they saw before booking; they object to rules they hear about for the first time at the counter.

Bridal packages deserve a page of their own

Bridal work is high value and booked months ahead, often by the bride and her mother together. A bridal page should set out:

  1. Packages by stage: pre-bridal skin and hair sessions, the trial, the wedding day look, and family or bridesmaid makeup
  2. What each package includes, such as number of sessions, products used and draping
  3. Whether the artist travels to the venue, and how travel is charged
  4. The trial: when to book it, how long it takes, and whether its cost is adjusted against the package
  5. How far ahead to book in wedding season, and the deposit needed to hold a date
  6. A gallery of real brides, shared with permission

A form asking for the wedding date, the events, the number of people needing makeup and the venue city lets you reply with a proper quote. If your senior artist also works independently, websites for makeup artists covers personal portfolio sites.

Memberships, packages and gift vouchers

Memberships and prepaid packages bring steady revenue, but clients are wary of them after hearing about expired balances. Write the terms plainly: what the membership costs and gives, how long it is valid, whether it can be shared with family, whether unused value carries over, and what happens if a branch closes. Prepaid service packages, such as six facials, need the same clarity. Gift vouchers can be sold online with a code the client redeems at the counter; gift cards in WooCommerce explains how that can be set up.

A gallery of your own work, with consent

Clients choose salons by looking at results. Use your own photos: haircuts, colour work, nail art, bridal looks and the salon itself. A few rules keep the gallery trustworthy:

  • Ask clients before photographing them and record their permission for website and social use
  • Avoid heavy filters on hair colour and skin results, because clients will expect what they see
  • Label photos by service and stylist, so a client can find the person whose work they like
  • Never use stock or competitor images as your own work
  • Compress images so the gallery loads quickly on mobile

Google Business Profile photos covers which of these images also belong on your profile.

Hygiene, products and safety

Since the pandemic, many clients look for a few words on cleanliness before they book, especially for spa treatments, waxing and nail services. Describe what you actually do: how tools are sterilised or disposed of, whether single-use files and buffers are used, how often towels and linen are changed, and how rooms are cleaned between clients. Name the product brands you use if clients ask for them, and say how you handle allergies and patch tests for colour and chemical treatments. Keep these claims accurate; one review that contradicts them does more harm than not mentioning them.

Unisex, women only, or separate sections

Many clients want to know this before they visit, and some will not book without knowing it. State on the homepage and the booking page whether the salon is unisex, women only or men only, whether there are separate sections or floors, and whether clients can request a therapist of the same gender for massage and waxing. For spas, say clearly what kind of massage you offer and that the spa offers professional therapeutic and relaxation treatments only.

Reviews, and the Google Business Profile

For a salon, the Google Business Profile often brings more bookings than the website, and the two should support each other. Keep hours, services and photos matching on both, add a booking link to the profile, and ask happy clients for a review at the end of a visit, using a short link or a printed code near the billing desk. Respond to each review, including the critical ones, calmly and without arguing. Getting more Google reviews explains how to ask without breaking Google's rules, and handling negative reviews covers replies.

On the website itself, show reviews only from real clients, with their permission, and add a link through to the full set on Google.

FAQs

Should a salon website show prices?

Yes, at least starting prices or ranges for common services, with durations. Explain what changes the final price, such as hair length or product choice.

Can clients choose their stylist when booking online?

Yes. Booking systems can show each stylist's calendar and the services they perform, with an "any available" option for clients who do not mind.

Should we take a deposit for bridal bookings?

Many salons do, to hold the date. Take it online by UPI or card and show the cancellation and refund terms on the same page.

Can we use client photos on our website?

Only with the client's permission, ideally recorded in writing. Label each photo with the service and avoid filters that change the result.

Do we need separate pages for each branch?

Yes. Each branch needs its own address, hours, services, stylists and booking link, matching its own Google Business Profile.
