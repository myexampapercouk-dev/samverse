Websites for D2C Food Brands: Product Pages, Label Details, Shipping Perishables, Subscriptions, Reviews and Gifting

How D2C food brands sell packaged food online: product pages with ingredients and shelf life, shipping by pin code, subscriptions, reviews and gifting.

Key takeaways
  • A food buyer cannot taste before ordering, so product pages must answer every label question: ingredients, nutrition, allergens, shelf life, storage, net quantity and your FSSAI details.
  • Ship what survives the journey: check serviceable pin codes and transit times, pack for heat and breakage, and keep short-shelf-life products to zones you can reach quickly.
  • Repeat orders are where a D2C food brand makes its margin, so plan subscriptions or reorder reminders, trial packs, genuine reviews and festival gifting from the start.

A website for a D2C food brand has to do what a shop shelf and a free sample normally do: show exactly what is in the pack, how long it lasts, how it reaches the customer and why it is worth trying. The brands that sell well online have product pages that answer every label question, shipping rules that match what their products can survive, and a plan for the second and third order, through subscriptions, bundles, reviews and gifting.

This guide covers what is different about selling packaged food.

Product pages that answer a food buyer's questions

Online, the customer can't pick up the pack and turn it over, so the product page has to do that for them. Put the key facts as text on the page, not only inside a photo of the label, so they are readable on a phone and by Google.

DetailWhy buyers look for it
Full ingredient listDiet, allergies, and checking there is nothing they don't want
Nutrition informationCalories, protein and sugar, usually per 100 g and per serving
AllergensNuts, gluten, dairy, soy and sesame, including "made in a facility that also handles" notes
Veg or non-veg markMany Indian buyers check this before anything else
Net quantity and priceComparing pack sizes and value
Shelf life and best beforeWhether it will last until they finish it
Storage after openingRefrigerate or not, and how long it keeps once opened
FSSAI licence number and manufacturer detailsProof that a real, registered business stands behind it
How to use itServing ideas, recipes and quantities

FSSAI's labelling rules and India's rules for packaged goods expect much of this information to be available to customers before they buy online, not only on the pack they receive. The details depend on your product and licence, so confirm the current requirements with your FSSAI consultant before launch, and add clear photos of the front and back of the actual pack as well.

Be careful with claims

"Healthy", "immunity", "sugar-free", "high protein" and "organic" are words regulators take seriously. Use only claims the rules allow for your product and that you can back up. Products sold as organic generally need certification, and health claims about curing or preventing illness are not something a food brand should make. Describing what you actually do, such as "cold-pressed", "stone-ground" or "no added sugar" where it is true, is both safer and more convincing.

Shelf life, batches and freshness

  • State shelf life from the date of manufacture, and say you dispatch from recent batches, only if your stock rotation genuinely works that way.
  • Make-to-order products, such as fresh bakes or sweets, need dispatch days rather than "in stock". Tell customers when their order will be made and shipped.
  • Batch and expiry tracking isn't built into WooCommerce. Many small brands manage it in their warehouse sheet; a plugin can add it to orders if you need traceability on each order.
  • Pack sizes such as 250 g, 500 g and 1 kg work best as variations of one product, each with its own price, weight and stock.

Shipping food across India

Food is where shipping rules matter most, because a damaged or spoiled parcel costs you the product, the shipping both ways and the customer. Match the rules to what each product can survive:

Product typeShipping approach
Dry staples: flours, millets, spices, tea, coffeePan-India by courier; weight-based rates
Oils, ghee, pickles and honey in glassLeak-proof sealing and breakage-safe packing; consider plastic or tin packs for long routes
Chocolates and heat-sensitive productsInsulated packing in summer, faster services, and no dispatch before a weekend or holiday
Fresh bakes, sweets and short-shelf-life itemsLocal zones only, or express services to cities you can reach in time

In WooCommerce, shipping zones can run from your city to your state to the rest of India, with weight-based or free-above-a-threshold rates, and products that can only travel locally can be given their own shipping class. Accurate weights and box sizes matter, because couriers charge on volumetric weight. A pin code delivery checker on product pages tells customers before checkout whether you deliver to them, how long it takes and whether cash on delivery is available. WooCommerce shipping setup for India covers zones, rates and courier integrations step by step.

Cash on delivery deserves extra thought for food. A refused parcel of a perishable product usually can't be resold, so many brands limit COD by order value or pin code, or offer it only on long-life products.

Subscriptions, bundles and repeat orders

A D2C food brand earns its margin on repeat orders, because staples run out on a predictable schedule.

  • Subscriptions suit coffee, tea, millets, oils and snacks bought every month. Automatic recurring payments in India depend on your gateway's support for card and UPI mandates and on RBI's rules for them, so check what your gateway offers; selling subscriptions with WooCommerce explains the options.
  • Reorder reminders are a simpler alternative: an email or WhatsApp message, with consent, when a pack is likely to be running low, linking to a one-tap reorder.
  • Trial and sampler packs lower the risk for first-time buyers, who can then move up to full sizes.
  • Bundles and combos, such as a breakfast box or a spice starter kit, raise the order value; see product bundles, upsells and cross-sells.

Reviews that help first-time buyers

Taste is personal, so honest reviews from real buyers carry a lot of weight. WooCommerce can show a "verified owner" label on reviews from people who bought the product, and can limit reviews to verified owners. Ask for a review a week or two after delivery, once the customer has actually used the product, and encourage photos of the food as they served it. Don't offer discounts in exchange for reviews, don't hide critical ones, and reply to complaints about taste or packaging calmly. WooCommerce product reviews covers collecting, moderating and displaying them.

Gifting and festival boxes

Food is one of the most common gifts in India, from Diwali and Raksha Bandhan to weddings and corporate thank-yous, and gifting brings new customers who taste your product for the first time.

  • Gift options at checkout: a gift message, "ship to a different address" (built into WooCommerce checkout), and a packing slip without prices in the parcel, with the invoice emailed to the buyer
  • Ready-made gift boxes at a few price points, with clear photos of exactly what is inside
  • Order-by dates for each festival, shown on the gift pages and in the cart
  • Bulk and corporate orders through an enquiry form asking for quantity, delivery date and any branding, rather than forcing a 200-box order through the cart

Plan festival pages early and reuse them every year; seasonal and festival campaigns on your website covers timing, offers and traffic spikes. If corporate orders become a big part of the business, websites for corporate gifting companies shows how to handle catalogues and bulk quotes.

GST, invoices and the paperwork side

Food products fall under different GST rates and HSN codes depending on what they are, and some combinations, such as a gift box mixing items, need care. WooCommerce can hold a tax class for each rate and charge CGST and SGST or IGST based on the shipping state, with an invoice plugin adding your GSTIN and HSN codes. Ask your CA to confirm the rates and invoice format before launch. Your returns and refunds policy should also be clear about food: most brands accept returns only for damaged, wrong or spoiled items, reported with photos within a short window.

A launch checklist for food brands

  1. Product data in a spreadsheet: ingredients, nutrition, allergens, shelf life, storage, net quantity, weight and price for every pack size
  2. Front and back label photos, plus real photos of the food served
  3. FSSAI licence number, manufacturer or packer details and your GSTIN
  4. Shipping zones, which products can travel where, and your COD rules
  5. A returns policy for damaged, wrong or spoiled items
  6. A plan for repeat orders: subscriptions, reminders or trial packs
  7. Test orders through every payment method, checking emails and invoices

Gifting is a big opportunity; see websites for florists and gift shops.

The same repeat-order ideas work for skincare, haircare and makeup, where ingredient lists and careful claims matter even more; see websites for beauty and cosmetics brands.

FAQs

Do I need an FSSAI licence to sell food online?

Food businesses in India generally need FSSAI registration or a licence, whichever applies to your size and type of business, and the number should be shown to customers on your website. Your FSSAI consultant can confirm which one you need.

Can I ship perishable food across India?

Some of it. Dry staples travel well pan-India; fresh bakes, sweets and other short-shelf-life items are safer kept to local zones or express services to cities you can reach in time. Set this up with shipping classes so customers can't order what you can't deliver.

Should a food brand sell on marketplaces or its own website?

Usually both. Marketplaces bring reach; your own store keeps the margin, the customer relationship and room for subscriptions, bundles and gifting.

Can customers subscribe to monthly deliveries?

Yes. WooCommerce can sell subscriptions, and automatic renewals depend on your payment gateway's support for recurring payments in India. Reorder reminders are a simpler first step.

How should I show nutrition information on product pages?

As text or a simple table on the page, matching the pack exactly, with a photo of the back label as well. Text is readable on a phone, can be found by search engines and is easy to update when a recipe changes.
