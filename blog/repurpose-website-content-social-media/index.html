How to Repurpose Website Content for Social Media: Carousels, Reels, LinkedIn Posts, WhatsApp Broadcasts and Newsletters, With UTM Links and a Weekly Workflow

Turn one website article into carousels, reels, LinkedIn posts, WhatsApp broadcasts and a newsletter, link back with UTM tags and follow a weekly workflow.

Key takeaways
  • Break each article into parts first, such as the core idea, a checklist, tips, a myth and a FAQ answer, then turn each part into the format that suits a platform.
  • Make every piece useful on its own, and link back to the full article with UTM-tagged links so GA4 shows which platforms bring visitors and enquiries.
  • Follow a simple weekly routine for one article, reshare evergreen pieces every few months, and drop formats that never bring anyone back to the website.

To repurpose website content for social media, take one useful article, pull out its core idea, a checklist, a few tips and the questions it answers, and turn each part into a format that suits a platform: an Instagram carousel, a short reel, a LinkedIn post, a WhatsApp broadcast, a Google Business Profile update and a section of your newsletter. Each piece should be useful on its own and link back to the full article with a UTM-tagged link, so you can see in GA4 which platforms bring visitors and enquiries.

Repurposing only pays off if the article you send people to is worth landing on and can be found on Google as well. WordPress SEO services deal with indexing, titles, structured data and internal links. Writing the articles stays with you, but the work includes a list of the pages and answers your site lacks, with what each should cover, and that list doubles as material to repurpose. For planning what to publish in the first place, see how to plan a website content calendar.

Choose articles worth repurposing

Not every post deserves a week of social content. The best candidates:

  • Answer a question customers ask on calls, WhatsApp or at the counter
  • Link to a service or product, so visitors who click through have somewhere to go next
  • Stay true for a long time, so you can reshare them in a few months
  • Contain natural parts: steps, a checklist, a comparison, common mistakes or a worked example
  • Already get impressions in Search Console, which shows people care about the topic

News posts, announcements and very short articles rarely have enough in them. Case studies, on the other hand, repurpose very well, as long as the client has agreed to being featured.

A worked example: one article, seven pieces

Take a fictional modular kitchen company that publishes an article comparing plywood, MDF and HDHMR for kitchen cabinets, with a table of where each works best and a section on common mistakes. From that one article:

  1. An Instagram carousel: a cover slide asking which board suits a humid kitchen, one slide per material, a slide on the most common mistake and a final slide pointing to the full comparison
  2. A short reel filmed in the showroom, holding up each board sample with one line of on-screen text about each
  3. A LinkedIn post written for architects and interior designers, focusing on the specification questions their clients ask
  4. A Google Business Profile update with a photo of the samples and a button linking to the article
  5. A WhatsApp broadcast to customers who have opted in, with a three-line summary and the link
  6. A newsletter section explaining why the question matters before a renovation
  7. A new FAQ on the modular kitchen service page, answering the question in two sentences and linking to the full article

None of these pieces is a copy of the article. Each takes one part of it and fits it to how people use that platform.

Write for each platform, not just a link with a caption

  • Carousels: a first slide that names the problem, one idea per slide, large text readable on a phone, and the article named on the last slide
  • Reels and Shorts: show something real in the first seconds, add captions because many people watch without sound, and keep it to one point
  • LinkedIn: lead with the insight in plain text, use short paragraphs, and add the link in the post or the first comment, testing which works better for you; see using LinkedIn to bring B2B visitors
  • WhatsApp: short, personal and occasional, sent only to people who agreed to hear from you, with one link; WhatsApp marketing for small businesses covers broadcast lists and consent
  • Google Business Profile: a photo, a few lines and a "Learn more" button to the article; Business Profile posts explains the post types
  • Newsletter: the context and why it matters now, then the link; starting an email newsletter covers tools and consent

For Instagram in particular, the profile bio link, Stories and Highlights all need to point somewhere useful; turning Instagram followers into website enquiries covers that.

Link back with UTM tags

Without tags, many visits from WhatsApp, email and some apps show up in GA4 as direct traffic, so you cannot tell what worked. Tag every link you share with a source, a medium and a campaign:

https://example.com/blog/kitchen-cabinet-materials/?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=kitchen-materials
  • Source: where the link was placed, such as instagram, linkedin, whatsapp, gbp or newsletter
  • Medium: a value GA4 recognises, such as social for social platforms and email for newsletters
  • Campaign: the article or theme, so every piece made from one article is grouped together

Keep everything lowercase, decide on one spelling per source, and record each link in a shared sheet. Never tag links between pages of your own website. UTM tags explained covers naming conventions, WhatsApp and QR codes in detail.

Make the article ready for visitors from social media

Social visitors arrive on a phone, often from inside an app's built-in browser, and they leave quickly if the page disappoints:

  • Fast on mobile data, with no heavy pop-up on arrival
  • A summary at the top that delivers what the post promised
  • A clear next step, such as a WhatsApp button, an enquiry form or a link to the related service
  • A proper share image and title, so the link preview looks right in WhatsApp and LinkedIn; this site creates a share image for every article, as described in how this site is built

If a post promotes an offer rather than an article, such as a festival discount or a free consultation, send it to a campaign page built for that offer, with tracking, rather than to a blog post. That is what landing page design is for.

A weekly workflow for one article

DayWhat to do
MondayPublish or pick the article, list its parts, and create the tagged links
TuesdayDesign the carousel and graphics, and write captions for each platform
WednesdayRecord and edit the short video
ThursdayPost on LinkedIn and Google Business Profile, and add the new FAQ to the service page
FridaySend the WhatsApp broadcast and schedule the newsletter section
The following MondayCheck GA4 for visits and key events by source, and note what worked

Scheduling tools, including the free ones built into some platforms, let you prepare a week's posts in one sitting. If one article a week is too much, do one every fortnight; consistency matters more than volume. Every few months, reshare your best evergreen articles with fresh visuals, and update the article itself if anything has changed.

Measure what comes back

  • In GA4, look at traffic acquisition by session source and medium, and by campaign, to see visits from each platform
  • Key events by source show which platforms bring enquiries, WhatsApp clicks and calls, not just visits
  • Platform insights, such as saves, shares and profile visits, show interest, but count them as secondary to website actions
  • Drop what does not work: if a format never brings anyone to the website after a fair trial, spend that time elsewhere

Social shares are not a direct Google ranking factor, but people who discover your article on social media may later search for your name, link to the article or come back when they are ready to buy.

FAQs

How many social media posts can one article make?

A detailed article with steps, a comparison and a FAQ can often make five to eight different pieces. A short post may only make one or two, which is a sign it could be expanded.

Should I post the whole article on LinkedIn or another platform?

Usually a summary with a link works better, because it brings readers to your site where they can enquire. If you do republish in full, wait until the original is indexed and link back to it clearly.

Is reusing my article text on social media duplicate content?

No. Duplicate content concerns are about whole pages repeated across websites. Social posts that summarise or quote your article and link to it are normal and expected.

Do I need UTM tags on every link I share?

On every link you place outside your website, yes, if you want to know what works. Never use them on links between your own pages.

How often can I reshare the same article?

Evergreen articles can be reshared every few months with new visuals or a different angle, especially when the topic becomes seasonal again or you have updated the article.
