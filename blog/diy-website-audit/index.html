How to Audit Your Own Website in 30 to 60 Minutes: A Step-by-Step Self-Audit With Free Tools and a Scorecard (No Technical Skills Needed)

Audit your own website in under an hour with free tools: mobile, speed, forms, SEO basics, security, content, trust and analytics, plus a simple scorecard.

Key takeaways
  • With a phone, a laptop and free tools you can check first impressions, mobile use, speed, contact paths, Google visibility, security, content, trust and analytics in under an hour.
  • Score each area from 0 to 2 as you go; anything that stops enquiries, such as a broken form or a wrong phone number, gets fixed first, whatever the total.
  • A few low scores usually mean targeted fixes or a refresh; low scores across mobile, speed, trust and Google visibility usually point to a redesign.

You can audit your own website in 30 to 60 minutes with a phone, a laptop and free tools. Check nine things: the first impression, how the site works on a phone, speed, whether forms, calls and WhatsApp actually reach you, how you appear in Google, basic security, whether the content is accurate, the trust signals visitors look for, and whether analytics records enquiries. Score each area as you go, fix anything that stops enquiries first, and use the total to decide whether you need a few fixes, a refresh or a redesign.

A professional website redesign starts with a more detailed version of this: an audit of which pages bring visitors, rankings and enquiries today, so nothing that works is lost. If your score shows only a few weak areas, fixes or a refresh may be enough, and if the problem is Google rather than the design, the technical audit in WordPress SEO services covers crawling, indexing, speed, schema and internal links.

This self-audit doesn't need technical skills. For a deeper technical check, use the WordPress technical SEO audit checklist afterwards.

Before you start: what you need

  • Your own phone, and if possible a second phone from a friend or family member.
  • A laptop with a private or incognito browser window, so you see the site as a stranger does.
  • Your three most important services or products, and the searches you'd expect customers to use.
  • Access to Google Search Console and Google Analytics, if you have them. The audit still works without.

Step 1: First impression (5 minutes)

  • Open the homepage in a private window. Can a stranger tell what you do, and where, within a few seconds?
  • Is there one obvious next step, such as call, WhatsApp or get a quote, without scrolling?
  • Does it look current, with real photos rather than dated stock images?

Step 2: Mobile (10 minutes)

  • Use your own phone, not a desktop browser squeezed narrow.
  • Is text readable without zooming, and are buttons easy to tap with a thumb?
  • Does anything scroll sideways, overlap or get cut off?
  • Does the menu open and close properly, and does every link in it work?
  • Can you call or WhatsApp in one tap from any page?
  • Do popups or cookie banners cover the content and refuse to close?

Repeat the quick checks on a second phone if you can; problems often appear on only one screen size.

Step 3: Speed (5 minutes)

Run your homepage and one main service page through PageSpeed Insights, on the mobile tab. The top section shows field data from real visitors, if the site has enough traffic; the lower section is a lab test with suggestions. Note whether the page passes the Core Web Vitals assessment, and the first few suggestions. Don't chase a perfect score: focus on how the page feels on a real phone and on what real visitors experience. Speed test tools explained shows how to read the results.

Step 4: Contact paths (10 minutes)

  • Submit your own contact form from your phone. Did a thank-you message appear, and did the email arrive, and not in spam?
  • Tap the WhatsApp button: does it open a chat with the right number and a sensible message?
  • Tap the phone number: does it dial the right line, and does someone answer during your listed hours?
  • Are the phone number, email and address the same on every page, in the footer and on your Google profile?

Score this area 0 if any of these fails, whatever else is right; a broken form costs enquiries every day it goes unnoticed. If the form is the problem, why your contact form isn't getting enquiries walks through the fixes.

Step 5: Google visibility and SEO basics (10 minutes)

  • Search your business name. Does your website appear first, and does your Business Profile show alongside it?
  • Search your main service plus your city. Where do you appear, and which of your pages shows up?
  • Search site:yourdomain.com to see a rough sample of the pages Google has indexed. Any missing key pages, or junk such as test pages?
  • Read your results: do the titles and descriptions describe each page clearly?
  • In Search Console, if you have it, check the Pages report for important pages that aren't indexed, and the Performance report for the searches you appear for.
  • In WordPress, confirm Settings, Reading doesn't have "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" ticked.

For each key page, the on-page SEO checklist covers titles, headings, content and links in more depth.

Step 6: Security and upkeep (5 minutes)

  • Does every page load on https, with no browser warning?
  • In WordPress, does Dashboard, Updates show a long list of pending updates? Does Tools, Site Health flag critical issues?
  • Under Users, filtered by Administrator, do you recognise every account?
  • Do you know when the last backup was taken, and is a copy stored away from your hosting?
  • Has your host sent warnings about PHP versions, malware or resource limits?

If several of these worry you, the WordPress security checklist lists what to do first.

Step 7: Content accuracy (5 minutes)

  • Are your services, prices, hours, team and offers all current?
  • Does each main service have its own page, or are they squeezed into one list?
  • Any old offers, past event dates or an outdated copyright year in the footer?
  • Click a few internal links at random: any "page not found" errors?

Step 8: Trust (5 minutes)

  • Are there real photos of you, your team, your premises or your work?
  • Are there genuine reviews, testimonials or case studies, used with permission?
  • Does the About page say who is behind the business and why customers can trust you?
  • Are registration details, certifications or memberships shown, where they apply to your field?
  • Is there a privacy policy, and does it match what the site actually collects?

The website trust signals checklist goes through each signal in more detail.

Step 9: Analytics (5 minutes)

  • Is Google Analytics recording visits today? Check the realtime report while you browse the site.
  • Are form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and phone taps tracked as key events, or only page views?
  • Is Search Console connected, and are both accounts owned by your business rather than a former developer or agency?

What your score means

Total (out of 18)What it usually meansLikely next step
15 to 18The basics are in placeSmall fixes, then content and SEO improvements
9 to 14Specific weak areasTargeted fixes or a refresh of content, photos, buttons and speed
Below 9, or 0 in mobile, contact or securityProblems that cost enquiries nowUrgent fixes first, then consider a redesign

This is a rough guide rather than a precise measure. The pattern matters more than the total: low scores in mobile, speed, trust and Google visibility together usually mean the site's foundations need rebuilding, while one or two low scores can often be fixed on their own. Fix in this order

  1. Anything that stops enquiries: broken forms, wrong phone numbers, dead WhatsApp buttons. Fix today.
  2. Security: pending updates, unknown administrators, missing backups.
  3. Mobile problems that make the site hard to use on a phone.
  4. Speed on your key pages.
  5. Missing service pages and proof, such as photos, reviews and case studies.
  6. SEO and content improvements: titles, descriptions, internal links and new content.

FAQs

How often should I audit my own website?

Every few months is a sensible rhythm, and always after a redesign, a hosting move, a big plugin update or a change of developer. Test the contact form more often, ideally every month.

Can I audit my website without Search Console?

Yes. Google searches, PageSpeed Insights and your own phone cover most of the checks. Search Console adds the indexing and search data, so set it up when you can; it's free.

What PageSpeed score should I aim for?

Focus less on the headline score and more on whether real visitors get a fast page, which is what the Core Web Vitals assessment in the field data shows. A page that feels quick on a mid-range phone matters more than a perfect lab number.

Are free online SEO audit reports worth using?

They can point you to issues, but they often list dozens of minor items with alarming labels. Treat them as a starting list, and be wary of cold emails offering "audits" that are really sales pitches.

Do low scores mean I need a new website?

Not necessarily. Broken forms, slow images and outdated content can be fixed on the current site. A redesign makes sense when the problems are structural: the site isn't built for phones, can't be updated safely or no longer fits your business.

Scored low across the board, or not sure what to fix first? A website redesign starts with a full audit of what already works, so the rankings and enquiries you have are protected. If the design is fine but Google isn't finding you, see WordPress SEO services.
