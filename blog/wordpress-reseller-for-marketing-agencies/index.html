Reselling WordPress Development and Maintenance: A Guide for SEO and Marketing Agencies

How SEO and marketing agencies can resell WordPress builds and maintenance through a partner: packaging, scoping, client contact, reporting and healthy margins.

Key takeaways
  • SEO and marketing agencies can resell WordPress builds, landing pages, SEO fixes and maintenance through a white-label partner, with the client dealing only with the agency.
  • Get the partner's written scope before quoting the client, and make the client contract mirror it, so you never promise more than the partner has agreed to deliver.
  • Margins usually slip on unscoped extras, open-ended revisions, content delays and rush jobs, so agree one change process and use it for every new request.

Clients who pay an SEO or marketing agency every month soon ask for website work too: a faster site, landing pages for a campaign, technical fixes from an audit or a full redesign. Without developers in-house, you can send them elsewhere and risk losing part of the relationship, or you can resell WordPress development and maintenance through a partner who works behind your brand. This guide covers how to package, scope and manage that work so it strengthens client relationships and stays profitable. For the white-label model itself, see white-label WordPress development for agencies.

Why website work fits alongside SEO and ads

  • Your recommendations get done: audit findings such as slow pages, broken redirects or missing schema stop sitting in the client's to-do list
  • Campaigns launch on time: landing pages are ready when the ads are, built to your brief
  • Tracking stays reliable: forms, thank-you pages and conversion events are set up and checked with your reporting in mind
  • One point of contact: the client deals with one agency for marketing and the website, which many prefer
  • Fewer surprises: no outside developer changing the site in ways that affect rankings or tracking without telling you

Packaging what you sell

Clients buy more easily from a short menu than from an open offer of "website services". Most marketing agencies start with a few packages that connect to work they already do:

PackageWhat it coversGood fit for
Campaign landing pagesPages built from your brief or design, with forms, tracking and speed checksClients running Google or Meta ads
SEO implementationTechnical fixes from your audits: speed, redirects, schema, headings and indexing issuesSEO clients whose sites need developer help
Builds and redesignsNew WordPress sites or redesigns, quoted per projectClients whose current site holds back their marketing
Maintenance and careUpdates, backups, security, uptime monitoring and small edits each monthMost clients with a WordPress site

Maintenance is often the easiest to add, because it's recurring and protects the work you're already doing; white-label care plans covers what to include and how to structure tiers. Name packages in client language, but define each one's deliverables in writing.

Scoping before you quote

The quickest way to lose money on resold work is to quote the client first and ask the partner second. Reverse the order:

  1. Gather the brief: pages, features, content, integrations, hosting and deadline
  2. Send it to your partner and get a written scope with exclusions, revision rounds and a timeline
  3. Ask about anything likely to grow, such as content entry, migrations or plugin licences
  4. Build your proposal on that scope, adding your own time for project management, SEO and account work
  5. Make sure the client contract mirrors the partner's scope, so the client isn't promised more than the partner has agreed to deliver

For redesigns, list redirects and SEO checks in the scope explicitly. A marketing agency is the last business that should let a relaunch cost a client its rankings.

Who talks to the client

In most reseller arrangements, the client deals only with your agency. In practice:

  • Your account manager is the client's single contact for website work as well as marketing
  • The partner sends questions and updates to your team, and you pass on what the client needs to see
  • Client feedback is gathered into one consolidated list per review round before it reaches the partner
  • If a technical call would help, the partner joins as part of your team, agreed beforehand
  • An NDA with a non-solicitation clause confirms the partner won't contact or take work from your clients

Agree response times with the partner first, and never promise the client anything faster.

Reporting website work with your marketing results

You probably already send a monthly marketing report. Add a short website section rather than a separate document:

  • Tasks completed, linked to the goals they support, such as "landing page for the festive campaign" or "redirect errors from the audit fixed"
  • Updates, backups, uptime and security status for clients on maintenance
  • Speed checks on key pages, where relevant
  • What's planned next, and anything waiting on the client

The partner can draft the website section each month; your team reviews it, adds context and sends it under your brand. Keep it as honest as the rest of the report, in the spirit of monthly reports without vanity metrics.

Keeping margins healthy with clear scope

Resold website work rarely loses money on the main job. It loses money on everything around it:

  • Unscoped extras: "can you also add…" requests absorbed instead of quoted
  • Open-ended revisions: no limit on review rounds in either contract
  • Content delays: a project stalled waiting for copy, with your team and the partner on hold
  • Rush jobs: deadlines agreed with the client before checking the partner's availability
  • Vague small edits: a maintenance allowance with no definition of what counts

Most of these are solved by agreeing a change process once and using it every time: new requests are logged, the partner estimates them, the client approves, and only then does work start. Explaining the difference between a bug and a change request to clients early makes those conversations easier. Every few months, review each client's website work and adjust packages that regularly take longer than planned.

Getting started

Begin with clients who already need website help, such as an SEO client with a slow site or an ads client waiting on landing pages. Run one or two small projects with your partner, refine your scope template and report format, then add website packages to your proposals and service pages.
