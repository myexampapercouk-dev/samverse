WooCommerce Order Management: Statuses, COD Confirmation, Dispatch, Returns and Staff Roles for Small Teams

A WooCommerce order workflow for small Indian teams: statuses explained, payment to dispatch, COD confirmation, labels, tracking, returns and staff roles.

Key takeaways
  • Agree what every order status means and who acts on it; add custom statuses such as Confirmed, Packed and Shipped only if each has a clear owner and next step.
  • Run one daily flow from payment check to dispatch: confirm COD orders, print slips in batches, create labels, add tracking and move orders to Shipped.
  • Give each person their own login with the narrowest role that works, check plugin compatibility before relying on HPOS, and log return and RTO reasons weekly.

A simple WooCommerce order workflow for a small team looks like this: check new orders and payments each morning, confirm Cash on Delivery orders by WhatsApp or phone, print packing slips and invoices in one batch, pick and pack against the slip, create shipping labels and book pickups, add tracking and move each order to Shipped so the customer is told automatically, then handle cancellations, returns and RTO parcels in the afternoon. Make it work by agreeing what each order status means, giving every person their own login with the right role, and reviewing return and RTO reasons every week.

With a few orders a week, the owner can keep everything in their head. Once orders arrive daily and two or three people share the work, things slip: a COD order ships without being confirmed, a parcel goes out with no tracking, a refund is forgotten. Setting up the statuses, emails and staff access for this is part of my WooCommerce development work, and this guide is the workflow written out so you can run it yourself.

The order statuses, in plain words

Everyone on the team should read the statuses the same way. These are WooCommerce's built-in ones:

Pending paymentOrder created, payment not received, such as an abandoned UPI or card paymentDon't ship. The Hold stock setting can cancel unpaid orders after a set time.

On holdAwaiting a payment you must confirm, such as a bank transferCheck the bank account, then move to Processing

Depending on your checkout, you may also see draft orders created while a customer is still checking out; these are normal and are not orders to fulfil. If a customer says they paid but the order is stuck at Pending payment, or missing altogether, see how to fix WooCommerce orders that aren't showing.

Custom statuses: only if they help

Many Indian stores find Processing too broad. A plugin or a little custom code can add statuses such as:

  • Confirmed: a COD order verified by WhatsApp or phone
  • Packed: ready for courier pickup
  • Shipped: handed over, with tracking added
  • RTO: parcel returning undelivered

Keep the list short; every status needs a clear owner and a next step, or it becomes a place where orders are forgotten. A Shipped status also fixes the common problem of the Completed email arriving days before the parcel; see customising WooCommerce order emails. After adding custom statuses, check that WooCommerce Analytics still counts those orders in sales reports.

The flow from payment to dispatch

  1. Check payments. Filter by Processing (and On hold for bank transfers), oldest first. Compare anything unusual with the gateway dashboard.
  2. Confirm COD orders. Send a WhatsApp or call to confirm the order and address, and hold any that don't respond. Move confirmed ones to Confirmed if you use that status.
  3. Print in a batch. Packing slips and GST invoices for every order ready to pack; invoice and packing-slip plugins can do this from the orders screen.
  4. Pick and check. Match each item against the slip, including size, colour and SKU.
  5. Pack and weigh. Note weight and dimensions for the courier. For high-value parcels, a short video while packing helps with disputes.
  6. Create labels and book pickup through your courier or shipping aggregator, ideally from inside WooCommerce if your aggregator's plugin supports it.
  7. Add tracking and mark Shipped, which notifies the customer.
  8. Mark Completed once delivered, manually or automatically if your shipping integration updates delivery status.

Set a daily dispatch cut-off, publish it on your shipping page and keep to it. For shipping zones, courier rates and aggregator plugins, read the WooCommerce shipping guide for India.

COD confirmation in practice

Confirming COD orders before packing is the single most effective habit for reducing refused deliveries. A workable routine:

  • Send a confirmation message soon after the order, with the items, total and address, asking the customer to reply to confirm
  • Call orders that haven't replied by your cut-off
  • Hold unconfirmed orders for an agreed time, then cancel with a polite message
  • Note repeat refusers in a private order note so the team can decide how to handle future orders

Rules such as COD limits by order value or pin code belong in the checkout itself; cash on delivery without losing money covers those.

Labels, tracking and the customer's view

Customers judge dispatch by the messages they receive. Make sure each order has:

  • A tracking number and courier name saved on the order, visible in the customer's account
  • A Shipped email with a working tracking link
  • The invoice attached or available to download

Spot-check a few shipped orders each week by opening the tracking link as a customer would.

Cancellations, returns and RTO

  • Cancelled before dispatch: cancel, confirm stock went back and refund if prepaid.
  • Returns: approve the request, arrange reverse pickup, inspect the item, restock only if it is sellable, then refund from the order screen. See returns and refunds for WooCommerce stores.
  • RTO parcels: mark them separately, contact the customer, then reship or cancel; restock once the parcel is back and checked.
  • Reason log: a simple sheet of why orders were cancelled, returned or refused shows which products, sizes or pin codes cause repeated trouble.

Staff roles and access

  • Shop Manager: WooCommerce's built-in role for staff who manage orders and products without full administrator control.
  • A narrower role for packing staff: a plugin or custom code can create a role that only views and updates orders.
  • One login per person, never a shared admin password. An activity log then shows who changed what; see WordPress activity logs.
  • Remove access promptly when someone leaves.

HPOS: what it means for your workflow

High-Performance Order Storage (HPOS) keeps orders in dedicated database tables rather than the general WordPress posts table, which is designed to make order screens and searches faster as order numbers grow. Recent WooCommerce versions use it by default on new stores, while older stores may still be on the previous storage or running both in sync. Before switching, check that your invoice, shipping, COD and custom-status plugins declare HPOS compatibility; WooCommerce lists incompatible plugins in its Features settings. Test the change on staging, because a plugin that reads orders the old way can quietly miss them.

FAQs

What is the difference between Processing and Completed in WooCommerce?

Processing means the order is paid (or placed as COD) and waiting to be fulfilled. Completed means it has been fulfilled. Many stores add a Shipped status in between so customers are told when the parcel actually leaves.

How do I confirm COD orders in WooCommerce?

Message or call the customer before packing, then move confirmed orders to a Confirmed status or add a note. Hold unconfirmed orders for an agreed time before cancelling.

Can staff process orders without full admin access?

Yes. The Shop Manager role manages orders and products without full administrator control, and a custom role can be limited to orders only.

Should I switch my store to HPOS?

Usually yes over time, but check every order-related plugin for compatibility first and test on staging, because older plugins may not read orders from the new tables.

How do I send WhatsApp order updates from WooCommerce?

Through a WhatsApp Business API provider and a WooCommerce integration, using approved message templates and the customer's opt-in.
