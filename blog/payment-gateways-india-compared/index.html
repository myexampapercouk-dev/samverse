How to Choose a Payment Gateway for Your Indian Website: What to Compare

Payment gateways in India compared on what matters: UPI and card methods, fee structure, settlements, KYC, international cards and WooCommerce support.

Key takeaways
  • Compare payment gateways in India on how your customers actually pay, the full fee structure, settlement timing, KYC fit, international support and plugin quality.
  • Fees change and vary by method and business type, so check each provider's current pricing page and agreement rather than relying on blog comparisons.
  • Test the gateway end to end on your own site, including a real UPI payment, a failed payment and a refund, before switching it on for customers.

To compare payment gateways in India, start with how your customers pay, then look at the methods each gateway supports, the full fee structure, how fast money settles, whether your business type passes their KYC, international card support, and how well their plugin works with WooCommerce or your platform. There is no single best gateway; the right one depends on your business, your customers and your site.

This guide gives you the questions to ask and a checklist to compare providers side by side. It does not rank brands or quote fees, because pricing changes and depends on your business; always check the provider's current terms. Start with how your customers pay

Look at your own customers before reading comparison tables. A D2C brand selling to young buyers on phones will see most payments through UPI. A B2B supplier may need net banking and cards for larger amounts. A coaching institute may want EMI options. A temple or NGO may need recurring donations and receipts. An exporter needs international cards.

Ask your team which methods customers request most, check past orders if you already sell online, and list your must-have methods before contacting any provider. How to accept online payments on a WordPress website in India covers the basics of each method.

Payment methods

Most established Indian gateways support UPI, debit and credit cards, net banking and some wallets. Differences show up in the details:

  • UPI experience: whether mobile users get an intent flow that opens their UPI app directly, a QR code on desktop, and how failed UPI payments are handled.
  • EMI and pay later: availability, which banks are covered and who pays the interest.
  • Recurring payments: support for subscriptions or UPI mandates, if you sell memberships or regular deliveries.
  • Payment links and QR: useful for orders taken on WhatsApp or phone.

Cash on delivery is not a gateway feature, but most stores still offer it alongside. Cash on delivery in WooCommerce covers limits and fees.

Fees and pricing structure

Do not compare only the headline rate. Ask each provider for a written breakdown covering:

  • Rates per method, since UPI, domestic cards, credit cards, international cards and wallets are often priced differently.
  • GST on the fees, which is charged on top.
  • Setup, annual maintenance or minimum monthly charges, if any.
  • Charges for instant settlement, refunds, chargebacks or payment links.
  • Whether the rate can be negotiated as your volume grows.

Estimate a typical month using your expected mix of methods, rather than assuming every payment costs the same. RBI rules and provider pricing change, so check the current position with the provider and your CA.

Settlements and reconciliation

Settlement is when money reaches your bank account. Ask how many working days after payment, whether weekends and bank holidays delay it, and whether faster settlement is available and at what cost. For a small business, a few days' difference can matter.

Reconciliation is matching payments to orders. Check whether the dashboard exports reports that show order ID, fees, GST and net amount for each payment, and whether these can be matched easily in your accounting software.

KYC and onboarding

Every gateway needs KYC documents, typically business registration proof, PAN, bank account details, address proof and website details. Some also check your website for terms, privacy, refund and contact pages before approving. Certain business categories may face extra review or may not be accepted. Ask early whether your category is supported, and keep your website's policy pages ready to avoid delays.

International cards and overseas customers

If you sell to customers outside India, check whether international cards are enabled by default or need separate approval, which currencies are supported, how conversion is shown to the customer and what extra fees apply. Exporters should also ask about documentation needed for foreign payments. Selling internationally with WooCommerce covers the store side.

Selling on Shopify instead? At the time of writing Shopify Payments is not offered in India, so these providers connect as third-party gateways; the guide to setting up a Shopify store in India covers payments, COD and GST invoices step by step.

WooCommerce and WordPress support

A gateway is only as good as its integration with your site. For WooCommerce, check:

  • Is there an official plugin, updated recently and tested with the current WooCommerce version?
  • Does it support the block checkout as well as the classic checkout?
  • Does it handle webhooks, so orders update even if the customer closes the browser after paying?
  • Can refunds be issued from the WooCommerce admin?
  • Does it support subscriptions if you need them? See WooCommerce subscriptions.

Shopify stores have a different set of options; Shopify payment gateways in India covers them. For donations, online donations for temples and NGOs covers receipts and recurring giving.

Refunds, failed payments and disputes

How a gateway handles things going wrong matters as much as how it handles successful payments. Ask how refunds work for each method, how long they take to reach the customer and whether they can be issued in part. For UPI, ask what happens when money is debited but the payment shows as failed, and how quickly such amounts are reversed or reconciled to an order.

Chargebacks and disputes are less common for small Indian stores than for international sellers, but they do happen, especially with cards. Ask how disputes are notified, what evidence you need to submit and how much time you get to respond. Keep delivery proof and order records for each order so you can answer quickly. Clear refund and cancellation policies on your website, written in plain language, also reduce disputes because customers know what to expect before they pay.

Selling on Shopify rather than WooCommerce? Indian gateways such as Razorpay, PayU and Cashfree connect there too, and you can get help setting up payments on a Shopify store.

A UPI QR code or a full gateway?

Very small businesses sometimes start with a static UPI QR code or a payment link instead of a full gateway. That works for occasional orders taken over phone or WhatsApp, but it does not connect payments to website orders automatically, so someone has to check each payment by hand. Once you take regular orders on the website, a gateway integrated with your checkout saves time and avoids mistakes, because the order status updates as soon as payment succeeds.

Test before you go live

Once a gateway is approved, test it on your own site before telling customers:

  1. Place test orders in the gateway's test mode with each method.
  2. Switch to live keys and place a small real UPI payment and a card payment.
  3. Try a failed payment and check the order status and customer message.
  4. Issue a refund and check it in the gateway and WooCommerce.
  5. Check order emails and that the settlement report shows the payment.

A quick comparison checklist

  • Supports every payment method on your must-have list.
  • Written fee breakdown received, with GST and extra charges.
  • Settlement timing fits your cash flow.
  • Your business category is accepted and documents are ready.
  • International cards available if you need them.
  • Maintained plugin for your platform and checkout type.
  • Support channel you can reach when payments fail.

FAQs

Which payment gateway is best in India?

It depends on your customers' payment methods, your business category, settlement needs, international sales and platform. Compare providers on those factors and test the integration on your site.

How do payment gateways in India compare on fees?

Fees vary by payment method, business type and volume, and GST is added. Ask each provider for a written breakdown and estimate a typical month based on your own mix of payments.

Can I use more than one payment gateway?

Yes. Some stores use one gateway for domestic payments and another for international cards, or keep a second as a backup. Each adds setup and testing work.

Do I need a registered business to use a payment gateway?

Requirements vary. Some providers accept individuals or sole proprietors with PAN and bank details, while others need business registration. Check the provider's current KYC list.

How long does gateway approval take?

It depends on the provider, your business category and how complete your documents and website policy pages are. Having everything ready speeds it up.
