Websites for Wholesalers and Distributors: Trade Catalogues, Price on Request, Dealer Logins, Bulk RFQs and B2B Ordering

Plan a wholesaler or distributor website: a trade catalogue, price on request or dealer login, bulk RFQs, dealer registration and WooCommerce B2B ordering.

Key takeaways
  • Organise the catalogue by category and brand with SKUs, pack sizes and case quantities, and claim authorised distributor status or use brand logos only where you genuinely can.
  • Decide how prices are shown: price on request for most trade catalogues, or a dealer login with role-based prices, so retailers see their rates and the public does not.
  • Give bulk buyers an enquiry basket or quick RFQ with WhatsApp, keep dealer registration on its own page, and add WooCommerce B2B ordering once repeat customers want to reorder online.

A wholesaler or distributor website has three jobs: show retailers and businesses exactly what you supply, give approved dealers a quick way to get prices and place repeat orders, and convince brands that you are a distributor worth appointing. That usually means a trade catalogue organised by category and brand, prices on request or behind a dealer login, an enquiry basket or bulk RFQ form that works as well as WhatsApp, a separate dealer registration page, and online B2B ordering once your regular customers are ready for it.

Distributors sell to businesses, so the website works like a manufacturer's: catalogue first, enquiries second. That last row matters: many distributors get calls from consumers wanting one item. A short "Where to buy" note or retailer list on the site saves your team those calls.

A catalogue built for trade buyers

Trade buyers know what they want and search for it precisely, often by brand, part number or pack size. Build the catalogue around that:

  • Categories first, then brands: for example Electrical, then Wires and Cables, then each brand's range, with brand pages as a second route into the same products
  • Trade details on every product: SKU or part number, pack size, case or carton quantity, minimum order, key specifications and an image
  • Search that understands SKUs and partial part numbers, and filters by brand, size and type once categories grow; see improving site search on WordPress and WooCommerce
  • Spreadsheet import, so a range of thousands of SKUs can be loaded and updated in one go rather than typed by hand
  • Stock wording you can keep true: "usually in stock" or "made to order" is safer than a live count that is wrong by afternoon

Only call yourself an "authorised distributor" for a brand if you hold that status, and use brand logos and official product images only with permission. How to build a product catalogue website covers category trees, spec tables and WooCommerce in catalogue mode in detail.

Bulk enquiries and RFQs

Many B2B buyers would rather send a photo of a handwritten list on WhatsApp than fill in a form, and your website should welcome both:

  • An enquiry basket: buyers add several products with quantities and send one RFQ, instead of one enquiry per item
  • A quick bulk form: business name, city or area, products and quantities, and an upload for a requirement list or photo
  • An optional GSTIN field, so quotes and invoices can be prepared correctly
  • A WhatsApp button on every product and category page, with the product name pre-filled
  • Routing by territory or product line, so each enquiry reaches the right salesperson

Vansh Group, an electronics manufacturer whose website I built, uses a service dropdown on its enquiry form so every enquiry arrives tagged and goes to the right person; a product-line dropdown does the same for a distributor. RFQ forms for B2B websites covers what to ask and how to follow up.

Dealer and retailer registration

Recruiting new retailers is often a distributor's main growth route, so give it its own page rather than a line on the contact page:

  • Who you supply and what a dealer gets: range, delivery schedule, credit terms if you offer them, marketing support
  • Eligibility, such as a registered shop or business and GST registration, and the territories you cover
  • A registration form asking for business name, owner, city, type of business, brands currently stocked and an approximate monthly requirement
  • What happens next, and which documents you will ask for later, rather than collecting them on a public form

Keep this separate from customer enquiries. Studio Agama Interiors, a studio I built a website for, has a "Become a Design Partner" page with its own form, so partner applications never get mixed up with customer quote requests. If brands approach you about distribution, a short "For brands" page explaining your reach, warehouse and sales team, with its own enquiry form, does the same job.

Online B2B ordering with WooCommerce

Once regular customers reorder the same products every week, an ordering portal saves both sides time. WooCommerce can work as a B2B store for approved accounts:

  • Role-based or customer-specific prices, visible only after login
  • Minimum order quantities and pack multiples, so cases are not split
  • A quick order form listing products in a table, and one-click reordering from past orders
  • Payment online, or on credit or invoice for approved accounts
  • GST invoices with the buyer's GSTIN; see WooCommerce GST setup for Indian stores
  • A mobile-friendly layout, because many shop owners order from their phones between customers

Start with your most regular customers and a limited range, and keep WhatsApp ordering open alongside it; few retailers switch overnight.

Proof and trust for B2B buyers

  • Warehouse and fleet photos, the areas you deliver to and your delivery schedule
  • The brands you distribute, with your status for each stated accurately
  • Your registered address, GST details and the people who run the business
  • Testimonials from retailers and business customers, used with permission
  • A company profile PDF buyers and brands can forward internally, as Vansh Group offers alongside its product catalogues

Service areas and local search

Buyers search for "{product} wholesaler in {city}", "{brand} distributor in Maharashtra" or "electrical wholesaler near me". To appear:

  • A Google Business Profile for your warehouse or trade counter, with accurate hours
  • A service area page listing the districts or cities you deliver to, and city pages only where you genuinely supply, each with real local detail
  • Category and brand pages with a short introduction, so they are more than product grids
  • Listings on B2B marketplaces and directories that link back to your own catalogue

Suppliers of building materials have their own mix of contractors and homeowners; see websites for hardware and building material suppliers.

Common mistakes on distributor websites

  • A list of brand logos and nothing else, with no products or pack sizes
  • "Authorised distributor" claims or logos used without permission
  • Trade prices visible to everyone, or an old price list PDF still online
  • One contact form for retailers, brands, job seekers and consumers
  • No mention of the areas you deliver to
  • A dealer portal launched before anyone has agreed to use it

FAQs

Should a wholesaler show prices on the website?

Usually not publicly, because trade prices depend on volume, customer and terms. Price on request with fast replies, or a dealer login showing each account its own prices, works better for most distributors.

Can retailers register as dealers online?

Yes. A registration form can collect business details and send them for approval, and approved accounts can then be given access to trade prices or online ordering.

Can WooCommerce handle B2B orders?

Yes, with role-based pricing, minimum quantities, quick order forms, credit terms for approved accounts and GST invoices. It is usually scoped separately from a standard store because the rules are specific to each distributor.

How do I add thousands of products?

Prepare them in a spreadsheet with SKUs, categories, pack sizes and specifications, and import them in one go. Updating the spreadsheet and re-importing is the easiest way to keep a large range current.

Should we list brands we distribute?

Yes, accurately. Say which brands you are authorised for and which you simply stock, and use logos and official images only with the brand's permission.
