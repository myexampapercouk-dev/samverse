Websites for Export Businesses: Specifications and HS Codes, MOQ and Incoterms Explained, Registrations and Certificates, Multilingual Pages and Enquiries From Abroad

Build an export website international buyers trust: full specs, MOQ and Incoterms explained, real certificates, multilingual pages and clear enquiries.

Key takeaways
  • An overseas buyer cannot visit before the first order, so product pages need full specifications, packing details, MOQ and lead times stated accurately.
  • Explain trade terms such as MOQ and Incoterms in plain words, and show only the registrations and certificates you actually hold, with dates.
  • Translated pages with hreflang, fast hosting for overseas visitors and an enquiry form built around trade questions turn international searches into usable leads.

An export business website does a job a sales visit would do at home: it convinces a buyer thousands of kilometres away that you make what they need, to their standard, in the quantity they want, and that you understand how international orders work. Buyers judge that from product detail, trade terms, documents and how quickly you reply, long before they ask for samples.

Most exporters are manufacturers or merchant exporters selling to businesses, so the site is built like my websites for manufacturers and B2B companies: a structured catalogue, quote forms, certifications and brochure downloads. Being found abroad depends on technical groundwork, which my WordPress SEO service covers, from structure and schema to Search Console.

The buyer's first-visit checklist

Importers and sourcing agents tend to run through the same questions, often comparing several suppliers in one sitting:

  1. Does this company make the exact product, or a close variant, with the specification I need?
  2. Is it the manufacturer, or a trader sourcing from others?
  3. Can it supply my quantity, and what is the minimum?
  4. Does it hold the certificates my market requires?
  5. Has it shipped to my country or region before?
  6. How does it quote: which trade terms, which currency, which ports?
  7. Who will I be dealing with, and will they reply in my working hours?

Every section below answers one or more of these. A site that answers all seven gets shortlisted more often than one with better photos and fewer facts.

Product pages written for a specification sheet

Buyers abroad often forward your page to a technical colleague, so the product page has to stand on its own. Include:

  • Product name in the terms the target market uses, plus your internal code
  • Specifications in a table: dimensions, material, grade, composition, tolerances, finishes, in metric units and, for markets that expect them, imperial too
  • Variants, such as sizes, colours or grades, on the same page where they differ only in one attribute
  • Packing: units per carton, carton size and weight, cartons per pallet, and how many fit a standard container if you know
  • MOQ and typical lead time, stated honestly
  • Whether samples are available, and on what terms
  • A downloadable datasheet with your contact details inside

HS codes: useful, with a caution

Listing an HS code on each product can help buyers estimate duties and check import rules. The first six digits of the Harmonized System are shared internationally, but countries extend them with their own digits, and classification can be disputed. If you publish codes, state that they are the codes you export under from India, and suggest buyers confirm the classification with their customs broker. Ask your own customs agent to check the codes before they go on the site.

MOQ, lead times and samples

A buyer who cannot find your minimum order quantity may assume it is too high and move on. State it per product or per product family, and explain whether it can be mixed across sizes or colours. Give lead times as a range from order confirmation, and say what extends them, such as custom packaging or peak season. If you supply samples, say whether they are charged, whether the cost is credited against the first order, and how they are sent.

Incoterms, explained for your products

Incoterms are rules published by the International Chamber of Commerce that set out who pays for and bears the risk of each stage of shipping. At the time of writing the current edition is Incoterms 2020; check whether a newer one applies. A short page explaining the terms you quote under saves many emails:

TermIn plain wordsWhen exporters often offer it
EXW (Ex Works)The buyer collects from your premises and handles almost everything elseBuyers with their own freight forwarder in India
FOB (Free On Board)You deliver the goods on board the vessel at the named Indian port; the buyer takes over from thereSea freight where the buyer arranges the main shipment
CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight)You pay freight and minimum insurance to the destination port, but risk passes to the buyer once goods are on boardBuyers who prefer a price to their own port
DAP or DDPYou deliver to the buyer's named place; under DDP you also handle import dutiesSmaller buyers who want a delivered price, if you can manage destination logistics

Name the ports or airports you ship from, and say which terms you normally quote. Have your freight forwarder or export adviser review the page, because the details of each term matter in disputes. Registrations and certificates: only what you hold

Buyers check documents, and some verify them. Show:

  • Your Importer Exporter Code, GST registration and membership of export promotion councils, only where you hold them and are comfortable publishing the details
  • Management system certificates, such as ISO standards, with the certifying body, scope and validity date
  • Product certificates for specific markets, such as conformity marks or test reports, against the products they cover
  • Any organic, food safety, sustainability or social compliance certifications, with their current dates

Remove anything that has lapsed, and never show a market mark for a product that has not been certified for it. Requirements set by DGFT and other authorities change, so check the current rules with your export adviser before stating any registration status. A PDF copy of each certificate that buyers can download makes verification easier.

Company profile and capability

Many importers want to know whether you are the manufacturer. Show the factory, production lines, quality control lab, warehouse and packing area with real photos and a short description of capacity. Give the countries and regions you have exported to, if your buyers do not mind being referred to generally. A downloadable company profile helps buyers circulate you internally; the Vansh Group site I built offers its company profile and product catalogues as PDF downloads alongside the web pages.

Languages, hreflang and international search

English covers many markets, but buyers in the Middle East, Latin America, Europe or East Asia may search in their own language. If a market matters to you:

  • Translate the key pages, such as home, main product families and contact, rather than the whole site at once
  • Put each language on its own URL, with hreflang tags so search engines show the right version
  • Use a translator, or at least a native-speaking reviewer, who knows the technical terms for your products
  • Use local units, date formats and, where relevant, currencies on the translated pages

Hreflang and multilingual SEO covers the setup in detail. For international search generally, choose a domain and folder structure early, host the site or use a CDN so it loads quickly for overseas visitors, and set up Search Console so you can see which countries your impressions come from. No one can promise rankings abroad, but these foundations give the site a fair chance.

Enquiry forms built around trade questions

An export enquiry is more useful when it arrives with the facts sales needs to quote. A good form asks for:

  • Company name, country, website and work email
  • Product and specification, with an option to attach drawings or a buyer's spec sheet
  • Quantity per order and expected frequency
  • Destination port or city, and preferred trade terms
  • Whether samples are wanted first

International forms attract spam, so filter it without making genuine buyers solve puzzles; stopping contact form spam describes the options. Reply quickly, ideally within the buyer's working day, and add a WhatsApp or email route for buyers who prefer it.

Trade shows and buyer meetings

If you exhibit at international fairs, a page listing upcoming shows with dates, hall and stand number lets buyers plan a visit, and an appointment form helps you fill meeting slots in advance. After the event, the same page can show what you exhibited, which gives buyers who met you a reason to return to the site.

Payment terms and paperwork

State in general terms how you are usually paid, such as advance payment, part advance with balance against documents, or letters of credit, and which currencies you invoice in. Do not publish anything you would not accept for every buyer. Detailed terms belong in your quotation, written with your bank and export adviser.

FAQs

Should an exporter publish HS codes on the website?

It can help buyers, if the codes have been checked by your customs agent. Note that countries extend the codes differently and buyers should confirm classification locally.

Which Incoterms should we show?

Only the ones you actually quote, with a short explanation of each and the ports you ship from. Ask your freight forwarder to review the wording.

Do we need the website in other languages?

For markets where buyers search in their own language, translated key pages with hreflang can help. Start with one market and expand if enquiries justify it.

Can we show our IEC number on the site?

Many exporters do. Show it only if you hold it and are comfortable publishing it, and keep registration details up to date.

Should export products have online prices?

Usually not for bulk B2B orders, since prices depend on quantity, terms and destination. A request-for-quote route on each product works better; for small retail orders abroad, see selling internationally with WooCommerce.
