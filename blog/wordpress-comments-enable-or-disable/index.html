WordPress Comments: Should a Business Website Enable Them? When to Keep or Disable Comments, Spam Control and Settings

Should a business website enable WordPress comments? When to keep or disable them, the exact settings, how to turn them off site-wide and stop comment spam.

Key takeaways
  • Most business websites are better with WordPress comments disabled; keep them only where real readers discuss posts and someone moderates daily.
  • Turning off comments needs three steps: Discussion settings for new posts, bulk edit for existing posts, and handling media attachment pages.
  • If you keep comments, require approval, use an anti-spam tool, disable links in comments and set old posts to close automatically.

Most business websites should disable WordPress comments, because they attract far more spam than genuine discussion and unanswered or spammy comments make the business look neglected. Keep comments enabled only when real readers discuss your posts and someone checks them every day; otherwise switch them off in Settings, Discussion and close comments on existing posts with bulk edit.

This guide gives a quick decision test, the exact settings, how to remove comments completely, how to control spam if you keep them, and better ways to collect questions and feedback.

Should you enable comments? A quick decision

SituationRecommendation
Service business, clinic, manufacturer or local shopDisable
Company blog posting a few times a monthDisable, or enable with strict moderation if readers actually reply
Community, publisher or education blog with active readersEnable with moderation
WooCommerce storeDisable on posts; use product reviews instead
No one available to moderate dailyDisable

Ask yourself one question: in the last six months, how many real comments did we get? If the honest answer is a handful, comments are costing more in spam handling and risk than they give back.

The Discussion settings that matter

Go to Settings, Discussion. The key options:

  • Allow people to submit comments on new posts: untick to disable comments on future posts.
  • Attempt to notify linked blogs / allow link notifications (pingbacks and trackbacks): untick both. They are almost entirely spam today.
  • Comment must be manually approved: tick if you keep comments.
  • Comment author must have a previously approved comment: tick, so new commenters always go to moderation.
  • Automatically close comments on posts older than: set to 14 to 30 days if you keep comments; most spam targets old posts.
  • Hold a comment if it contains two or more links: lower to one.
  • Show avatars: untick if not needed; Gravatar images add external requests.

Note that unticking the first option affects only new posts. Existing posts keep their own setting, which is why many people think they switched comments off when they did not.

Turning off comments on existing posts, pages and media

  1. Go to Posts, All Posts, set the screen to show many posts per page, select all, choose Edit from Bulk actions and click Apply.
  2. Set Comments to "Do not allow" and click Update.
  3. Repeat for Pages, since pages can have comments too.
  4. Media attachment pages can also accept comments. Recent WordPress versions turn attachment pages off for new sites; on older sites, redirect attachment pages to the file or parent post with your SEO plugin.
  5. Delete existing spam and pending comments from the Comments screen.

Approved genuine comments can stay; closing comments keeps old comments visible while stopping new ones. If you want to hide them too, delete or unapprove them.

Removing comments site-wide

If you never want comments anywhere, a "disable comments" plugin removes them from all post types, hides the Comments menu, and blocks the comment endpoints spam bots use directly. That last point matters: bots often post straight to wp-comments-post.php without loading your page, so a form hidden by the theme does not stop them.

Developers can do the same with a few lines in a site plugin that remove comment support from post types and close comments with a filter. Either route is fine; avoid installing a plugin that does far more than you need. Also remove leftover comment sections from your theme template or page builder so no empty "Leave a reply" box remains. If the theme still shows it after these steps, a cache may be serving the old page; see WordPress changes not showing.

Plugin or code: which way to disable comments?

With a plugin

A "disable comments" plugin can switch off comments for all post types or selected ones, hide the Comments menu and admin bar item, remove comment widgets and block comment-related requests. It is the easiest choice for site owners, and reversible from the settings screen. Check that it lets you keep WooCommerce product reviews if you want them.

With a few lines of code

A developer can add a short snippet to a site-specific plugin or child theme that closes comments and pings, removes comment support from post types and hides the admin menu. This avoids another plugin, but the code must not go into a parent theme, where updates would erase it. Either way, test a post, a page and a media attachment afterwards to confirm the form is gone.

If you keep comments: spam control and moderation

  • Anti-spam tool: Akismet is the usual choice; licence terms vary for commercial sites, so check them. Alternatives include Antispam Bee and honeypot-based tools.
  • CAPTCHA only as a backup: invisible checks such as Cloudflare Turnstile reduce spam without annoying real readers. Our article on reCAPTCHA not working explains common setup problems.
  • Block links: set the link limit to one or remove the website field from the comment form; most spam exists to place links.
  • Disallowed keywords: add common spam words and domains to the Disallowed Comment Keys box.
  • Moderate daily: reply to real comments within a day or two. An unanswered question looks worse than no comments at all.

If spam also arrives through forms or fake user registrations, deal with those separately; see stop contact form spam and stop spam user registrations.

Do comments help or hurt SEO?

Genuine, relevant comments can add useful words and show a page is alive, and Google can index comment text. But spam comments with outbound links to dubious sites hurt trust and can drag a page into low-quality territory. WordPress adds nofollow and ugc to comment links automatically, which limits the link risk but not the quality problem.

For most small business blogs, the SEO value of comments is tiny compared with writing better posts. Spend the time on content; our guide to writing blog posts that rank is a better use of effort. If you do keep comments and get many, paginate them so very long threads do not bloat the page.

Comments, speed and privacy

The comment section can load extra scripts: the comment reply script, Gravatar images from an external server, and anti-spam scripts. Disabling comments removes these. If you keep them, lazy-load the comment section or load it only when a visitor clicks "Show comments".

Comments also collect personal data (name, email, IP address). Mention this in your privacy policy, explain how long you keep it, and offer the cookie consent checkbox WordPress provides. Our DPDP Act website basics covers data handling for Indian sites in general terms; check current rules with a professional for your case.

Moderation workflow if you run an active blog

If comments are part of your content strategy, treat them as customer service. Decide who checks the queue and how often, set a reply target of a few working days, and agree what gets deleted (spam, abuse, self-promotion) versus approved and answered. Turn repeated questions into updates to the post itself. Send new-comment notifications to a shared inbox rather than one person, so replies don't stop when someone is on leave. Review the queue's spam patterns monthly and add recurring words or domains to the disallowed list.

Comments on membership and course sites

Learning, coaching and membership sites are the main exception to the "disable" advice. Comments on lessons let students ask questions in context, and answers help later students with the same doubt. Restrict commenting to logged-in members, which removes most spam at once, and decide who answers questions and how quickly. If discussions grow, a dedicated forum or community plugin may suit better than post comments, as it allows threads, notifications and search.

A quick checklist after changing comment settings

  • Open a recent post, an old post, a page and a media attachment in a private window and confirm the comment form is gone or working as intended.
  • Check that WooCommerce product reviews still behave the way you want.
  • Clear any page cache so visitors see the change.
  • Empty the spam and trash folders once you are sure nothing genuine is there.

Alternatives that work better for most businesses

  • An FAQ section: answer common questions on the post or service page itself. See FAQ pages for SEO.
  • A WhatsApp or enquiry button: readers with real questions often prefer a private chat and are more likely to become customers.
  • Google reviews: social proof belongs on your Google Business Profile, where future customers look for it.
  • Product reviews: on WooCommerce, keep verified-buyer reviews and disable post comments.
  • Social posts: share articles on LinkedIn or Instagram and let discussion happen there.

Comments on WooCommerce product pages

On WooCommerce, product reviews use the WordPress comment system, so disabling comments site-wide with a heavy-handed plugin can also switch off reviews. If you sell online, disable comments on posts and pages only, and manage reviews from WooCommerce, Settings, Products. Tick "Reviews can only be left by verified owners" to cut fake reviews, and enable star ratings so they can appear in search results through product schema. Reply to negative reviews politely and publicly; it shows future buyers that someone is listening.

Cleaning up years of old comment spam

Sites that left comments open for years can hold tens of thousands of spam and pending comments, which bloat the database and slow the admin. Empty the Spam and Trash folders from the Comments screen, then bulk delete pending comments you will never approve. On very large backlogs, WP-CLI can delete them in one go after a backup. FAQs

How do I disable comments in WordPress?

Untick "Allow people to submit comments on new posts" in Settings, Discussion, then bulk edit existing posts and pages to "Do not allow" comments. For a complete removal, use a disable comments plugin or a small code snippet.

Should a business website have comments enabled?

Usually not. Unless readers genuinely discuss your posts and someone moderates daily, comments mostly bring spam. FAQs, WhatsApp and Google reviews serve business goals better.

Why am I still getting spam comments after disabling them?

Existing posts may still have comments open, or bots are posting directly to the comment endpoint. Close comments on old posts with bulk edit and use a plugin that blocks the endpoint.

Do comments help SEO?

Genuine comments can help a little, but spam comments hurt quality signals. For most business blogs, better content matters far more than comments.

Will disabling comments delete existing comments?

No. Closing comments stops new ones, but approved comments stay visible unless you delete or unapprove them or use a plugin option that hides them.

Should I turn off pingbacks and trackbacks?

Yes. They are almost entirely spam today and add little value. Untick both options in Settings, Discussion.
