WordPress Comments: Should a Business Website Enable Them? When to Keep or Disable Comments, Spam Control and Settings

Should a business website allow WordPress comments? When to keep or disable them, Discussion settings, spam control, SEO effects and how to turn them off.

Key takeaways
  • Most business websites are better with comments off; keep them only if your articles attract genuine questions and someone will moderate and reply within a day or two.
  • The Discussion settings screen only controls new posts, so close comments on existing posts and pages with bulk edit, and turn off pingbacks and trackbacks either way.
  • If you keep comments, hold first comments for approval, add one spam filter and close comments on older posts; if you remove them site-wide, make sure WooCommerce product reviews still work.

For most business websites, WordPress comments are best switched off: brochure and service sites rarely get useful comments, and the spam they attract makes a site look neglected. Keep comments only if you publish articles that genuinely spark questions and someone will moderate and reply within a day or two. Either way, change the defaults: no pingbacks, first comments held for approval, a spam filter, and comments closed automatically on older posts.

Comment spam, plugin updates and database clean-up are the kind of routine jobs that pile up quietly on a business site. My WordPress maintenance plans cover updates, off-site backups, security scans and small content changes each month, which stops that routine work from piling up.

This site, samverse.space, is custom-coded and hosted on Hostinger rather than built on WordPress, and it has no comments at all: readers ask questions through the contact form or WhatsApp instead. That suits a business site; a community blog would make a different choice.

The Discussion settings that matter

Everything starts in Settings > Discussion. Labels change slightly between WordPress versions, but these are the ones to check:

Turning comments off on existing posts, pages and media

The setting for new posts doesn't touch anything already published. Close comments on existing content separately:

  • Existing posts in bulk: go to Posts > All Posts, select all the posts on the page, choose Edit from Bulk actions and click Apply, then set Comments and Pings to "Do not allow" and click Update. Increase the number of items per page in Screen Options to do more at once.
  • A single post: in the block editor's post sidebar, open the Discussion setting. In recent versions it appears in the summary panel as Open or Closed; older versions show an "Allow comments" checkbox.
  • Pages: new pages have had comments off by default for years, but older sites can still have them open. Bulk edit Pages > All Pages the same way.
  • Media: attachment pages are switched off by default on sites installed since WordPress 6.4, but older sites may have a page, and a comment form, for every uploaded image. An SEO plugin setting that redirects attachment pages to the file or parent post removes them.

Closing comments stops new ones but keeps approved comments visible. To hide those too, delete them, or use one of the site-wide methods below.

Removing comments site-wide

If you never want comments, switching them off everywhere is cleaner than closing them post by post. There are two routes.

With a plugin

Several "disable comments" plugins turn comments off everywhere or by post type, hide the Comments menu and stop existing comments displaying. Choose one that is actively maintained, and check its post-type options carefully if you run WooCommerce, because product reviews are comments too.

With a few lines of code

This snippet closes comments and pings on everything except WooCommerce products and hides existing comments elsewhere. Add it to a child theme's functions.php or a small custom plugin, and test it on staging first:

add_filter( 'comments_open', function( $open, $post_id ) {
  return 'product' === get_post_type( $post_id ) ? $open : false;
}, 20, 2 );

add_filter( 'pings_open', '__return_false', 20, 2 );

add_filter( 'comments_array', function( $comments, $post_id ) {
  return 'product' === get_post_type( $post_id ) ? $comments : array();
}, 20, 2 );

Some themes still print a "Comments" heading or count from their template. In a block theme, remove the Comments block from the Single Posts template under Appearance > Editor; in a classic theme, the change belongs in a child theme. If you keep comments: spam control and moderation

  • Use one spam filter. Akismet, from the company behind WordPress.com, checks comments against a shared spam service and, at the time of writing, needs a paid plan for commercial sites. Antispam Bee is free and works without sending comments to an outside service. Honeypot fields and Cloudflare Turnstile are other options. One well-configured tool beats three overlapping ones.
  • Moderate on a schedule. Check the queue every working day, approve genuine comments, reply within a day or two, and empty the spam folder rather than letting it grow.
  • Edit out links from otherwise useful comments if they point somewhere unrelated, and never approve a comment just because it is flattering.
  • Turn off pingbacks everywhere. If nothing on your site needs XML-RPC, it can be restricted too; see WordPress XML-RPC explained.

The same bots that post comment spam fill in contact forms; stopping contact form spam covers honeypots, Turnstile and filters that also suit comment forms.

Do comments help or hurt SEO?

Google treats comments as part of the page. Genuine questions and answers can add useful, relevant content. Spam does the opposite: dozens of "great post" comments with links to unrelated sites make a page look unmaintained, and a site overrun with spam can receive a manual action for user-generated spam in Search Console.

  • Links in comments: WordPress automatically adds rel="nofollow ugc" to links in comments and to commenters' website links, telling search engines they are user-generated and not endorsed by you. Leave that in place.
  • Comment pagination: "Break comments into pages" creates extra comment-page URLs. Leave it off unless your threads are genuinely long.
  • Turning comments off won't hurt a business site's rankings. Pages rank on their content, links and usefulness, not on having a comment box.

Comments, speed and privacy

Comments add a little weight to a page: a reply script on posts with threaded comments, and avatar images fetched from Gravatar for each commenter. Spam that sits in the database also bloats the comments tables over time; WordPress database optimisation covers cleaning it out safely.

WordPress stores each commenter's name, email address and IP address, and the avatar feature sends a hashed version of the email to Gravatar. If you keep comments, say so in your privacy policy; privacy policy and cookie basics for Indian websites explains what to include.

Alternatives that work better for most businesses

  • An FAQ section on each service page or article, updated with questions customers actually ask; see FAQs for SEO
  • A short prompt at the end of each article: "Questions about this? Message us on WhatsApp"
  • A contact form with spam protection, which keeps questions private
  • Sharing articles on LinkedIn or social media, where discussion happens anyway

FAQs

Do comments help SEO?

Only when they add genuine, relevant questions and answers. Spammy or off-topic comments can make a page look neglected, and turning comments off doesn't hurt a business site's rankings.

Will turning off comments delete existing ones?

No. Closing comments stops new ones, but approved comments stay visible until you delete them or hide them with a plugin or code.

How do I disable comments on pages?

Bulk edit Pages > All Pages and set Comments to "Do not allow", or close them on a single page in the editor's Discussion setting. New pages already have comments off by default.

Why does the comment form still show after I turned comments off?

The Discussion setting only affects new posts. Close comments on existing posts with bulk edit, and check whether your theme's template still prints a comments section.

Should I turn off pingbacks and trackbacks?

Yes. On a business site they are almost entirely spam. Turn off both options in Settings > Discussion and set Pings to "Do not allow" on existing posts.
