Websites for Paint Dealers and Paint Shops: Shades, Quantities and Contractor Orders

How paint dealers and shops can plan a website: brand ranges, colour help, quantity calculators with honest assumptions, painter referrals and bulk orders.

Key takeaways
  • Organise brand ranges by category, brand and job, use brand assets within each company's dealer guidelines, and claim authorised status only if appointed.
  • Point customers to brand colour tools and shade cards, explain that screen colours differ from paint on a wall, and encourage test patches.
  • Show the assumptions behind any quantity calculator, set clear terms for painter referrals, and give bulk buyers a proper enquiry form and per-store pages.

A paint shop serves very different customers across the same counter. A homeowner wants help choosing a colour and working out how much to buy. A painter wants the right product tinted quickly and ready when they arrive. A builder or housing society wants a quotation for a large order delivered to site. A good website helps all of them before they visit or call. This guide is for dealers and retailers; if you do the painting yourself, see websites for painting contractors instead.

Who visits a paint dealer's website

VisitorWhat they wantWhere to send them
HomeownerColour ideas, the right product for each surface, how much to buy, a painterColour pages, product guides, the calculator and the painter referral page
Painter or contractorStock, tinting, quick collection, trade termsA trade page with WhatsApp ordering and opening hours
Builder, society or facility managerA quotation, consistent supply, delivery, GST invoiceThe bulk enquiry form

Put the main paths on the homepage: "Find a colour", "Work out how much paint" and "Get a bulk quote", along with your address, hours and a WhatsApp button.

Brand ranges, presented within brand guidelines

Most customers arrive knowing a brand or a type of product, so organise your catalogue both ways, plus one more:

  • By category: interior and exterior emulsions, enamels, wood finishes, primers, putty, waterproofing, textures, and tools such as rollers, brushes and masking tape
  • By brand: a page for each brand you stock, listing the ranges you actually keep or can order
  • By job: repainting a bedroom, protecting exterior walls before the monsoon or polishing a wooden door, linking to the products needed

Paint companies usually have rules for how dealers use their names, logos, product images and promotions, so ask your brand representative for official assets and guidelines before building these pages. Call yourself an authorised dealer only if you have genuinely been appointed. Keep product descriptions in line with the manufacturer's own material instead of inventing performance claims, and link to the brand's technical data sheets for coverage, drying time and surface preparation. Avoid ranking brands against each other; explain which product type suits which surface and let customers choose.

If you have a tinting machine, say which brands' shades you can mix in the store and how long tinting usually takes.

Colour visualisers and shade cards

Choosing a colour is where most customers get stuck. You don't need to build your own visualiser: many paint brands offer online colour tools and digital shade cards, and you can link to them, or embed them where the brand allows. What you can add is local, practical help:

  • Invite customers to send a photo of their room on WhatsApp so you can suggest a few shade options
  • Explain that colours on a screen never match paint on a wall exactly, because screens, lighting and finish all change how a colour looks
  • Encourage sample pots or test patches, and physical shade cards from the store, before anyone buys a full quantity
  • Let customers type a shade name or code into the enquiry form so you can confirm you can tint it
  • Publish colour combination ideas for living rooms, bedrooms, kitchens and exteriors, linking to the products used

Quantity calculators with honest assumptions

"How much paint do I need?" is one of the questions a paint shop hears most, and a calculator on your site can answer it at any hour. The key is to show your assumptions instead of hiding them:

InputWhy it matters
Wall length and height, minus doors and windowsGives the actual area to be painted
Number of coatsMost jobs need more than one, and a big colour change may need extra
Coverage of the chosen productTaken from the manufacturer's data sheet; it varies from product to product
Surface conditionNew plaster, porous or textured walls usually take more paint
Primer and puttyWorked out separately, as they have their own coverage

Show the result as an approximate range in the pack sizes you sell, with a note that a painter's site measurement will be more accurate. Suggest buying all the tinted paint for a job together, since separate batches can differ slightly in shade. Ask for a phone number only if the customer wants the estimate sent to them or a quote prepared, never just to see the result. Painter referrals, with a clear relationship

Customers often ask the shop to recommend a painter. A referral page can be genuinely useful, as long as everyone understands the arrangement:

  • Get each painter's consent before listing their name, phone number, areas and photos of their work
  • Explain whether painters are independent, how you know them and whether you have checked their work
  • Make clear that the customer agrees the job, price and payment with the painter, and who to contact about workmanship
  • Avoid promising results or timelines on a painter's behalf

Painters are customers too: a trade page can cover early collection, WhatsApp ordering and any brand loyalty schemes, described exactly as the brand runs them.

Bulk and contractor enquiries

Larger orders from builders, housing societies, schools and offices need a proper enquiry form, not just a phone number. Ask for:

  • Project type and location, and whether it covers interiors, exteriors or both
  • Products, brands and shade codes, or an option to upload a bill of quantities or specification
  • Approximate area or quantities, and the delivery date needed
  • Whether a GST invoice is required, with the company name and GSTIN

State your delivery areas and how you handle large tinting orders, and mention credit or trade accounts only if you offer them. If you also stock tiles, plywood, sanitaryware or hardware, see websites for hardware and building material suppliers for presenting a wider catalogue.

Store locator and local SEO

Most paint is bought close to home, so local search matters most:

  • Give each branch its own page with address, map, hours, phone, WhatsApp, brands stocked and whether tinting is available; see websites for businesses with multiple branches
  • Keep a complete Google Business Profile for every store, with real photos of the counter, tinting machine and displays
  • Target searches such as "paint shop near me", "paint dealer in {area}" and "{brand} paint dealer {city}", using only brands you genuinely deal in
  • Plan seasonal content and offers around festivals and the drier months when many people repaint, within each brand's promotion rules
