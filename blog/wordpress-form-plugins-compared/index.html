WordPress Form Plugins Compared: Which One Should You Use?

A practical comparison of popular WordPress form plugins, including Contact Form 7, WPForms, Gravity Forms, Fluent Forms and Forminator, plus what matters more than the plugin: delivery and spam protection.

Key takeaways
  • Choose a form plugin for ease, saved entries, spam protection and integrations.
  • Email delivery via SMTP and SPF/DKIM/DMARC matters more than the plugin.
  • Keep forms short and track submissions with a thank-you page or event.

Contact and quote forms are where your website turns visitors into leads, so the form plugin matters. There are many options, and each suits different needs. Here's a practical comparison of popular choices.

What to look for

  • Easy form building and editing
  • Reliable email notifications
  • Saving entries in the dashboard (so nothing is lost if an email fails)
  • Spam protection
  • Conditional logic, file uploads and multi-step forms if needed
  • Integrations: CRM, Google Sheets, payments
  • Impact on page speed

Popular options

Email delivery matters more than the plugin

Many "form not working" problems are really email problems. WordPress's default mail often lands in spam or never arrives. Use an SMTP plugin with a proper email service, and set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC; see email deliverability.

Spam protection

Use honeypots, time checks or invisible challenges rather than annoying puzzles; see stopping contact form spam.

Keep forms short

Whatever plugin you use, ask only for what you need: name, phone or email, and a message or service. Every extra field reduces submissions.

Track submissions

Send visitors to a thank-you page or fire an analytics event on success; see thank-you pages.

Not using WordPress forms at all?

Some sites (like this one) use a lightweight custom form with a serverless function that emails enquiries, which keeps pages fast. It's a good option for static or custom sites.
