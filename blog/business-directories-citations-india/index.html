Key takeaways
  • A small set of listings matters: maps platforms, a few general local directories, the platforms for your industry, and the associations you belong to.
  • Write one master version of your name, address and phone before listing anywhere, claim existing listings before creating new ones, and clean up duplicates and old details.
  • Skip packages that submit you to hundreds of directories and invoices for search engine listing; judge paid listings only on the enquiries they bring, tracked in one sheet.

A citation is any online mention of your business name, address and phone number, known as NAP, usually on a directory, maps app or listing site. In India, a small set of listings matters: your Google Business Profile, Bing Places and Apple's business listings, a few general local directories, the platforms used in your industry, and the associations you belong to. Keep the details identical everywhere, clean up duplicates, and ignore packages that promise hundreds of directory submissions.

Consistent business details are part of the local setup in my WordPress SEO services: the name, address and phone on your website match your Business Profile exactly, and LocalBusiness schema confirms them. This guide covers the listings beyond your website and profile, so you can set them up and keep them accurate yourself.

Citations are one step in local SEO; the local SEO guide for small businesses in India shows where they fit, and the Google Business Profile checklist covers the most important listing of all.

What citations do, and what they don't

  • They help customers find you on platforms they already use, and some listings send calls and enquiries directly.
  • They confirm your details. When your name, address and phone match across trusted sources, search engines can be more confident they are correct.
  • They are not a big ranking lever on their own. Your Business Profile, reviews, website and distance from the searcher matter far more. A few accurate listings help; a few hundred junk ones don't.
  • Wrong details cost you. An old phone number on a popular directory sends customers to a dead line or, worse, to whoever has that number now.

Write your master NAP before listing anywhere

Decide one exact version of your details and copy it everywhere, rather than typing it fresh each time:

Business name:  Example Dental Clinic
Address:        Shop 4, Example Plaza, Ground Floor,
                near Example Chowk, Kothrud, Pune 411038
Phone:          +91 98000 00000
Landline:       020 0000 0000
Website:        https://example.com/
Hours:          Mon to Sat, 10 am to 8 pm; Sunday closed
Category:       Dentist
  • Name: exactly as on your signboard and registration, without extra keywords such as "best dentist in Pune", which can get listings rejected or suspended.
  • Address: Indian addresses vary a lot, so fix the building name, floor, landmark, locality spelling and PIN code once. "Near" and "opp." landmarks help customers; keep the same one everywhere.
  • Phone: one main number that stays with the business, ideally not a staff member's personal mobile. Add a landline with its STD code if you have one.
  • Website: the same address everywhere, using the https version you actually use.

How to list properly

  1. Search before creating. Many directories create listings automatically from other data, so search each platform for your phone number and name first and claim what exists.
  2. Verify with a number and email the business controls, not a former employee's, so you never lose access.
  3. Paste your master NAP, then choose the most accurate category.
  4. Add a short, original description, real photos and accurate hours.
  5. Link to the most relevant page, and add UTM tags so you can see visits from each listing in GA4; see UTM tags explained.
  6. Decline upsells at first. Start with the free listing and see what it brings.
  7. Record the login and listing URL in your tracking sheet.

Find and clean up duplicates and old details

Duplicates and outdated listings are common after a move, a change of number or a rebrand. To find them, search for each old and current phone number in quotes, your old address, and variations of your business name. Then:

  • Claim each duplicate and ask the platform to merge or remove it through its support or edit options
  • Update the address and phone on every listing when you move, starting with the platforms that send the most enquiries
  • On Google Maps, use the suggest an edit option to report a duplicate or a closed old location; business not showing on Google Maps covers duplicates that affect your profile
  • Keep an old number forwarding to the new one for a while if you can

Consistent details also help Google connect everything to you when people search your name; see how to rank for your own business name.

Spammy directories and paid "SEO listing" scams

  • "Submit to 500 directories" packages: mostly irrelevant or foreign sites nobody visits. They add nothing and can leave a trail of junk listings.
  • Invoices for "search engine listing" or "SEO for your domain": official-looking letters and emails that are really sales offers. You never need to pay to be listed in Google; see domain and SEO scam emails.
  • Calls promising a top position on a directory or on Google for a fee. Google Business Profile is free, and nobody can sell you a guaranteed map ranking.
  • Offers of reviews bundled with a listing. Fake or bought reviews break platform policies.
  • Listings that charge to correct or remove your own details. Report them rather than pay.

Should you pay for a premium listing?

Some directories sell premium placements, and for certain industries they bring genuine leads. Decide on evidence, not on the sales call:

  • Run the free listing first and track the calls and enquiries it brings
  • Ask for real lead data for businesses like yours in your area
  • Prefer a short trial over a long contract, and read the renewal terms
  • Compare cost per real enquiry, not views or "impressions"
  • Use a separate phone number or tracked link so you know which leads came from it

Service-area businesses and multiple branches

  • No public premises: if customers never visit you, such as a plumber or pest control service, hide the street address where the platform allows it and list the areas you serve instead. Don't list a home address you don't want customers turning up at.
  • Several branches: give each branch its own listing with its own address, phone and hours, and link each one to that branch's page on your website.
  • No virtual offices: many platforms, Google included at the time of writing, don't accept an address where you aren't present during business hours, and such listings risk removal.

Connect it all to your website

Your website should carry the same master NAP in the footer and on the contact page, a map, and LocalBusiness structured data listing your official profiles; see adding Google Maps to your website and schema markup explained. When your website, profile and listings all agree, customers and search engines get the same answer wherever they look.

FAQs

What is a citation in local SEO?

Any online mention of your business name, address and phone number, usually on a directory, maps app, industry platform or social profile, with or without a link to your website.

Which directories should a small business in India list on?

Your Google Business Profile, Bing and Apple's business listings, one or two general local directories, the platforms your industry's customers use, and any associations you belong to. Check which ones appear when you search for your service in your city.

Should I pay for a premium directory listing?

Only if the free listing already shows that customers find you there, and only on a trial you can measure. Judge it on real enquiries, not views.

How do I fix wrong business details on a directory?

Claim the listing, verify it with a business number or email, and edit the details to match your master NAP. If you can't claim it, use the platform's support or report option, and keep a note of the request.

Is a "submit to 500 directories" package worth it?

No. Most of those sites have no visitors and no relevance to your customers. A handful of accurate listings on platforms people actually use does more.
