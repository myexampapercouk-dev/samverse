How Much Does an E-commerce Website Cost in India? What Affects the Price

What an online store costs to build and run in India: what drives the price, costs by type of store, one-time vs ongoing costs, and WooCommerce vs Shopify.

Key takeaways
  • The platform, catalogue complexity, design and checkout needs drive most of an online store's build cost.
  • Payments, shipping rules and integrations add development time, and some add monthly or yearly fees.
  • Budget for running costs such as hosting, licences, gateway fees and maintenance, not just the build.

An online store is a bigger project than a regular business website. As well as pages about your brand, it needs a product catalogue, a cart, a checkout, payments, shipping rules, order emails and a way to manage it all every day. That's why quotes for "an e-commerce website" can vary so widely.

This guide explains what actually drives the cost of an e-commerce website in India, how costs differ by type of store, which costs are one-time and which keep coming, and how WooCommerce and Shopify compare, so you can plan a sensible budget and compare quotes on the same terms. For a regular business site, see how much a WordPress website costs in India.

The short answer

There's no fixed price for "an online store", because two stores can need very different amounts of work. Six things decide most of the cost:

  1. The platform: WooCommerce, Shopify or custom-built
  2. How many products you have, how complex they are and who adds them
  3. Whether the design is adapted from a quality theme or created from scratch
  4. Payment, cash on delivery and GST invoice requirements
  5. Shipping rules and delivery areas
  6. Integrations with other software, and extra features such as subscriptions or dealer pricing

On top of the build, every store has running costs. Both are covered below.

The platform you choose

Your platform affects both what you pay to build the store and what you pay every year to run it.

  • WooCommerce (on WordPress): the software is free and you own the site. You pay for hosting, a domain, any premium plugins and the developer's time to set it up. It's flexible, so it suits stores with specific needs.
  • Shopify: a hosted platform with a monthly subscription. Setup can be quicker, but many features come from paid apps, and depending on your plan and payment provider there may be extra transaction fees. Check current plans before you decide.
  • Custom-built: usually the most expensive to build and maintain. It rarely makes sense unless your business model is unusual.

Your products and catalogue

The number of products matters less than how complicated they are and who does the work of adding them.

FactorKeeps cost lowerAdds to the cost
Product typesSimple products with one priceVariations (sizes, colours, weights), bundles and custom options
Product dataA clean spreadsheet you prepareDetails scattered across catalogues, PDFs and WhatsApp messages
UploadingBulk import from a spreadsheetManual entry, one product at a time
Photos and descriptionsReady, consistent photos and written copyPhoto editing, background removal and copywriting by the developer
NavigationA few clear categoriesDeep categories and filters by size, price, brand or material

If you have a large catalogue, preparing the product data yourself in a spreadsheet is one of the easiest ways to save money.

Design and the shopping experience

A store has more templates than a brochure site: home, category, product, cart, checkout, account pages and order emails. Cost depends on whether a good theme is adapted to your brand or each template is designed from scratch.

Other decisions that affect the effort involved:

  • How much the product page needs beyond the basics, such as size charts, delivery estimates, reviews and related products
  • Search and filters, which matter more as the catalogue grows
  • How carefully the mobile experience is designed and tested, since many Indian shoppers browse and buy on their phones
  • Speed optimisation, which takes more work on stores with many images and plugins

Payments and checkout

Indian shoppers expect UPI, cards, net banking and wallets, and many still want cash on delivery. Connecting a payment gateway is usually straightforward, but the work around it adds up:

  • Gateway account approval and testing real transactions before launch
  • Cash on delivery rules, such as limits by order value or pincode
  • GST-ready invoices and correct tax settings (confirm the details with your CA)
  • Checkout changes like extra fields, gift messages or delivery date selection

Payment gateways generally charge a fee on each transaction, which is a running cost rather than a build cost. See how to accept online payments in India for the main options.

Shipping and delivery

Shipping is where many Indian stores get complicated. A single flat rate is quick to set up. Rates by weight, zone or order value, free shipping thresholds, pincode serviceability checks and integration with a courier or shipping aggregator all take longer to build and test. If you deliver locally yourself, delivery slots and area restrictions add work too.

Integrations and extra features

Each connection to another system needs development and testing, and some need paid plugins or apps with yearly or monthly fees. Common examples:

  • Syncing stock with inventory, billing or accounting software
  • Order updates by SMS, email or WhatsApp
  • Product feeds for Google Merchant Center
  • Wholesale or dealer pricing for B2B buyers
  • Subscriptions, memberships or digital downloads
  • Multiple languages or currencies for international customers

A good way to control cost is to separate the must-haves for launch from features that can wait until the store is selling.

How to keep the cost under control

  • Launch with the features you need to start selling, and add the rest once orders come in
  • Prepare product data, photos and descriptions yourself, in a consistent format
  • Adapt a quality theme rather than designing every template from scratch, unless your brand really needs it
  • Choose plugins and apps carefully, as each one adds a fee, maintenance or both
  • Start with simple shipping rules and refine them as you learn what customers order
  • Avoid very cheap builds that skip testing, security and speed, because fixing them later usually costs more; see the risks of very cheap websites

How to get an accurate quote

  1. List your product types, a rough product count and any variations
  2. Decide which payment methods and shipping rules you need
  3. Note any software the store must connect to
  4. Share two or three stores you like, and what you like about them
  5. Mark each feature as "needed at launch" or "later"
  6. Share your rough budget, so the developer can suggest what fits within it
  7. Ask for an itemised quote that shows what's included, such as product upload, training and support after launch, and which licences and fees you'll pay separately

With this, developers can quote on the same scope, and you can compare quotes fairly; see how to compare website quotes. For a quick ballpark first, try the website cost calculator.

FAQs

How much does an e-commerce website cost in India?

It depends on the platform, the number and complexity of your products, the design, payments, shipping and integrations. A small store with simple products on an adapted theme sits at the lower end, while custom-designed, B2B and multi-vendor stores cost considerably more. Use the calculator for a ballpark, then ask for an itemised quote.

Is WooCommerce cheaper than Shopify in India?

WooCommerce has no platform fee, but you pay for hosting, licences and maintenance. Shopify includes hosting in its monthly plans, but apps and any transaction fees add up. Which works out cheaper depends on your features, so compare the total over two or three years.

Is a WooCommerce store a WordPress website?

Yes. WooCommerce adds a store to WordPress, so the usual WordPress website development costs apply, plus the store-specific work: products, cart, checkout, payments, shipping and order emails.

What are the running costs of an online store?

Hosting or a Shopify plan, the domain, plugin or app licences, gateway fees on each sale, shipping and notifications, maintenance and marketing. Several of these grow with your order volume.

Can I start small and add features later?

Yes, and it's often the best use of your budget. Launch with the essentials, learn what customers actually want, then add filters, subscriptions or integrations once the store is earning.

Why do e-commerce quotes vary so much?

Quotes often cover different things. One may include product upload, payment testing, speed optimisation and support after launch, while another leaves them out. Comparing itemised quotes for the same scope is the only fair comparison.
