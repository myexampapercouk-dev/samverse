Shopify Emails Going to Spam? Fixing Order Confirmations and Notifications With Sender Email and Domain Authentication

Shopify order emails going to spam? Set a sender email on your domain, authenticate it with DNS records, add DMARC and clean up your email templates.

Key takeaways
  • Shopify emails most often go to spam because the sender email is a free Gmail address or a domain that has not been authenticated in Shopify.
  • Use a sender address on your own domain, add the DNS records Shopify gives you for authentication, and publish a DMARC record for that domain.
  • Simple, text-led templates with accurate order details, a clear reply-to address and a clean marketing list keep order emails and newsletters out of spam.

If your Shopify emails are going to spam, start with the sender email: use an address on your own domain (such as orders@yourbrand.in), not a Gmail or Yahoo address, then authenticate that domain in Shopify by adding the DNS records it shows you. Add a DMARC record for the domain. These three steps fix most cases of order confirmations, shipping updates and Shopify Email campaigns landing in spam or not arriving.

Below is the full fix, the checks to run when emails still go missing, and how to keep customers in India receiving order and COD confirmation emails reliably.

Shopify emails going to spam: the quick fix

  1. Set a sender email on your domain. In Shopify admin, open the Notifications settings (in some admin versions the sender email sits under Store details) and set the sender email to an address on your own domain.
  2. Authenticate the domain. Shopify will show a prompt to authenticate. It gives you a set of CNAME records to add at your DNS provider. Add them exactly as shown.
  3. Publish a DMARC record. Add a TXT record at _dmarc on your domain. A starting policy of p=none with a reporting address is common, then tighten it later.
  4. Wait and verify. Return to Shopify and confirm the domain shows as authenticated. DNS changes can take up to 48 hours.
  5. Send a test order. Place a test order with Gmail and Outlook addresses and check where the confirmation lands.

If SPF, DKIM and DMARC are new terms, our guide to SPF, DKIM and DMARC explained covers what each record does and how to test them.

Why Shopify emails land in spam

Inbox providers such as Gmail decide where a message goes based on whether the sending domain is verified, the reputation of that domain, and what recipients do with your mail. For Shopify stores, the usual problems are:

  • A free email as sender. If the sender is yourbrand@gmail.com, Shopify cannot send as Gmail on your behalf. It sends from its own address and puts yours as the reply-to, which looks less trustworthy and can confuse customers.
  • Domain not authenticated. Without the DKIM records Shopify provides, messages are not signed for your domain, and Gmail and Yahoo treat unauthenticated mail claiming to be from your brand with suspicion.
  • No DMARC record. Large inbox providers now expect bulk senders to have DMARC. Even small stores benefit, because it tells receivers how to treat mail that fails checks.
  • Marketing reputation spilling over. If newsletters from the same domain get many spam complaints or go to old, purchased or unengaged lists, the domain's reputation drops and order emails can suffer too.
  • Template content. Image-heavy emails, many links, all-caps subject lines or heavy discount wording in order notifications look like promotions.
  • Wrong or mistyped customer emails. Some "not received" cases are simply addresses like name@gmial.com typed at checkout.

Set the sender email properly

Choose an address customers recognise and that someone actually reads, such as orders@ or support@ on your domain. That mailbox can live in Google Workspace, Zoho Mail, Microsoft 365 or your hosting email; Shopify only needs to send as that address after authentication. If you do not yet have mailboxes on your domain, compare business email options before choosing one.

Keep the same domain for the sender as your store domain where possible. A store on yourbrand.in sending from a completely different domain is a common source of mismatch and confusion.

Authenticate your domain in Shopify

When you enter a sender email on your own domain, Shopify asks you to authenticate it. The admin shows the DNS records you need, usually several CNAME records for DKIM signing. Copy them into the DNS panel where your domain is managed (GoDaddy, Hostinger, BigRock, Cloudflare and so on).

  • Some DNS panels add your domain to the end of the host name automatically. If Shopify shows a full host name, you may need to enter only the part before your domain.
  • Do not delete your existing SPF, MX or DKIM records for your mailbox provider. Shopify's records sit alongside them.
  • If the domain was bought through Shopify, the records may be added for you automatically.

For a Hostinger mailbox, the Hostinger business email setup guide shows where MX, SPF and DKIM sit so you can add Shopify's records without disturbing them.

Add DMARC and check SPF

A basic DMARC record is a TXT record on the host _dmarc with a value such as v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@yourbrand.in. Start with p=none to collect reports, then move to quarantine once you know every service that sends mail for your domain, such as Shopify, your mailbox provider, any marketing tool and any invoicing software.

Your domain should have only one SPF record. If you see two TXT records that start with v=spf1, merge them into one. Shopify's DKIM authentication is what aligns Shopify mail with your domain for DMARC, so you normally do not need to add Shopify to SPF unless its current help pages ask you to.

Fix the content of your notification templates

Shopify's default notification templates are clean. Problems usually start after heavy editing. In the Notifications settings:

  • Keep order confirmations mostly text: order number, items, amount, payment method (prepaid or cash on delivery), delivery address and expected dispatch time.
  • Avoid turning order emails into sales emails with large banners, many product links and discount codes.
  • Use a plain subject such as "Order #1234 confirmed" rather than capital letters or emojis.
  • Make the reply-to address a monitored mailbox, so customers can answer instead of marking mail as spam.
  • Add your phone or WhatsApp support number in the footer so customers can confirm orders another way.

COD confirmations and customers who never see the email

For cash on delivery orders, a missed confirmation email can lead to refused deliveries. Do not rely on email alone:

  • Send order and COD confirmation by SMS or WhatsApp through an approved app, alongside email.
  • Show a clear thank-you page with the order number and what happens next.
  • Ask customers to check the Promotions and Spam folders in Gmail if they report a missing email, and to mark your message as Not spam.

Shopify Email newsletters and domain reputation

Marketing email from the same domain affects how inboxes treat all your mail. To protect order emails:

  • Only email customers who agreed to receive marketing, and honour unsubscribes.
  • Never upload bought or scraped lists.
  • Remove addresses that bounce or have not opened anything for many months.
  • Send at a steady pace rather than sudden large blasts during sales.

How to test where your emails land

  1. Place test orders using a Gmail, an Outlook and a company address.
  2. In Gmail, open the message, use Show original, and check that SPF, DKIM and DMARC say PASS and that DKIM is signed for your domain.
  3. Check the sender line. It should show your domain address, not a Shopify address with "via" text.
  4. Repeat after any change to DNS, sender email or templates.

Be careful with emails that claim your domain has delivery problems and offer paid fixes; many are scams. See fake domain and SEO emails for how to spot them.

If records look correct but Gmail still shows failures, or your DNS has several services competing for SPF and DKIM, a short audit of the whole zone usually finds the conflict. FAQs

Why are my Shopify emails going to spam?

Usually because the sender email is a free address or a domain that is not authenticated in Shopify, there is no DMARC record, or the domain's reputation has been hurt by marketing emails. Fix the sender and authentication first.

How do I authenticate my domain in Shopify?

Set a sender email on your own domain in the notification settings, copy the CNAME records Shopify shows you into your DNS panel, then return to Shopify to confirm the domain is authenticated.

Can I use a Gmail address as my Shopify sender email?

You can enter one, but Shopify will not send as Gmail. It sends from its own address with yours as reply-to, which is more likely to land in spam. Use an address on your own domain.

Why are Shopify order confirmation emails not being received?

Check the customer's email address for typos, look in Spam and Promotions, confirm the notification is enabled, and verify your domain authentication. Also check that your DNS has only one SPF record.

Does Shopify need an SPF record?

Shopify relies on the DKIM records it gives you for authentication, so most stores do not add Shopify to SPF. Keep a single valid SPF record for your mailbox provider and follow any current instructions in Shopify's admin.

Do I need DMARC for a small Shopify store?

Yes, it is recommended. Gmail and Yahoo expect DMARC from bulk senders, and a simple p=none record also gives you reports showing which services send mail for your domain.
