Websites for Photographers: Fast Portfolio Galleries, Client Proofing, Enquiry Forms and Image SEO

Plan a photography website that loads fast and wins bookings: portfolio galleries, image sizes and lazy loading, client proofing, enquiry forms and image SEO.

Key takeaways
  • Organise the portfolio by specialism and show complete stories, but export images at display size, serve WebP and lazy-load everything except the first image so galleries stay fast on phones.
  • Deliver work through private proofing galleries with favourites, downloads and an expiry date, kept out of search and away from your main hosting if storage is tight.
  • Use an enquiry form that asks for date, city, venue, events and coverage, and support it with image SEO: descriptive file names, alt text, captions and a story for every shoot.

A photographer's website is judged twice: once on the photos, and once on how fast those photos appear on a phone. Clients want to see work in their style, complete stories rather than a few highlights, a sense of packages and price, and an easy way to check whether you are free on their date. The sites that win bookings organise galleries by specialism, keep huge images fast with the right sizes and lazy loading, deliver work through private proofing galleries, and use an enquiry form that asks for the date, city and venue up front.

Lead the homepage with the work you want more of, a line such as "Candid wedding photographer in Pune", and buttons for "Check availability" and WhatsApp.

Portfolio structure: specialisms and complete stories

  • A gallery per specialism: weddings, pre-wedding, maternity and newborn, product, corporate, so each client type finds relevant work quickly
  • Complete stories: for weddings, publish full stories from individual weddings, from getting ready to the reception, with a short write-up of the venue, the events and how you worked; couples want to know you can cover a whole day, not just take one great portrait
  • Captions that help people match your work to their plans: by venue type, city, season or style, such as "Haldi in a courtyard, Jodhpur" or "Studio product shots on white"

Captioning is a small habit that helps visitors and search engines. Studio Agama Interiors, an interior design studio I built a site for, captions its project images by style and room, such as "The Minimalist: Modern Interior", so a homeowner can see quickly whether the studio suits their taste. A photographer's galleries work the same way.

Portfolio performance: big images, fast pages

Photography sites are the heaviest small business websites there are, and a gallery that loads slowly on mobile data loses the couple before they see your best frame.

Export at the right size and format

Never upload straight from the camera or from a full-resolution export. Export each image at the largest size it is actually displayed at, allowing for sharp high-resolution screens, then compress it. WordPress creates several smaller copies of every upload and lets each phone or laptop download the size it needs, and an image optimisation plugin can serve WebP versions automatically. Image optimisation for WordPress walks through sizes, formats and compression step by step.

Lazy loading, done right

Lazy loading delays images further down the page until the visitor scrolls towards them, which makes long galleries usable on a phone. Two rules matter: never lazy-load the first image visitors see, because it delays the main picture, and give every image its width and height so the layout does not jump as images arrive. Lazy loading explained covers the details and when not to use it.

Choose light galleries over heavy sliders

  • A single strong hero image usually beats a full-screen slider of ten photos
  • Use a lightweight gallery or grid with a lightbox, rather than a plugin that loads its own scripts on every page
  • Masonry layouts look good but can shift as images load if sizes are not reserved
  • Embed films from YouTube or Vimeo with a thumbnail until someone clicks play, rather than uploading video files to your hosting

Good hosting matters more for photographers than for most businesses, and a CDN can help if your clients are spread across cities or countries.

Client proofing and delivery galleries

Private galleries for proofing and delivery make a photographer look organised and save endless file transfers. A good setup lets clients view their photos behind a password, mark favourites for the album, leave comments, and download web-size or full-resolution files, with an expiry date after which the gallery is archived.

Whichever you use, keep private galleries out of search results, give each client their own access, and decide how long you keep their photos. Client photos are personal data, so explain in your contract and privacy notice how you store, share and delete them.

An enquiry form that checks availability

Couples often contact several photographers in the same evening, and the one who replies first with a clear answer usually gets the call. A form that collects the right details lets you reply in one message:

  • Event type, and for weddings the events to cover, such as haldi, mehendi, sangeet, wedding and reception
  • Dates, city and venue, or "venue not booked yet"
  • Approximate guest count and the coverage needed: photos, films, drone, albums
  • A budget range and how they heard about you
  • A phone number for WhatsApp

For product photographers, ask instead for the number of products, the type of shots (white background, lifestyle, on-model), where the images will be used and the deadline. Show packages, or at least what a typical package includes and a "starting from" note, so clients with very different budgets filter themselves. Wedding planners and filmmakers receive the same kind of enquiries; see websites for event and wedding planners and websites for video production companies.

Image SEO for photographers

Search engines understand an image mostly from the words around it. For every gallery and story:

  • Descriptive file names, such as candid-haldi-ceremony-jodhpur.webp rather than DSC_4821.jpg
  • Alt text that describes what is in the photo for people who cannot see it, without stuffing keywords
  • Captions and story text that name the venue, city and type of shoot
  • Page titles that match searches, such as "Pre-wedding photographer in Lonavala" or "Product photographer in Mumbai"
  • Images included in your sitemap, which most WordPress SEO plugins handle
  • Location data removed from photos taken at clients' homes, for their privacy

Image SEO for Google Images, Lens and Discover covers this in more depth.

Consent, copyright and watermarks

  • Add a clause to your contract about whether you may publish the client's photos, and respect clients who say no
  • Take extra care with newborn, maternity and children's photos: get written consent from parents and avoid naming children
  • For commercial work, check what your client agreement allows you to show, and when
  • Say who owns copyright and what the client may do with the images
  • Upload web-size images and use a subtle watermark if your field expects one; disabling right-click mostly annoys genuine visitors

Local SEO and referrals

Most photography searches include a place: "wedding photographer in Pune", "maternity photoshoot near me", "product photographer in Bhiwandi". Complete your Google Business Profile with your best work and the areas you serve, and ask happy clients for reviews soon after delivery; see getting more Google reviews. If you shoot destination weddings, create pages for places where you have genuinely worked, each with real stories from there, rather than copies with the city name swapped. Planners, decorators, makeup artists and venues you work with often link to each other's sites, which helps all of you.

Common mistakes on photography websites

  • Full-resolution images uploaded straight from the camera
  • A homepage slider of a dozen large photos that delays everything
  • Hundreds of images in one gallery instead of a curated selection and full stories
  • An enquiry form that asks only for name and message, so every reply starts with questions
  • Private client galleries that appear in Google
  • File names like IMG_0001.jpg and no text on gallery pages

FAQs

How do I keep a photography website fast with so many images?

Export images at display size, compress them, serve WebP, lazy-load everything except the first image, avoid heavy sliders and use decent hosting. A speed audit can show which images and scripts are slowing your pages.

Should I put client galleries on my own website?

You can, with a gallery plugin for proofing and favourites, as long as your hosting has room for the files. Many busy photographers use a dedicated gallery service for delivery and keep the website for the portfolio.

Should photographers show prices?

Showing packages or a "starting from" figure filters out poor-fit enquiries. If every shoot is quoted individually, explain what affects the price, such as days, events, team and deliverables.

Does Instagram replace a photography website?

Instagram is good for discovery, but a website lets you show full stories, explain packages, rank for local searches and collect enquiries with the details you need.

How do I get my photos found on Google?

Use descriptive file names, honest alt text, captions and story text naming the place and type of shoot, page titles that match searches and a sitemap that includes images.
