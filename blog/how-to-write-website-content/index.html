How to Write Content for Your Business Website: A Page-by-Page Process for the Homepage, About, Services and Contact Pages

How to write website content for a business: plan pages, write service pages first, then home, about and contact, with SEO basics and a brief template.

Key takeaways
  • Plan before you write: decide who each page is for, the one action it should lead to, and the questions customers ask before they buy.
  • Write service pages first, then the homepage, About and contact pages, using plain words, the customer first and a specific fact behind every claim.
  • Make every page scannable on a phone, use the words customers search for naturally, and fill in a short content brief for each page before drafting.

To write content for your business website, plan first: list the pages you need, decide who each page is for and the one action it should lead to, and collect the questions customers ask before they buy. Then write in a set order, starting with your service pages, then the homepage, About and contact pages. Use plain words, lead with the customer's problem, back every claim with a specific fact, and make each page easy to scan on a phone.

Content is the part that most often holds a website project up. In a WordPress website development project, if your content isn't ready, I suggest a page structure and what to write for each page; the facts and the voice still come from you, because nobody knows your customers better. This guide is that process, written for business owners who aren't professional writers.

If you already have a site and the words aren't working, website copywriting mistakes is the quicker fix. This page is for writing pages from scratch or rewriting them properly.

Before you write: four decisions

1. Who the site is for

Write down your two or three main types of customer, what problem brings each one to you, and what worries them before they hire someone like you: price, quality, reliability, time or trust. A clinic's patients, a manufacturer's procurement buyers and a coaching institute's parents need different words, even for similar services.

2. What you want visitors to do

Pick the main action: call, WhatsApp, a quote form, a booking or a visit. Every page should lead there, with one clear button rather than five competing ones.

3. Which pages you need

Most service businesses need a homepage, an About page, one page per main service, a contact page, and some proof such as case studies, a gallery or reviews. A blog is useful later, once the core pages exist. If you're briefing a developer, the website brief template helps you list pages and features in one place.

4. The questions customers ask

Go through recent WhatsApp chats, emails and calls, and write down every question people asked before they bought. Ten to twenty questions is plenty. These become headings, paragraphs and FAQs, and they're often the exact phrases people type into Google.

Service pages

Give each main service its own page. These pages do most of the work for both enquiries and Google, because they match what people search for: a specific service, often with a place. A reliable structure:

  1. Headline naming the service, and the area if you're local.
  2. The problem in the customer's words, in two or three sentences.
  3. What you do and what's included, as a list.
  4. How it works, in three to five steps.
  5. Proof: a relevant project, review or photo of real work.
  6. Price guidance if you can give it, or what affects the price.
  7. FAQs about timing, cost, process and service area.
  8. One call to action, repeated at the end.

How to write service pages that rank and convert goes through each section with examples.

The homepage

The homepage has one job: make it obvious, within a few seconds, what you do, who it's for and what to do next. Lead with a headline that says what you do, for whom and where, follow it with one primary call to action, then summarise each service with a link to its page, show proof, explain the process briefly, answer common questions and end with a clear next step. How to write a homepage that converts covers each section in order.

The About page

People buy from people. Your About page should answer "can I trust you?" rather than tell the whole company history: who you are, who you help, how you work, your relevant qualifications or registrations, real photos of you, your team or your premises, and a call to action at the end. How to write an About page that builds trust has a simple structure to follow.

The contact page

  • Phone, WhatsApp and email, each tappable on a phone.
  • A short form: name, phone or email, what they need, and a message. Every extra field loses some enquiries.
  • Your address and a map if customers visit you, and the areas you serve if you visit them.
  • Opening hours, and what happens after someone gets in touch.

Voice: how your content should sound

  • Write as you speak to customers, in short, plain sentences.
  • Lead with "you", then explain what "we" do about it.
  • Be specific. Replace "quality service" with what that means in practice.
  • Only claim what you can back up. No "best", "No. 1" or numbers you can't show.
  • Keep one voice across pages, even if several people write.

A fictional example of the difference:

Before: "We are a leading provider of innovative interior solutions with a commitment to excellence."

After: "We design and build modular kitchens and wardrobes for apartments in Pune, from the first measurement to installation, with one person managing your project."

Make it easy to scan

Most visitors skim, especially on a phone. Help them:

  • Use headings that say something, such as "What's included" or "How long it takes", not "Overview".
  • Keep paragraphs to two to four sentences.
  • Use lists for features, steps and inclusions, and a table for comparisons.
  • Put the most important point first in every section.
  • Add a call to action after major sections, not just at the end.
  • Read every page on your phone before publishing.

SEO basics for website content

  • One main topic per page. Two pages targeting the same search compete with each other.
  • Use the words customers use, including local terms and spellings. Keyword research for small businesses shows how to find them with free tools.
  • Put the main phrase where it matters: the page title, the H1 and early in the text, then write naturally.
  • Write a unique title and meta description for every page.
  • Link related pages, such as the homepage to each service, and services to relevant case studies.
  • Describe images with alt text and sensible file names.
  • Turn customer questions into FAQs; FAQs for SEO covers how to write good ones.

Before publishing each page, run through the on-page SEO checklist.

A content brief template you can copy

Fill this in for each page before you write. It takes ten minutes and saves hours of rewriting:

Page:              Modular kitchens, Pune
Who reads it:      Apartment owners planning a kitchen
Their problem:     Small space, worried about cost and delays
Main action:       Book a free site measurement on WhatsApp
Main search term:  modular kitchen Pune
Related terms:     modular kitchen cost, L-shaped kitchen design
Questions to answer:
  - What does it cost, and what affects the price?
  - How long does it take from design to installation?
  - Which materials do you use, and why?
Proof to include:  Two recent projects with photos, one review
Links to:          Wardrobes page, projects gallery, contact page
Facts to check:    Warranty terms, service areas

The example is fictional. The brief also makes it easy to hand a page to a copywriter or developer without long explanations.

Using AI tools, carefully

AI writing tools can help with a first draft, an outline or a tidier sentence. They can't know your prices, process, service area or customers, and their drafts tend to sound like every other business. Use them as an assistant, check every fact, and add the details only you can give. Using AI tools for website content responsibly covers the risks.

Before you publish: a final check

  1. Does the first screen say what the page is about and who it's for?
  2. Is there one clear next step, and does it work?
  3. Is every claim specific and true?
  4. Are prices, timings and service areas current?
  5. Does it read well on a phone?
  6. Have you read it aloud and cut anything you stumbled over?
  7. Does it have a unique title, meta description and links to related pages?

FAQs

How much content should each page have?

Enough to answer the questions a buyer has at that stage, and no more. A service page usually needs several sections; a contact page needs very little. Every section should earn its place.

Should I write my own website content or hire a copywriter?

Many owners can write good content themselves, because they know their customers best. A copywriter helps when you have many pages, little time or a new brand, but they still need your facts, so the planning above isn't wasted either way.

How do I write website content for SEO?

Give each page one main topic, use the words customers search for in the title, H1 and text, answer their questions clearly, and link related pages. Write for people first; keyword stuffing puts readers off and doesn't help rankings.

Can I use AI to write my website content?

For drafts and outlines, yes, but edit heavily, check every fact and add specifics only you know. Unedited AI text tends to be generic and can contain mistakes.

What if I'm not a good writer?

Start with the questions customers ask, and answer each one in a few sentences as if replying on WhatsApp. That raw material, tidied up, makes better website content than polished but vague copy.
