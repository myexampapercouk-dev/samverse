Websites for Domestic Help, Nanny and Caretaker Placement Agencies

What a website for a maid, nanny or caretaker agency in India needs: service pages, verification process, replacement policy, enquiry forms and local SEO.

Key takeaways
  • Families choose a placement agency on trust, so explain your verification, training and replacement process clearly before asking for an enquiry.
  • Build separate pages for each role you place, such as full-day maids, nannies, cooks and elder caretakers, because families search for the specific role.
  • Keep the enquiry short and mobile-friendly, offer WhatsApp, and handle worker and family personal data carefully with a clear privacy policy.

A website for a maid and nanny agency in India has to earn trust before it earns an enquiry. Families are letting someone into their home, often to care for a child or an elderly parent, so the site should explain who you place, how you check them, what happens if things do not work out and how quickly you can respond.

This guide covers the pages, trust signals, forms and local SEO that a domestic help, nanny or caretaker placement agency needs, plus the mistakes that make families hesitate.

What families look for before they call

A family searching for "nanny agency near me", "live-in maid for home" or "caretaker for elderly at home" usually has an urgent need and some anxiety. They want to know:

  • Which roles you place and in which areas.
  • How workers are verified and whether background or police verification is part of the process.
  • Whether the worker is live-in or comes for fixed hours.
  • What the agency fee covers and what the replacement policy is.
  • How fast you can send profiles or arrange a meeting.

If the website answers these in plain language, the phone call becomes a confirmation rather than an interrogation. If it does not, families move to the next listing.

Explain your verification process clearly

This is the heart of the site. Describe each step you actually follow, for example identity document checks, address confirmation, previous employer references, an interview, skills checks for cooks or nannies, and whether you assist with police verification. Rules and procedures for domestic worker verification vary by state and city, so phrase it carefully and check current local requirements.

Avoid vague promises such as "100% verified staff" unless you can explain exactly what that means. A short, honest list of steps is more convincing than a badge.

It also helps to tell families what they should do on their side: keep copies of documents, register with the local police if required in their city, and agree duties and leave in writing.

Role pages that match how families search

Each role deserves its own page because the questions differ:

  • Nanny or babysitter: experience with infants or toddlers, hygiene, how screen time and outdoor play are handled, whether training in basic first aid is part of your process.
  • All-day or live-in maid: typical duties, accommodation expectations, weekly offs.
  • Cook: cuisines, vegetarian or Jain cooking, trial meal arrangements.
  • Elder caretaker or patient attendant: mobility support, medication reminders, the difference between a caretaker and a trained nurse. Be careful not to suggest medical care unless your staff are qualified for it.
  • Japa maid or newborn care: if you offer it, explain the duration and duties.

Write these in your own words, with the questions families actually ask you on the phone. If you place caretakers for older people, the guide to elder care and home nursing websites covers that audience in more depth.

Fees, trial period and replacement policy

Many complaints about placement agencies come from unclear terms. Put yours on a page of its own: how the agency fee is calculated, when it is paid, whether there is a trial period, how many replacements are included and within what time, and what happens if the worker leaves. You do not have to publish every amount, but the structure should be clear. The article on whether to show prices on your website explains the trade-offs.

Enquiry forms, WhatsApp and response time

Keep the family enquiry form short: name, phone, area, role needed, live-in or hourly, and preferred start date. Anything more can be asked on the call. Add a WhatsApp button so busy parents can message during work hours. See adding WhatsApp to a business website.

Speed matters in this business. A family that enquires in the evening may have called three agencies by morning. Set up instant email or WhatsApp notifications for every form, and reply quickly. The guide on following up website leads fast has practical ideas.

Keep the worker registration form separate from the family form, so the two lists never mix and each gets the right follow-up.

Handling personal data responsibly

Agencies collect sensitive information: worker ID documents, addresses, family details and sometimes health information about an elderly parent. On the website side:

  • Do not publish worker documents, full names with photos, or phone numbers on public pages.
  • Store uploaded documents securely rather than in a public media folder, and limit who can access them.
  • Publish a clear privacy policy explaining what you collect and why. India's data protection law applies to this kind of data, so check the current requirements with a legal adviser.
  • Keep the site, plugins and forms updated and use HTTPS everywhere.

Local SEO for placement agencies

Most searches are local, so a complete Google Business Profile with your real office address, hours and photos is essential. Ask placed families for reviews after the first month, when they can judge the placement. The Google Business Profile checklist covers setup.

On the site, mention the localities you serve naturally on role pages, and only create separate area pages when you have something genuinely local to say. Helpful articles, such as what to ask a nanny in an interview or how live-in arrangements usually work, attract families early in their search.

Recruiting workers through the website

An agency is only as good as its pool of candidates, and the website can help with recruitment too. A simple "Join us" page in plain language, ideally also in Hindi or the main local language of your city, tells candidates what roles you place, what documents they need to bring and how the interview works. If you do not charge workers any fee, say so clearly; it reassures candidates and also tells families you run an ethical process.

Keep the worker form very short, since many candidates fill it on a basic phone: name, phone, area, roles they can do, languages spoken and experience in years. A missed-call number or WhatsApp option helps those who find forms difficult. The guide to Hindi and English WordPress websites explains how to add a second language without duplicating the whole site.

Choosing the right platform and keeping it simple

Most placement agencies do not need a complex portal. A WordPress site with well-written role pages, two separate forms, WhatsApp, a blog and secure document storage covers the needs of a small or growing agency. If you later want families to log in, track replacements or sign agreements online, that can be added in stages rather than built on day one.

What matters more than features is that the site loads quickly on mobile data, the phone and WhatsApp buttons work on every page, and the content is kept current. Out-of-date areas, old phone numbers or a policy page that no longer matches your terms cause more lost enquiries than missing features. Review the site every few months, add new photos of your office and team, and update FAQs with the questions families keep asking.

FAQs

What should a website for a maid and nanny agency in India include?

Role pages for each type of worker, a clear verification and placement process, fees and replacement policy, areas served, a worker registration page, a short family enquiry form, WhatsApp and a privacy policy.

Should I show worker profiles publicly on the website?

Generally no. Share profiles privately with families after an enquiry. Public profiles expose personal data and can be misused.

How can my agency get more enquiries online?

Build role-specific pages, keep your Google Business Profile active with reviews, reply to enquiries quickly and make the site fast and easy on mobile.

Can the website collect worker documents?

This can be set up with a secure upload form that stores files privately with restricted access. Only collect what you need and explain why in your privacy policy.

Do I need a separate page for elder caretakers?

Yes. Families looking for elder care search with different words and have different worries, such as mobility help and medical needs, so a dedicated page answers them better.
