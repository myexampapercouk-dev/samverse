How to Write Service Pages That Rank and Convert: Structure, Copy, Proof and a Section-by-Section Template

How to write service pages that rank on Google and turn visitors into enquiries: one page per service, a section-by-section template, proof and FAQs.

Key takeaways
  • Give every main service its own page, named the way customers search, because each service is a separate search with its own questions.
  • Answer questions in the order buyers ask them: what it is and who it is for, what is included, how it works, what affects the price, proof, FAQs and how to start.
  • Use the customer's words, real proof and one clear call to action repeated down the page, and keep every service page's text unique even when the layout is shared.

A service page that ranks and converts focuses on one service, names it the way customers search for it, and answers their questions in the order they ask them: what it is and who it's for, what's included, how it works, what affects the price, proof that you've done it before, the remaining doubts in a short FAQ, and a clear way to get in touch, repeated down the page. Google ranks it because it fully answers a "hire" or "buy" search; visitors enquire because it removes their doubts.

In my WordPress website development projects, the page plan comes before any design: I map out the pages and what each one has to answer, and every main service gets its own page because each one is a separate search. I suggest what each page should say and polish your text, and this guide shows you how to draft it yourself.

Why each service needs its own page

A single "Services" page with a bullet list rarely ranks for anything. A physiotherapy clinic offering sports injury rehab, back pain treatment and home visits is answering three different searches from three different people, with different questions about each. A CA firm's GST registration, income tax filing and company incorporation services are the same. Separate pages let each one match its search, answer its own questions and carry its own proof.

Combine services only when customers always buy them together and search for them as one thing. And if you serve several cities, put the areas on the service page first; separate city pages are worth it only when each has something genuinely local to say, as local landing pages done right explains.

Before you write: five pieces of research

  1. The search phrase. How do customers describe the service? "AC servicing" or "AC maintenance"? "Website maintenance" or "website AMC"? Check Search Console's queries if your site already has some visibility.
  2. The intent. Search the phrase on your phone. If the results are service pages and Business Profiles, a service page can rank; if they are all guides, you need a different phrase or an article as well. See search intent explained.
  3. The questions. Go through recent enquiries, WhatsApp chats and sales calls and list what people asked before they bought. Add the People Also Ask questions from Google.
  4. The objections. Price, trust, timing, quality and "will this work for my situation?" are the usual ones. Each needs an answer somewhere on the page.
  5. The proof. Projects, photos, testimonials you have permission to use, and qualifications or registrations you genuinely hold.

The headline and opening

Name the service the way customers say it, with the area if you're local: "AC repair and servicing in Kothrud" or "GST registration for small businesses". Follow it with one line on the outcome, and put the main action, such as "Get a quote", "WhatsApp us" or "Book a site visit", where it is visible without scrolling on a phone. Avoid openings like "Welcome to our company" that make visitors work out whether they're in the right place.

What's included, and what isn't

Specifics persuade where adjectives don't. "Gas top-up, filter cleaning, coil cleaning and a cooling check" beats "complete AC care". Stating what isn't included, such as parts, hosting or travel beyond a certain area, prevents enquiries you can't serve and arguments later. The service pages on this site, which is custom-coded rather than built on WordPress, include a "what is included, and what is not" section for that reason.

Price, or what affects it

Buyers want some idea of cost before they contact you. Show prices or ranges if your work allows it; if every job is different, explain what moves the price. On Sahni Power Solutions, a generator rental site I built, each capacity page explains what affects the rental price: hire duration, the fuel arrangement, whether an operator is included and the distance from the yard. Should you show prices on your website? covers the options.

Proof

Use proof that relates to this service, not generic praise: a case study of a similar job, before-and-after photos, a testimonial with the customer's name and context, or a registration number. Never invent reviews, numbers or client logos. If you're new, explain your process and qualifications in detail instead; specific detail is itself a form of proof.

FAQs

Answer the questions from your research in two to four sentences each. FAQ rich results in Google have been limited to a small group of authoritative sites since 2023, but the answers still help visitors decide, and they help the page match longer searches. FAQs for SEO covers how to choose and write them.

Writing copy that converts

  • Use the customer's words. Lift phrases from enquiries: "my site keeps going down" beats "uptime assurance".
  • Tie each feature to its benefit: "monthly backups, so a broken update can be rolled back".
  • Be specific about where and for whom: "Baner, Wakad and Hinjewadi" says more than "all areas".
  • One main action, repeated after the key sections. A form, a WhatsApp button and a phone number are fine together because they lead to the same enquiry; a second, unrelated offer is not.
  • Pre-fill WhatsApp messages with the service name, so a tap starts the conversation and you know which page it came from.
  • Keep forms short, and let them sort enquiries. On Vansh Group's site, the contact form has a service-type dropdown so enquiries arrive already sorted by service.
  • Explain jargon the first time you use it, or leave it out.
  • Write as long as the questions need, and no longer. Padding to hit a word count makes the page worse.

SEO essentials for service pages

  • A title tag of about 60 characters or fewer with the service (and area), and a meta description that gives a reason to enquire.
  • One H1 naming the service, and H2s that say something, such as "What AC servicing includes" rather than "Our offering".
  • A short, readable URL that you won't need to change.
  • Links to the page from the main menu, the homepage, related services and relevant blog posts, and links from the page to guides for visitors still researching.
  • Structured data describing the business and the service. Rank Math includes a Service schema type at the time of writing; other plugins may need an add-on or custom code.
  • Unique text on every service page. A shared layout is fine; copying paragraphs and swapping the service or city name is not.
  • Real photos, compressed and with descriptive alt text, and a page that loads quickly on mobile data.

Building service pages in WordPress

  • Use Pages, with a parent "Services" page that introduces and links to each one, so the structure is clear to visitors and Google.
  • Share the layout, not the words. Save the section layout as an Elementor template or a block pattern so every page has the same structure, then write each page's content from scratch.
  • Make the call-to-action block reusable, as an Elementor global widget or a synced pattern, so a new phone number or WhatsApp link is changed once, everywhere.
  • Track enquiries by page. With form submissions and WhatsApp clicks set up as key events in GA4, the landing page report shows which service pages bring enquiries and which need work.

Common service page mistakes

  • A heading, two sentences and a contact form.
  • The same text on every service page or city page with one word swapped.
  • Describing the company instead of the customer's problem.
  • No hint of price and no explanation of the process.
  • Generic or invented testimonials, or only stock photos.
  • The only call to action at the very bottom, or five competing ones.
  • Trying to rank a blog post for a "hire" or "near me" search that needs a service page.

FAQs

What should a service page include?

The service named clearly, who it's for, what's included and excluded, how the process works, price or the factors behind it, proof such as case studies and testimonials, FAQs, and a call to action repeated through the page with a form, WhatsApp and phone.

How long should a service page be?

Long enough to answer what buyers ask before they enquire, and no longer. A simple local service may need a few hundred words; a complex B2B service with a detailed process and FAQs needs much more. Let your list of customer questions decide.

Should I put prices on my service pages?

If your pricing is standard, showing it filters enquiries and builds trust. If every job differs, give ranges, a starting point or the factors that affect the price, so visitors can judge whether you fit their budget.

Should I create a separate page for each city I serve?

Only when you have genuinely local content for each city, such as local projects, team, address or area-specific details. Otherwise list your areas on the main service page and keep a strong Google Business Profile.

Do service pages need FAQs?

They help. FAQs answer the last doubts that stop people enquiring, and they let the page match longer, question-style searches. Keep them to real questions with short, direct answers.
