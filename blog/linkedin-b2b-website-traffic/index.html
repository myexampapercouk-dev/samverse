How to Use LinkedIn to Bring B2B Visitors and Enquiries to Your Website

How to get B2B leads from LinkedIn to your website in India: profile links, posts that earn clicks, landing pages, UTM tracking and follow-up that works.

Key takeaways
  • LinkedIn builds trust with buyers, but enquiries happen on your website, so every profile, post and DM should point to a specific, relevant page.
  • Send LinkedIn visitors to focused landing pages with one clear action, and tag every link with UTM parameters so GA4 shows what actually works.
  • Consistent, useful posts from a real person beat company-page broadcasting; reply fast to website enquiries because B2B buyers compare several vendors.

To get B2B leads from LinkedIn to your website, treat LinkedIn as the place where buyers meet you and your website as the place where they decide. Put a specific page link on your profile, write posts that solve one buyer problem and link to a page that goes deeper, and track every click with UTM tags so you know which posts bring enquiries.

This guide is for Indian B2B firms: manufacturers, IT and SaaS companies, consultants, logistics providers, training companies and agencies who sell to other businesses and want LinkedIn effort to turn into real conversations.

Why LinkedIn works for B2B, and where it stops

Purchase managers, founders, HR heads and plant managers in India are on LinkedIn, and they read it during working hours. It is good at three things: making your name familiar, showing that real people with real expertise stand behind the company, and starting conversations. It is weak at the part where a buyer needs detail: specifications, capacity, certifications you genuinely hold, case studies, pricing approach and a form they can fill in for a quote.

That detail lives on your website. A buyer who likes your post will usually click your profile, then your website, and judge you within a minute. If the site is slow, vague or out of date, the LinkedIn effort is wasted.

Set up your profile and company page to send clicks

Most people link their homepage and stop there. A better approach is to link the page that matches what you want to be known for.

  • Personal profile: add a custom button or featured link to a service page or a short "work with us" page, not just the homepage.
  • Featured section: pin two or three items: a case study, a useful guide on your site and the quote page.
  • Headline: say who you help and with what, in words a buyer would search for, such as "Precision CNC parts for automotive OEMs in Pune".
  • Company page: keep the website URL, location, industry and description accurate; it often appears in Google when people search your company name.
  • Team profiles: ask sales and leadership to list the company correctly so their profiles link back too.

Posts that earn website clicks

LinkedIn tends to show posts with outbound links to fewer people, so many B2B writers put the link in the first comment or simply invite readers to their profile. Whatever you choose, the post itself has to be useful on its own. A few formats that suit Indian B2B buyers:

  1. Buyer questions answered: "What to check before choosing a contract manufacturer", with three points in the post and the full checklist on your site.
  2. Mini case stories: the problem, what you changed and what the client gained, told honestly, with the full case study on your website.
  3. Process explainers: how a typical project runs, timelines and what you need from the client. These reduce fear and pre-qualify buyers.
  4. Behind the scenes: a new machine, a site visit, a training batch. Proof that you are real and active.
  5. Opinions with substance: a clear view on a common mistake in your industry, backed by experience.

Post from a real person's profile where possible. Founder and team posts usually travel further than company-page posts, and buyers trust people over logos.

The page a visitor lands on matters more than the post that sent them. Match the page to the promise in the post.

What a B2B landing page needs

  • A headline that repeats the post's promise
  • Who it is for, and who it is not for
  • Proof: client logos you have permission to use, case studies, photos of real work
  • A short form with only the fields you genuinely need, plus phone and WhatsApp options
  • Fast loading on mobile, because many people open LinkedIn on their phone

Track LinkedIn traffic with UTM tags and GA4

LinkedIn's own numbers show reactions, not enquiries. To see which posts lead to forms, add UTM parameters to every link you share, for example:

https://example.in/services/cnc-machining/?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=oct-checklist-post
  • Keep source and medium consistent (linkedin and social) so reports group cleanly.
  • Use the campaign value to name the post or theme.
  • Track form submissions, WhatsApp clicks and phone taps as events in GA4. GA4 events explained covers how.
  • If you run LinkedIn ads, the LinkedIn Insight Tag can be added through Google Tag Manager, with consent handled properly.

Review monthly: which landing pages got LinkedIn visitors, how long they stayed and how many enquired. Drop the formats that bring nobody and repeat the ones that do.

How to get B2B leads from LinkedIn to your website: turn visitors into conversations

B2B buyers in India often shortlist three or four vendors at once. Speed and clarity of reply decide a lot.

  • Reply quickly: set up email notifications for every form, and make sure they actually arrive. Fixing WordPress emails with SMTP helps if they do not.
  • Offer a next step: a short call, a sample, a site visit or a written proposal.
  • Collect emails with permission: a monthly newsletter keeps you in front of buyers whose project is months away.
  • Connect on LinkedIn after the enquiry: it keeps the relationship visible between projects.

Common mistakes Indian B2B firms make on LinkedIn

  • Posting only festival greetings and award photos, with nothing a buyer can learn from
  • Linking every post to the homepage
  • A website that still shows an old address, old phone number or broken pages
  • No way to tell which posts led to enquiries
  • Asking for a meeting in the first connection message
  • Stopping after a few weeks; LinkedIn rewards consistency over months

A simple weekly routine

  1. Two posts a week from a founder or senior person, each answering a real buyer question.
  2. Ten minutes a day commenting thoughtfully on posts from buyers and partners.
  3. One website page updated or added each month: a case study, an FAQ or a service detail.
  4. Monthly review of GA4: LinkedIn sessions, landing pages and enquiries.

If you sell online too

Some B2B firms also run a wholesale or trade store on Shopify or WooCommerce. The same rules apply: link the trade pricing or bulk order page from posts aimed at retailers, keep the login or registration process simple, and tag those links separately so you can see whether LinkedIn brings trade buyers or only curious readers. FAQs

How do I get B2B leads from LinkedIn to my website?

Link specific pages from your profile and featured section, post useful content that points to deeper pages on your site, and give visitors a clear way to enquire. Track it with UTM tags so you know what works.

Should I put links in LinkedIn posts or in the comments?

Many B2B writers put the link in the first comment because posts with links can reach fewer people. Test both on your own audience and compare clicks in GA4.

Is a company page or personal profile better for B2B?

Personal profiles of founders and team members usually get more reach and trust. Keep the company page accurate and active, but do not rely on it alone.

Do I need LinkedIn ads?

Not to start. Organic posting and a strong website come first. Ads can help later for specific campaigns, but they need a landing page that converts.

How long before LinkedIn brings enquiries?

It depends on your niche, consistency and offer. Most firms need several months of regular, useful posting before enquiries become steady.
