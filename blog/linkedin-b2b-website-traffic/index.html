How to Use LinkedIn to Bring B2B Visitors and Enquiries to Your Website

How B2B businesses can use LinkedIn profiles, company pages, posts and articles to send decision-makers to their website, and turn those visits into enquiries.

Key takeaways
  • A clear personal profile, a complete company page and useful posts give B2B buyers a reason to visit your website.
  • Publish full articles on your own site first and link from LinkedIn to the specific case study or product page a post is about.
  • Track LinkedIn visits with UTM tags and an enquiry source field, and judge results over months rather than weeks.

For businesses that sell to other businesses, LinkedIn is where many buyers, procurement managers and founders spend their professional time online. Used well, it does two jobs: it puts you in front of the right people, and it sends the curious ones to your website, where your products, case studies and enquiry form can do the rest. Here's how to make that path work, whether you run a manufacturing unit, an IT firm or a consultancy.

Why LinkedIn and your website work together

LinkedIn is good at attention and relationships. Your website is better at detail and conversion. A buyer might notice your post, glance at your profile, then visit your site to check capabilities, certifications and past work before sending an enquiry. If any step feels thin or broken, they drop off. So the goal isn't just more followers; it's a clear route from LinkedIn to a relevant page that answers the buyer's questions.

Start with your personal profile

In B2B, people tend to engage with people more than logos, so a founder's or sales head's profile often reaches further than the company page. Make it work as a signpost:

  • Headline: what you do and for whom, such as "Custom control panels for OEMs and process plants", not just "Director"
  • About section: the problems you solve, industries you serve and how to get in touch, including your website address
  • Featured section: pin links to your best case study, product catalogue or a key service page
  • Contact info: add your website, and link your current role to the company page

A clear, professional photo and a banner image that states what you offer help too.

Set up the company page properly

The company page is where buyers go to check you're a real, established business. Fill in every field: description, industry, location, company size and website. Use the page's call-to-action button (options include visiting your website or contacting you) and point it at a useful page, not always the homepage. Ask colleagues to list the company correctly in their experience, so their profiles link back to the page. If you have distinct product lines, LinkedIn's showcase pages can give each one its own presence, but only if you can keep them active.

Post content that earns clicks

Posts are how most people will discover you. Share things buyers find useful, not just announcements:

  • A problem you solved for a client (with their permission), and how you solved it
  • Photos or short videos from the factory floor, a site installation or a product test
  • Practical advice, such as how to choose between two materials or specifications
  • Answers to questions you keep hearing in sales meetings
  • New certifications, capacity or products, framed around what they mean for the buyer

Give real value in the post itself, then offer the link for people who want the full details. LinkedIn doesn't publish exactly how it ranks posts, and many users feel posts with outside links reach fewer people, so test what works for you: a link in the post, a link in the first comment, or no link and a mention of your website. Posting regularly, and commenting thoughtfully on buyers' and partners' posts, builds more visibility than occasional bursts. LinkedIn articles, newsletters and your own blog

LinkedIn also lets you publish long-form articles and, for many profiles and pages, newsletters that followers can subscribe to. They can build reach, but content published only on LinkedIn lives on a platform you don't control and does little for your own website in search. A sensible approach:

  1. Publish the full article on your website first
  2. Share a summary or shorter adapted version on LinkedIn, linking back to the original
  3. Use LinkedIn articles or newsletters for commentary and updates that point readers to your deeper resources

Send visitors to the right page

Many people browse LinkedIn on their phones, often inside the app's built-in browser, so the page they land on needs to load fast and make sense straight away. Match the page to the post:

  • A post about a project should link to the full case study
  • A post about a product should link to its product page, with specifications and a quote button
  • A post about an event or offer can link to a focused landing page

Each page should make the next step obvious: a short enquiry form, a WhatsApp button, a phone number or a downloadable catalogue. Manufacturers will find more on this in getting more B2B enquiries from your website.

Measure what LinkedIn brings you

  • UTM tags: add them to the links you share so Google Analytics separates profile, company page and post traffic; see UTM tags explained
  • Referral traffic: without tags, LinkedIn visits usually show up as referrals from linkedin.com or its lnkd.in short links
  • Enquiry source: add an optional "How did you hear about us?" field to your form, because some people see you on LinkedIn but later arrive by searching your name
  • LinkedIn analytics: page and post analytics show which topics get engagement; compare that with which ones bring website visits

Judge LinkedIn over months, not weeks. The value often shows up as warmer enquiries and shorter sales conversations, not just traffic numbers.

Keep it professional

  • Personalise connection requests, and don't send a sales pitch the moment someone accepts
  • Avoid automation tools that send mass messages or connection requests; LinkedIn's terms restrict them and accounts can be limited
  • Don't tag people who have nothing to do with a post just for reach
  • Only share client names, logos and photos you have permission to use
