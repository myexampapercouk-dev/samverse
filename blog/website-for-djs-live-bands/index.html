Websites for Wedding DJs, Live Bands and Performers

How wedding DJs, live bands and performers can get more bookings from a website: performance videos, clear packages, event types, availability and enquiries.

Sameer Gupta 28 September 2026 · 6 min read

Key takeaways
  • Show real performance clips organised by occasion, from sangeet and baraat to corporate nights, with a line of context under each.
  • Spell out each package, including hours, sound and lights, performers, travel and overtime, so clients can compare without calling.
  • Ask for date, venue, event type and guest count in the enquiry form, add WhatsApp, and explain how a date is confirmed.

Couples, wedding planners and corporate event managers rarely book a DJ or band on the strength of a poster and a phone number. They want to hear you, see you working a crowd and know exactly what they get for their budget, usually within a few minutes of scrolling on their phone. Instagram shows your vibe, but a website is where a serious client checks your packages, confirms you play the kind of event they're planning and sends a proper enquiry.

Here's what a website for a wedding DJ, live band or performance act needs to turn that interest into bookings.

What clients want to know before booking

Whether it's a couple planning a sangeet or an HR team organising an annual party, the questions are much the same:

  • Will you suit our crowd? Genres, languages and the kind of events you usually play
  • What do you sound like live? Real performance footage, not just studio tracks or posters
  • What's included? Hours, sound system, lights, musicians, an anchor or dhol players
  • Are you free on our date? Especially in peak wedding season
  • Can we rely on you? Punctuality, backup equipment and how you handle a mixed-age audience
  • Does it fit our budget? Even a rough idea helps

Answer these clearly and you'll get fewer "just checking" messages and more enquiries from people who are ready to book.

Show performances, not just posters

Video is your most persuasive content, so put a short highlight reel near the top of the homepage: a packed dance floor, the baraat entry, a crowd singing along with your band. Then organise clips by occasion so visitors quickly find what's relevant to them:

  • Weddings: sangeet, mehendi, haldi, baraat, cocktail and reception
  • Corporate events, award nights and product launches
  • Private parties, anniversaries and birthdays
  • College fests, clubs and public events

Host videos on a platform such as YouTube and use click-to-play previews on your site, so pages stay fast on mobile data. Clips built on commercial recordings can sometimes be muted or blocked by video platforms, so live performances and your own arrangements are usually safer to showcase; check each platform's current rules. Add a line of context under each clip: the event type, the city and roughly how big the crowd was. For more on using video well, see video on your business website.

Event types, line-up and packages

Give each main offering its own page or a clearly separate section. A couple looking for a Sufi band for their sangeet doesn't want to scroll past your club nights.

For DJs

List the genres you play (Bollywood, Punjabi, regional, EDM, retro, English pop), whether you take requests, the equipment you bring and whether you supply lighting, LED screens or special effects yourself or through a partner.

For live bands and performers

Show the line-up (vocalists and instrumentalists), your repertoire, a sample song list and the formats you offer: an acoustic duo for a cocktail evening, a full band for a reception, a dhol group for the baraat. Mention the stage space and power you need, and offer a downloadable technical rider for venues and sound vendors.

Packages

Packages make comparison easy. For each one, state:

DetailWhy it matters
Performance hours and number of setsAvoids disputes about overtime later
Sound and lighting includedClients need to know whether to hire extra equipment
Performers and crewShows exactly who will turn up
Travel and outstation termsDestination weddings are common, and costs add up
Overtime and add-onsAnchors, dhol, extra lights or a second setup for another function

Whether to publish prices is your call. "Starting from" figures filter out mismatched budgets without locking you in; see should you show prices on your website.

Availability and enquiries

In this business, the date is everything. Make the enquiry form ask for what you need to check availability and quote:

  • Event date and timings
  • City and venue, and whether it's indoors or outdoors
  • Event type and function (for example sangeet or reception)
  • Approximate guest count
  • Whether the venue or planner is providing sound and lights
  • Budget range (optional)

Add a WhatsApp button too, because many clients prefer to send a quick "are you free on the 14th?" message. If you show an availability calendar, keep it accurate: a calendar showing dates as free when you've already booked them causes more trouble than having no calendar at all.

Explain how booking works: how a date is confirmed, the advance (by UPI or bank transfer), what happens if the event is cancelled or postponed, and that you follow venue and local rules on sound levels and late-night music. Clear terms upfront prevent awkward conversations later.

Trust signals

  • Google reviews from couples, planners and corporate clients; ask soon after the event while the excitement is fresh (see how to get more Google reviews)
  • Venues, hotels and cities you've performed in, stated honestly
  • Photos of your actual setup, so clients know what they're paying for
  • Backup plans: spare equipment and a standby performer if someone falls ill
  • Team photos and short bios for the band or DJ crew
  • TV appearances, festival slots or brand collaborations, only if genuine

A page for planners and venues

Wedding planners, event companies and hotels can send you repeat work, so make their job easy. A simple "for planners" page or press kit can hold high-resolution photos, a short bio, your logo, the technical rider, the formats you offer and a direct contact. Planners building a proposal then won't have to chase you for material. To understand what planners need from their own sites, see websites for event and wedding planners.

Getting found

Clients usually search with an occasion and a city: "wedding DJ in Jaipur", "live band for sangeet in Delhi", "Sufi band in Mumbai", "DJ for corporate event in Bengaluru". To appear for these:

  • Create pages for the event types and genres you're genuinely known for, with real clips and details
  • Mention the cities you're based in and regularly travel to, naturally, rather than creating dozens of copy-paste city pages
  • Set up a Google Business Profile (as a service-area business if clients don't visit you at an address) and add event photos and reviews
  • Link your Instagram and YouTube to the matching pages on your website
  • Keep pages light so they load quickly for someone tapping the link in your Instagram bio on mobile data
