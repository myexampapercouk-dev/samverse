Websites for Eye Clinics and Opticians: Keeping Clinic Services and the Optical Store Apart, Precise Doctor Credentials, Lens Guides and Appointment Booking

Plan an eye clinic and optician website: clinic and surgery pages under medical advertising rules, a frames and lens store, prescriptions and booking.

Key takeaways
  • Treat the clinic and the optical store as two front doors on one site: careful, factual medical pages on one side, and a catalogue that sells like a shop on the other.
  • State doctor and optometrist credentials precisely, explain tests and surgery in plain language without promised outcomes, and check current medical advertising guidance before launch.
  • Explain lens options without upselling, handle prescriptions with care, offer separate appointment types for each service, and design for visitors who cannot see well.

An eye clinic with an optical counter is really two businesses sharing a name. One is a medical practice, where patients need careful, factual information about eye tests, treatment and surgery. The other is a shop, where customers compare frames, lenses and prices. The website works best when it keeps them apart, with each side written in its own tone, and joins them with one booking system and one clear brand.

The clinic side follows the same approach as my websites for doctors and clinics: doctor profiles with verifiable qualifications, treatment pages, appointment enquiries, WhatsApp, Google Maps and local SEO. If you want to sell frames, sunglasses or contact lens solutions online, a WooCommerce store handles the catalogue, payments and delivery on the same site.

One site, two front doors

Patients worried about blurred vision and shoppers looking for new sunglasses should not have to wade through each other's pages. Put two clear routes on the homepage, such as "See an eye doctor" and "Glasses, lenses and eye tests", and plan each side separately:

Clinic sideOptical side
Who it is forPatients with symptoms, referrals, people considering surgeryPeople needing glasses, contact lenses or sunglasses
Main pagesDoctors, conditions, tests, treatments and surgery, insuranceFrames, lenses, contact lenses, eye tests for glasses, offers
ToneFactual, calm, no promotional languageFriendly retail, with prices and photos
BookingConsultations and follow-upsEye tests, frame selection, contact lens fittings
Rules to watchProfessional conduct guidance for doctorsConsumer rules on pricing, offers and returns

If the clinic and the store are separate legal entities, or the store also sells to customers of other doctors, say so plainly. Patients should never feel they must buy glasses where they were examined.

The clinic side

Eye tests and check-ups

"Eye test" means different things to different people. Explain what each test at your practice involves and who performs it: a full eye examination by an ophthalmologist, a vision and refraction test by an optometrist, children's eye checks, eye checks for people with diabetes, or pressure checks for glaucoma. For each, say roughly how long it takes as you run it, and what to bring, such as current glasses and a list of medicines. If drops may be used to dilate the pupils, warn patients that their vision may be blurred for a while afterwards and that they should follow the doctor's advice on driving.

Treatment and surgery pages

Cataract surgery, refractive surgery such as LASIK, glaucoma care, retina services, squint and paediatric eye care each deserve a page. A useful page covers who the treatment is for, how suitability is assessed, what happens on the day, recovery as your doctors describe it, the general risks, and follow-up visits.

Cost is a real question, especially for cataract surgery, where the choice of lens changes the price. Explain the lens types you offer in plain terms and what each is meant to do, and list insurers or cashless arrangements only if you genuinely work with them, with a note to confirm cover with the insurer. Avoid outcome promises such as "freedom from glasses" or "painless surgery", and avoid before and after claims. Medical practitioners in India work under professional conduct rules that limit self-promotion, and that guidance has changed more than once recently, so read the National Medical Commission's current version before launch.

Credentials for every role, stated precisely

"Experienced eye surgeon" says little. Degrees with the university and year, fellowship training, the registration number and named professional memberships can all be checked. The Dr. Nitish Gupta site I built for a pulmonologist in Agra opens with his degrees, each with the university and year, a former teaching role and his memberships of named chest societies; the same approach suits ophthalmologists.

Eye care adds a complication: patients meet several kinds of professional, and everyday speech calls all of them "eye specialists". Give each person a profile that names their actual role:

RoleWhat they do at your practiceWhat their profile should state
OphthalmologistMedical doctor who diagnoses and treats eye disease and performs surgeryMedical degree and postgraduate qualification in ophthalmology with university and year, fellowships, registration number, procedures performed
OptometristTests vision, prescribes glasses and contact lenses, and refers patients who need a doctorOptometry qualification and institution, and the tests they carry out
OpticianHelps choose frames and lenses, takes measurements, fits and adjusts glassesTheir training and the services they handle at the counter

Registration arrangements for optometrists in India have been changing, so list a registration only where the person actually holds one. Websites for specialist doctors covers credentials, procedure pages and second opinions in more depth.

The optical side

Frames and sunglasses

Customers browse frames by shape, colour, material and price, so the catalogue should let them filter on those. For each frame, show several angles, the frame measurements printed on the inside of the arm (lens width, bridge and temple length), the material and the price. Say whether a frame is in stock at the store or needs ordering. Name the brands you stock, and call yourself an authorised retailer for a brand only if you are one. WooCommerce product page optimisation covers photos and layouts that help people choose.

Lenses, explained without upselling

Lens choices confuse most customers, and confusion often ends in either a cheap choice they regret or an expensive one they did not need. A short guide helps:

  • Single vision: one prescription across the lens, for distance or reading
  • Bifocal and progressive: for people who need help with both near and far, with progressives giving a gradual change and needing a short adjustment period
  • Thinner high-index lenses: for stronger prescriptions, where standard lenses would be thick
  • Coatings: anti-glare, scratch-resistant and blue light filtering, described by what the coating does rather than health claims you cannot support
  • Photochromic lenses: that darken outdoors, with a note on how they behave inside a car

Explain what affects the price of each and let customers see the options side by side before they come in.

Prescriptions, try-ons and online orders

If customers can order glasses online, they need to give a current prescription and, for many frames, a pupillary distance. A prescription upload at checkout works, but prescriptions are health information, so collect them through the order system rather than an open contact form, keep only what you need, and add a consent line. Contact lenses should be supplied against a valid prescription; check the current rules on selling them online before you add them to the store.

Many customers still want to try frames on. A "reserve to try in store" button that holds a few frames for a day, or a home try-on service if you genuinely run one, is often more useful than a virtual try-on tool, which can be slow on phones and is only a rough guide to fit.

One booking system, several appointment types

Patients booking a consultation for flashes and floaters and a customer booking a frame fitting need different slots. Offer separate appointment types, each with its own duration and staff:

  1. Doctor consultation, new or follow-up
  2. Eye test for glasses with an optometrist
  3. Contact lens fitting and training
  4. Frame and lens selection with an optician
  5. Post-surgery check-up

Keep forms short: name, phone, appointment type and preferred time. Do not ask for symptoms or medical history in an open form; the doctor can take those at the visit. The clinic website checklist for doctors lists other details patients expect, from timings to maps.

A site people with poor eyesight can use

An eye clinic's visitors include people with cataracts, glaucoma and recent surgery, and many are older. The design has to work for them:

  • Large body text and strong contrast between text and background
  • Pages that still work when zoomed in, without text running off the screen
  • No important information inside images, such as timings in a poster
  • Large buttons for calling and booking, and a phone number that is always easy to find
  • Plain words, short sentences and headings that describe what follows

Making your website easy for older visitors goes through these in detail. Hearing care practices face a very similar mix of clinical testing and device sales; websites for hearing aid centres handles it from that side.

Local search for both halves

People search for each half separately: "eye hospital in Kothrud", "cataract surgery in Pune", "optical shop near me", "eye test near me". Give each its own pages and headings in those words. Whether the clinic and the store need separate Google Business Profiles depends on how they are set up in reality, such as separate entrances, hours and phone numbers, so follow Google's current guidelines rather than creating extra listings for the sake of it. Structured data can then describe the clinic and the optical store separately, each with its own hours.

FAQs

Can an eye clinic website show surgery prices?

It can show price ranges and explain what changes them, such as the type of lens in cataract surgery, as long as the information is factual and does not promise outcomes. Check the current professional guidance for doctors before publishing.

Can customers order glasses online with their prescription?

Yes. A WooCommerce store can collect the prescription and measurements at checkout and keep them with that order, so your team can pass the complete job to the lab.

Should the clinic and the optical store share one website?

Usually yes, with two clearly separate sections and one booking system, so patients and shoppers each find their own route. Separate sites make sense when the two are run as different businesses.

What should a doctor profile on an eye clinic website include?

Degrees with university and year, fellowships, registration number, professional memberships and the treatments that doctor performs, written factually, without words such as "best" or "top".

Can patients book an eye test online?

Yes. Offer an eye test as its own appointment type with the right duration, and send a confirmation and a reminder before the visit by WhatsApp or email.
