Search Intent Explained: The Types of Intent, How to Read Them From Google and How to Match Your Pages

Search intent explained with examples: informational, navigational, commercial, transactional and local, how to read it from Google and match your pages.

Search intent is the goal behind a search: to learn something, reach a particular website, compare options, buy or hire, or find somewhere nearby. Google ranks the type of page that best satisfies that goal, so a page that doesn't match it rarely ranks well, however good the writing. The quickest way to judge intent is to search the phrase yourself and look at what kind of pages Google already shows.

Matching each search to the right page is where on-page work starts in my WordPress SEO services: each target search is mapped to one page, so the right page type answers it and no two pages compete for the same phrase. This guide shows how to do that yourself.

Intent is the step after finding the words people use; for that part, see keyword research for small businesses.

The main types of search intent

NavigationalA specific website or page"razorpay login", "samverse contact"That brand's own page
Commercial investigationTo compare options before deciding"wordpress vs wix", "best payment gateway for small business india"Comparisons, buying guides, reviews
TransactionalTo buy, book or hire now"hire wordpress developer", "generator on rent in bangalore"Service pages, product and category pages

Local intent overlaps with the others: "AC repair in Kothrud" is both local and transactional. Google's own guidelines for its search quality raters use different labels (Know, Do, Website and Visit-in-person), which shows that the names matter less than the question underneath: what kind of page would satisfy this person?

How to read intent from Google's results

Google has already tested what satisfies people for most searches, so its results are the best evidence you have. Search the phrase in a private window, ideally on your phone and in the city your customers are in, since results for local searches change with location. Then look at:

Mostly guides and articlesInformational intentA thorough guide that answers the question early

Look at who ranks, too. If the first page is all large marketplaces and national directories, a small business may do better with a more specific version of the search; long-tail keywords explained covers how to find those.

Words that signal intent

  • how, what, why, guide, ideas, checklist: usually informational.
  • best, vs, review, top, alternatives, comparison: commercial investigation.
  • price, cost, charges, quote, packages: somewhere between comparing and buying. In India, "charges" is a common wording, as in "website maintenance charges".
  • hire, buy, book, on rent, near me, in plus a city or area: transactional or local.
  • a brand name plus login, contact or address: navigational.

These are clues, not rules. Always confirm by checking the results.

Mixed and shifting intent

Some searches serve more than one goal, and Google shows a mix of page types. "wordpress malware removal" is a good example: some searchers want to clean the site themselves, others want someone to do it now. This site, which is custom-coded rather than built on WordPress, answers it with two separate pages, a step-by-step cleanup guide for the do-it-yourself reader and a service page for people who want it fixed, each titled clearly for its own audience, so Google can choose the right one for each searcher instead of one page trying to be both.

Intent also changes. Festival and seasonal searches swing towards offers and buying in the weeks before the event, new tools turn "what is" searches into "how to" searches, and Google sometimes changes which page types it prefers for a phrase. Re-check the results before you update an older page, not only before you write a new one.

How to match a page to intent

Matching intent comes down to five decisions:

  1. Page type. On WordPress, services are usually Pages, guides are Posts, and "buy" searches are best served by WooCommerce category or product pages.
  2. Format. Steps for a how-to, a table for a comparison, the factors that affect price for a cost search, address, hours and a map for a local search.
  3. Angle. Beginner or expert, and whether the searcher expects Indian specifics such as UPI, GST or prices in rupees.
  4. The opening. The title, heading and first paragraph should confirm in seconds that the searcher is in the right place, and answer the main question early.
  5. The next step. Readers of a guide get a soft link to a related service; visitors on a service page get a prominent form, call and WhatsApp button.

On this site, Search Console shows the split working as intended. For "wordpress speed optimisation service", Google shows the WordPress speed optimisation service page; for "wordpress site slow how to fix", it shows the guide why is my WordPress site slow. Same topic, two intents, two pages. For transactional searches, how to write service pages that rank and convert covers what the service page itself needs.

Common intent mistakes

  • A sales page for a "how to" search. People who want to learn bounce off a page that only sells.
  • A blog post for a "hire" or "near me" search. These searchers want a service page or a Business Profile, not an essay.
  • One page trying to serve every intent, part guide, part sales pitch and part comparison, which ends up satisfying nobody.
  • Two pages chasing the same intent, such as a service page and a blog post both targeting "website maintenance charges", so they compete with each other. Topic clusters and pillar pages explains how to avoid this keyword cannibalisation.
  • Ignoring the format Google prefers, such as writing a long essay when the results show quick answers, calculators or product grids.
  • Local pages with nothing local on them, just a city name swapped into the same text.

Connect intents with internal links

People rarely move from first question to enquiry in one visit. Someone who reads a guide on why their site is slow may be ready to hire help a week later. Link informational articles to the related service page for readers who are ready, link service pages to guides for visitors still researching, and let comparison pages link to both. That path also shows Google how your pages relate; see internal linking explained. For local businesses, the Business Profile and the local service page work as a pair; local SEO for small businesses in India covers that side.

A quick intent check before you write any page

  1. Write down the main search phrase the page is for.
  2. Search it on your phone, in a private window, from the area your customers are in.
  3. Note the page types and features in the top results.
  4. Choose the page type and format that match, or a more specific phrase if big sites own the results.
  5. Check you don't already have a page answering the same intent.
  6. Write the title, heading and first paragraph to confirm the match.
  7. A few weeks after publishing, check which searches the page actually appears for in Search Console. If they differ from what you intended, adjust the page or create a separate one.

FAQs

What is search intent in SEO?

It is the goal behind a search: learning, finding a specific site, comparing, buying or hiring, or finding something nearby. Google ranks pages that satisfy that goal, so matching intent comes before keywords, length or any other optimisation.

What are the four types of search intent?

Informational, navigational, commercial investigation and transactional. Many guides add local intent as a fifth, since searches like "near me" or "in Pune" trigger a map and local results.

How do I find the search intent of a keyword?

Search it and study the results: the kinds of pages that rank, the features Google shows such as a map, shopping results or People Also Ask, and the words in the ranking titles. Keyword tools sometimes label intent too, but the live results are more reliable.

Can one page target more than one intent?

Closely related intents can share a page, such as a cost guide that also explains what's included. Very different intents, such as learning to do a job yourself and hiring someone to do it, are better served by separate pages linked to each other.

Does search intent matter for local businesses?

Very much. Most local searches are transactional, so they need a clear service page with your area, contact options and proof, plus a well-kept Google Business Profile, rather than blog posts.
