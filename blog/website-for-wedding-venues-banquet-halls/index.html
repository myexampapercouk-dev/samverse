Websites for Wedding Venues and Banquet Halls: Halls and Lawns With Capacities, Packages, Date Enquiries, Virtual Tours and the Path to a Site Visit

Plan a wedding venue or banquet hall website: halls and lawns with real capacities, per-plate packages, date checks, tours and a path to a site visit.

Key takeaways
  • Give every hall, lawn and terrace its own page with capacities stated by layout, because "up to 1,000 guests" means nothing until families know whether that is seated or standing.
  • Explain packages by what they include and what costs extra, and let families check a date quickly without pretending to show a live calendar you do not keep.
  • Treat the site visit as the goal: real photos and video of each space, partner and policy details, then a fast route from enquiry to a booked visit.

Families shortlisting a wedding venue or banquet hall want four answers before they will visit: is our date free, will our guest list fit, roughly what will it cost per plate, and does the place look as good as the photos. A venue website that answers those clearly, space by space, and then makes booking a site visit easy, wins visits that a listing on a wedding portal alone does not.

Venues sit close to hospitality, and much of the build mirrors the hotel and resort websites I design: large photo and video galleries, amenities and policies, rooms for guests, booking enquiries and WhatsApp, and a properly linked Google Business Profile. During the wedding season, when you advertise, a dedicated landing page for each campaign, such as a lawn package or a weekday corporate offer, turns ad clicks into enquiries better than a general homepage.

One page for every hall, lawn and terrace

Many venue sites show a single gallery mixing every space, which leaves families unsure what they would actually be booking. Give each space its own page with its own photos, capacities, features and a "check this space for my date" button. Families compare spaces within your venue as well as against other venues, and separate pages make that easy.

State capacity by layout, not as one number

"Capacity 800" causes more arguments than almost any other line on a venue website, because the figure changes completely with the layout. State it the way families plan:

Capacity to stateWhat it meansWhy families ask
Floating or standingGuests moving around, with some seating along the sidesReceptions and cocktail evenings
Seated, theatre styleRows of chairs facing a stageSangeet performances, ceremonies, corporate events
Seated at round tablesGuests seated for a served or buffet mealFormal dinners and receptions
Dining at one sittingHow many can eat at the buffet area togetherLarge weddings where dinner runs in batches
With mandap and stageCapacity once the mandap, stage and buffet counters are set upWedding ceremonies held in the same space

Add the floor area, ceiling height for decorators, whether the space is air-conditioned, and whether two spaces can be combined. A simple floor plan with entrances, stage position and buffet area helps decorators and planners as much as families.

Packages: what is included, what is extra

Price is usually the second question, and the hardest to answer on a website because menus, dates and guest numbers all change it. You do not need a full rate card, but each package page should make the structure clear:

  • Venue-only or package: whether you rent the space alone, or only with your catering
  • Per-plate menus: vegetarian, non-vegetarian and Jain options, with sample menus for each tier as downloads
  • Minimum guarantee: the minimum number of plates charged for each space and slot
  • Included: basic decor, stage, lighting, sound, changing rooms, rooms for the family, valet, generator backup, as applies
  • Extra: decor upgrades, extra hours, additional rooms, outside vendor fees, and taxes
  • Slots: lunch and dinner timings, and the hours included in each

"Starting from" per-plate prices for each menu tier filter out enquiries that will never fit the budget and make serious families more likely to visit. Whether to publish them at all is weighed up in the guide to showing prices.

Date availability without pretending

A live availability calendar sounds ideal, but it only helps if someone updates it the moment a date is booked or released. A calendar that shows a date free when it is not will cost you more goodwill than having no calendar at all. For most venues, a quick date check works better:

  1. Event date, with a box for alternative dates
  2. Function type: wedding, reception, sangeet, engagement, birthday, corporate
  3. Lunch or dinner slot
  4. Expected guests, as a range
  5. Space of interest, or "suggest one for us"
  6. Menu preference: vegetarian, non-vegetarian or Jain
  7. Name, phone and WhatsApp consent

Reply with availability, package options and an invitation to visit. Explain your hold policy on the site too: whether a date can be held tentatively, for how long, and what advance confirms it. On the most popular dates of the season, families appreciate knowing the rules before they ask.

Photos, video and virtual tours

Families judge venues on photos first, so they need to be honest as well as attractive:

  • Photograph each space empty, in daylight, so families can see its real size and condition
  • Add photos of the same space decorated for different functions and at night, crediting the decorator and photographer with their permission
  • Show the less glamorous parts too: the entrance, parking, washrooms, changing rooms and the dining area
  • A short walk-through video of each space, from the gate to the stage, answers more questions than twenty photos
  • A 360-degree tour helps out-of-town families, as long as it loads quickly on a phone

Drone footage is popular, but drone flights need the registrations and permissions the current rules require, and some areas are restricted, so check before commissioning it. Keep videos on YouTube or a similar host and embed them so they do not slow the page; video on your business website explains when video helps. The same photos should go on your Google Business Profile, which Google Business Profile photos covers in detail.

Catering and decor partners

Families need to know early whether they can bring their own caterer or decorator, because it changes their whole budget. State your policy plainly:

  • In-house catering only, an approved list of caterers, or outside caterers allowed with a fee
  • The same for decorators, and any rules on fire, flowers, nails or tape on walls
  • Whether tasting sessions are offered before booking, and how they are arranged
  • Approved partners for photography, DJ, mehendi and makeup, if you keep a list

A partner page with each approved vendor's name, what they offer and a link to their site helps families and builds goodwill with vendors who refer clients to you. Caterers and decorators who work with venues have their own needs, described in websites for catering services and websites for wedding decorators.

Parking, rooms and the practical details

Practical details decide as many bookings as the decor does. Put them on one page and link it from every space:

  • Parking for cars and two-wheelers, valet service, and space for buses carrying guests
  • Rooms for the families, a bridal room, and nearby hotels for out-of-town guests
  • Power backup that covers air conditioning as well as lighting
  • Lift, ramps and accessible washrooms for elderly guests
  • Music timings: local rules restrict amplified music late at night, so state the hours that apply to your venue
  • Alcohol: whether it is allowed and how permits are arranged, since rules differ by state and event
  • Location with landmarks, a map pin and directions for baraat processions if you allow them

From enquiry to site visit to booking

The site visit is where most venues are booked. Make the path to it short and predictable:

  1. A family checks a date or sends a WhatsApp message from any page
  2. Your team replies with availability, package options and the sample menus
  3. The family picks a site visit slot, ideally at the time of day their event will happen
  4. The visit covers the space, parking, rooms and a menu discussion, with a tasting offered where you do them
  5. A written quote follows the same day, with the hold policy and payment schedule
  6. An advance confirms the date, and a written agreement sets out inclusions, timings and cancellation terms

Speed matters, because families enquire with several venues at once and often visit the first that replies. Responding to website enquiries fast covers alerts and auto-replies that help.

Local search for venues

Families search for "banquet hall in [area]", "wedding lawn near me" or "party hall for 300 guests". Use the area and city in page titles and headings where they are true, keep the venue's name, address and phone consistent everywhere, choose accurate Google Business Profile categories, and reply to reviews, including the critical ones. Wedding listing portals bring enquiries too; your own site is where those families come to check details before they visit.

FAQs

Should a banquet hall show per-plate prices online?

"Starting from" prices for each menu tier help families shortlist and reduce enquiries that will never fit the budget. Full quotes can follow the date check.

Is a live availability calendar a good idea?

Only if your team updates it as soon as dates are booked or released. Otherwise a quick date check form with a fast reply is more reliable.

How should capacity be shown?

By layout: floating, theatre-style seating, seating at round tables and dining at one sitting, plus capacity once a mandap and stage are set up.

Can families pay the booking advance online?

Yes. A payment link or gateway can take an advance by UPI, card or net banking, with the receipt showing the balance due and cancellation terms.

Do we need a separate page for each hall and lawn?

Yes. Each space has its own capacities, photos and best uses, and families compare spaces within a venue as well as between venues.
