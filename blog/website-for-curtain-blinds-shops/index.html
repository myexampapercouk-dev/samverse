Websites for Curtain and Blinds Shops: Swatches, Measuring and Installation

What curtain and blinds shops need online: real swatch photos, measuring and installation details, motorised blinds, sample requests and trade enquiries.

Key takeaways
  • Show real swatch photos in daylight and against the light, so customers can compare texture, colour and opacity.
  • Explain your measuring and installation process, motorised options and sample policy clearly.
  • Ask for window sizes and photos in a short form, and add a separate trade page for interior designers and builders.

Curtains and blinds are bought by touch and by measurement. Customers want to feel the fabric, see how much light it blocks and be sure it will fit their windows, which is why most curtain shops sell through a showroom or home visit rather than a shopping cart. Your website's job is to help people reach the right shortlist and then book that visit. This guide is for specialist curtain, blinds and soft-furnishing shops; if you run a broader decor store, see websites for home decor and furnishing stores.

What customers want to know before they call

  • Which products you offer: curtains, sheers, blackout options, roller, zebra, roman, Venetian and vertical blinds, and tracks and rods
  • Whether you measure and install in their area
  • How long it takes from measurement to installation, and what affects that
  • How they can see fabrics before deciding
  • Whether you also make cushion covers, bed linen and upholstery, or supply wallpaper

Give each main product its own page, with photos, the rooms it works best in and a clear button to book a measurement visit or request samples.

Fabric catalogues with real swatch photos

A fabric catalogue is the heart of a curtain shop's website, and real photos are what make it useful. Supplier images are often edited or show the fabric in a staged room; your own photos, taken in daylight, show what customers will actually get. If you do want to use supplier images, check you have permission first.

  • A flat swatch photo and a close-up showing weave and texture
  • A photo with light behind the fabric, so customers can compare sheer, dim-out and blackout options
  • Composition, fabric width, care instructions and whether it suits sun-facing windows
  • Filters by colour, opacity, pattern and room
  • An honest note that colours vary between screens and that a physical sample is the best way to judge

Most curtain work is made to measure, so a catalogue with "request sample" and "get a quote" buttons usually works better than fixed-price products. If you also sell ready-made curtains, set sizes and colours up as variations of one product; see WooCommerce product variations.

Helping customers visualise their options

Many customers don't know the difference between blind types. A simple comparison helps them arrive with a clearer idea of what they want:

OptionOften chosen forWorth explaining
Roller blindsA clean look in bedrooms, studies and officesAvailable in different opacities, from light-filtering to blackout
Zebra blindsAdjustable light in living roomsAlternating sheer and solid bands that can be lined up or offset
Roman blindsA soft, fabric lookThey fold up in pleats, so fabric choice matters
Venetian blindsControl over light and privacySlats tilt; aluminium and wooden versions suit different rooms
Vertical blindsWide windows and sliding doorsCommon in offices and large openings

Back this up with galleries of completed installations sorted by room: living rooms, bedrooms, kids' rooms, offices and cafés. Room-visualiser tools exist, but for a local shop a well-organised gallery and an invitation to send window photos on WhatsApp for suggestions often does the job.

Motorised blinds and curtains

Motorised blinds and curtain tracks interest people with tall windows, large glass walls or smart homes, and they raise questions a normal product page doesn't answer. Give them their own page explaining:

  • Control options such as remotes, wall switches and apps, and whether a system works with smart home assistants (always confirm with the manufacturer)
  • Wired versus battery or rechargeable motors, and the need for a power point near wired installations
  • Why it helps to plan wiring before painting or false ceiling work
  • A short demo video from your showroom, set up so it doesn't slow the page

Builders and interior designers specifying motorised systems want technical detail, so offer product sheets on request rather than overloading the page.

Your measuring and installation service

Explain the whole process so customers know what to expect:

  1. Book a visit: pick a date and share the address and number of windows
  2. Measure and advise: your team measures, checks the wall or ceiling for fixing, and brings sample books
  3. Quotation: itemised by window, covering fabric, stitching style, tracks or brackets and installation
  4. Making: stitching or fabrication, with timelines that depend on fabric availability and order size
  5. Installation: fitting, finishing and a quick demo for blinds and motorised systems

List the areas you cover, whether the measurement visit is free or adjusted against the order, and what's included, such as removing old curtains or fixing tracks. If some customers prefer to measure themselves, a simple measuring guide with diagrams reduces mistakes.

Sample requests and enquiry forms that ask the right questions

A sample request form lets customers pick a few swatches to see at home or collect from the shop. State clearly whether samples are free, chargeable or adjusted against an order.

For quotes, ask only for what you actually need, and keep most fields optional:

  • Number of windows and the rough width and height of each
  • Room type and product interest, such as blackout curtains or zebra blinds
  • Photos of the windows, uploaded or sent on WhatsApp
  • Area or pin code and a preferred visit date

A multi-step form can collect all this without feeling long; see multi-step forms for lead qualification.

Serving interior designers and builders

Interior designers, architects and builders can bring repeat, multi-window orders. A trade page should cover the project types you handle (apartments, villas, offices, hotels), your capacity for large orders, sample books for designers, coordination with site timelines and GST invoicing. Add a separate project enquiry form so trade leads don't get mixed up with retail ones. Designers judge suppliers by finish, so show completed projects with their permission; see websites for interior designers and architects for what they show their own clients.

Local SEO and speed

  • Target searches like "curtain shop in {city}", "blinds in {area}", "motorised blinds {city}" and "blackout curtains near me"
  • Keep your Google Business Profile updated with showroom photos, opening hours and recent installations
  • Write your own fabric and product descriptions instead of copying supplier text
  • Compress swatch and gallery images so catalogue pages load quickly on mobile
