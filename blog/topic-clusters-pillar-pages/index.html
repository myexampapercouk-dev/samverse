Topic Clusters and Pillar Pages: Organise Your Content to Rank

How topic clusters work: a pillar page (often a service page) supported by related articles linked together, why it helps SEO and users, and how to plan clusters for a small business site.

Key takeaways
  • A pillar page (often a service page) is supported by detailed articles linked to it.
  • Clusters build topical depth, pass authority and create better reader journeys.
  • Plan clusters from customer questions, link deliberately and avoid overlapping pages.

Publishing random blog posts rarely builds rankings. Organising content into topic clusters, where related pages support and link to each other, shows search engines and visitors that your site covers a subject in depth.

What a topic cluster is

  • Pillar page: a broad, central page on a topic. For businesses, this is often a service page
  • Cluster content: articles covering specific questions and subtopics in detail
  • Links: cluster articles link to the pillar, and the pillar links to the articles

An example

On this site, the WordPress speed optimisation service page acts as a pillar. Supporting articles such as why your site is slow, Core Web Vitals explained and caching explained link back to it and to each other.

Why clusters help

  • Topical depth: covering a subject thoroughly builds relevance
  • Internal links: pass authority to your most important pages
  • Better user journeys: readers find related answers easily and move towards enquiring
  • Less overlap: each page has a clear, distinct job

How to plan clusters

  1. Pick pillars: your main services or product categories
  2. List questions: what customers ask before buying each service; see keyword research
  3. Group them: each question becomes an article, or a section of the pillar if it's short
  4. Link deliberately: every article links to its pillar with descriptive anchor text
  5. Fill gaps over time with a content calendar; see content calendar

Avoid cannibalisation

If two pages target the same keyword, they compete with each other. Give each page a distinct focus, and merge or redirect overlapping content.

Show clusters in your navigation

Category or topic pages that group related articles help visitors and crawlers see the structure. This blog groups articles into topics like speed, security and maintenance.

Quality over quantity

A cluster of genuinely useful pages beats dozens of thin posts. Write each article to fully answer its question.
