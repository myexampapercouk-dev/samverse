Topic Clusters and Pillar Pages Explained: How to Plan Them and Build Them in WordPress

Pillar page WordPress guide: what a pillar page is, how to plan a topic cluster, and how to build pillar and cluster pages with links, blocks and menus.

Key takeaways
  • In WordPress, build the pillar as a page, usually the service page you want to rank, and the cluster articles as posts that link to it in the body text.
  • Plan the cluster from real searches first, give each article one question, and avoid two pages targeting the same query.
  • Contextual links do most of the work; categories, breadcrumbs and a table of contents on the pillar support them.

A pillar page in WordPress is a broad page, usually built as a WordPress Page rather than a post, that covers a topic as a whole and links out to detailed blog posts, each answering one specific question, with every post linking back to it. For most business sites the pillar is the service page you want to rank, and the posts form the topic cluster around it.

Organised this way, your content shows visitors and search engines that you cover the subject properly, instead of scattering unrelated posts.

Site structure and internal linking are part of my WordPress SEO services, alongside titles, schema and Search Console. This guide explains how to plan a cluster yourself and build it in WordPress.

Clusters depend on two other skills: choosing what to write about, covered in keyword research for small businesses, and linking pages well, covered in internal linking explained.

What is a pillar page?

A pillar page is the central page for a topic. It gives a complete overview, answers the main question directly, and hands readers on to detailed articles for everything it only summarises. There are three common types:

  • A service page as the pillar. For most businesses this is the right choice, because it's the page you most want to rank and the one that turns visitors into enquiries.
  • A long guide. A thorough guide to a topic, useful when the subject is informational, such as a guide to choosing a solar system for a home.
  • A hub page. A curated page that introduces a topic and organises links to every related article, often built on top of a category.

A good pillar isn't simply the longest page on your site. It covers each subtopic in a paragraph or two, links to the article that covers it fully, and is kept up to date as articles are added.

Why topic clusters help SEO and visitors

  • Coverage: a set of linked pages answering the questions around a subject helps search engines understand what your site is about and which page is the main one.
  • Internal links: links from related articles help Google find and understand your most important pages, and pass some of the value those articles earn.
  • Clear page roles: when each page has one job, your pages stop competing with each other for the same searches.
  • Better journeys: someone who arrives on an article about one problem can move to the service page that solves it, which is where enquiries come from.

Clusters aren't a trick. They're a way of organising content that's genuinely useful; a cluster of thin posts helps nobody.

How to plan a topic cluster

  1. Pick your pillars. Start with your main services or product categories. A small business might have three to six.
  2. List the questions customers ask before buying: on calls, on WhatsApp, in emails and in sales meetings. Add queries from Search Console and the "People also ask" boxes in Google.
  3. Decide article or section. If a question can be answered in a paragraph, it belongs on the pillar. If it needs its own explanation and people search for it on its own, it deserves an article.
  4. Give every page one main question and write it down, so two pages never target the same thing.
  5. Link deliberately: each article links to the pillar with descriptive anchor text, and the pillar links to each article where its subtopic comes up.
  6. Fill the gaps over time, starting with the questions closest to a buying decision; a simple content calendar keeps it going.

How to build topic clusters in WordPress

Pillars as pages, clusters as posts

Make the pillar a page and the cluster articles posts in a matching category. You don't need nested URLs such as /solar/net-metering/; the links between pages matter far more than folders in the address, and changing existing URLs to create folders risks losing rankings.

Contextual links do most of the work

Add links inside the body text, where the subtopic comes up, with anchor text that describes the destination. On the pillar, a short "Guides on this topic" section listing every cluster article helps too. Related-post widgets are a bonus, but they pick links automatically and don't replace deliberate ones. Some SEO plugins, including Rank Math and Yoast, offer internal link suggestions, although at the time of writing these may depend on the plan you use.

Categories, tags and breadcrumbs

A category archive can act as a hub if you add a proper introduction, which most themes show from the category description. Keep tags under control; dozens of tags with one or two posts each create thin archive pages. Turn on breadcrumbs in your SEO plugin, so every article shows its place in the structure and Google gets breadcrumb structured data.

Pillar page WordPress setup: step by step

  1. Choose the topic and the page. Pick one service or subject you want to rank for and decide whether an existing page becomes the pillar.
  2. Create it as a Page. Pages sit outside the blog date archive and can use a clean URL such as /service-name/.
  3. Write the overview. Answer the main question at the top, then give each subtopic a short H2 section with a summary.
  4. Add a table of contents. A simple list of jump links helps long pillars on mobile.
  5. Link each section to its cluster post. Use descriptive anchor text in the sentence, not a bare "read more".
  6. Link every post back. Each cluster post links to the pillar early in the body, plus to one or two sibling posts where it helps.
  7. Group the posts. One category per cluster keeps archives and breadcrumbs tidy.
  8. Add the pillar to the menu. Main navigation links tell visitors and Google it is an important page.
  9. Keep it fast. Long pillars with many images need compressed images and lazy loading below the first screen.

Avoid keyword cannibalisation

If two of your pages target the same search, they compete, and often neither ranks well. In Search Console's Performance report, click a query and open the Pages tab: if two pages keep swapping places for it, they overlap. Fix it by merging the weaker page into the stronger one and redirecting it, or by rewriting each so it answers a clearly different question, then pointing your internal links at the right page. Common mistakes

  • A pillar that is just a list of links with no content of its own
  • Cluster articles that never link back to the pillar or the service page
  • Publishing dozens of posts while the service pages themselves stay thin; see ranking a service business without a blog
  • Writing about topics your customers don't search for or that don't lead to anything you sell
  • Orphan posts with no internal links pointing to them

FAQs

How do I create a pillar page in WordPress?

Create a WordPress Page for the broad topic, summarise each subtopic in its own section, link each section to the detailed blog post, and make sure every post links back. Add the pillar to your main menu so it gets the most internal links.

What is the difference between a pillar page and a cluster page?

The pillar covers the whole topic at overview level and links out. A cluster page answers one specific question in depth and links back to the pillar.

How long should a pillar page be?

Long enough to cover every subtopic briefly and answer the main question properly; there's no target word count. A focused service page with clear sections and links to each article can work better than a very long page.

Should a pillar be a page or a post in WordPress?

Usually a page, because it's evergreen and sits in your main structure. Long guides sometimes work as posts, which is fine as long as they're linked prominently and kept updated.

Do topic clusters still work for SEO?

Yes, because they're simply good organisation: related pages that answer real questions and link sensibly. They don't work as a shortcut for thin content.

How many articles does a topic cluster need?

There's no fixed number. Start with the few questions customers ask most before buying and add more as you find new questions in enquiries and Search Console.
