How to Start an Email Newsletter for Your Small Business: Tools, Content and Sign-Ups

How to start an email newsletter for a small business in India: picking a tool, growing sign-ups on your website, what to send, deliverability and consent.

Key takeaways
  • An email list is a channel you own; a useful email once a month to people who asked for it can bring back past customers and move undecided ones to enquire.
  • Send from a proper email marketing tool on your own domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up, never from a personal Gmail or Outlook inbox.
  • Grow the list with clear sign-up forms on your website, consent you can show, and content that answers customers' real questions rather than pure promotion.

To start an email newsletter for a small business: choose an email marketing tool, connect it to a sign-up form on your website, set up your domain so emails reach inboxes, and send one genuinely useful email on a fixed schedule, usually monthly. That is the whole system. The rest of this guide covers each step for Indian small businesses, with what to send and what to avoid.

A newsletter is one of the few marketing channels you actually own. Social reach rises and falls with each platform's algorithm, but a list of people who asked to hear from you stays with you. For attracting sign-ups with a free offer, read lead magnets and newsletter sign-ups; this guide is about starting and running the newsletter itself.

Is a newsletter right for your business?

A newsletter works best when customers take time to decide, buy more than once, or benefit from regular advice. Good fits include:

  • B2B suppliers, manufacturers and distributors with long buying cycles.
  • CAs, consultants, coaches and agencies whose expertise is what they sell.
  • Clinics and wellness businesses with seasonal reminders.
  • Online stores and D2C brands with launches, restocks and repeat buyers.
  • Schools, coaching institutes and training providers with admissions cycles.

If most customers already talk to you on WhatsApp, email sits alongside it: WhatsApp for short updates and conversations, email for fuller content people can save and return to.

Choose an email marketing tool

Don't send newsletters from your Gmail or Outlook inbox. Bulk sending from a normal mailbox looks like spam to email providers, gives you no unsubscribe handling and can get your business email blocked. Use a dedicated tool instead. What to compare:

What to checkWhy it matters
Free plan limitsMost tools have a free tier based on contacts or monthly sends; check whether it covers your first year
Pricing as the list growsCosts usually rise with contacts, so compare the plan you will need later, not just today
Signup forms and WordPress integrationYou want forms that drop into your site or connect to your existing form plugin
AutomationA welcome email for new subscribers is the most useful automation to start with
Billing in INR and GST invoicesSome tools bill in dollars; your accountant may prefer an Indian invoice
Custom domain sendingEmails should come from your domain, not the tool's

For a WooCommerce or Shopify store, choose a tool that syncs customers and orders, so you can email past buyers about restocks or related products. Respect what customers agreed to when they bought.

Set up your domain so emails reach the inbox

Your sending domain needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC records in DNS. The email tool tells you which records to add, and they prove to Gmail, Outlook and others that the tool is allowed to send on your behalf. Without them, newsletters drift into spam or the Promotions tab. Business email deliverability explained walks through each record. Also send from a real address people can reply to, such as hello@ your domain, rather than a no-reply address.

Grow the list from your website

Your website is where most sign-ups come from. Places a sign-up form works well:

  • The end of blog posts, where a reader has just found something useful.
  • The footer on every page, short and quiet.
  • The thank-you page after an enquiry or order, where people already trust you. Thank-you pages for website forms covers what else to put there.
  • A dedicated newsletter page you can link from social profiles and email signatures.
  • Checkout, as an unticked opt-in box for stores.

Say exactly what people will get and how often: "One email a month with GST deadlines and tax-saving tips" converts better than "Subscribe to our newsletter". Ask only for an email address, and perhaps a first name.

Consent and the rules

Only email people who signed up or who are clearly existing customers expecting to hear from you, and include an unsubscribe link in every email. Don't import a bought list, a list of visiting cards from an expo without permission, or your whole phone contact list. India's Digital Personal Data Protection framework is relevant when you collect and use personal data; DPDP Act basics for websites gives an overview, and it is worth checking current requirements with a professional. Keep a record of where and when each person signed up.

What to send

The most common reason newsletters fail is that they become monthly advertisements. Aim for mostly useful content with a clear, light link back to what you sell:

  • Answers to questions customers ask you every week, written up properly.
  • Seasonal guidance: monsoon maintenance, festival ordering deadlines, financial year-end reminders, exam-season tips.
  • New products, services or projects, with real photos and what problem they solve.
  • Links to new blog posts, so each article reaches people who already know you.
  • Behind-the-scenes updates such as a new location, a team member or a process change, kept short.

A simple monthly routine

WeekTask
Week 1Pick one main topic and one or two shorter items
Week 2Write the email; reuse or summarise a blog post if you have one
Week 3Send a test to yourself on mobile and desktop, then schedule it
Week 4Check results and replies, note ideas for next month

Consistency matters more than length. A short, regular email builds more trust than an occasional long one.

The welcome email: your most-read message

The email a new subscriber receives straight after signing up is usually opened more than anything you send later, because the person has just thought about you. Use it well:

  • Thank them and confirm what they will receive and how often.
  • Deliver the lead magnet if you promised one, such as a checklist, price guide format or sample catalogue.
  • Link to the two or three most helpful pages on your website, for example a buying guide, your services page and a case study.
  • Ask one simple question they can reply to, such as what they are working on or what they would like to read about. Replies also signal to email providers that your messages are wanted.

For stores, a short series of two or three welcome emails can introduce bestsellers, explain delivery, COD and returns, and answer common doubts before the first order. Keep each one focused on helping the reader decide, not on discount codes alone.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with no plan for content, then sending nothing for six months and confusing people when the next email arrives.
  • Hiding the unsubscribe link or making people log in to leave, which leads to spam complaints instead.
  • Sending every email as a sale, which trains subscribers to ignore you until there is a discount.
  • Using a purchased list to grow quickly; it damages your sending reputation for every future email.
  • Forgetting the website side: broken sign-up forms, no confirmation message, or links in the email pointing to pages that no longer exist.

Write and design for mobile

Most people read email on their phones. Use a simple single-column template, a clear subject line that says what is inside, short paragraphs and one main button. Avoid sending the whole newsletter as one large image: it loads slowly, can't be read with images off and tends to land in spam.

Measure what matters

Open rates are less reliable than they used to be because of privacy features in email apps, so look at clicks, replies, enquiries and orders. Add UTM tags to links so your analytics shows which visits and enquiries came from the newsletter. Remove contacts who have not opened or clicked for a long time; a smaller, engaged list delivers better than a large, cold one.

FAQs

How do I start an email newsletter for my small business?

Pick an email marketing tool, set up your sending domain, add a sign-up form to your website, write a welcome email, and send one useful email on a regular schedule. Start small and improve as replies come in.

How often should a small business send a newsletter?

Monthly suits most small businesses. Stores with frequent launches may send more often, but only if every email has something worth reading.

Can I send newsletters from Gmail?

Not for bulk sending. Use an email marketing tool, which handles unsubscribes, protects your sending reputation and lets you send from your own domain.

Can I add my existing customers to the list?

Customers who bought from you and expect updates are often reasonable to include, with an easy unsubscribe, but avoid adding people who never agreed to hear from you. Check current consent rules if unsure.

What should my first newsletter say?

Introduce what subscribers will receive and how often, then give one genuinely useful piece of content. Invite replies; questions from readers become future topics.
