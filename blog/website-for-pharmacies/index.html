Websites for Pharmacies and Medical Stores: What You Can Sell Online, Prescription Requests, OTC and Wellness Products, Home Delivery, Store Locator and Pharmacist Contact

Plan a pharmacy or medical store website: online medicine sale rules to check, prescription requests, OTC products, home delivery and a store locator.

Key takeaways
  • The rules on selling medicines online in India are not settled, so take legal advice before adding a cart for medicines, and treat prescription drugs with particular care.
  • For many local pharmacies the practical route is an enquiry-led site: customers share a prescription request, the pharmacist reviews it, and the order is confirmed by phone or WhatsApp.
  • Over-the-counter, wellness and device ranges can often be sold online more simply, alongside a delivery area checker, a store locator and a named pharmacist to contact.

A pharmacy website should first help people find your store, check whether you deliver to them and reach a pharmacist quickly. Selling medicines online is a separate decision, because the rules for e-pharmacies in India are still unsettled at the time of writing. Many medical stores do well with an enquiry-led site, plus an online shop for over-the-counter and wellness products only after checking what is permitted.

The online shop side is a WooCommerce store: a catalogue in categories, payment gateways such as Razorpay, COD and delivery rules, stock management and order alerts. The information side has more in common with the clinic websites I build for doctors, where accuracy, clear contact details and trust carry most of the weight. A pharmacy needs both, and the boundary between them should be drawn carefully.

Three ways a pharmacy website can work

Decide which level you are building before planning pages. Each one carries different legal and operational demands:

LevelWhat it doesWhat it needsMain caution
Information siteStore details, hours, services, delivery area, call and WhatsAppAccurate details and a phone that gets answeredKeep hours and contact details current
Enquiry-led orderingCustomers request medicines or share a prescription; the pharmacist reviews it and confirms by phone or WhatsAppA secure way to receive prescriptions, and staff to review them promptlyHandling of health data, and dispensing only against a valid prescription where one is required
Online storeA cart for OTC, wellness and device products, possibly medicines if the law allowsProduct data, payments, delivery and order managementWhat may be sold online, and how; take legal advice first

Many neighbourhood pharmacies combine the first two and add a small online shop for non-medicine products. Chains with a central warehouse sometimes go further, but they usually have legal teams reviewing each step.

Selling medicines online: what to check before you build

This is the part to get right before any design work. At the time of writing, the sale of medicines in India is governed by the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and its rules, which were written for physical shops. Draft rules for e-pharmacies have been published in the past but, as far as I am aware, not finalised, and the question of online medicine sales has been before the courts. The position may have changed by the time you read this.

That leaves several questions only a lawyer or regulatory adviser can answer for your business:

  • Whether your existing retail drug licence allows you to take orders through a website and deliver them, and within what area
  • Which categories of medicines, if any, you can accept online orders for, and under what conditions
  • How prescriptions must be checked, recorded and retained when the order arrives digitally
  • Which medicines should never be supplied through a website order at all, such as those under the strictest schedules
  • What your licence details, pharmacist details and other information must show on the site

I can build the features once you know the answers. What I would not do is build a full medicine cart on the assumption that it is allowed. Please confirm the current rules first; they are the single biggest risk on a pharmacy website.

Prescription requests, handled with care

Customers like being able to send a photo of a prescription and have the medicines ready or delivered. A careful process looks like this:

  1. The customer opens a "Request medicines" page and enters name, phone, delivery address or preferred store, and whether a prescription is involved.
  2. The prescription is shared through a channel that does not leave it sitting in an ordinary email inbox, such as a secure upload with restricted access, or a WhatsApp business number the pharmacy controls.
  3. A registered pharmacist reviews the prescription and the request, and calls or messages to confirm availability, substitutes if any, and price.
  4. The order is dispensed and delivered or kept for pick-up, with the original prescription checked on delivery where the rules require it.
  5. Uploaded files are deleted or archived according to a retention policy you have agreed with your adviser.

Prescriptions show a person's name, conditions and medicines, which makes them sensitive personal data. Collect only what you need, explain how it is used and stored in your privacy policy, and restrict who can see it. For what India's data protection law expects, see DPDP Act basics for website owners.

Do not let the website suggest that a pharmacist can replace a doctor. If a customer describes symptoms rather than sharing a prescription, the response should guide them to a doctor where appropriate.

OTC, wellness and devices: the simpler online range

Many products in a pharmacy are not prescription medicines: personal care, baby care, nutrition, first aid, supports and braces, and devices such as glucometers, blood pressure monitors and thermometers. These are often easier to sell online, though some still have their own rules, and Ayurvedic products, cosmetics and supplements each fall under separate regulation. Check the category before listing it.

For each product, show the brand, pack size, MRP and your price, the manufacturer, and the expiry policy, such as a minimum shelf life on delivery. Copy any claims from the pack, not from advertising, and avoid adding health claims of your own. Products that need a demonstration, such as a nebuliser, can link to a short how-to video. If you also hire out wheelchairs, oxygen concentrators or hospital beds, give that service its own page; websites for medical equipment suppliers covers rental and sales of equipment.

Home delivery: say exactly where and when

A delivery promise is only useful if it is specific. State:

  • The areas or pin codes you deliver to, with a pin code checker on the site
  • Delivery hours, the order cut-off for same-day delivery, and any minimum order value
  • Delivery charges, and whether payment can be made on delivery by UPI or cash
  • What happens out of hours: whether night delivery is possible for urgent needs, or the nearest store open late

Customers can test their address before ordering with a pin code checker on the store, and a WhatsApp button for delivery questions saves calls; adding WhatsApp to a business website covers the options.

A store locator for more than one shop

For chains, the most visited page is often "find a store". Give each store its own page with:

  • Address, landmark and a map
  • Hours, highlighted if the store is open 24 hours or late at night
  • Phone and WhatsApp numbers for that store
  • Services at that location, such as blood pressure checks, diagnostic sample collection or vaccination where offered and permitted
  • Its own Google Business Profile, with the same name, address and hours

A list sorted by area, with a "near me" option on mobile, helps people find the closest store quickly, and consistent store details support local SEO too. Websites for multi-location businesses covers the structure in more detail.

Pharmacist contact and licence details

People trust a pharmacy when they can see who is responsible. Name the registered pharmacist in charge at each store, with their registration details as your adviser recommends, and show the drug licence numbers for each store in the footer or on its page. Give customers a clear way to ask a pharmacist a question by phone or WhatsApp during working hours, and say how quickly they can expect a reply.

Health information without overstepping

Short, accurate articles can help: how to store insulin in summer, how to use an inhaler properly, how to read the expiry date on a strip, how to dispose of old medicines. Keep them general, date them, and avoid advice that belongs with a doctor, such as dosages or switching medicines. Never promote a prescription medicine by name to the public.

FAQs

Can a medical store sell medicines through its website?

The rules on online medicine sales in India are unsettled at the time of writing. Take advice from a lawyer or regulatory adviser on what your licence allows before adding any online ordering for medicines.

Can customers upload prescriptions on the website?

It can be done, but prescriptions are sensitive health data. Use a restricted, secure channel, let a pharmacist review every request, and delete files according to an agreed retention policy.

What can a pharmacy sell online more easily?

Personal care, baby care, first aid and many devices are generally simpler than medicines, though categories such as supplements, Ayurvedic products and cosmetics have their own rules to check.

Should the website show our drug licence number?

Showing the licence numbers and the pharmacist in charge for each store builds trust, and may be required. Ask your adviser what must be displayed for your licence.

Do we need a page for each store?

Yes, if you have several. Each store page should carry its own address, hours, phone, services and map, matching its Google Business Profile.
