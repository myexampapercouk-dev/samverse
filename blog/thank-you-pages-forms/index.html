Lead Form Thank You Page Redirect: What to Put on the Page, How to Set It Up in WordPress and How to Track It

How to set up a lead form thank you page redirect in WPForms, Elementor or CF7, what to put on the page, how to fix it, and track it in GA4 and Google Ads.

  • Redirect only after a successful submission, keep the page out of Google with noindex rather than robots.txt, and give each important form its own thank-you URL.

A lead form thank you page redirect sends a visitor to a dedicated thank-you page after they successfully submit a form on your website. A good thank-you page confirms the enquiry arrived, says honestly what happens next, offers a faster route such as WhatsApp or a call, and points to something useful to read while they wait. It also gives you a reliable conversion to track in Google Analytics 4, Google Ads and Meta. Set your form to redirect there only after a successful submission, keep the page out of Google, and make sure each enquiry is counted once.

Every landing page in my landing page design service comes with a thank-you page, and conversions are counted on successful submissions, through the thank-you page or the form's success event, never on clicks of the submit button, which also count failed attempts.

A thank-you page can't help if submissions fail or the emails never arrive. If enquiries have dried up, check that first with why your contact form isn't getting enquiries.

Thank-you page or inline message?

OptionStrengthsWeaknessesBest for
Redirect to a thank-you pageRoom for next steps, easy to track, a separate page for each form or campaignPeople can reach it without submitting, so counts need careEnquiry and quote forms, ad landing pages, downloads
Inline confirmation messageNo extra page load, the visitor stays where they wereEasy to miss on a phone if it appears off screen; tracking needs a success eventPop-ups, multi-step forms, newsletter sign-ups, forms that hand over to WhatsApp

This site, which is custom-coded rather than built on WordPress, uses the inline approach because its form hands over to WhatsApp. Once an enquiry passes the form's spam checks, it is emailed, the form shows "Thanks! Your enquiry has been sent. Opening WhatsApp...", a GA4 generate_lead event records the lead along with whether the email went through, and WhatsApp opens with the details already typed in. Either approach works if the confirmation is obvious and tracking fires only on success.

What to put on a thank-you page

  1. A clear confirmation in the heading, such as "Thanks, we've received your enquiry".
  2. What happens next, honestly: who will reply, how (phone, WhatsApp or email) and roughly when. Only promise a response time you keep; responding to enquiries fast has a simple routine.
  3. A faster route for people in a hurry: a WhatsApp button with a pre-filled message and a tap-to-call number.
  4. What to have ready, such as a floor plan for an interior project or recent reports for a clinic, so the first conversation is useful.
  5. Something worth reading: a relevant case study, a guide to the process or answers to common questions.
  6. Genuine proof, such as a real testimonial or a link to your Google reviews, which reassures people they made a good choice.
  7. A spam-folder note if you send an automatic email reply, since first emails from a new address sometimes land there.

Leave off another form asking for the same details, pop-ups, pushy offers and anything that makes the page a dead end; keep your normal header and footer so visitors can carry on browsing. Different forms need slightly different pages:

FormWhat its thank-you page should add
Quote requestWhat you'll need from them and when they can expect the quote
Call-back requestWhen to expect the call and the number you'll call from, so they pick up
Appointment requestThe details they sent, your address with a map link and how to reschedule
Brochure or price listThe download link itself, plus related products or services
Newsletter sign-upA reminder to confirm their email if you use double opt-in

How to redirect a lead form to a thank-you page

Whichever form plugin you use, a lead form redirect follows the same pattern:

  1. Publish the thank-you page first, with a clear URL such as /thank-you-quote/, and set it to noindex.
  2. Open the form's confirmation or after-submit settings and swap the on-screen message for a redirect to that page. Most WordPress form plugins offer this as a choice between showing a message, showing a page or going to a URL.
  3. Make sure the redirect fires only on success. Built-in confirmation settings do this already; a custom script should listen for the plugin's success event, not the button click.
  4. Use one thank-you URL per lead form, so each page view maps to exactly one type of lead.
  5. Set up the conversion on that page view in GA4, Google Ads or Meta, as explained below, and send a real test enquiry to confirm the lead, the email and the conversion all arrive.

The tracking angle is the main reason to redirect at all: a dedicated URL that only successful submitters reach is the simplest conversion signal there is, and it works even when an ad platform's own form tracking doesn't.

When the lead form redirect does not work

  • The form shows a message instead of redirecting: a caching or optimisation plugin may be serving an old copy of the form settings. Clear the cache and test again in a private window.
  • The redirect fires but no email arrives: the form is working but mail delivery is not. Fix email sending, usually with SMTP, before trusting the conversion count.
  • The redirect goes to a 404: the thank-you page slug changed or the page is still a draft. Publish it and update the URL in the form.
  • Conversions are much higher than enquiries: the page is being reached without a submission, often from search, a menu link or a refresh. Check noindex, remove links to it and set counting to once.
  • The redirect fires on a failed submission: a custom script is listening for the button click. Switch it to the plugin's success event.
  • The redirect works on desktop but not on a phone: a pop-up blocker, a slow mobile connection or a script error on the page can interrupt it. Test on a real phone over mobile data, and check the browser console for errors on the form page.

Setting the redirect in WordPress form plugins

Create the thank-you page as a normal WordPress page first, then point the form at it. At the time of writing, the settings are here:

PluginWhere to set it
WPFormsIn the form builder, Settings > Confirmations, then choose "Show Page" or "Go to URL (Redirect)"
Gravity FormsForm Settings > Confirmations, with the type set to Page or Redirect
Fluent FormsSettings & Integrations > Confirmation Settings, then "To a Page" or a custom URL
Elementor Pro form widgetActions After Submit > add Redirect, then enter the URL in the Redirect section
ForminatorThe Behaviour tab, with the after-submission action set to redirect
Contact Form 7No built-in setting; use a short script or an add-on plugin

For Contact Form 7, this script, added to the page's footer or through a code snippets plugin, redirects only after the email has been sent successfully. Replace 123 with your form's ID, shown in its shortcode:

<script>
document.addEventListener( 'wpcf7mailsent', function( event ) {
  if ( '123' == event.detail.contactFormId ) {
    location = 'https://example.com/thank-you/';
  }
}, false );
</script>

Give each important form its own page, such as one for quote requests and one for brochure downloads, so your reports show which form produced each lead. WordPress form plugins compared helps if you're choosing a plugin.

Keep thank-you pages out of Google

  • Set the page to noindex. In Yoast, open the Advanced tab in the editor and answer No to showing the page in search results; in Rank Math, tick No Index under Advanced > Robots Meta. Both then leave it out of the sitemap.
  • Don't block it in robots.txt. Google needs to crawl the page to see the noindex.
  • Don't link to it from menus, footers or other pages.
  • Gated downloads: a brochure link on a thank-you page can be shared, which is fine for most brochures. For anything you want to control, email the file instead.

Track it, and count each enquiry once

In GA4

  1. Go to Admin > Data display > Events and click Create event.
  2. Name it generate_lead, Google's recommended name for a lead, and set two conditions: event_name equals page_view, and page_location contains thank-you (or the slug of your page).
  3. Mark generate_lead as a key event under Admin > Data display > Key events.
  4. Set its counting method to once per session, available at the time of writing, so a refresh or a second visit to the page in the same session isn't counted twice.

If your form uses an inline message instead, fire the event from the form's success event, usually through Google Tag Manager; GA4 events explained covers the options.

In Google Ads and Meta

Use the same thank-you page or success event for your Google Ads conversion action and set it to count one conversion per click, which is Google's recommended setting for leads. Fire the Meta Pixel's Lead event on the same trigger. Use either the Google Ads tag or a GA4 import as the primary conversion for each action, not both. Google Ads conversion tracking on WordPress walks through the setup.

Test before you rely on it

Send a real test enquiry from your phone. Check that the thank-you page loads, the event appears in GA4's Realtime report or DebugView, the conversion shows in Google Ads' and Meta's testing tools, and the notification email reaches the right inbox. Then refresh the thank-you page and confirm nothing is counted twice.

The WooCommerce version: the order received page

In a WooCommerce store, the thank-you page is the order received page at the end of checkout. It is where purchase tracking fires, so use a tracking plugin or integration that records each order once even if the shopper refreshes. It is also worth customising: confirm delivery expectations, explain what happens next for cash on delivery orders, and give a WhatsApp link for questions. Block themes let you edit it as the Order Confirmation template under Appearance > Editor; classic themes need a developer or a plugin. A smooth path to that page matters too; see WooCommerce checkout optimisation.

The thank-you page is only the first reply

The page reassures the visitor, but the enquiry still needs a person. Make sure the notification reaches someone who acts on it, send a short automatic email that repeats what the page promised, and follow up if the first attempt doesn't connect. FAQs

What is a thank-you page on a website?

It is the page visitors see after submitting a form or completing an order. It confirms the submission, explains what happens next and gives you a clean way to count conversions.

Should a thank-you page be noindexed?

Yes. It is useless as a search result and visits from search would inflate your conversion counts. Set it to noindex in your SEO plugin, keep it crawlable and don't link to it from menus.

How do I set up a lead form thank you page redirect?

Create and noindex the thank-you page, then change the form's confirmation setting from a message to a redirect to that page. Submit a test lead and check that you land on the page, the enquiry email arrives and your analytics records one conversion.

How do I redirect Contact Form 7 to a thank-you page?

Contact Form 7 has no redirect setting. Add a short script that listens for its wpcf7mailsent event and sends the browser to your thank-you page, or use an add-on plugin that adds a redirect option to each form.

How do I track a thank-you page as a conversion in GA4?

Create a generate_lead event that fires when page_view happens on your thank-you page's URL, mark it as a key event and set it to count once per session. Then test it with a real submission in DebugView.

Is a thank-you page better than a confirmation message?

A page gives you more room for next steps and simpler tracking; an inline message suits pop-ups, multi-step forms and forms that continue into WhatsApp. Either works if it is obvious and tracking fires only after a successful submission.
