10 Signs Your Business Needs a New Website, How to Check Each One, and Whether a Refresh or a Full Redesign Will Fix It

10 signs your business needs a new website, from poor mobile use and slow pages to hacks and no enquiries, with a table showing when a refresh is enough.

Key takeaways
  • The clearest signs are a site that is hard to use on a phone, slow, dated, impossible to edit, insecure, short of enquiries or no longer describing your business.
  • One or two specific problems can usually be fixed with a refresh; a site that fails on phones, cannot be updated safely or no longer fits the business usually needs rebuilding.
  • Before any redesign, record which pages bring visits and enquiries today, so the new site keeps them and redirects every address that changes.

Your business needs a new website if it is hard to use on a phone, loads slowly, looks out of date, can't be edited without a developer, keeps getting hacked, gets visitors but no enquiries, or no longer describes what you sell. One or two of these can often be fixed without starting again. Three or more, especially poor mobile use, unsafe updates or a changed business, usually mean a redesign will cost less over time than patching.

When a business owner sends me their site for a website redesign review, the first job is working out which of these signs actually apply, and whether a smaller refresh would do. This guide shows how to check each sign yourself, so you can go into that conversation knowing what is wrong.

If you already know the site needs work and are choosing between the two options, website refresh vs redesign has a short decision test. Here the focus is on spotting the problems in the first place.

1. It's hard to use on a phone

Most of your visitors are probably on phones, and Google mainly looks at the mobile version of your pages when deciding how to rank them. Open your site on your own phone, ideally on mobile data, and try to do what a customer would: find a service, check the price or area, and contact you.

  • Do you have to pinch and zoom to read anything?
  • Does the page scroll sideways, or do images get cut off?
  • Are buttons and links big enough to tap without hitting the wrong one?
  • Does the phone number dial when tapped, and does WhatsApp open a chat?

A site that was never built for phones is the strongest single sign that it needs rebuilding, because fixing the layout means redoing most templates anyway.

2. Pages are slow to load

Test your homepage and one service page with PageSpeed Insights in mobile mode, and look at the Core Web Vitals report in Search Console if you have it. Then do the human test: load the site on a phone on 4G and count how long before you can read and tap something.

Slowness has many causes: oversized photos, a heavy multipurpose theme, a page builder carrying years of unused add-ons, too many plugins or cheap, overloaded hosting. Some of these can be fixed in place; others are built into the site's foundations. Speed test tools explained shows how to read the results without getting lost in the scores.

3. It looks out of date

Visitors decide quickly whether a business looks current and capable. Common giveaways:

  • Stock photos of models instead of your team, premises and work
  • A copyright year several years old, or last Diwali's offer still on the homepage
  • Auto-playing sliders, tiny grey text and crowded layouts
  • A news or blog section whose latest post is years old
  • Old services, prices, team members or branches still listed

Dated content is a refresh job. A dated design on a theme that can't be modernised without breaking it is a redesign.

4. You can't update it, or don't fully control it

If changing a phone number or adding a photo means waiting days for a developer, the site stops being updated, and an outdated site erodes trust. Check whether you have your own administrator login, whether the domain and hosting are registered in your business name, and whether the site was built in a way you can edit, such as WordPress with a visual builder or the block editor. A site built by someone who has since disappeared, on a platform only they understand, is a risk even if it looks fine today. The website ownership checklist lists what you should hold in your own name.

5. Security warnings, hacks or breakages after updates

  • The browser shows "Not secure" because the site has no working SSL certificate.
  • The site has been hacked more than once, or Google has shown a warning about it.
  • The theme or key plugins haven't been updated by their makers in years.
  • Updates are avoided because they break the layout every time.
  • It runs on an old PHP version that your host is pushing you to upgrade.

A single hack can be cleaned and the site hardened. Repeated hacks on an abandoned theme with nulled or outdated plugins usually mean the foundations need replacing. Why WordPress sites get hacked explains the usual causes.

6. Visitors arrive, but enquiries don't

If Google Analytics shows steady visits but the phone rarely rings, the site isn't convincing people or isn't making contact easy. Before blaming the design, rule out the simple causes: submit your own form from a phone and check the email arrives, tap every call and WhatsApp button, and look at what a visitor sees on the first screen of your main service page. Why a contact form stops getting enquiries covers the technical failures. If everything works and enquiries are still rare, the problem is usually structure and content: no clear next step, no proof and pages that don't answer customers' questions.

7. Your business has changed

New services, a new location, a different type of customer, a new name or logo: if the website still describes the business you were five years ago, it is selling the wrong thing. Adding one service page is a refresh. A change in what you sell, who you sell to or what you are called usually changes the structure and the brand together, which is a redesign. Rebranding your website covers the extra steps when the name changes.

8. The SEO foundations are missing

Search for your main service and city. If competitors appear and you don't, the site may lack the basics Google needs, whatever its design:

  • All your services squeezed onto one page instead of a page each
  • Missing or duplicate page titles and descriptions, or no main heading
  • Important text inside images, sliders or PDFs rather than on the page
  • Addresses like ?page_id=12 instead of readable URLs
  • No Search Console account, so nobody knows which pages are indexed

Titles, descriptions and headings can be fixed on almost any site. A structure with no room for a page per service, or a platform that limits what you can change, is harder to work around.

9. It can't do what you need now

Online bookings, payments through UPI and cards, a product catalogue, enquiry forms that feed a CRM, a Hindi or Marathi version: if your current setup can't support the feature you need, or adding it means a stack of workarounds, that points towards a rebuild on a platform that can.

10. Competitors' websites make yours look small

Customers compare. Look at the three businesses that appear above you on Google and open each site on your phone. If theirs are clearer, faster and easier to contact, they will win enquiries even when your service is better. This sign alone isn't a reason to rebuild, but it tells you how much the others matter.

Refresh or rebuild? Match the fix to the sign

Each sign can point either way, depending on what is causing it. Use this table to decide which applies to your site:

SignA refresh or targeted fix is enough whenA redesign makes sense when
Hard to use on phonesOnly a few elements misbehave, such as a menu or one tableThe layout was never built for phones
SlowImages, caching or hosting are the causeA heavy theme or builder setup is the cause
Looks datedPhotos, text and offers are oldThe theme itself can't be modernised
Can't edit itYou simply need training or accessThere is no editor, or only the old developer understands it
Security problemsOne incident, now cleaned and hardenedRepeated hacks or an abandoned theme and plugins
No enquiriesBroken forms or weak buttonsPages don't answer questions or match how customers choose
Business has changedA service or branch to addNew services, customers or name
Weak SEO foundationsTitles, headings and content need workThe structure has no room for a page per service
Missing featuresA plugin or integration adds it cleanlyThe platform can't support it

If most of your signs land in the middle column, start with a refresh. If two or more land in the right-hand column, patching the old site will probably cost more over time than replacing it. On a tight budget, the work can be phased: redesigning on a tight budget shows how.

Before you redesign: protect what already works

An old site that looks tired may still bring in visits from Google and enquiries from particular pages. Losing those during a redesign is the biggest risk, and it is avoidable:

  • Record which pages get clicks in Search Console and which lead to enquiries in Google Analytics.
  • Keep URLs that work, and redirect every address that changes to its closest new page.
  • Keep and improve content on pages that rank, rather than trimming it for a cleaner look.
  • Build the new site on a staging copy while the old one stays live.

FAQs

How do I know if my website is outdated?

Open it on your phone and compare it with competitors' sites. If it is awkward on a phone, slow, full of old information or stock photos, or you can't update it yourself, it is outdated in the ways that cost enquiries, whatever year it was built.

How often should a business website be redesigned?

There is no fixed cycle. Redesign when the signs appear, such as poor mobile use, an unmaintained theme or a changed business, and refresh content and photos regularly in between so the site dates more slowly.

Is it cheaper to fix my old website or build a new one?

For one or two specific problems, fixing is usually cheaper. When the site fails on phones, can't be updated safely and needs new content anyway, each repair costs money without solving the underlying problem, and a redesign tends to be the better value.

Will a new website improve my Google rankings?

Nobody can honestly promise that. A faster, better-structured site with a page per service gives your pages a better chance, but a redesign done carelessly can lose rankings, which is why URLs, redirects and ranking content need protecting.

Can I keep my domain and content in a redesign?

Yes. The domain normally stays the same, and good existing content, photos and testimonials can be reused and improved. What changes is the design, the structure and how the site is built.
