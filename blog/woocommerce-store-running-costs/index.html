WooCommerce Store Running Costs: What to Budget For After Launch

Key takeaways
  • The fixed yearly costs of a WooCommerce store are small: a domain at about ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year, hosting at about ₹2,500 to ₹15,000 a year, and any premium plugin or theme licences.
  • The costs that grow with sales are payment gateway fees, shipping, packaging, returns and marketing, so budget for them as a share of each order, not as a flat amount.
  • Maintenance matters more for a store than a brochure site; freelance plans for WordPress sites typically cost about ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month, with stores at the higher end.

The WooCommerce store cost per month in India is made of two parts. Fixed costs are small: a domain (about ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year), hosting (about ₹2,500 to ₹15,000 a year), premium plugin renewals and, if you want one, a maintenance plan (typically ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month from a freelancer, with stores at the higher end). The bigger costs grow with every order: payment gateway fees, shipping, packaging, returns and marketing.

WooCommerce itself is free, which is why many owners underestimate what a store costs to run. This guide lists every cost you should budget for after launch, explains what drives each one and ends with a simple running-cost sheet.

Hosting that suits a store

Stores need better hosting than brochure sites because cart, checkout and account pages cannot be fully cached, and every order writes to the database. Cheap shared plans often struggle during sales or festival traffic. Look for enough resources for your product count and traffic, daily backups, a staging site, good support and servers close to Indian visitors. Watch renewal prices too: introductory offers often renew at a much higher rate, so check the renewal rate before you buy. For plan comparisons, see WordPress hosting price in India.

Payment gateway fees

Gateways such as Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree and others charge a percentage of each online payment, which varies by payment method and plan, and GST applies on the fee. Rates and offers change, so check the current pricing page of your gateway rather than relying on any article. When comparing gateways, also look at settlement time, international card support, refund handling and how well the WooCommerce plugin is maintained.

Because this cost scales with sales, budget it as a percentage of revenue, not a fixed monthly amount.

Shipping and packaging

For most Indian stores, shipping is one of the largest running costs. It depends on weight (including volumetric weight), distance, courier partner, and whether the order is prepaid or cash on delivery. Aggregators such as Shiprocket give access to several couriers through one account. Add packaging: boxes, mailers, tape, fillers and printed invoices.

COD adds its own cost: COD handling charges and return-to-origin (RTO) shipping when buyers refuse orders. The guides to WooCommerce shipping setup in India and cash on delivery for WooCommerce explain how to control these.

Premium plugin and extension renewals

A basic store can run on free plugins, but many stores add premium extensions for subscriptions, bookings, product filters, advanced shipping rules, GST invoices or backups. Most are licensed yearly; if you stop renewing, the plugin usually keeps working but you lose updates and support, which becomes a security risk over time. Keep a list of every paid plugin, its renewal date and cost, and remove any you no longer use. Avoid nulled or pirated plugins; they often contain malware.

Maintenance and security

A store has more moving parts than a brochure site: WooCommerce updates, payment and shipping plugins, theme templates that need updating and a checkout that must never break. Maintenance covers updates tested on staging, backups, uptime and security monitoring, speed checks and fixing issues when something stops working.

You can do this yourself if you have the time and skills, or use a maintenance plan. Freelance WordPress maintenance plans for small and medium sites typically cost about ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month, with stores and business-critical sites at the higher end. The guide to website maintenance cost in India explains what plans include, and the WooCommerce maintenance checklist lists the tasks.

Marketing to bring shoppers in

A store with no visitors earns nothing, so most owners spend something on marketing: Google Shopping and search ads, Meta and Instagram ads, email and WhatsApp campaigns, influencer collaborations and SEO content. This budget is entirely your choice and should be judged against the profit each order brings, not revenue alone. Start small, track conversions and grow what works. Festival seasons such as Diwali often bring more competition for ad space, so plan those budgets early and decide in advance how much you are willing to spend to win each order.

Hidden costs owners often forget

Beyond the obvious items, a few smaller costs catch many store owners by surprise:

  • Business email: a professional address on your domain may be included with hosting or may be a separate monthly or yearly plan.
  • SMS and WhatsApp notifications: order and delivery messages sent through a provider are usually charged per message or by plan.
  • Email marketing tools: free tiers often have limits on contacts or sends, and paid plans grow with your list.
  • Product photography: new products need photos, and good images are an ongoing cost, not a one-time one.
  • Accounting and GST compliance: your CA's fees, invoicing tools and filing. Ask your CA what applies to your store.
  • Your own time: processing orders, answering customer questions, updating stock and handling returns. If you hire help, include it in the sheet.

None of these is large on its own, but together they can change your margin, so list them alongside the main costs.

Returns, refunds and failed deliveries

Returns cost you twice: the original shipping and the return shipping, plus any damaged goods. Failed COD deliveries add RTO charges. Budget a share of revenue for this based on your own order history once you have a few months of data, and keep a clear returns policy to reduce disputes.

WooCommerce vs Shopify running costs in India

With WooCommerce you pay for hosting, domain and any plugins you choose, and you are responsible for maintenance. With Shopify you pay a monthly platform subscription that includes hosting and security, plus paid apps and, depending on your setup, transaction fees. Shopify plans and app prices change and are billed differently, so check Shopify's pricing page for current figures. In general, WooCommerce gives more control and can cost less at a fixed level, while Shopify trades a higher monthly subscription for less technical upkeep. The guide to WooCommerce vs Shopify for India compares them in detail.

Putting it together: a running-cost sheet

  1. Yearly fixed: domain, hosting, premium theme and plugin licences. Divide by 12 for a monthly figure.
  2. Monthly fixed: maintenance plan, email marketing tool, any paid apps or services.
  3. Per order: gateway fee, shipping, packaging, COD charges.
  4. Percentage of revenue: returns and RTO allowance, marketing budget.

Fill this in with your own quotes and gateway rates, then check that your product prices leave a margin after all four. Review it every few months, because renewal prices and shipping rates change.

FAQs

What is the WooCommerce store cost per month in India?

Fixed costs are low: a domain at about ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year, hosting at about ₹2,500 to ₹15,000 a year and any plugin licences, plus an optional maintenance plan at about ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month. Gateway fees, shipping, returns and marketing then grow with your sales.

Is WooCommerce free to run?

The plugin is free, but a store needs hosting, a domain, usually some premium extensions, payment gateway fees, shipping and maintenance, so it always has running costs.

Is WooCommerce cheaper than Shopify in India?

Often at a fixed-cost level, because you avoid a monthly platform subscription, but you take on hosting and maintenance. The right choice depends on your products, team and how much control you need.

Do I need a maintenance plan for my WooCommerce store?

Not necessarily, if you can safely test updates, take backups and fix problems yourself. Many owners prefer a plan because a broken checkout costs sales immediately.

Which WooCommerce costs grow as my sales grow?

Payment gateway fees, shipping, packaging, COD charges, returns and usually marketing. Hosting may also need upgrading as traffic increases.

How can I lower my WooCommerce store cost per month?

Remove plugins you no longer use, check whether premium renewals still earn their keep, compare gateway and courier rates once a year, and keep the store fast so ad spend converts better. A regular maintenance routine also prevents the costly emergency fixes that blow a monthly budget.
