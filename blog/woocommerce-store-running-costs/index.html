WooCommerce Store Running Costs: What to Budget For After Launch

Ongoing costs of a WooCommerce store explained: hosting, payment gateway fees, shipping, plugin renewals, maintenance, marketing and returns, and how to budget.

Key takeaways
  • A WooCommerce store has fixed running costs, like hosting, licences and maintenance, plus variable costs that rise with every order.
  • Payment gateway fees are usually a percentage per transaction and vary by payment method and provider, so build them into your prices.
  • Shipping, packaging, returns and COD return-to-origin charges can eat into margins, so work out the full cost of a typical order.

Building a WooCommerce store is a one-time project, but running it is a monthly commitment. Hosting, payment fees, shipping, plugin renewals, maintenance, marketing and returns continue for as long as the store is open, and some of them grow with every order. Here's what drives each one and how to keep it under control. For the build itself, see how much an e-commerce website costs in India.

Hosting that suits a store

A WooCommerce store asks more of its server than a brochure website. Cart, checkout and account pages are different for every shopper, so they can't be served from a simple page cache, and every order writes to the database. A cheap shared plan that copes with a five-page site can struggle during a sale or a festival rush.

What drives hosting cost for a store:

  • Server resources: CPU, memory and PHP workers decide how many shoppers can browse and check out at the same time
  • Catalogue and order volume: more products, images and order history mean a bigger database and more storage
  • Traffic peaks: plan for your busiest days, not an average Tuesday
  • Extras: object caching, staging sites, off-site backups and a CDN may be included or charged separately
  • Renewal pricing: introductory prices often apply only to the first term

When comparing plans, check the renewal price and whether you can upgrade without migrating. A slow checkout loses orders quietly, so hosting is rarely the best place to save money.

Payment gateway fees

Most payment gateways charge a fee on each successful transaction, usually a percentage of the order value and sometimes a fixed amount as well. The rate isn't one number: it often varies by payment method (UPI, debit cards, credit cards, net banking, wallets, international cards), by provider and by the plan or agreement you're on. GST is generally charged on the gateway's fee too, so check how it appears on your statements with your CA.

Points to compare between providers:

  • The fee for each payment method your customers actually use, not just the headline rate
  • Any setup, annual or maintenance charges
  • Charges for refunds, chargebacks, international cards or currency conversion
  • Settlement timing, since slower settlements tie up your cash

Because the fee is a share of every sale, build it into your product pricing rather than treating it as an overhead. See how to choose a payment gateway in India for a fuller comparison checklist.

Shipping and packaging

For many Indian stores, shipping is the biggest cost per order after the product itself. What you pay depends on:

  • Weight and size: couriers often charge by volumetric weight, so light but bulky items can cost more than you expect
  • Zones: local, within-state, metro-to-metro and remote pincodes are usually priced differently
  • Courier or aggregator: direct courier contracts and shipping aggregators have different rates, minimums and pickup terms
  • Cash on delivery: COD orders often carry an extra collection charge
  • Packaging: boxes, mailers, bubble wrap, tape, labels and branded inserts add a little to every order

Decide whether you'll absorb shipping, pass it on or offer free shipping above an order value, and make sure the numbers still work for your heaviest and most remote orders.

Premium plugin and extension renewals

WooCommerce itself is free, but most stores rely on a few paid extras: a premium theme, payment or shipping integrations, GST invoices, product filters, subscriptions, backup or security tools. Many are sold as yearly licences. If a licence lapses, the plugin usually keeps working but stops getting updates, which becomes a security and compatibility risk over time.

  • Keep a list of every paid plugin, its renewal date and the account it's registered to
  • Make sure licences are bought in your business's name
  • Review the list each year: remove plugins you no longer use and stop paying twice for overlapping features

Some services, such as SMS or WhatsApp notifications, charge per message instead, so their cost rises with your orders.

Maintenance and security

A store holds customer details and takes payments, so it needs more care than a simple website. Regular work includes updating WordPress, WooCommerce and plugins (ideally tested on a staging copy first), off-site backups that include recent orders, security monitoring, uptime checks and test orders to confirm payments, emails and invoices still work.

You can do this in-house, pay for individual tools or use a maintenance plan. Compare plans by what they actually include: how often updates run, whether a staging site is used, how quickly problems are handled and whether small content changes are covered. The WooCommerce maintenance checklist lists the weekly, monthly and quarterly tasks.

Marketing to bring shoppers in

A store doesn't sell without visitors. Common ongoing spend includes Google Shopping and search ads, Meta ads, SEO and content, email or WhatsApp marketing tools, and photography for new arrivals. Discounts and coupons are a real cost too, even though no invoice arrives: they reduce your margin on every order that uses them.

Track cost per order by channel where you can, so you know which spend is working.

Returns, refunds and failed deliveries

Returns are easy to leave out of a budget, but they can eat into margins, especially in fashion and footwear. The costs include:

  • Reverse pickup charges, on top of the original shipping you've already paid
  • Products that come back damaged or can't be resold
  • Return-to-origin (RTO) charges when COD orders are refused or undeliverable
  • Gateway fees that may not be returned when you refund an order (check your provider's terms)
  • Staff time to inspect, restock and process refunds

Clear size guides, accurate photos and confirming COD orders before dispatch can all reduce returns.

Putting it together: a running-cost sheet

Split your costs into fixed costs, which you pay whether you sell or not, and variable costs, which rise with every order:

CostTypeUsually billed
Hosting and domainFixedMonthly, yearly or multi-year
Plugin and theme licencesFixedMostly yearly
MaintenanceFixedMonthly or yearly
Payment gateway feesVariablePer transaction
Shipping and packagingVariablePer order
Messaging and shipping softwareVariablePer message or shipment
Returns and RTOVariablePer return
MarketingEitherMonthly budget or per click

Work out the variable cost of a typical order and check your prices still leave a healthy margin after it. Then add up the fixed costs to see how many orders a month the store needs just to cover itself.
