How to Get Your Website Indexed by Google Faster: What Actually Works

How to get your website indexed by Google faster: internal links, honest sitemaps, URL Inspection, page quality, crawl budget and IndexNow.

Key takeaways
  • Link every new page from pages Google already crawls often, such as the homepage, main menu and indexed posts, and keep it in a sitemap with an accurate lastmod.
  • Use Search Console's Request indexing only for your most important URLs, because the daily quota is small and repeated requests do not speed anything up.
  • Google indexes selectively on new sites, so fewer, fuller and unique pages on a fast server get indexed sooner than many thin ones; use IndexNow to tell Bing directly.

To get your website indexed by Google faster, link to it from pages Google already crawls often, such as your homepage, main menu and existing posts, list it in your XML sitemap with an accurate last-modified date, and request indexing for your most important URLs in Search Console. Beyond that, speed depends on whether Google thinks the page is worth indexing: substantial, unique pages on a fast site are indexed sooner than thin or near-duplicate ones. No button or plugin forces Google to index anything.

Sites whose pages sit in "Discovered" or "Crawled, currently not indexed" are exactly who my WordPress SEO services are for. Common causes are weak internal links, too many low-value URLs competing for Google's attention and a new domain Google doesn't trust yet, often all three at once.

This guide is about speeding up a site Google can already reach. If your site doesn't appear on Google at all, rule out blocks first with why a WordPress site isn't showing on Google. For what each status means, see Search Console page indexing errors, and if your sitemap shows an error, sitemap "couldn't fetch" explained.

Most "slow indexing" is really one of the middle two stages, and the fixes for each are different.

1. Link new pages from pages Google already crawls

Google recrawls your homepage and main navigation far more often than a post published last month. A new page linked from those is found on the next visit; a page linked from nowhere waits until Google gets round to your sitemap.

  • New service or location page: add it to the main menu or services menu, and link to it from the homepage and the closest related service pages.
  • New blog post: show recent posts on the homepage or blog page, and add a link from two or three older posts on the same topic that are already indexed (check them in URL Inspection first).
  • New products: make sure the category page lists them on its first page, not only on page six of the pagination.
  • Use real links. Google follows normal a href links. Buttons that navigate with JavaScript, or links that only appear after a click, may not be followed.

Links from other sites help too, such as your Google Business Profile website link, business directories you're genuinely listed in, and partners or clients who mention you. But internal links are the part you control completely, and the part most new sites neglect; internal linking explained covers how to plan them.

2. Keep the sitemap clean, with an honest lastmod

Your sitemap tells Google which URLs exist; on WordPress, Yoast or Rank Math add new posts to it automatically. Two things make it more useful for speed:

  • Only indexable URLs. A sitemap padded with redirects, noindexed pages and thin archives teaches Google that the list isn't a reliable guide to what matters.
  • Accurate lastmod dates. Google uses lastmod to decide what to recrawl, but only when the dates prove trustworthy. If every URL shows today's date after every small change, the field gets ignored.

This site is custom-coded rather than built on WordPress, and its sitemap now changes a page's lastmod only when that page's content changes, so the dates stay meaningful. Google retired its sitemap "ping" address in 2023, so plugins or settings that "ping Google" no longer do anything; Google finds sitemap changes by rereading the sitemap. XML sitemaps explained covers what to include.

3. Use "Request indexing" for your most important URLs

  1. Paste the full URL into the inspection bar at the top of Search Console. If you haven't verified your site yet, see setting up Search Console.
  2. If it says the URL is not on Google, click Test live URL to confirm Google can fetch the page and that indexing is allowed.
  3. Click Request indexing. Google runs a quick check and adds the URL to a priority crawl queue.

There is a daily limit per property. Google doesn't publish the exact number, and it has changed over time, but it is small: think a handful to around ten URLs a day, not hundreds. When you hit it, Search Console says the quota has been exceeded. Spend requests on your homepage, key service pages, important new posts and pages you have substantially updated. Requesting the same URL again doesn't move it up the queue, and a request is not a promise of indexing; it only gets the page looked at sooner.

Avoid plugins or services that push ordinary pages through Google's Indexing API. Google limits that API to job postings and livestream pages, and has warned that attempts to abuse it can get access revoked.

4. Make every page worth indexing

Google doesn't index everything it crawls, and it is choosier with new sites that have no track record. Pages that tend to sit in the not-indexed statuses:

  • Thin pages with a few lines of text, such as a service page that is a heading, two sentences and a form.
  • Near-duplicates, such as city pages with only the place name swapped, or product descriptions copied from the manufacturer; see duplicate content explained.
  • Low-value archives: tag pages with one post, attachment pages and internal search results.

The fix is fewer, fuller pages: merge thin ones into one strong page, add information only you can give (prices or what affects them, process, real examples, local detail), and noindex archives nobody needs to find in Google. I'm doing exactly this on this site: short early posts that Search Console listed as "Discovered, currently not indexed", this one included, are being rewritten into complete guides rather than left thin.

5. Crawl budget on a new site

Crawl budget is how many URLs Google is willing and able to crawl on your site in a given period. Google says most sites with fewer than a few thousand URLs don't need to worry about it, and that is true for established sites. A new domain is different: with no links and no history, Google's interest is low, so the URLs it does crawl should count.

  • Launch with your core pages, then add more steadily, rather than publishing hundreds of near-identical pages on day one.
  • Cut junk URLs: tag and attachment archives, sorting and filter parameters on stores, and http or www duplicates that should redirect.
  • Keep the server fast and stable. In Search Console, Settings > Crawl stats shows Google's average response time and any host problems. Slow responses and server errors make Google crawl less.
  • Fix redirect chains and internal links to 404s, which spend crawls on URLs that lead nowhere.

For the wider first-months plan, see SEO for a new website.

6. Tell Bing directly with IndexNow

IndexNow is a protocol that lets your site notify search engines the moment a URL is added, updated or deleted. At the time of writing, Bing, Yandex, Seznam and Naver support it; Google does not use it. Bing still matters: it powers its own results and is used by other search and AI tools.

  • On WordPress, Rank Math's Instant Indexing module, Microsoft's IndexNow plugin and Cloudflare's Crawler Hints setting can all send IndexNow notifications. Use one, not several.
  • In Bing Webmaster Tools, import your site from Search Console, submit your sitemap and use URL Submission for important pages.

This site uses a small script that submits the URLs in its sitemap to IndexNow after each update, so Bing hears about new pages without waiting for a crawl.

What doesn't speed up indexing

  • Resubmitting the sitemap every day, or requesting indexing for the same URL repeatedly.
  • "Instant indexing" services, link blasts and ping lists.
  • Changing published dates without changing the page.
  • Running Google Ads, which has no effect on organic indexing.
  • Social media shares on their own; they can help people find you, but they are a weak route for Google discovery.

How long does indexing take?

Google's own answer is anywhere from a few days to a few weeks, and new domains sit at the slower end. Track progress with URL Inspection for your key pages and the Pages report over several weeks; a site: search on Google gives only a rough picture. If important pages are still not indexed after several weeks despite good internal links and a clean sitemap, look again for a technical block, then at whether the pages are distinct and substantial enough.

FAQs

How do I get Google to index my website quickly?

Verify the site in Search Console, submit your sitemap, link new pages from your homepage and menu, request indexing for the key URLs and make sure each page is substantial and unique. That gives Google every reason to crawl and index promptly.

Can I get my website indexed instantly?

No, not for normal pages. Requesting indexing in Search Console only adds a URL to Google's crawl queue; it doesn't guarantee when, or whether, the page is indexed. Google's Indexing API is documented only for job posting and livestream pages at the time of writing, so tools promising instant indexing for ordinary pages are best avoided. A few days is a good result for a new page, and on a new domain it can take weeks.

How many URLs can I request indexing for per day?

Google doesn't publish the limit and it has changed over time, but it is a small number per property each day. Use requests for your most important new or updated pages and let internal links and the sitemap handle the rest.

Why does Search Console say "Discovered, currently not indexed"?

Google knows the URL exists but hasn't crawled it yet. Google's own explanation is that it postponed the crawl, typically so as not to overload the site; on a small new site it more often reflects low priority, or many low-value URLs competing for attention. Stronger internal links, fewer junk URLs and fuller pages are the usual fixes.

Does submitting a sitemap make indexing faster?

It helps Google discover URLs, which matters most on new sites, but it doesn't make Google index them. Internal links, page quality and site speed decide how quickly discovered pages are crawled and indexed.

Does Google use IndexNow?

Not at the time of writing. IndexNow notifies Bing and other participating search engines. For Google, rely on internal links, your sitemap and URL Inspection.
