Equipment and Generator Rental Websites: Structure, SEO and Enquiries (With a Real Example)

SEO for generator hire and equipment rental websites: capacity pages, local search, Business Profile, quick quote forms and a real generator rental site.

An equipment or generator rental website gets enquiries when buyers can find the exact capacity they need, see that you serve their area and industry, and ask for a quote or callback in seconds. For SEO, that means a page for each capacity band or equipment type, titles that match "on rent" and "hire" searches with your city, a complete Google Business Profile, and FAQs written in the words customers actually use.

Below is how I'd structure one, using the generator rental website I built for Sahni Power Solutions as a worked example. It's the same approach I use for websites for solar and power companies, and for other rental businesses through WordPress website development.

What rental buyers need to know in the first minute

Someone renting a generator, a crane or a sound system usually has a deadline: a power cut at a factory, an event this weekend, a site that starts work on Monday. They want answers fast:

  • Do you have the right equipment and capacity?
  • Is it available on my dates, for my duration, at my location?
  • Do you deliver, install and maintain it, and is an operator included?
  • What does the price depend on, and how quickly can I get a quote?
  • Does it meet the emission and noise rules my site or venue has to follow?

Every page should answer as many of these as possible and then make contacting you effortless.

Structure the site the way buyers specify equipment

Buyers rarely search by your model numbers. They think in capacity, type and duration, so organise your fleet the same way:

  • By capacity: kVA bands for generators, tonnage for cranes, wattage for sound systems
  • By type: portable, silent, welding, mobile and industrial generators, for example
  • By rental term: daily, weekly, monthly and annual

SEO for generator hire: how to rank

Use the words people search with

Indian buyers search "generator on rent", "DG set on rent", "genset rental" and "generator hire", usually with a capacity, a city or a locality. Use those phrases naturally in page titles, headings and FAQs. A capacity page title might read "125 kVA Generator on Rent in Bangalore | Silent DG Sets", with the H1 and first paragraph confirming the same thing.

Give each capacity band its own content

Copying the same paragraph onto every capacity page with only the number changed gives Google no reason to rank any of them. Write what's specific: typical uses for that size, what affects its price, approximate fuel use, and how it compares with the next size up or down.

Local search and your Google Business Profile

For "near me" and city searches, your Business Profile matters as much as the website. Choose the closest rental category available, set your service areas, add real photos of the fleet and installations, and ask happy customers for reviews. Keep the business name, address and phone number identical on the website and the profile; see the Google Business Profile checklist.

Only create area pages for places you genuinely serve and can say something specific about, such as delivery times from your yard or jobs you've done there. Dozens of near-identical city pages tend to be ignored or treated as doorway pages; see SEO for service-area businesses.

FAQs written in customers' words

Answer the questions people ask on the phone: emergency hire, delivery and setup, maintenance during the rental, fuel, choosing a size, portable versus silent, and monthly rentals. Google rarely shows FAQ rich results for business sites now, but the answers still match real searches and reassure buyers; see FAQ page SEO.

Technical basics

Use an SEO plugin such as Rank Math or Yoast for titles and LocalBusiness schema, compress fleet photos, keep pages fast on mobile data, and submit your sitemap in Search Console so new capacity pages get found.

Make enquiring instant

  • A "Request a Call Back" button in the header on every page, and an "Enquire Now" button at the end of every section.
  • Click-to-call on mobile, because urgent rentals are often arranged by phone.
  • WhatsApp with a page-specific message, such as "Hi, I need a 125 kVA generator on rent", so you know what they want before replying; see adding WhatsApp to your website.
  • A short form: capacity (with a "not sure, help me choose" option), site location, start date, duration, phase, name and phone.
  • A separate route for long-term hire, such as a monthly rental enquiry button, because those enquiries are worth more and need different questions.

Make sure every enquiry reaches someone immediately by email and phone notification. A rental buyer who waits an hour has usually called someone else.

Pricing: show it, or explain what drives it

Most rental companies quote per job, and that's fine. What helps is explaining what the price depends on: capacity, hire duration, whether fuel is supplied, whether an operator is included, distance from your yard, transport and installation, and expected running hours. If you do publish indicative rates, label them clearly and only show figures you'll honour.

Build trust before the call

  • Years in business and project numbers you can back up
  • Photos of your own fleet and real installations, not stock images
  • The sectors and kinds of sites you serve
  • Compliance details, such as the emission standard your generators meet and noise limits for silent units, checked against the current rules
  • What happens if equipment breaks down during the rental
  • Your yard or office address, phone and email on every page

Real example: Sahni Power Solutions

Sahni Power Solutions, which works from a yard in Yelahanka, Bangalore, rents diesel generators for commercial, industrial, institutional, event and infrastructure needs, on daily, weekly, monthly and annual terms. The website I built for them follows the structure above:

  • Short navigation: Home, Our Generator Range and Contact Us, with a "Request a Call Back" button in the header.
  • Capacity pages: the generator range is split into seven capacity bands, from 5 to 62.5 kVA up to 1,500 kVA, each with its own page.
  • Range by type as well as size: single-phase portable units from 5 to 15 kVA, three-phase units up to 125 kVA, silent generators for noise-sensitive sites, welding generators from 200 to 400 amps, and large industrial DG sets.
  • Specific content per band: the 82.5 to 125 kVA page lists typical uses, explains what affects the rental price (duration, fuel arrangement, operator and distance from the Yelahanka yard), gives approximate fuel consumption at full load, and compares 100 kVA with 125 kVA for a small hospital.
  • Sectors named: refineries, mining, government projects such as PWD, Smart City and metro work, data centres, events, banks, schools, hospitals, warehouses, malls and construction sites.
  • Trust and compliance: project counters, a note that the business began as Sahni Traders in 1992, an installation gallery, and references to CPCB-IV+ emission standards and state noise limits for acoustic units.
  • Local FAQs: sizes available in Bangalore, emergency hire, delivery and setup, and monthly rental, with localities such as Whitefield, Electronic City, Peenya and Hebbal named alongside Pan India service.

It's built with WordPress, Elementor, the Astra theme and Rank Math SEO.

FAQs

How do I do SEO for a generator hire business?

Create a page for each capacity band and generator type, use "on rent" and "hire" phrases with capacities and your city in titles and headings, add FAQs in customers' words, keep your Google Business Profile complete with real photos and reviews, and make sure the site is fast on mobile.

Do I need a separate page for each generator size?

A page per capacity band is usually the right balance. Grouping sizes into sensible bands avoids dozens of thin pages while still matching searches like "125 kVA generator on rent". Each page needs its own uses, price factors and FAQs.

Should I show rental prices on my website?

You don't have to. Explaining what affects the price answers the first question and leads naturally to a quote request. If you show indicative rates, label them as approximate and keep them up to date.

Should I create a page for every city or area I serve?

Only for areas you genuinely serve and can write something specific about. Naming the localities you cover in your FAQs and service area text is often enough, and avoids near-duplicate pages.

What should a generator rental enquiry form ask?

Capacity (with a "not sure" option), site location, start date, duration, single or three phase, name and phone number. Keep it short; details such as fuel and operator can be confirmed on the call.

How much does an equipment rental website cost?

It depends on the number of pages and features. My website cost guide puts most small business sites in India at ₹15,000 to ₹40,000, and larger business or B2B sites at ₹35,000 to ₹80,000.
