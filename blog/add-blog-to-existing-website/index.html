How to Add a Blog to Your Existing Business Website

How to add a blog to an existing website the right way: subfolder vs subdomain, WordPress setup, design integration, categories, linking to services and a sustainable publishing plan.

Sameer Gupta 27 September 2026 · 3 min read

Key takeaways
  • Put the blog in a /blog/ subfolder on your main domain.
  • Design posts for reading and enquiries, with categories aligned to services.
  • Link posts to service pages and start with answers to common questions.

A blog helps your website answer customer questions, attract search traffic and show expertise. If your site doesn't have one yet, it's usually easy to add, as long as it's set up the right way.

Not sure a blog is worth it yet? See how to rank a service business website without a blog.

Subfolder vs subdomain

A blog at yourdomain.com/blog/ (subfolder) keeps everything under one site and is generally the simplest, most effective choice for small businesses. A subdomain (blog.yourdomain.com) is treated more like a separate site and needs its own authority built up.

If your site is on WordPress

  • Create a Blog page and set it as the posts page
  • Design post and archive templates that match your site
  • Set up categories aligned with your services
  • Configure your SEO plugin for posts (titles, schema, sitemap)

If your site isn't on WordPress

You can use your platform's built-in blog, install WordPress in a /blog/ subfolder on the same hosting, or migrate the whole site. Choose what's easiest to maintain long term.

Design it for reading and enquiries

  • Readable typography and clear headings
  • Author information and dates
  • Related services and calls to action within and after articles
  • Related articles to keep readers exploring

This site's article pages show one approach: key takeaways, a contents sidebar, a help box and related services.

Connect posts to services

Every article should link to the relevant service page, and service pages should link to helpful articles; see internal linking.

Plan content you can sustain

Start with 5–10 articles answering the most common customer questions, then publish consistently; see content calendars.
