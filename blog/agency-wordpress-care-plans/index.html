White-Label WordPress Care Plans: What Agencies Can Offer Clients

How agencies can offer WordPress care plans under their own brand: what to include, tiers by scope, client reports, exclusions and working with a partner.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • A care plan bundles updates, backups, uptime and security monitoring, functional checks and an allowance of small edits into one monthly service.
  • Build tiers around scope and response times, from brochure sites to stores, and define exactly what counts as a small edit.
  • A white-label partner works through your agency with separate logins and an NDA, while you send the branded report and spell out exclusions.

For most agency websites, launch isn't the end. A few weeks later the client wants a new team photo, a plugin needs updating, or a form quietly stops sending emails. A care plan turns that scattered after-launch work into a defined monthly service, and a white-label partner can handle the technical side while your client only ever deals with your agency. This guide covers what to include, how to structure tiers and what to leave out. For outsourcing the build itself, see white-label WordPress development.

Why care plans suit agencies

  • Recurring work: predictable monthly income alongside one-off projects
  • The relationship continues: clients stay with you instead of drifting to whoever fixes their site next
  • Fewer emergencies for your designers: updates, backups and monitoring happen on schedule, not in a panic
  • Your portfolio stays healthy: a slow or hacked site you built still reflects on you
  • A natural route to new projects: regular contact brings up needs for landing pages, redesigns or SEO

What a care plan includes

The core is similar for most WordPress sites. The table shows what the partner does and what your client actually sees.

TaskWhat the work involvesWhat the client sees
UpdatesWordPress core, plugins and theme updated on a schedule; bigger updates tested on staging firstAn update summary in the monthly report
BackupsAutomatic off-site backups with an agreed retention period, and restores tested from time to timeConfirmation that backups ran and were checked
Uptime monitoringRegular automated checks, with alerts going to the partner and your teamAn uptime record, with any downtime explained
SecurityFirewall, malware scanning, login protection, two-factor authentication for admins and a review of user accountsSecurity status and anything found
Functional checksTest form submissions, and checkout for stores, after updatesConfidence that enquiries and orders still arrive
Small editsText, image and price changes, a new team member or a blog post upload, within an agreed time allowanceRequests completed, listed in the report
RenewalsTracking domain, SSL and premium licence expiry datesReminders in advance, with no surprise expiries

Structuring tiers by scope

Tiers work best when they differ in what's covered and how fast you respond, not just in price. A common structure has three levels:

TierSuited toTypically covers
EssentialBrochure sites that rarely changeUpdates, backups, uptime and security monitoring, and a short monthly report
GrowthLead-generation sites that change oftenEverything in Essential, plus a monthly allowance for small edits, form testing, speed checks and a Search Console review
Commerce or PriorityWooCommerce stores and business-critical sitesEverything in Growth, plus more frequent backups, checkout testing after every update and faster agreed response times

You set your own prices, but the partner's cost will depend on site size, the number of plugins, whether it's a store and the response times you promise. Decide these points upfront:

  • What counts as a small edit: for example, a task that fits within a set amount of time and needs no new design work
  • Unused time: does it roll over or reset each month?
  • Response times: separate targets for emergencies (site down, hacked) and routine requests
  • Onboarding: sites you didn't build need a health check and any urgent fixes before they join a plan

How the partner works behind your brand

  • Requests come to you: clients email your support address or helpdesk, and you pass requests to the partner through a shared project tool
  • No direct contact: the partner never emails or calls your clients unless you've agreed otherwise, and this is written into an NDA with a non-solicitation clause
  • Separate logins: the partner gets their own administrator account on each site, never a shared password, so activity is traceable and access can be removed cleanly
  • Branded tools: several maintenance dashboards and reporting tools let you add your own logo and colours; check current plans
  • An emergency path: agree who receives downtime alerts out of hours and who tells the client
  • A site register: one document per client listing hosting, DNS, plugins, licences and who owns each account

Reporting to clients

The monthly report is the only part of maintenance most clients ever see, so it carries a lot of weight. Keep it short, branded as your agency and written in plain language:

  1. A two-line summary at the top: all healthy, or what needs attention
  2. Updates applied, and anything that needed fixing afterwards
  3. Backup status and uptime for the month
  4. Security scan results
  5. Edits completed from the client's requests
  6. Upcoming renewals and recommendations

A practical split is for the partner to prepare the data and a draft, and for your account manager to review it, add context and send it. Recommendations are often where the next project starts, so don't skip them.

What's excluded

Many disagreements about care plans come from unspoken assumptions about scope. Spell out what isn't covered, both in the plan document and in your client contract:

  • New pages, new features and redesigns
  • Content writing, ongoing SEO campaigns and ad management, unless you sell these separately
  • Hosting fees and premium plugin licences, unless the plan explicitly includes them
  • Fixing problems caused by changes made outside the plan, such as a client installing new plugins
  • Malware clean-up: some plans include it and some charge separately, so say which yours does
  • Outages at third-party services such as hosting providers or payment gateways, which you can monitor but not fix

Clients often mix up bugs and change requests; bug or change request? is a useful explainer to share with them.

Getting started

Start small. Put two or three existing clients on a plan, run it with your partner for a few months, and refine the request process and report before offering it more widely. On new projects, offer the plan at handover, when its value is easiest to explain and the site is in its best shape.
