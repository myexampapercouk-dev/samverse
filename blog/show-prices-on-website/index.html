Should You Show Prices on Your Business Website? Fixed Prices, Ranges, Starting-From, Packages, Calculators or Price on Request

Should you show prices on your website? Compare fixed prices, ranges, starting-from prices, packages, calculators and price on request, with examples.

Key takeaways
  • Most service businesses benefit from some price guidance, such as a range, a starting-from price or packages, even when every job is quoted individually.
  • A price needs context next to it: what is included, what is not, whether GST is extra and what moves the cost up or down.
  • If you are unsure, add price guidance to one page or landing page and compare the number and quality of enquiries over a couple of months.

Show prices on your website if most of your work fits predictable packages; if every job is custom, show a price range or a "starting from" price with what affects it, rather than only "price on request". Most visitors want a rough idea before they contact you, and a little price guidance usually filters out poor-fit enquiries and builds trust. Keep "price on request" for genuinely bespoke or fast-changing work, and even then explain what drives the cost.

Why price guidance usually helps

  • It filters enquiries. People far outside your price range are less likely to contact you, so you spend less time on calls that go nowhere.
  • It builds trust. Openness about money feels honest, especially for first-time buyers who fear being overcharged.
  • It saves repeated conversations about the same basic question.
  • It answers real searches. People search for the cost of a service, often with a city name. A page that answers that question has a chance to appear for it.
  • It sets an anchor. A visitor who has seen your range arrives with realistic expectations, which makes the quote conversation easier.

When "price on request" makes sense

Some businesses have good reasons not to publish prices: projects that are entirely bespoke, prices tied to volatile material costs, tender-based B2B work, or professions with rules on advertising fees. Even then, give visitors something to go on:

  • What affects the price, in plain language.
  • Typical project sizes or minimum order quantities.
  • What information you need to quote, so the first enquiry is useful.
  • How quickly you'll respond with a quote, if you can commit to it.

A short quote form that asks the right questions does part of this work; multi-step forms show how to gather details without putting people off.

How to show prices well

Starting-from prices

Say what the starting price includes, and make sure a real customer can actually buy at that level. A starting price that every quote ends up well above damages trust more than no price at all.

Price ranges

Give a range for each type of job and explain where projects fall within it. A few worked examples, such as "a three-bedroom flat with standard finishes usually sits in the middle of the range", make a range far more useful.

Packages

Two to four tiers work best. Name each one by who it's for, list what's included and what isn't, and keep the differences obvious. Many businesses highlight the middle tier as the usual choice.

Calculators

A calculator suits prices driven by a few clear inputs. Show the result as a range, label it as an estimate, state the assumptions, and offer an easy next step to an exact quote.

Minimum budgets or order sizes

If you can't price individual jobs but don't take very small ones, say so: a minimum project budget for agencies and consultants, or a minimum order quantity for manufacturers and wholesalers. It's one line of text, and it quietly removes a whole category of enquiries you'd have to turn down anyway.

Cost guides

For complex work, a detailed article explaining costs, what drives them and typical ranges can rank in search and pre-qualify readers at the same time. WordPress website cost in India is an example of this format.

What to put next to a price

A bare number invites comparison shopping; a number with context feels fair. Next to every price or range, add:

  1. What's included, as a short list.
  2. What isn't, such as materials, travel, third-party fees or licences.
  3. Tax: whether GST is included or extra.
  4. What moves the price up or down.
  5. When it was last updated, if prices change often.
  6. A clear next step to an exact quote.

Prices on landing pages and in ads

If an ad mentions a price or offer, the landing page must show the same price clearly, with any conditions. A mismatch wastes the click and can breach ad platforms' policies on misleading claims, so check the current rules for the platforms you use. When the price is your main advantage, put it near the headline; when it isn't, place it after the benefits and proof, so visitors see the value before the number.

Landing pages are also the easiest place to test pricing. Run two versions, one with a starting price and one without, to similar traffic, and compare the number and quality of enquiries rather than clicks.

Regulated professions and legal points

Some professions, such as law and medicine, have rules on advertising and on how fees can be presented. Check your professional body's current guidelines before publishing fees. If you sell directly to consumers, check the current rules on showing prices, including taxes and charges; your CA or lawyer can confirm what applies to you.

How to tell whether it's working

Add price guidance to one important page, then compare the following month or two with the period before: the number of enquiries, how many were a good fit, and how many became customers. Track form submissions, calls and WhatsApp clicks as key events, as GA4 events explained shows, and keep a simple lead sheet with the source and outcome of each enquiry. Pricing pages for services in India: practical details

Indian buyers compare prices across WhatsApp, marketplaces and competitor sites before they call, so small details on a price page make a real difference:

  • Say whether GST is included. "Plus GST" or "inclusive of GST" next to every price avoids an awkward conversation later. If you are not GST-registered, say prices are final rather than leaving it unclear; check with your CA how to word it.
  • Show what is excluded. Travel outside the city, materials, government fees, third-party licences or hosting renewals are common surprises. List them under the price, not in a separate terms page.
  • Explain payment terms. Advance and balance, milestones, UPI or bank transfer, and whether you accept card payments online. For products, mention COD if it is available.
  • Use the rupee symbol and Indian number format. Write amounts the way your buyers read them, with lakh and thousand grouping, and avoid mixing currencies on one page unless you serve overseas clients too.
  • Date your prices. A short "prices valid from" line tells visitors the page is maintained and protects you when costs change.

Price pages and search queries

People search with phrases such as "website maintenance charges", "price list", "packages" and "cost in Pune" or another city. If you want a pricing page to appear for those searches, use those words naturally in the page title, one heading and a question in the FAQs, and answer the question in the first lines rather than after a long introduction.

When to update prices on the site

Review the page whenever your rates change, when you add or drop a package, and at least once a year. Old prices left online lead to disputes and erode trust faster than having no prices at all. If you run seasonal offers, show them as a separate line with an end date rather than editing the main price and forgetting to change it back.

FAQs

Should a service business show prices on its website?

Usually some price guidance helps, even if it's only a range or a starting price with what affects it. Fully hidden pricing suits only genuinely bespoke or fast-changing work.

Will competitors undercut me if I show prices?

They may see them, but most competitors can find your prices anyway by asking for a quote. Customers who choose only on price were unlikely to be your best customers; clear inclusions and proof help you compete on value.

Is "starting from" pricing misleading?

Not if a real customer can buy at that price and you say what it includes. It becomes misleading when almost every quote ends up far higher.

Should prices on my website include GST?

Say clearly either way. For consumer-facing prices, check the current rules on showing taxes, and ask your CA what applies to your business.

What if my prices change often?

Show ranges or starting prices rather than exact figures, add the date they were last updated, and keep them in one place, such as a pricing section or cost guide that other pages link to, so there's only one page to update.

Does showing prices help SEO?

It can. People search for the cost of services, and a page that answers that question clearly has a chance to rank for it. A detailed cost guide often works better than a bare price list.
