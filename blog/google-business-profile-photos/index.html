Google Business Profile Photos: Which Photos to Upload, Sizes and Formats, Customer Photos, Videos and the Geotagging Myth

Key takeaways
  • Cover the full set: a square logo, a representative cover photo, exterior shots from where customers arrive, interior, team, at-work, and real products or finished work.
  • Follow the current guidance from Google on formats and sizes, keep photos real and lightly edited, and add a few new ones whenever something changes rather than uploading hundreds at once.
  • Geotags and keyword file names have no confirmed ranking benefit; what helps is honest, current photos that match your website and win the click, the call and the visit.

Upload a square logo, a cover photo that sums up the business, exterior photos taken from where customers actually arrive, interior, team and at-work photos, and real photos of your products or finished work. Add a few new ones whenever something changes, keep an eye on photos customers add, and ignore tricks such as geotagging, which has no confirmed ranking benefit. Photos mainly win the click, the call and the visit from people already looking at your profile.

The photo set every profile needs

PhotoWhat to showTips
LogoYour logo as it appears on your signboard and websiteSquare, readable at small sizes, on a plain background
CoverThe single image that best represents you: shopfront, dining room, team at work or a signature projectLandscape, sharp, well lit, no text or offers on it
ExteriorHow to recognise you on arrivalShoot from the road and the approach customers use, with the signboard and a nearby landmark visible
InteriorReception, waiting area, showroom, seating, treatment roomsLights on, clutter cleared, wide angle but not distorted
TeamThe owner, doctor, chef or techniciansNatural, with consent; uniforms help
At workThe job being doneHands and process rather than posed smiles
Products or workBest sellers, popular dishes, finished projects, before and afterReal items you sell or work you did, never stock images

Depending on your category, Google may offer extra types such as food and drink, menu, rooms or products. Google may also choose a different photo to show first, including a customer's, if it judges that more relevant to the search, so every photo in the set should represent you well.

Photo ideas by type of business

  • Clinics: the entrance and signboard, reception, a clean consultation room, equipment you mention on your website, and the doctor. Never photograph patients without explicit consent, and avoid anything that shows patient records on screens or desks.
  • Restaurants and cafes: the dining area at a busy but tidy moment, signature dishes in natural light, the counter, outdoor seating and the menu board, kept current when prices change.
  • Service-area businesses such as plumbers, pest control or AC repair: branded vehicles, uniforms, the team on site, tools and finished jobs. Skip exterior shots if you have no customer-facing premises.
  • Shops and showrooms: the shopfront from the street, aisles and displays, popular products and the billing counter, refreshed when stock or layout changes.
  • Hotels and homestays: every room type, bathrooms, the view, common areas, food and the approach road, which guests in hill and rural areas genuinely need.
  • Manufacturers and B2B firms: the factory gate, production lines, quality checks, packing and dispatch, with permission for anything confidential.
  • Coaching institutes and schools: classrooms, the library, labs and the building, with written consent for any students shown.

Sizes, formats and quality

At the time of writing, Google's help pages ask for JPG or PNG files between 10 KB and 5 MB, at least 250 pixels on each side, with 720 by 720 pixels recommended, in focus, well lit and without heavy filters or alterations. Video guidance is up to 30 seconds and 75 MB, at 720p or higher. Google updates these figures from time to time, so check the current help page before a big upload.

In practice, a recent phone camera is more than enough. A few rules matter more than resolution:

  • Real and recent: photos should show your business as it is now, not before the renovation or after a professional shoot that doesn't match reality.
  • No text overlays: offers, phone numbers and slogans belong in posts, not in photos.
  • Light editing only: straighten, crop and correct brightness; avoid filters that change colours.
  • No stock or borrowed images: photos of someone else's work can be removed and undermine trust; see stock photos vs real photos.

Uploads can take time to appear, and occasionally one isn't approved. If that happens, check it against the guidance above and try a sharper, more representative image.

How to take photos customers trust

The basics of lighting and framing are covered in how to prepare photos for your website. For your profile specifically:

  • Shoot the exterior during opening hours with shutters up and lights on, so it looks like what customers will find
  • Take exterior shots from both directions of traffic, and one that includes the nearest landmark
  • Photograph the entrance up close if it is hard to find, such as a first-floor clinic or a shop inside a complex
  • Ask staff before photographing them, and take extra care with customers and children
  • Show the real size of the space; customers notice when a waiting room looks bigger online than in person

How often to add photos

Google gives no official number, and there's no evidence that uploading daily or in huge batches helps. A steady rhythm keeps the profile current and shows the business is active:

  • When something changes: renovation, new signboard, new menu, new products or a new team member
  • As work is completed: contractors, designers and installers can add finished jobs every few weeks
  • Seasonally: festival displays, new collections or seasonal menus
  • Every few months: review the full set and remove photos of old signboards, former staff or a branch that has closed

Customer photos: managing and reporting them

Customers can add photos to your profile on their own or with a review. They often persuade more than yours because they are unpolished and independent. You can't delete them, but you can manage them:

  • Check the customer photos tab in your profile regularly, not just your own uploads
  • Report photos that break Google's policies: offensive content, spam, a different business, or photos exposing someone's private information
  • Don't report photos simply because they're unflattering; fix the messy counter or faded signboard instead
  • Invite happy customers to add a photo with their review, but never offer anything in return

Reporting doesn't guarantee removal, because Google reviews each report against its own policies. If a photo comes with an angry review, reply to the review calmly; how to handle negative reviews covers what to say.

Videos on your profile

Short videos work well for things photos can't show: a walkthrough from the entrance to the counter, a dish being plated, a machine running, or a before-and-after reveal. Keep them short, steady and silent-friendly, since many people watch without sound. A longer video belongs on YouTube and your website; video on your business website covers hosting and speed.

Geotagging and other photo myths

MythWhat is actually known
Geotagging photos boosts local rankingsNo confirmed benefit. Google hasn't said it uses photo GPS data for local rankings, and location data is widely reported to be stripped on upload. Your verified address or service area already tells Google where you are.
Keyword file names help the profile rankNo confirmed benefit on your profile. Descriptive file names do help images on your own website, which is a separate matter.
More photos means higher rankingsGoogle describes local results as based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Photos help people choose you once they see you.
Daily uploads keep you at the topBatches of near-identical photos add nothing for customers. Useful, varied photos added over time do.

Be wary of anyone selling photo tricks as a ranking shortcut. The reliable work is in accurate categories and details, genuine reviews and a website that backs up the profile, as the local SEO guide explains.

Keep profile and website photos consistent

Customers often check the website after the profile, or the reverse. Use the same exterior photo on your contact page that you use on your profile, the same team photos on your About page, and the same product and project photos on service pages. For the website, compress and resize them, use WebP where possible and write descriptive alt text; image SEO covers file names, alt text and Google Images. When you take a new batch, update both places on the same day.

FAQs

What size should Google Business Profile photos be?

At the time of writing, Google recommends JPG or PNG files of 720 by 720 pixels, with a minimum of 250 pixels on each side and a file size between 10 KB and 5 MB. Check the current help page, as the figures change occasionally.

Why isn't my chosen cover photo showing first?

Google may show a different photo, sometimes a customer's, if it judges it more relevant to the search. You can't fully control this, so make sure every photo represents you well.

Do geotagged photos help my profile rank?

There's no confirmed benefit. Location data is widely reported to be removed when photos are uploaded, and your verified address or service area already tells Google where you are.

Can I delete a photo a customer added?

No, but you can report it if it breaks Google's policies, for example if it is offensive, spam, shows a different business or exposes private information. Google decides whether to remove it.

How many photos should I add?

There's no magic number. Cover every photo type in the set above, then add a few new real photos whenever something changes or a job is finished.
