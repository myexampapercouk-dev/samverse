Google Business Profile Photos: What to Add, How Often and Myths to Ignore

Which photos to add to your Google Business Profile, choosing a cover and logo, how often to add more, customer photos, and why geotags aren't a ranking trick.

Key takeaways
  • Add real exterior, interior, team, at-work and product or project photos, plus a clean logo and a strong cover photo.
  • Add new photos steadily as things change, and report customer photos only when they break Google's policies.
  • Geotagging and keyword file names have no confirmed ranking benefit; photos mainly help you win clicks, calls and visits.

For many local searches, your Google Business Profile photos are the first look a customer gets at your business, often before they ever see your website. They help people recognise your shopfront, judge the quality of your work and decide whether to call. Our Google Business Profile checklist covers the whole profile, and our guide to posts, offers and keeping your profile active covers regular updates. This article goes deeper on photos: what to add, how often, how to handle customer photos and which popular tips to ignore.

Why photos matter on your profile

When someone compares three businesses in the map results, photos answer questions the text can't. Does the clinic look clean? Is the restaurant right for a family dinner? Has this contractor done jobs like mine? A profile with only a logo and one blurry exterior shot makes people wonder whether the business is still open.

Be realistic about what photos do for rankings. Google suggests adding photos as part of keeping your profile complete, but it describes local results as based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Think of photos as the way you win the click, the call and the visit from people who are already looking at your profile.

Cover photo and logo

  • Logo: a clean, square version of your logo that's readable at small sizes and matches your website and signage
  • Cover photo: the image that best represents your business at a glance, such as your shopfront, your dining area, your team at work or a signature project; choose a sharp, well-lit landscape photo without text on it

Google doesn't always show your chosen cover photo first. It may display a different image, sometimes one a customer uploaded, if it judges that more relevant to the search. You can't fully control this, but a strong set of photos across every type means whichever one appears still represents you well.

Which photos to add

Photo typeWhat it tells customersIdeas
ExteriorHow to recognise you when they arriveShopfront and signboard from the street, the building entrance, the floor sign or gate
InteriorWhat it's like insideReception or waiting area, showroom, seating, treatment rooms, workspaces
TeamWho they'll deal withOwner and staff, the doctor in the clinic, technicians in uniform
At workHow you do the jobAn installation in progress, a chef in the kitchen, a stylist with a client (with consent)
Work and productsWhat they'll getFinished projects, before-and-after shots, best-selling products, popular dishes

Depending on your category, Google may offer extra photo types such as food and drink, rooms or products. Whatever the type, use real photos of your own business. Stock images, text-heavy posters and photos of somewhere else don't belong on your profile, and Google's guidelines ask for photos that represent reality without heavy filters or editing.

Service-area businesses without a shopfront can skip the exterior and focus on branded vehicles, uniforms, the team on site and finished jobs.

Taking photos that work

  • Shoot in daylight and keep the subject in focus; a recent phone is fine
  • Tidy up first: clear counters, switch on the lights and move clutter out of frame
  • Get permission from customers, patients and staff before photographing them, and take extra care with children
  • Keep photos honest; if the waiting area is small, show it as it is, because customers notice when reality doesn't match
  • Check Google's current help pages for supported file formats and sizes before uploading

The same photos often work on your website too; see how to prepare photos for your website.

How often to add new photos

There's no official number. A steady rhythm works better than uploading a hundred photos once and then forgetting the profile for two years:

  • Add a few new photos whenever something changes: a renovation, new stock, a new menu or a new team member
  • Add photos of recent work as you complete it, especially if you're a contractor or designer
  • Replace photos that are out of date, such as an old signboard, a former staff member or a closed branch
  • Look over the full set every few months as part of your regular profile check

Recent photos show that the business is active and set accurate expectations. New uploads can take a while to appear, and occasionally one isn't approved; if that happens, check it against Google's photo guidelines and try a better image.

Customer photos, and reporting bad ones

Customers can add photos to your profile, either on their own or with a review. These are often more persuasive than yours because they're unpolished and independent. You can't delete them, but you can manage them:

  • Look through customer photos regularly, not just your own uploads
  • Report photos that break Google's policies, such as offensive content, spam, images of a different business or photos that expose someone's private information
  • Don't report photos just because they're unflattering; an honest photo of a messy counter is a reason to fix the counter
  • Invite happy customers to add a photo with their review, but never offer anything in return

Reporting doesn't guarantee removal, because Google reviews each report against its own policies.

Myths to ignore: geotags, file names and daily uploads

A lot of photo advice in local SEO isn't backed by evidence:

  • Geotagging: adding GPS coordinates to photo files before uploading is often sold as a ranking boost. There's no confirmed ranking benefit. Google hasn't said it uses geotags for local rankings, and it's widely reported that location data is stripped from photos when they're uploaded. Your verified address or service area already tells Google where you are.
  • Keyword file names: renaming files to "best-dentist-in-jaipur.jpg" before uploading them to your profile has no confirmed benefit either. Descriptive file names do help images on your own website, which is a different matter.
  • Uploading every day: adding large batches of near-identical photos won't make your profile rank higher. Useful, varied photos added over time do more for customers.

Be wary of anyone selling photo tricks as a shortcut. The reliable local SEO work is in accurate categories and details, genuine reviews and a website that backs up your profile.
