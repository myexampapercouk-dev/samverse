Key takeaways
  • Website maintenance cost in India has two parts: the monthly maintenance plan and the yearly running costs of domain, hosting and licences.
  • The monthly price depends mostly on the type of site, the number of plugins and integrations, content-change time and how fast emergencies are handled.
  • Compare maintenance packages by written scope, backup and restore process and response times, not by the headline monthly charge.

For small and medium business websites in India, freelance WordPress maintenance plans typically cost about ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 per month, depending on the size of the site, how often it changes and whether it is an online store. On top of maintenance, every site has yearly running costs: a domain (₹500 to ₹1,200 a year), hosting (₹2,500 to ₹15,000 a year) and any premium theme or plugin licences.

This guide breaks down the monthly and yearly cost of running a WordPress website in India, what website maintenance packages should include, what usually costs extra, and how to choose between a freelancer, an agency, your host or doing it yourself.

What a website maintenance package includes

A proper package should cover:

  • WordPress core, plugin and theme updates, with a backup before each round and checks afterwards
  • Daily off-site backups and a tested restore process
  • Security monitoring, malware scanning and login protection
  • Uptime monitoring with alerts that someone acts on
  • Monthly tests of contact forms and, for stores, checkout and payment
  • A fixed amount of content-change time each month
  • A monthly report of what was done

Types of website maintenance packages

  • Basic care: updates, backups, security and uptime monitoring for brochure sites that rarely change.
  • Standard: basic care plus monthly content-change time and form testing; suits most service businesses.
  • Store or business-critical: more frequent backups, checkout testing after every update, payment gateway and shipping plugin attention and faster emergency response; suits WooCommerce stores and booking sites.
  • AMC (annual maintenance contract): any of the above billed yearly, often with a lower effective monthly price but less flexibility to cancel.
  • Pay-as-you-go: no plan; you pay per task when something breaks or needs changing. Cheap in quiet months, risky because nobody is watching the site.

What affects the monthly price

  • Type of site: a five-page brochure site is far simpler to maintain than a WooCommerce store with payments, shipping and stock.
  • Number of plugins and integrations: each plugin is something to update and test; integrations with CRMs, ERPs or courier services add more checks.
  • Page builder and theme: heavy builders and outdated custom themes need more care during updates.
  • Content-change time: plans with more included edits cost more.
  • Response time: same-day or weekend emergency cover costs more than next-working-day support.
  • Hosting included or not: some plans bundle managed hosting.
  • Condition of the site: a site with many outdated plugins usually needs a one-time clean-up before a regular plan makes sense.

What usually costs extra

  • New pages, new features and design changes
  • Major upgrades, such as moving an old theme to a newer PHP version
  • Malware cleanup after a hack, in some plans, which is usually quoted as a separate repair job
  • SEO, content writing and ad management
  • Premium licences, hosting and domain renewals, unless bundled

Other costs that surprise site owners include email, paid plugin renewals and SSL on some hosts; check each one before you renew.

How costs differ for WooCommerce and Shopify stores

A WooCommerce store usually sits at the higher end of the maintenance range because there is more to test and more that can break: payment gateways such as Razorpay, COD rules, shipping integrations like Shiprocket, GST invoices and stock sync. The WooCommerce maintenance checklist lists the extra checks. A Shopify store has no core or hosting updates to manage, so maintenance is mainly app reviews, theme updates, speed checks and content changes; the cost is usually lower, but paid apps become the running cost to watch.

One-time costs before a maintenance plan starts

Many sites need catch-up work before regular maintenance is practical. Typical one-time tasks include updating plugins that are months or years behind, replacing abandoned plugins, moving to a supported PHP version, removing unused themes and admin accounts, fixing a broken backup setup and cleaning malware if the site was compromised. A good provider audits the site first and quotes this separately, so the monthly charge reflects ongoing care rather than past neglect. If your site was built long ago and never updated, expect this step.

Monthly plan or AMC: which is cheaper?

An annual maintenance contract (AMC) is common in India, especially for businesses used to AMCs for equipment and software. Paying yearly can lower the effective monthly charge and simplifies accounts, with one GST invoice instead of twelve. The trade-off is commitment: if the service disappoints, you are tied in until renewal. A sensible path is a few months on a monthly plan, then an AMC once you trust the provider. Either way, the scope document should be identical, so you compare the two on price alone.

How to lower maintenance costs without cutting corners

  • Remove plugins you don't need; fewer plugins means fewer updates and conflicts.
  • Choose a well-supported theme rather than an obscure one that may stop receiving updates.
  • Batch content changes into one request per month instead of many small ones.
  • Host with a reliable provider so less time goes on server problems.
  • Learn simple text edits yourself and leave updates and security to the plan.

Red flags in cheap maintenance offers

  • No written scope, just "complete maintenance" or "full support".
  • Backups stored only on the same server as the site.
  • Updates run automatically with nobody checking the site afterwards.
  • Hosting or domain registered in the provider's name rather than yours.
  • No report, or a generic report that never mentions your site's specific plugins.
  • Admin access withheld from you, the owner.

A low monthly charge with these gaps often costs more in the end, when a broken update or hack has to be fixed as an emergency job.

Budgeting the yearly total

To budget the full yearly cost, add your domain and hosting renewals, premium licence renewals, twelve months of the maintenance plan (or the AMC amount) and a small reserve for one-off changes such as a new page or feature during the year. Note renewal dates in one calendar so nothing expires unexpectedly; an expired domain or SSL certificate takes the site offline just as surely as a hack.

What you should receive for the money each month

Whatever the price, a maintenance plan should leave visible evidence of work. Each month you should be able to see which updates ran and when, that backups exist and where, uptime for the month, the result of form and checkout tests, the content changes completed, and any issue found with a recommendation. If you can't see this, ask for it; a provider doing the work properly will have the information ready.

Does the cost differ between cities?

Maintenance is remote work, so your city matters less than the provider's experience and the scope. A business in Mumbai, Delhi or a smaller city can work with a provider anywhere in India. What matters is overlap in working hours, a clear way to reach someone in an emergency, and GST invoicing if your business needs it. If you prefer someone local, see the WordPress developer in India page for the cities I cover.

Who pays for plugin and theme licences?

Premium licences are a recurring cost that is easy to overlook. Some providers use their own agency licences on client sites; that is convenient, but if you leave, the licence goes with them and updates stop. Ideally the licences are bought in your business name, so renewals and access stay with you. Ask which licences your site depends on, when each renews, and who pays.

Is website maintenance worth the cost?

It is worth it when the website brings in enquiries or orders. One unnoticed broken form, an expired SSL certificate or a hacked site can cost more in lost business and repair than a year of maintenance. If the site brings in nothing, fix that first; maintaining an ineffective site carefully doesn't help. Website maintenance vs management explains the difference between keeping a site running and actively improving it.

How to choose a maintenance plan

  1. Ask for the written scope, including what is excluded.
  2. Ask where backups are stored and when a restore was last tested.
  3. Ask how updates are checked afterwards.
  4. Confirm response times for routine changes and emergencies.
  5. Make sure hosting, domain and licences stay in your name.
  6. Ask for a sample monthly report.

FAQs

What are website maintenance charges in India?

Freelance WordPress maintenance plans for small and medium sites typically cost about ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month. Simple brochure sites sit at the lower end and WooCommerce stores at the higher end.

What is the monthly cost of a WordPress website?

If you pay for hosting yearly, the only monthly cost is a maintenance plan, typically ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month from a freelancer, or nothing if you maintain the site yourself.

What is the yearly cost of a WordPress website?

At minimum, your domain (about ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year) and hosting (about ₹2,500 to ₹15,000 a year), plus any premium theme or plugin licences and a maintenance plan if you use one.

What do website maintenance packages include?

Updates with backups and checks, daily off-site backups, security and uptime monitoring, form and checkout tests, some content-change time and a monthly report. New features and redesigns are usually extra.

Is a website AMC better than a monthly maintenance plan?

An AMC is billed yearly and can work out cheaper per month, but you commit for the year. A monthly plan costs a little more but is easier to cancel if the service isn't right.

Can I maintain my WordPress website myself?

Yes, if you are comfortable taking backups, updating carefully, testing forms and restoring when something breaks. Many owners start this way and switch to a plan when the site becomes business-critical.
