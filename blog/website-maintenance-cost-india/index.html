For small and medium WordPress websites in India, freelance maintenance plans typically cost about ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 per month, depending on the size of the site, how often it changes and whether it's an online store. Agencies and large or business-critical sites cost more. On top of maintenance, every site has yearly running costs: a domain (₹500 to ₹1,200 a year), hosting (₹2,500 to ₹15,000 a year) and any premium theme or plugin licences.

These are typical market ranges, not fixed prices. Below is what each cost covers, what maintenance packages include, what moves the price and how to compare plans. For my own plans, you get a fixed monthly quote after a free review of your site; see WordPress maintenance.

WordPress itself is free. What you pay for after launch is the infrastructure and the care:

Types of website maintenance packages

Most providers offer a few levels. Names and inclusions vary, but they usually look like this:

PackageUsually includesSuits
BasicUpdates, backups, security scans and uptime monitoringSmall brochure sites that rarely change; the lower end of the range
BusinessEverything in basic, plus content changes, speed checks, form tests and a reportService businesses that update offers, prices or pages regularly
StoreEverything above, plus test orders after updates, order email checks, more frequent backups and database careWooCommerce stores; the higher end of the range, or above it for large stores
Pay per jobFixes and changes quoted individuallyOwners who handle updates themselves and need occasional help

Many Indian businesses know this as a website AMC (annual maintenance contract). An AMC paid yearly and a monthly plan can cover exactly the same work; the difference is the billing and how easily you can leave. Month-to-month plans keep the provider earning your business.

What affects the monthly price

  • Type of site: a WooCommerce store needs test orders, closer database care and faster response than a five-page brochure site
  • Number and type of plugins: page builders, payment gateways, memberships and booking plugins need more careful updating and testing
  • How much content work you expect: an hour of changes a month is very different from weekly product uploads
  • Backup frequency and retention: daily backups kept for a month cost less to run than near real-time backups for a busy store
  • Response time: how quickly problems are looked at, and whether that includes weekends and festivals
  • Condition of the site: a neglected site with outdated plugins needs catch-up work before regular maintenance can start
  • Who does it: freelancers, agencies and hosting companies have different overheads and levels of service

If you run a store, the WooCommerce maintenance checklist shows the extra work involved.

What usually costs extra

  • New pages, new features, redesigns and larger design changes
  • Large content uploads, such as hundreds of products
  • Premium plugin licences and hosting fees, which are paid to the vendors
  • Cleaning up a site that was already hacked before the plan started; see WordPress malware removal
  • SEO campaigns, content writing and ad management, which are separate services

Is website maintenance worth the cost?

  • Prevention is cheaper than repair. Cleaning a hacked site or rebuilding after data loss usually costs far more than months of regular upkeep, and takes the site offline while it happens.
  • Lost enquiries are invisible. A contact form that stops sending can cost leads for weeks before anyone notices.
  • Sites slow down over time. New plugins, images and content gradually drag speed down without someone checking.
  • Renewals get missed. An expired domain or premium licence can take a site or a key feature offline without warning.

How to choose a maintenance plan

  1. Ask exactly what's included each month, especially how much content work, and what happens to unused time
  2. Ask how updates are tested and what happens if one breaks the site
  3. Confirm backups are stored off-site, separate from your hosting, and that restores are included
  4. Ask for a sample monthly report
  5. Prefer month-to-month terms so you're not locked in
  6. Make sure you keep ownership of your hosting, domain and admin access

FAQs

How much does website maintenance cost in India?

Freelance WordPress maintenance plans for small and medium sites typically cost about ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month. Simple brochure sites sit at the lower end and WooCommerce stores at the higher end. Agencies, large sites and business-critical sites cost more.

What is the monthly cost of a WordPress website?

WordPress has no monthly fee of its own. If you pay for hosting yearly, the only monthly cost is a maintenance plan, typically ₹1,500 to ₹6,000 a month from a freelancer, or nothing if you maintain the site yourself.

What is the yearly cost of a WordPress website?

At minimum, your domain (about ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year) and hosting (about ₹2,500 to ₹15,000 a year), plus any premium theme or plugin licences, such as a premium theme or Elementor Pro at up to about ₹6,000 a year. Add a maintenance plan if you want someone to handle updates, backups and security.

What do website maintenance packages include?

A good package covers tested updates, off-site backups with restores, security scans, uptime monitoring, form and checkout checks, speed checks, a set amount of small content changes and a monthly report. New features, redesigns and large uploads are usually extra.

Is a website AMC better than a monthly maintenance plan?

Not necessarily; they can cover the same work. An annual contract may suit your accounts, but a month-to-month plan is easier to leave if the service isn't good. Compare what's actually done each month rather than the billing period.

Can I maintain my WordPress website myself?

Yes, if you're comfortable taking backups, updating carefully and fixing problems when an update breaks something. Use a checklist, free tools for backups and uptime monitoring, and set aside time every week or two. Many owners hand it over once the site becomes important to their business.
