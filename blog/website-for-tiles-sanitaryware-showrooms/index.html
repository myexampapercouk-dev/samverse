Websites for Tiles, Sanitaryware and Bathroom Fittings Showrooms

How tiles, sanitaryware and bathroom fittings showrooms can win more visits: catalogues by room and style, authorised brands, visualisers and builder enquiries.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Organise the catalogue by room, look, size and finish, with box coverage and honest notes about batch shade variation.
  • List only the brands you are authorised to sell, and write your own notes instead of copying brand catalogues.
  • Make booking a showroom visit the main action, and give builders and architects a separate project enquiry route.

People buying tiles, sanitaryware and bathroom fittings rarely decide in one visit. They browse for weeks on their phones, share options with family, an architect or a contractor, and only then come to the showroom to see colours, finishes and sizes in person. A good showroom website supports that whole process: it helps people shortlist, makes the visit easy to plan, and gives builders a quick way to ask for quantities and rates.

If you supply a wide mix of building materials such as cement, steel, plywood and paint, start with websites for hardware and building material suppliers. This guide is for showrooms where display, selection and design advice are the business.

What showroom buyers are trying to work out

Your visitors usually fall into two groups. Homeowners renovating a bathroom or kitchen, or building a house, want ideas and reassurance. Builders, contractors, architects and interior designers want availability, rates and delivery. Both are asking similar questions:

  • Do you have the look I have in mind, in the size and finish I need?
  • Which brands do you stock, and are you an authorised dealer?
  • Roughly what price range am I looking at?
  • Can I see it displayed, and when are you open?
  • Can you supply enough for the whole project, from the same batch, and deliver to site?

Every section of your website should answer at least one of these.

Organise the catalogue by room, style and size

Customers think in rooms and looks, not in your supplier's product codes. Let them browse in several ways:

  • By room: bathroom, kitchen, living and bedroom floors, balcony and parking, outdoor and elevation walls
  • By type and look: vitrified, ceramic, porcelain, GVT and PGVT, wood-look, marble-look, stone-look and anti-skid
  • By size and finish: common sizes such as 600 x 600 mm or 600 x 1200 mm, and matt, satin or glossy finishes
  • Sanitaryware and fittings: wall-hung and floor-mounted WCs, wash basins, faucets, showers, concealed cisterns and bath accessories

Each product page should show a close-up and, ideally, an installed photo, plus size, thickness, finish, where it can be used (floors, walls, wet areas, outdoors), pieces per box and the area each box covers. For WCs and fittings, add dimensions and practical details such as outlet type (S-trap or P-trap) or whether a concealed cistern is needed, with a note to confirm with their plumber.

Be honest about colour. Screens vary and tile shades can differ between batches, so encourage visitors to check in the showroom before ordering large quantities.

Show brands only where you are authorised

Brand names drive a lot of searches, so it's tempting to list every brand you can get hold of. Don't. List the brands you are actually authorised to sell, and if you can also source others on order, say so separately and plainly.

  • Ask your brand contacts about their rules for logo use, product photos and descriptions; many provide dealer assets
  • Never call yourself "official" or "exclusive" unless the brand has confirmed it in writing
  • Add your own notes to each range, such as what it suits and what customers like about it, rather than copying the brand catalogue word for word

Being exact here protects you, and it stops customers driving across town for a brand you don't carry.

Visualisers and inspiration, used sensibly

Room visualisers that let buyers try a tile on a sample room, or on a photo of their own room, can help some customers decide. Some tile brands offer their own and third-party tools also exist, so check what your brands allow and what any tool costs before committing. These tools are often heavy, so keep them on a dedicated page rather than the homepage.

Simpler options often work just as well:

  • Galleries of real installations, tagged with the products used
  • Curated collections such as "warm wood-look bathrooms" or "easy-clean kitchen walls"
  • A "send us a photo of your room" option, so your staff can suggest a few combinations

Make the showroom visit the main call to action

For most showrooms, the website's real job is getting the right people through the door. Make that easy:

  • Address, timings, parking, a map and photos of your displays, including any working bathroom setups
  • A short "book a showroom visit" form asking for the rooms involved, rough area, preferred time and whether an architect or contractor will join
  • A shortlist feature, so visitors can save products and show the list to your staff when they arrive
  • A WhatsApp button that pre-fills the product name or code; see adding WhatsApp to your website

If you're comfortable showing price bands or "starting from" prices per square foot, they help filter out mismatched enquiries. Just keep them updated, because supplier prices change.

A separate route for builders, contractors and architects

Trade buyers don't want inspiration galleries. Give them a clear "project and bulk enquiries" page with a form for project type, location, quantities per area, timeline and an option to upload a BOQ or drawing. Explain what you can offer, such as samples, site delivery, batch-matched supply for large orders, GST invoices and credit terms if you provide them.

A short list of projects you've supplied, shared with permission, and details of any architect or designer programme you run will help you win repeat trade business.

Local SEO and speed for an image-heavy catalogue

Most showroom customers search locally: "tiles showroom in {area}", "sanitaryware shop near me", "bathroom fittings in {city}" or "{brand} dealer in {city}". To show up for these searches:

  • Keep your Google Business Profile complete, with the right categories, timings and plenty of showroom photos; see the Google Business Profile checklist
  • Create brand pages only for brands you're authorised to sell, each with your own content
  • Publish buying guides that answer real questions: vitrified vs ceramic tiles, choosing anti-skid bathroom tiles, wall-hung vs floor-mounted WCs

Hundreds of large product photos can make a catalogue painfully slow on mobile data. Resize and compress images, use modern formats and make sure filters respond quickly; see image optimisation for WordPress.
