Landing Page Cost: What Affects the Price (and How to Brief One)

What drives the cost of a landing page: copy, template vs custom design, sections, forms, tracking, speed, A/B tests and campaign edits, and how to brief one.

Key takeaways
  • Landing page quotes vary because scope varies: copywriting, custom or template design and the number of sections all change the work involved.
  • Forms, integrations, conversion tracking and speed work are often where quotes differ most, so ask what each one includes.
  • Send every developer the same brief covering offer, traffic source, sections, tracking and campaign changes so quotes are comparable.

Ask for a landing page quote and you might hear anything from "a few hours' work" to a project that takes weeks. Both can be honest answers, because "landing page" covers everything from a template with your logo swapped in to a researched, custom-designed page connected to your CRM and ad tracking. Here's what drives the cost, and how to brief a landing page so the quotes you get are actually comparable.

Copywriting: who writes the words

The words do most of the selling on a landing page, and writing them well takes time. The cost depends on:

  • Who writes it: you, the developer or a specialist copywriter
  • Research: understanding your offer, audience, competitors and the ads that will point to the page
  • Versions: separate headlines or pages for different ad groups, cities or services
  • Languages: a Hindi or regional-language version means extra writing and checking, not just translation

Even if you write the copy yourself, having someone review it for clarity and for a close match with your ads is usually money well spent.

Design: template or from scratch

A ready-made template or page builder layout, adapted with your colours, fonts and photos, is the quickest route. A custom design, mocked up first in a tool like Figma and then built, takes longer but fits your brand and offer exactly.

ApproachSuitsTrade-off
Adapted templateQuick campaigns, testing a new offer, tight budgetsCan look generic and may carry unused code that slows it down
Custom design on your existing siteOngoing campaigns that should match your brandMore design and build time
Fully custom, lightweight buildLarger campaigns where speed and conversion matter mostMost time up front

Where the page lives matters too. A page on your existing WordPress site reuses its hosting and setup, while a separate domain or microsite needs its own hosting, SSL and upkeep.

Length and number of sections

A page for a simple offer, such as a free consultation, might need only a headline, benefits, proof and a form. A page for a considered purchase, like a real estate project, a course or a B2B service, often needs more: features, plans, a gallery, FAQs, comparison tables, a location map and testimonials. Each extra section means more writing, design and mobile testing, so the number of sections is one of the clearest cost drivers.

Supply your own photos, videos and testimonials where you can, because sourcing or creating them adds to the work.

Forms and integrations

A basic form that sends an email is simple. Costs rise with:

  • Multi-step forms or conditional questions
  • Sending leads to a CRM, Google Sheets or an email marketing tool
  • Instant WhatsApp, SMS or email alerts to your sales team
  • Appointment booking, payments or brochure downloads after the form
  • Spam protection and a proper thank-you page

Each connection needs setting up and testing end to end, ideally with real test submissions before the ads go live.

Tracking setup

If you're paying for clicks, tracking isn't optional. Setup can include Google Analytics 4 events, Google Ads conversion tracking, the Meta Pixel and Conversions API, Google Tag Manager, call and WhatsApp click tracking, and UTM tags so you know which campaign each lead came from. It takes longer when you advertise on several platforms or want leads matched back to campaigns in your CRM. Ask whether each quote includes tracking and which conversions will be tracked; see Google Ads conversion tracking on WordPress.

Speed work

Many ad clicks come from phones, often on patchy mobile data, and a slow page wastes clicks you've already paid for. Making a page load fast means compressing images, limiting fonts and scripts, avoiding heavy sliders and testing on real devices. A page built on a heavy theme, or carrying several tracking scripts, can need extra work to perform well. Ask whether speed testing is part of the quote and on which devices.

A/B testing and changes during a campaign

A landing page is rarely finished on launch day. Budget for:

  • A/B tests: building a second version of the headline, offer or form, setting up the test and reading the results, which only makes sense with enough traffic (see A/B testing landing pages)
  • Campaign edits: new offers, festival deadlines, price changes or extra sections based on questions your sales team hears
  • Turnaround: same-day changes during a live campaign usually cost more than changes that can wait

Agree up front whether changes are included for a set period, charged per change or covered by a monthly arrangement.

How to brief a landing page so quotes are comparable

Quotes vary most when each developer is guessing at a different scope. Send everyone the same short brief:

  1. Goal and offer: what visitors should do and what they get
  2. Traffic source: Google Ads, Meta ads, email or a mix, plus the ad copy if you have it
  3. Copy: supplied by you, edited, or written from scratch
  4. Design: template, custom design or matching your existing site, with two or three pages you like
  5. Sections: a rough list, from headline to FAQ
  6. Form and integrations: fields, where leads should go and who gets alerts
  7. Tracking: ad platforms and the conversions to track
  8. Where it lives: your existing site or a new domain
  9. Changes and testing: how long the campaign runs and whether you want A/B tests
  10. Deadline: when the ads are due to start

Then check every quote against the same list; how to compare website quotes explains what else to look for. And before choosing the cheapest option, read landing page mistakes that waste ad budget to see where cutting corners costs you later.
