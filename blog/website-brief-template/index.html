Website Brief Template: What to Send Your Web Developer for an Accurate Quote, With a Copy-and-Fill Version

Key takeaways
  • A website brief should cover your business, goals, audience, pages, features, content, examples, timeline and budget range, in plain language.
  • Features, page count, who writes content and integrations such as payments or CRM drive cost most, so describe those sections in the most detail.
  • Send the same brief to every developer you approach so quotes are comparable, and treat their questions as a sign of how carefully they read it.

A website brief is a short document that tells a web developer what your business does, what the website must achieve, which pages and features it needs, who will provide content, and your timeline and budget range. Sending a clear brief gets you accurate, comparable quotes instead of guesses; the copy-and-fill template below covers everything a developer needs and takes about an hour to complete.

It does not need to be technical or long. Two to four pages of honest answers beat a polished document full of jargon. Below is what each section is for, the template itself, and how to use it to compare quotes.

What a good brief does for you

  • Accurate quotes: developers price what you describe. Vague briefs get padded quotes to cover the unknown, or low quotes that grow later.
  • Comparable quotes: when everyone quotes the same scope, you can compare price, approach and timeline fairly.
  • Fewer disputes: the brief becomes the basis of the scope, which later decides what is a bug and what is a change.
  • Clearer thinking: writing it often shows you which features you actually need.

The website brief template, section by section

1. About your business

What you do, where you operate (one city, all of India, abroad), and what makes you different from competitors. Include your current website link if you have one.

2. Goals

What the site must achieve, in measurable terms if possible: more enquiries, online orders, appointment bookings, dealer applications, credibility for tenders. Rank your top three.

3. Audience

Who visits, what they want, and which device they mostly use. For many Indian businesses, most visitors arrive on phones, often from WhatsApp or Instagram links, which shapes the design. Mention languages if you need Hindi or a regional language.

4. Pages

A list of pages (a sitemap). Note which are standard (About, Contact) and which are many similar items (20 products, 50 projects, 10 doctors).

5. Features

Forms, WhatsApp buttons, booking, online payments, product catalogue, store, member login, multilingual content, blog, search, filters, calculators. Say which are must-have and which are nice-to-have.

6. Content

Who writes the text, who supplies photos, and whether you need copywriting, photography or translation. This is the most common cause of delays.

7. Design

Two or three websites you like and why (layout, colours, simplicity), plus your logo, brand colours and fonts if you have them. Mention any you dislike.

8. Technical

Preferred platform if any (WordPress, Shopify), current domain and hosting, business email, and integrations such as Razorpay, Shiprocket, a CRM, Google Analytics or an ERP.

9. Timeline and budget

Your launch date and whether it is fixed, plus a budget range. Sharing a range is not giving away your bargaining position; it helps developers propose what fits instead of quoting something you cannot use.

10. After launch

Who will update the site, whether you want training, and whether you need ongoing maintenance, hosting management or SEO.

How to fill in the sections that drive cost

Some sections change the quote far more than others. Spend most of your time on these:

SectionWhy it affects costWhat to specify
FeaturesEach integration or custom feature is separate development and testingExactly what happens, e.g. "visitor books a slot, pays via Razorpay, gets email and WhatsApp confirmation"
Page count and templatesUnique layouts take longer than repeated onesWhich pages share a layout and which are unique
ContentWriting, translating and photographing add time or third partiesWho does what, and whether text is ready now

Extra sections for redesigns, stores and agencies

Redesigns: add your current traffic sources, top pages from Google Search Console, and what does not work on the current site. Note that existing URLs and rankings must be protected; see redesign without losing rankings.

Online stores: add product count, payment and shipping partners, GST invoicing needs, COD rules and return policy. Our e-commerce website cost guide explains what each adds.

Agencies outsourcing a build: add design files (Figma links), the hosting environment, coding standards, deadlines for each milestone, who talks to the end client, and whether the work is white-label.

Using the brief to compare quotes

Send the same brief to every developer, then compare:

  • Does each quote list what is included and what is not?
  • Did they ask sensible questions about unclear points?
  • Do timelines include your content and feedback rounds?
  • Is post-launch support, warranty and training mentioned?
  • Who owns the domain, hosting, code and licences at the end?

A much lower quote often leaves out items others included. Our guide on how to compare website quotes walks through this in detail.

Common brief mistakes

  • "Like Amazon, but simpler": naming huge platforms without saying which features you mean.
  • Listing every possible feature: separate must-haves from later phases to keep the first launch affordable.
  • Hiding the budget: developers then guess, and the quotes are hard to compare.
  • Ignoring content: assuming the developer will write all the text without saying so.
  • No single decision-maker: feedback from five people with different views delays everything.

A filled-in example: a physiotherapy clinic

To show the level of detail that helps, here is how a two-branch physiotherapy clinic might answer the key sections. The details are illustrative, not a real client.

  • Goals: 1. More appointment requests from people searching nearby. 2. Fewer phone calls asking for timings and fees policy. 3. Look trustworthy to patients referred by doctors.
  • Audience: adults with back, knee or sports injuries, plus older patients and their children booking for them. Most arrive on phones from Google Maps or WhatsApp.
  • Pages: Home, About, 8 condition pages (back pain, knee pain and so on), 2 branch pages, Team (6 therapists), Blog, Contact.
  • Must-have features: appointment request form choosing branch and preferred time, WhatsApp button, click-to-call, Google Maps on each branch page.
  • Nice-to-have: online booking with real-time slots and payment, Hindi and Marathi versions later.
  • Content: therapists will write condition pages in rough form; we need editing. Photos of both branches needed.
  • Timeline: launch before a new branch opens; date fixed.

Notice the nice-to-have list. Booking with live slots and payments is a separate piece of work; parking it for phase two keeps the first launch focused while letting the developer plan for it.

What happens after you send the brief

A careful developer usually replies with questions before quoting. Expect questions about content readiness, integrations, who approves designs, and what "done" means for each feature. Then you should receive a written proposal with scope, exclusions, milestones, payment terms, timeline and support terms. Read the exclusions first; that is where quotes differ most. Once agreed, the brief and proposal together become the reference for the whole project, so keep both in one shared folder.

Technical details and access to share

Developers lose days chasing logins. In the brief, say who controls each account, and be ready to share access securely once the project starts (through a password manager or by adding the developer as a user, never by posting passwords in a group chat):

  • Domain registrar account and where the domain is registered.
  • Hosting control panel, if you already have hosting.
  • Current website admin, for redesigns.
  • Google Search Console, Analytics and Business Profile.
  • Business email provider, so email settings are not broken during launch.
  • Payment gateway, shipping and CRM accounts for stores and integrations.

Make sure these accounts are in the business's name, not a former employee's or developer's.

Briefs for design-only work and small websites

Design only: if you want a designer to create Figma designs that someone else will build, the brief should also list the pages that need unique designs, the breakpoints (phone, tablet, desktop), and whether a style guide or component library is needed for the developer.

Small websites: even a five-page site benefits from a one-page brief. Goals, page list, content readiness and budget range take ten minutes to write and still prevent most misunderstandings.

Is the brief the same as the project scope?

No. The brief is your description of what you want. The scope (or statement of work) is the developer's written response: exactly what will be delivered, what is excluded, the milestones and the acceptance criteria. The brief feeds the scope, and the signed scope is what decides later whether something is a bug or a change request; see bug vs change request.

Attach what you already have

Along with the brief, attach anything that saves the developer guessing: your logo files in the highest quality available, brand guidelines, a company profile or brochure PDF, existing product lists in a spreadsheet, photos you own, and screenshots of competitor or reference pages with notes on what you like. Label files clearly so they are easy to find later.

FAQs

What should a website brief include?

Your business overview, goals, audience, list of pages, features, who provides content, design examples, technical details, timeline, budget range and what support you need after launch.

How long should a website brief be?

Two to four pages is enough for most business websites. Clear answers matter more than length.

Should I share my budget with a web developer?

Yes, as a range. It helps the developer suggest a scope that fits instead of quoting something too big or too basic, and makes quotes easier to compare.

Is there a free website brief template?

Yes, the copy-and-fill version on this page is free to use. Paste it into a document or email and fill in each section.

What if I don't know which features I need?

Describe what visitors should be able to do, such as book, pay or download a brochure, and let the developer suggest how. Mark anything uncertain as a question.

Can I change the brief after getting a quote?

Yes, but tell every developer so quotes stay comparable. After the project starts, changes to the agreed scope are usually quoted as change requests.
