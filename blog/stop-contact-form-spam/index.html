How to Stop Contact Form Spam on WordPress: Honeypots, Turnstile, Akismet and Filters That Work

Stop contact form spam on WordPress with honeypots, Turnstile, Akismet and keyword filters, plus settings for Contact Form 7, WPForms and Elementor.

Key takeaways
  • Identify the kind of spam first: bots fall to honeypots, time checks and Turnstile, while pitches typed by real people need Akismet or keyword filters.
  • Layer light defences that are verified on the server, and avoid puzzles, country blocks and silent filters that turn real customers away.
  • Store form entries so nothing filtered is lost, strip visitor text from auto-replies, and send a test enquiry after every change.

To stop contact form spam on WordPress, layer a few light defences instead of relying on one: a honeypot field and a time check to catch simple bots, Cloudflare Turnstile or reCAPTCHA for persistent ones, and Akismet or keyword rules for spam that people type by hand. Make sure every check runs on the server, keep a spam folder you can review, and test the form yourself after each change so real enquiries still get through.

Which layer you need depends on the kind of spam you're getting, so start there. Form checks are part of my WordPress maintenance plans for a reason: a spam fix that quietly blocks genuine customers costs far more than the spam ever did.

Layer 1: honeypot fields and time checks

A honeypot is a form field hidden from people but visible to bots in the page code. Real visitors leave it empty; simple bots fill in everything, so any submission with the honeypot filled is discarded. A time check works alongside it: a person takes at least a few seconds to type a name and message, while a bot often submits within a second of the page loading. Neither adds anything for genuine visitors to do.

Most form plugins include a honeypot, though the setting is easy to miss. At the time of writing:

  • WPForms: anti-spam protection is under each form's Settings, in the spam protection section, and is normally on by default.
  • Gravity Forms: tick "Anti-spam honeypot" in the form's settings.
  • Elementor Pro forms: add a Honeypot field from the field types list.
  • Fluent Forms: the honeypot is in the global security settings.
  • Contact Form 7: has no honeypot of its own, so add one with a maintained add-on such as Honeypot for Contact Form 7.

Whatever the plugin, the honeypot and timer must be checked on the server that receives the form. Some bots post straight to the form's address without loading the page at all, so a check that only runs in the browser protects nothing.

This website's own contact form uses a honeypot, a simple addition question and a check that drops anything sent within three seconds of the page loading, with no reCAPTCHA. It isn't built on WordPress, but the same layers apply; the Samverse case study explains how it works.

Layer 2: Turnstile, reCAPTCHA or hCaptcha for persistent bots

When smarter bots get past the honeypot, add a challenge service:

  • Cloudflare Turnstile is free at the time of writing, usually invisible or a single click, and never asks visitors to pick out traffic lights. It works even if your site doesn't run through Cloudflare, and many form plugins support it directly or through an add-on.
  • Google reCAPTCHA v3 scores each visit in the background, and your form plugin rejects low scores. Set the threshold too high and visitors on VPNs or office networks get blocked without anyone noticing.
  • hCaptcha is another option that many plugins support.

Pick one, add it to every public form (contact, quote, newsletter and booking), and load it only once per page. If reCAPTCHA shows "Invalid domain for site key", a missing badge or forms that won't send, work through fixing reCAPTCHA on WordPress rather than switching it off. Avoid image puzzles on a lead form; they cost enquiries, especially on phones.

Layer 3: content filters for spam people type by hand

No CAPTCHA stops a person who is paid to fill in forms. For those messages you need filters that look at what was written:

  • Akismet checks submissions against a large shared spam database and works with Contact Form 7, WPForms, Gravity Forms, Elementor Pro forms and others. At the time of writing, its free plan is for personal, non-commercial sites, so a business site generally needs a paid plan.
  • Disallowed keywords. Contact Form 7 also checks messages against the "Disallowed Comment Keys" list in Settings > Discussion, so a word or domain added there blocks matching form messages as well as comments. Other plugins, including WPForms, have their own keyword filters, some in paid plans.
  • Link limits. Genuine enquiries rarely contain several links. Reject messages with more than one or two.
  • Language filters, used carefully. If your customers write in Hindi or Marathi, don't block Devanagari. Filter only scripts you genuinely never receive enquiries in.

A developer can add a link limit to Contact Form 7 with a small snippet in a site-specific plugin or child theme. Visitors who trip it see a friendly message instead of losing their enquiry silently:

add_filter( 'wpcf7_validate_textarea', 'my_limit_links', 20, 2 );
add_filter( 'wpcf7_validate_textarea*', 'my_limit_links', 20, 2 );

function my_limit_links( $result, $tag ) {
  $message = isset( $_POST[ $tag->name ] ) ? wp_unslash( $_POST[ $tag->name ] ) : '';
  if ( substr_count( strtolower( $message ), 'http' ) > 2 ) {
    $result->invalidate( $tag, 'Please remove some links and send your message again.' );
  }
  return $result;
}

Layer 4: firewall rules for floods

If a bot hammers the form hundreds of times an hour, stop it before it reaches WordPress. A cloud firewall such as Cloudflare can rate-limit requests to the address your form posts to: Contact Form 7 submits through the WordPress REST API, while many other plugins use admin-ajax.php. Be careful with admin-ajax.php, because other features on your site use it too, so set limits generously and test. WordPress firewalls explained covers rate limiting and false positives.

Blocking whole countries is tempting but risky. If you get enquiries from NRIs, export buyers or overseas clients, a country block turns them away without either of you knowing.

Close the other routes into your inbox

  • Hide your email address from scrapers. An address printed as plain text gets harvested and spammed directly, whatever your form does. Use the form as the main contact route, or have a developer encode the address with WordPress's antispambot function.
  • Fix the auto-reply. If your form emails a confirmation to whatever address is typed in, and that email repeats the visitor's message, bots can use it to send spam to strangers from your domain. Leave the message out of auto-replies, or show a thank-you page instead.
  • Send form emails properly. Mail sent through your web server is more likely to be filtered, and real enquiries can end up in spam alongside the junk. Use SMTP with a proper sending service; see fixing WordPress emails with SMTP.

Don't lose real enquiries in the process

  • Store entries on the site. Contact Form 7 doesn't save submissions by default; its companion plugin Flamingo stores them, with Akismet-flagged messages in a separate spam folder. Check whether your form plugin stores entries, since some do so only in paid versions.
  • Review the spam folder weekly for the first month after any change, then occasionally.
  • Test after every change, on a desktop and on a phone using mobile data, and confirm the email arrives. If enquiries still seem to vanish, work through why a contact form stops getting enquiries.
  • Prefer rejections people can see. A visible error lets a real customer fix the problem; a silent discard loses them.

A quick setup for most business sites

  1. Switch on your form plugin's honeypot or anti-spam protection.
  2. Add Turnstile, or reCAPTCHA v3, to every public form.
  3. Add Akismet or keyword filters if hand-typed pitches keep coming.
  4. Store entries, so anything filtered can be recovered.
  5. Remove the visitor's message from auto-reply emails.
  6. Send test enquiries from a desktop and a phone, and check they arrive.

FAQs

Why am I suddenly getting so much contact form spam?

Bots find forms by crawling the web, and once your form is on a spam list the volume tends to grow. A plugin update or a change of form plugin can also switch off a protection that used to be on, so check the settings first.

How can I stop contact form spam without a CAPTCHA?

Combine a honeypot field, a time check and a link limit, all verified on the server, and add Akismet or keyword filters for hand-typed spam. For many small business sites that's enough. Add Turnstile only if bots still get through.

How do I stop spam in Contact Form 7?

Add a honeypot with a maintained add-on, connect Turnstile or reCAPTCHA v3, enable Akismet if you have a licence, and add problem words to the Disallowed Comment Keys list. Install Flamingo so submissions are stored and spam can be reviewed.

Does reCAPTCHA stop all form spam?

No. It stops many bots, but not people paid to fill in forms, and a badly set threshold can block real visitors. Use it as one layer alongside a honeypot and content filters.

Why do I keep getting SEO and web design offers through my form?

Those messages are usually typed by people or sent by advanced bots, so CAPTCHAs don't stop them. Akismet and keyword filters catch many of them. Don't reply or click their links; domain and SEO scam emails explains the common tricks.

Can contact form spam harm my website?

Mostly it wastes time, but an auto-reply that bots can abuse can damage your domain's email reputation, and stored spam fills your database over time. The bigger risk is a spam fix that blocks genuine customers without anyone noticing.
