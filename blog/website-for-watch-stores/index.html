Websites for Watch Stores and Authorised Watch Retailers: Dealer Status Stated Precisely, Collections by Brand, Product Photography, Servicing and Store Visits

Plan a watch store website around trust: dealer status stated precisely, collections by brand, honest photos, careful warranty wording and visit booking.

Key takeaways
  • Authorised dealer status is your strongest trust signal, so state it brand by brand and branch by branch, exactly as your agreements allow.
  • Organise the catalogue by brand, then collection, then reference, and photograph your own stock so buyers can judge size, finish and condition.
  • Warranty and authenticity language should repeat what the brand or your own written policy actually says, not stronger promises, and high-value pieces sell best through a booked visit.

A watch store website wins customers when it removes doubt: which brands you are authorised for, whether a watch is genuine, who honours the warranty and whether someone will service it in five years. Show your dealer status precisely, organise the collection the way buyers browse, photograph your own stock well and make it easy to book a visit, and the site supports the sale that usually happens across the counter.

Dealer status, brand by brand

Buyers check this before anything else, and getting it wrong is the fastest way to lose credibility or breach an agreement. Be exact about which of these applies to each brand you carry:

Your status for a brandWhat you can usually sayWhat to avoid
Authorised retailerThe partner title the brand gives you, for the branches the agreement coversExtending it to other branches or other brands in the same group
Authorised service centreThe service status, separately from retail statusImplying retail authorisation means service authorisation, or the reverse
Independent seller of new watchesThat you source independently, and how warranty claims are handled"Official", "authorised" or brand logos used as your own
Pre-owned or vintage dealerYour inspection process and your own warranty, if anyDescribing pieces as new or as carrying the manufacturer's warranty unless confirmed

Put a short "Our brands" page listing each brand with your status beside it, and repeat the status on each brand page. When an agreement ends, remove the logo and the wording the same week. Check your dealer agreements for the exact phrases you may use; many brands specify them.

A catalogue organised by brand and collection

Watch buyers browse in a predictable order: brand, then collection or family, then a specific reference. Build the catalogue the same way.

  1. Brand pages: a short introduction in your own words, the collections you stock, which branches carry them and an enquiry button.
  2. Collection pages: what defines the collection, such as dress, diver, chronograph or pilot, and the references available.
  3. Product pages: reference number, case size and material, movement type, water resistance, strap or bracelet, and stock status.

Add filters that match how people shop across brands: case size, movement (quartz, automatic, manual), dial colour, strap type, price band and gender or "unisex". WooCommerce product filters covers the setup.

Working within brand guidelines

Authorised retailers usually have to follow a brand's rules on presentation. Before building brand pages, check your agreement and any retailer guidelines for:

  • Use of logos, official images and campaign material, often supplied through a retailer portal
  • Whether prices may be shown online, and whether online sales or only enquiries are allowed
  • Approved wording for describing the brand and its technology
  • Rules on discounts, offers and paid advertising using the brand name

Avoid copying the brand's own website text. A few sentences of your own about why customers choose a collection, and which pieces your staff recommend for daily wear, read better and help your pages stand apart. Writing product descriptions that sell has practical tips.

Product photography that answers buyers' questions

Official images show a watch in perfect studio light. Buyers also want to see what they would actually receive, and for pre-owned stock your own photos are essential. A consistent setup in the store is enough:

  • Angles: dial straight on, three-quarter view, side profile showing thickness, caseback, clasp, and on the wrist
  • On-wrist shots: on a medium wrist, with the wrist size noted, so buyers can judge case size
  • Light: soft, diffused light to control reflections on the crystal and polished surfaces
  • Background: neutral and the same for every product, so the catalogue looks consistent
  • Detail shots: the dial texture, lume, bracelet links and any engraving
  • Short video: a few seconds rotating the watch, to show how the dial changes in light

Resize and compress every image before upload, because a catalogue of high-resolution photos becomes slow very quickly; preparing photos for a website explains how. Descriptive file names and alt text, such as the brand, collection and reference, also help image search.

Authenticity and warranty language

Words like "guaranteed genuine" and "full warranty" are easy to write and hard to stand behind if they are not precise. Say what you can support, in terms that match the brand's own documents or your written policy.

RiskyMore accurate
"100% original guaranteed""New watches are supplied through our authorised partnership with the brand, with the manufacturer's warranty card"
"Full international warranty""Manufacturer's warranty as set out in the brand's terms, which we can explain before purchase"
"Lifetime service guarantee""We offer servicing for the brands and movements listed on our service page"
"Certified pre-owned""Pre-owned, inspected by our team using the checks described below, with our own warranty of the stated period"

On each brand or product page, explain how warranty registration works (a stamped card, digital activation or both), what is usually covered and what is not, such as straps, crystals or accidental damage, following the brand's terms. Link to the brand's official warranty information where it exists. Any extended warranty of your own needs its terms written out in full. Consumer protection rules apply to warranty and authenticity claims, so check unusual wording with an adviser.

Servicing and repairs

Service work brings customers back between purchases. Give it its own section:

  • Services offered: battery replacement, strap and bracelet sizing, crystal replacement, water-resistance testing if you have the equipment, servicing of automatic and mechanical movements
  • Which brands and movement types you can work on, and which you send to the brand's service centre
  • How estimates work, and that the customer approves before work starts
  • Typical turnaround, described as a range and explained honestly
  • Whether genuine parts are used, stated only where true
  • A repair request form with photo upload, and status updates by WhatsApp

Pages for battery replacement and watch repair match common local searches, which often bring first-time customers into the store.

Pre-owned watches with honest grading

If you sell pre-owned or vintage pieces, publish your grading scale with a plain definition of each grade, then for each watch show photos of that exact piece including any wear, the reference, case size, movement and year where known, whether box and papers are included, service history and polishing where known, and the checks you carried out. Keep records of where each piece came from.

Store visits for high-value pieces

Most buyers of expensive watches want to try them on and talk to someone first. Design the site to lead to that conversation:

  • "Enquire about this watch" buttons that carry the reference into a form or a WhatsApp message
  • A visit booking form with branch, date and time, and the pieces the customer wants to see, so staff can have them ready
  • Private viewings or video calls for buyers in other cities, if you offer them
  • "Price on request" where your agreements require it or prices change often
  • A named person replying quickly, since high-value buyers often enquire at more than one store

Online appointment booking compares ways to offer visit slots. For pieces sold online, explain insured and tracked delivery, what happens if a parcel is damaged, and your returns policy.

Store pages and local search

Each branch should have a page with its address, map, hours, the brands carried there, service available there and a booking link. Searches like "watch shop near me", "{brand} watches in {city}" and "watch battery replacement in {area}" are mostly local, so a complete Google Business Profile for each branch, with real store photos and accurate hours, matters as much as the website. Show your store, team and full business details clearly; the website trust signals checklist covers the rest.

FAQs

Can I sell watches online if I am an authorised dealer?

It depends on your agreement with each brand. Some allow online sales, some allow only enquiries or reservations. Check before adding a checkout for any brand.

Can I use official brand images on my website?

Usually only as your agreement and the brand's retailer guidelines allow. Your own photos of stock are a good addition and essential for pre-owned pieces.

How should I describe the warranty?

Repeat what the brand's terms or your own written policy say, explain registration and exclusions, and link to the brand's official warranty information where it exists.

Should I show prices?

Where agreements allow and prices are stable, yes. For high-value or fast-changing pieces, price on request with a quick reply works well.

Can customers book a store visit online?

Yes. A visit form with branch, date, time and the pieces they want to see lets staff prepare and makes the visit more likely to end in a sale.
