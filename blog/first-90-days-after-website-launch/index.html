The First 90 Days After Your Website Launches: A Week-by-Week Plan for Indexing, Search Console, Tracking, Reviews, Content and Upkeep

What to do in the first 90 days after your website launches: week-by-week checks, indexing, Search Console, analytics, reviews, first content and upkeep.

Key takeaways
  • Spend the first week proving everything works and is measured, then the first month getting key pages indexed and fixing what Search Console reports.
  • Months two and three are for trust and content: your Google Business Profile, genuine reviews, a few useful posts and improvements based on real search data.
  • Keep backups, updates and monitoring running from day one, and expect impressions to grow before clicks and enquiries do.

In the first 90 days after a website launches, work in three stages. In week one, prove that everything works and is being measured. Over the first month, get your key pages found and indexed, and fix whatever Search Console reports. In months two and three, build trust and content: your Google Business Profile, genuine reviews, a few genuinely useful posts, and improvements to key pages based on the data you now have. Keep backups and updates running throughout, and expect impressions in Google to grow well before clicks and enquiries do.

Much of this is what WordPress SEO services cover after a launch or redesign: Search Console set up as a domain property in your Google account, the sitemap submitted, indexing requested for key pages, and the Pages report watched as Google recrawls the site. The upkeep side, from updates and off-site backups to uptime monitoring and form checks, is what WordPress maintenance plans handle.

This plan picks up where launch day ends. If you haven't launched yet, run through the website launch checklist first.

Days 1 to 7: make sure everything works

  • Send real test enquiries from your own phone and a friend's: the contact form, WhatsApp button and click-to-call on every key page. Check the emails arrive, and not in spam.
  • Check the thank-you page or success message shows, and that GA4 records the submission as a key event. GA4 events explained shows how to track forms, calls and WhatsApp clicks.
  • Remove staging leftovers. In WordPress, Settings, Reading, "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" must be unticked, and no page should carry a stray noindex tag.
  • Check HTTPS and redirects. Every page should load on one version of the domain, and if this replaced an old site, old URLs should redirect to their new equivalents.
  • Run a speed test on a phone for the homepage and one key service page, and note the results as your baseline.
  • Confirm backups are running automatically and stored away from the hosting server, and set up uptime monitoring so you hear about downtime before customers do.

Weeks 1 to 2: help Google find the site

  1. Set up Google Search Console as a domain property, verified through DNS, in your own Google account, and add Bing Webmaster Tools if you like.
  2. Submit your XML sitemap. Most SEO plugins create one automatically.
  3. Request indexing for your most important pages using URL Inspection: the homepage and main service pages. There's no need to submit every URL or to repeat requests daily.
  4. Link to the site from places Google already crawls: your Business Profile, social profiles, directory listings and your email signature.

Weeks 2 to 4: read Search Console and fix what it shows

Search Console usually starts showing data within a few days of verification, and the picture fills in over the following weeks.

  • Pages report. On a new site, many pages sit as "Discovered, currently not indexed" or "Crawled, currently not indexed" for a while. Not every status is a problem, but key service pages that stay unindexed are worth investigating; page indexing errors explained shows which statuses matter.
  • 404 errors. Old addresses still linked from elsewhere, or shared on WhatsApp, should redirect to the closest new page.
  • Performance report. The first impressions and queries appear here. They're often your business name and a few specific searches, which is normal.
  • Core Web Vitals. A new site with little traffic may show "not enough data" for a while; use PageSpeed Insights on key pages in the meantime.
  • Security issues and manual actions, which should both be clear. If not, deal with them before anything else.

Also fix the small problems real visitors find: a broken link, a typo in a phone number, a button hidden on one phone model. There are always a few.

Weeks 3 to 6: local trust and links

  • Update your Google Business Profile: the website link pointing to the most relevant page, accurate hours, services and recent photos. The Google Business Profile checklist covers each setting.
  • Ask recent happy customers for reviews, with a direct link, and reply to every review you get. How to get more Google reviews covers asking within Google's rules.
  • Update the website address everywhere it appears: social profiles, directory listings, WhatsApp Business, quotation templates and invoices.
  • Ask partners, suppliers and associations you genuinely work with to link to the new site, or to update an old link. These are the honest links that help a new site most.

Month 2: first content and stronger key pages

With the foundations working, put effort into the pages that bring enquiries:

  • Strengthen each main service page with the questions customers actually ask, real photos, proof such as testimonials or project examples, and a clear call to action.
  • Publish a few genuinely useful posts answering common questions before people buy, such as what affects the price or how to choose, each linking to its service page.
  • Add internal links from the homepage and posts to the service pages you most want to rank.
  • Share new content on LinkedIn, WhatsApp status or wherever your customers already are.

A few strong pages beat many thin ones. On a new site, Google is cautious about crawling and indexing pages that say little, so depth matters more than volume.

Month 3: review the data and improve

By now you have real data. Use it:

Then write a short plan for the next 90 days: which pages to improve, which posts to write, and which local profiles to keep active.

Throughout: backups, updates and security

  • Update WordPress, the theme and plugins regularly, taking a backup first and testing forms afterwards.
  • Check that backups are still running, and that a restore would actually work.
  • Watch uptime alerts, and act on any hosting warnings about PHP versions, disk space or resource limits.
  • Remove any temporary access the developer used, unless they're maintaining the site.
  • Put domain, hosting and licence renewal dates in a calendar, remembering that first-year discounts often renew higher.

What to expect by day 90

Every site is different, so treat these as common patterns rather than promises. Impressions usually grow before clicks. The first clicks often come from your business name and very specific searches, while broader and more competitive searches take longer, especially on a new domain with few links. Technical fixes take effect as Google recrawls the site, usually over a few weeks, and competitive rankings can take many months. Common mistakes in the first 90 days

  • Leaving "discourage search engines" ticked or a noindex tag in place after launch.
  • Requesting indexing for the same URLs every day.
  • Changing URLs again soon after launch without redirects.
  • Buying links, reviews or "SEO packages" that promise quick rankings.
  • Judging the website by its traffic in week two.
  • Not noticing that the contact form stopped sending.
  • Assuming the host's backups are enough without checking where they're stored.

FAQs

How long does it take for a new website to show up on Google?

There's no fixed timeline. The homepage is usually the first page to appear, typically for your business name, once Search Console and a sitemap are set up and a few links point to the site. Other pages are indexed gradually, and ranking for competitive searches takes much longer.

Do I need to submit my website to Google?

Google can find a site through links alone, but setting up Search Console and submitting a sitemap helps it discover your pages and lets you see what's happening. It doesn't speed up rankings by itself.

How often should I check Search Console after launch?

Every few days in the first two weeks, to catch errors and redirects that were missed, then weekly for the first three months. After that, monthly is usually enough, plus whenever you make big changes.

What if my pages still aren't indexed after a month?

Check that they aren't blocked by noindex or robots.txt, that they're in the sitemap and linked from other pages, and that each one is useful and distinct. On new sites with few links, Google can simply be slow, so improve the pages rather than repeatedly requesting indexing.

Should I keep making changes during the first 90 days?

Yes, to content, titles and calls to action, based on what the data shows. Avoid changing URLs or the site structure again, and make changes after a backup so you can roll back if needed.
