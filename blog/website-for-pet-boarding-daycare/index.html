Websites for Pet Boarding, Daycare and Pet Hotels

What pet boarding and daycare websites need: real facility photos, vaccination rules, daily routines, online booking, owner updates and vet arrangements.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Show every area of the facility with your own photos and video, and invite owners to visit before they book.
  • Publish vaccination and health requirements, a typical daily routine, your night supervision and your real emergency and vet arrangements.
  • Make booking by date range easy, promise only the updates you can deliver, and explain what affects the price of a stay.

Leaving a dog or cat with strangers, even for one night, is a big decision for most owners. Before they book, they study your website closely: where their pet will sleep, who will be there at night, what happens if something goes wrong and how they will know their pet is fine. A boarding or daycare website that answers those questions honestly turns anxious browsers into regular customers, especially before Diwali, summer holidays and long weekends.

This guide is for dedicated pet boarding facilities, dog daycare centres and pet hotels. If boarding is one part of a pet shop or grooming business, see websites for pet shops, grooming and boarding services.

Show the facility with real photos

Owners want to picture exactly where their pet will spend the day and night. Show every area with your own photos and a short walk-through video:

  • Sleeping areas: kennels, rooms or suites, with their size and bedding
  • Indoor and outdoor play areas, fencing and flooring
  • A separate, quiet area for cats, away from barking dogs
  • How you keep pets cool in summer and dry during the monsoon
  • Feeding, bathing and isolation areas, and your cleaning routine between stays

Never use stock images or photos from another facility. Invite owners to visit before booking, and say when visits are possible.

Vaccination and health requirements, stated up front

Clear entry rules protect every pet in your care and save awkward conversations at check-in. Publish them on a dedicated page and repeat them in the booking form:

  • Up-to-date vaccinations, such as rabies and the core vaccines your vet recommends, with a certificate to upload
  • Tick and flea treatment and deworming, and how recent they must be
  • Age limits for puppies and kittens, and your policy for senior pets or pets with medical needs
  • A temperament assessment or trial day before group play or a long stay
  • Your policy on unneutered pets and females in heat, if you have one

Base these on your vet's advice and write them in plain language. A downloadable checklist helps owners prepare.

A typical day and how pets are grouped

A sample routine reassures owners more than any slogan:

Part of the dayWhat to describe
MorningToilet break, walk, breakfast and any medicines
MiddayPlay sessions, rest time and shade or indoor time in hot weather
EveningSecond walk, dinner and settling in for the night
NightWho is on site, and how often pets are checked

Explain how you group pets for play, such as by size, age and temperament, and how many staff supervise each group. Say whether owners can send their pet's own food, bedding and toys, and how you give medicines.

Booking and availability

Boarding is booked by date range, so the booking flow should make dates and capacity clear:

  1. Check dates: an availability calendar, or a quick form that asks for check-in and check-out dates
  2. Pet profile: name, species, breed, age, size, temperament, diet, medical notes, vaccination upload and the owner's own vet
  3. Emergency contact: someone reachable if the owner is travelling
  4. Confirm: a deposit through UPI for peak dates, with a clear cancellation policy

Publish check-in and check-out times, late pick-up rules and any pet taxi service. For daycare, offer full-day, half-day and multi-day packs, and let regular customers book recurring days. The guide to online appointment booking covers confirmations and reminders.

Live updates and photos for owners

Updates are often what owners value most. Tell them exactly what to expect:

  • Daily photos or short videos on WhatsApp, and roughly when they arrive
  • A short daycare report on eating, play and mood at pick-up
  • Live camera access only if you genuinely provide it, password-protected and never public
  • Photos of pets on your website or social media only with the owner's permission

It's better to promise one reliable update a day than hourly updates your staff can't keep up with.

Emergency and vet arrangements

Owners will ask what happens if their pet falls ill or gets hurt. Answer it plainly:

  • Which vet or clinic you work with, only if the arrangement is real, and the nearest emergency hospital
  • A consent form authorising treatment in an emergency and explaining who pays for it
  • When and how you contact owners, and what you do if you can't reach them
  • What you do if a pet stops eating, seems stressed or has a minor injury during play

If you offer training during stays or work with a local trainer, see websites for dog trainers and behaviourists for how to present that service.

Pricing explained by what affects it

Owners compare prices, so either publish your rates or explain clearly what changes them:

FactorWhy it matters
Pet size and typeLarger dogs need more space, food and handling time
Room typeShared kennel, private room or suite
Length of staySome facilities offer different rates for longer stays
Peak datesFestivals, holidays and long weekends
ExtrasBath before pick-up, special diets, medicines, extra walks or pick-up and drop

"Starting from" prices with a list of extras avoid surprises at checkout. For more on this decision, see should you show prices on your website.

Local SEO and speed

Owners search for "dog boarding in {area}", "pet hotel in {city}", "cat boarding near me" and "dog daycare near me". Create separate pages for boarding, daycare and cat boarding, keep your Google Business Profile complete with real photos, and ask happy owners for reviews at pick-up. Most owners browse on their phones, so compress photos and load videos only when tapped.

A pet boarding website works when owners can see the facility, understand your rules and routine, trust your emergency plan and book their dates easily. If you want one built, see WordPress website development, or landing page design for holiday-season campaigns.
