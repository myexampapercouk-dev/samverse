How to Sell Internationally From an Indian WooCommerce Store

Selling abroad from an Indian WooCommerce store: showing vs charging other currencies, international payments, shipping, customs duties and GST/LUT points.

Key takeaways
  • Decide whether to only display other currencies or actually charge in them, and keep the currency consistent through to invoices.
  • Plan international shipping zones, courier options and who pays customs duties, and state it clearly on product pages and at checkout.
  • Export points such as IEC, LUT and export invoices depend on your business, so confirm them with your CA before you start selling abroad.

Indian products such as handicrafts, textiles, jewellery, Ayurvedic products and packaged foods have buyers all over the world, including the many Indians living abroad. A WooCommerce store can sell to them, but international orders raise questions a domestic store never faces: which currency to charge in, how to get paid, what shipping costs, who pays customs duty, and what paperwork you need. This guide covers selling directly to overseas customers through your online store. If you mainly want bulk enquiries from importers, see websites for export businesses instead.

Start with a few countries

You don't have to sell everywhere on day one. Start where you already have demand: the countries your Instagram followers, past enquiries or website visitors come from. Then check:

  • Whether your products can be shipped there at all. Food, cosmetics, liquids, batteries, plant material and some medicines often face restrictions from couriers or destination countries.
  • Whether shipping costs still leave a sensible price for your typical order
  • Whether you can handle returns or replacements from that country

In WooCommerce → Settings → General, you can limit selling and shipping locations to specific countries, so customers elsewhere can't place orders you can't fulfil.

Showing other currencies vs charging in them

WooCommerce works with one store currency by default. There are two ways to go beyond it:

ApproachHow it worksWatch out for
Display onlyPrices are shown converted into the visitor's currency, but payment is taken in rupeesThe amount on the customer's card statement can differ from the price shown, and their bank may add foreign transaction fees
Charging in other currenciesCustomers see and pay in their own currency, using a multi-currency plugin and a gateway that supports those currenciesMore setup, exchange rate decisions, and settlements and reports to reconcile

If you charge in other currencies, consider setting rounded prices for each currency rather than relying on live exchange rates, so products don't show awkward prices. Whatever you choose, keep the currency consistent across the cart, checkout, order emails and invoices, and make it clear what customers will actually be charged.

Taking international payments

Many Indian payment gateways can accept international cards, but it is often a separate activation with extra checks and documents. Global payment providers are another option, and some stores use one gateway for Indian customers and a second for overseas buyers. Before deciding, ask each provider:

  • Which currencies customers can pay in, and how payments are converted and settled into your Indian bank account
  • What fees apply to international payments and currency conversion
  • What documentation they provide for foreign payments received, which your bank and CA may need
  • How disputes and chargebacks are handled

For a fuller checklist, see how to choose a payment gateway in India. Turn off Cash on Delivery for international shipping zones, and look carefully at unusual international orders, such as large first orders with inconsistent details, before you dispatch.

Shipping rates and courier options

Set up international shipping zones in WooCommerce, grouping countries your courier prices similarly, for example the USA, the UK and Europe, the Gulf, and Australia. Weight-based rates usually work better than flat rates abroad, and volumetric weight matters a lot on air shipments, so compact packaging saves money. The basics of zones and rates are covered in WooCommerce shipping setup for India.

Your main courier options are:

  • International express couriers: fast and well tracked, usually the most expensive
  • India Post international services: often more economical for small parcels, but slower
  • Shipping aggregators with cross-border services: compare several couriers' rates and print labels from one dashboard

Show realistic delivery times for each region on product pages and in your shipping policy, and send tracking links automatically.

Customs duties: who pays?

When a parcel reaches its destination, that country may charge import duty and taxes depending on the product, its value and local rules. You need to decide who pays them:

  • The customer pays on delivery (often called DDU or DAP). Simpler for you, but an unexpected bill at the door can lead to refused parcels and unhappy reviews.
  • You pay, and build it into the price (DDP). A smoother experience for buyers, but it needs a courier service that supports it and careful pricing.

Either way, say clearly on product pages, at checkout and in your shipping policy whether duties and taxes are included. Some markets, such as the UK and the EU, have their own rules on how VAT is collected on lower-value imports, which can create obligations for the seller, so check before you start selling there. Always declare goods accurately on customs paperwork; under-declaring the value to save the customer duty can get parcels held and cause bigger problems.

Export paperwork, GST and LUT

This part needs your chartered accountant, because the right approach depends on your business, your products and how you ship. Points to discuss with them:

  • IEC: exporters of goods generally need an Importer Exporter Code; ask whether it applies to the way you ship
  • GST on exports: exports are generally treated as zero-rated, and businesses commonly either export under a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) without paying IGST, or pay IGST and claim a refund
  • Export invoices: these usually need different details from domestic GST invoices; see WooCommerce GST setup and invoices for the domestic side, then confirm the export format with your CA
  • Foreign payment records: keep each order, shipment and payment linked, as your bank and CA may need to match them

WooCommerce can be set up to apply different tax rules to international orders and to print the invoice details your CA specifies, but the rules themselves should come from your CA, not from a plugin's defaults.

Make the store work for overseas visitors

  • Address and phone fields: WooCommerce adjusts address formats by country, so make sure checkout customisations haven't forced Indian-only fields such as a six-digit pin code
  • Sizes and units: give sizes and dimensions in both centimetres and inches where relevant
  • Speed: a CDN helps pages load quickly for visitors far from your server
  • Support across time zones: say when you reply, and answer email and WhatsApp messages promptly
  • Clear policies: international returns are expensive, so state plainly whether overseas orders get refunds, replacements or store credit
