Pre-Orders and Backorders in WooCommerce: How to Sell Before Stock Arrives

Run pre-orders and backorders in WooCommerce: the built-in backorder setting, pre-order plugins, when to charge, honest dates and Merchant Center availability.

Key takeaways
  • Backorders are built into WooCommerce, while proper pre-orders usually need an extension or plugin.
  • Most Indian stores take full payment or an advance upfront, and should show honest expected dispatch dates everywhere.
  • Cap pre-orders at incoming stock and send matching preorder or backorder availability, with a date, to Google Merchant Center.

Sometimes you want to take orders for products you don't have on the shelf: a new launch that hasn't arrived yet, a bestseller waiting for restock, or a festive collection still being made. WooCommerce can handle this in two ways, backorders and pre-orders. Done well, they keep sales coming in while you wait for stock. Done badly, they fill your inbox with customers asking where their order is. Here's how to set them up and run them honestly.

Backorders vs pre-orders

AspectBackorderPre-order
What it isA product you normally stock is temporarily out, and you keep taking orders until it's restockedA product that hasn't been released or received yet, sold ahead of a launch date
In WooCommerceBuilt inUsually needs an extension or plugin
Typical waitUntil the next restockUntil the launch or arrival date

Made-to-order products are a slightly different case. If everything is made after ordering, it's usually clearer to keep them "in stock" and state the making time, rather than labelling them as backordered.

Setting up backorders in WooCommerce

Backorders are a built-in setting on each product's Inventory tab. With "Manage stock" ticked, you'll see "Allow backorders?" with three choices:

  • Do not allow: the product can't be bought once stock reaches zero
  • Allow, but notify customer: the product page shows that it's available on backorder, and the order marks which items were backordered
  • Allow: orders are accepted without telling the customer, which is rarely a good idea

Stock then goes below zero as backorders come in, so you can see how many units you owe. If you don't manage stock for a product, you can set its stock status to "On backorder" instead. For variable products, set this for each variation.

Core WooCommerce doesn't show an expected date for backorders, so add a short note near the price or use a plugin that displays one. For the wider stock settings, see WooCommerce inventory management.

What pre-order plugins add

WooCommerce's own Pre-Orders extension and several third-party plugins add proper pre-order handling. Features vary, so check current details and compatibility with your payment gateway and checkout, but they typically offer:

  • A release or availability date shown on the product page
  • Custom button text such as "Pre-order now" instead of "Add to cart"
  • A choice between charging upfront and charging on release
  • A separate order status, so pre-orders don't get mixed into normal dispatch
  • Automatic emails when an order is placed, when the date changes and when the product is released
  • Bulk actions to complete or cancel pre-orders

For a small launch, you may not need a plugin at all. A "notify me" waitlist collects interest without taking money, which is often the honest choice when you don't yet have a firm date.

Charge upfront or on release?

OptionProsCons
Full payment upfrontCommitted buyers, cash to fund the stock, simple to runYou hold customers' money, and delays mean refunds and complaints
Charge on releaseCustomers pay only when you can shipThe gateway must charge a saved payment method later, which many Indian setups don't support
Advance now, balance laterFilters out casual orders while asking for less upfrontMore admin: collecting balances and chasing unpaid ones

In India, rules around saved cards and recurring payments make "charge later" harder than in some other countries, so most stores take full payment upfront or a partial advance. Ask your gateway what its WooCommerce plugin supports before you promise customers anything. Cash on Delivery is usually a poor fit for pre-orders: the longer the wait, the more likely a buyer is to change their mind.

Communicate dates honestly

Problems with pre-orders and backorders usually start with expectations. To keep them realistic:

  • Show the expected dispatch date on the product page, in the cart, at checkout and in the order email
  • Say "expected to dispatch by" rather than promising a delivery date you don't control
  • Build in a buffer: if your supplier says two weeks, don't promise two weeks
  • Decide what happens with mixed carts: wait and ship everything together, or ship in-stock items now and absorb or charge the extra shipping. Some plugins simply stop pre-order items being bought with other products, which keeps things simple.
  • If a date slips, tell customers before they ask, by email or WhatsApp, with a new date and an easy option to cancel for a full refund

Indian consumer protection rules for e-commerce generally expect clear information on delivery, cancellation and refunds, so keep your policies consistent with what product pages say (check current rules if unsure); see returns and refunds for WooCommerce stores.

Managing stock, cancellations and refunds

  • Cap orders at what's coming: for a pre-order, set the stock quantity to the number of units you actually expect, so you can't sell more than will arrive
  • Keep them out of the dispatch queue: use "On hold" or a dedicated pre-order status so your team doesn't try to pack something that isn't there
  • Fulfil in order: when stock arrives, ship the oldest orders first and update stock counts
  • Make cancellation easy: customers waiting weeks may change their minds, and a quick refund is better than a dispute
  • Refund through the gateway: if your gateway plugin supports it, refunds can be issued from the WooCommerce order screen, keeping records tidy
  • Review weekly: list open backorders and pre-orders with their expected dates, so nothing is forgotten

How they show in Google Merchant Center

If your products appear on Google through Merchant Center, the availability you send must match what the product page says. Google's product data uses these availability values:

  • in_stock: ready to ship
  • out_of_stock: not available to buy
  • preorder: not yet released, but can be ordered
  • backorder: temporarily out of stock, but can be ordered

For preorder and backorder, Google also asks for an availability date, so check the current product data specification. Your product schema should say the same thing, using the PreOrder or BackOrder availability values. Check what your feed plugin actually sends: WooCommerce's "On backorder" status may map across automatically, while products from a pre-order plugin may need their availability and date set in the feed plugin's settings. Mismatches between the feed and the page are a common cause of disapprovals; see Google Merchant Center for WooCommerce.
