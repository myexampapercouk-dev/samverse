Websites for Ice Cream Parlours and Gelato Shops: Flavour Menus, Party Orders and Delivery

Plan an ice cream parlour or gelato shop website: a flavour board you update daily, seasonal launches, party and bulk orders, delivery choices and FSSAI basics.

Key takeaways
  • Run the flavour menu as an editable "flavour board" with today, coming back and seasonal sections, and reuse the same seasonal page every year so it keeps its search history.
  • Give party, bulk and event orders their own page with a quote form that asks for date, guests, flavours and delivery area, and keep your own ordering ahead of delivery app links.
  • Show your FSSAI details, describe frozen desserts and dietary options accurately, and keep hours and flavours consistent across your website, Google and delivery apps.

Nobody needs convincing to eat ice cream. What people need is a reason to choose your parlour over the one down the road: the flavours you have today, whether there's something eggless for a fussy cousin, how late you're open, and whether you can send tubs to a birthday party on Saturday. An ice cream website is unusual because its most important page, the flavour list, changes every week. If you also serve coffee and snacks, our guide to websites for cafes and coffee shops covers seating and events; this one is only about ice cream parlours, gelato counters and frozen dessert shops.

What customers check before they visit

  • Is it open now? Many dessert visits happen after dinner, so late hours and weekend timings matter
  • What's on the counter today? Especially seasonal favourites such as mango or sitaphal
  • Dietary options: eggless, vegan, no added sugar or nut-free, shown only where true
  • Price range: per scoop, cup, cone, sundae or tub
  • Party orders: birthdays, weddings and office celebrations
  • How to get there, or how to get it delivered still frozen

Put hours, location, a "Today's flavours" button and one-tap WhatsApp at the top of the homepage.

A flavour board built for rotation

A printed card or a photo of the menu board goes out of date within days. Build the menu as a web page staff can edit from a phone; our guide to putting your menu online the right way covers the basics. For ice cream, organise it as a flavour board with clear sections:

  • Always on: core flavours that never leave the counter
  • On the counter today: rotating and limited flavours, each with an "available" or "sold out" switch
  • Coming back soon: popular flavours between batches or out of season, so people know to return
  • Formats and sizes: single and double scoops, cups, cones, sundaes, shakes, family packs and tubs, with prices

Make updating the board part of the opening routine, done by whoever opens the shop, and show a small "updated today" line. A board that is current every morning becomes a reason for regulars to check your site before they leave home. Point in-store QR codes to the same page; see QR codes for your business website.

Plan a seasonal flavour calendar

Seasonal flavours are the biggest reason to update the site, so plan them a year ahead. A typical calendar for an Indian parlour might include:

  • Summer: mango in its many forms, plus light sorbets and fruit flavours
  • Monsoon: warm brownie sundaes, hot fudge and coffee flavours
  • Festive season: kesar pista, gulkand, paan, kaju and dry fruit specials, and gift packs for Diwali
  • Winter: strawberry, sitaphal, chikoo, chocolate and Christmas or new-year specials

Fruit seasons vary by region and year, so follow your own suppliers rather than a fixed date. One useful habit: give each recurring season a permanent page, such as /flavours/mango-season/, and update it every year instead of creating a new one. The page keeps its links and search history, so it is ready to be found as soon as people start searching "mango ice cream near me" again.

Dietary markers and allergens

  • Mark veg, eggless, contains nuts, vegan and no-added-sugar options only when you are sure they are accurate for every batch
  • List common allergens such as milk, nuts and gluten in cones, waffles and toppings
  • Add a line explaining that cross-contact is possible at a shared counter with shared scoops

Party, bulk and event orders

A single party order can be worth more than a busy evening of walk-ins, and it often starts with a search like "ice cream for birthday party near me". Give bulk orders their own page, separate from the menu, covering:

  • Tubs and family packs for home parties, with a simple serving guide based on your own tub sizes and how much notice you need
  • Ice cream cakes and dessert boxes, if you make them, with photos and lead times; our guide to websites for bakeries and cake shops covers custom cake orders
  • Counters and carts for weddings, birthdays and corporate events, with what's included: staff, cups, cones, toppings and setup time
  • Corporate orders: office celebrations and gifting, and whether you can issue a GST invoice
  • Delivery range and storage: how far you can deliver while keeping products frozen, and how customers should store tubs on arrival

What the enquiry form should ask

Ask for the event date and time, venue or delivery area, number of guests, preferred flavours, dietary needs, whether they need a counter or just tubs, and a phone number for WhatsApp. With those answers you can quote in one reply instead of five. For larger events, take an advance by UPI or a payment link and confirm flavours, quantities, timings and setup in writing.

Delivery apps vs direct orders

Delivery apps bring customers who already use them, but they charge commission and keep most of the customer relationship. Direct ordering lets regulars and party customers come to you.

OptionWorks well forWatch out for
Delivery app linksSingle servings and impulse ordersCommission, and little customer data
WhatsApp orderingRegulars, tubs and small party ordersSomeone has to reply quickly
Website ordering with online paymentTubs, packs, gift boxes and pre-ordersDelivery radius, packaging and time slots
Order ahead, pick up in storeParty orders collected by the customerClear pickup times, so tubs aren't left waiting

Show delivery app buttons if customers expect them, but don't put them ahead of your own options. Ice cream travels badly, so limit website delivery to areas you can reach quickly, use insulated packaging, and let customers check their pincode before they pay; see pincode delivery checks for WooCommerce. The ideas in our guide to direct orders for cloud kitchens apply here too.

FSSAI, labels and honest claims

People buying for children and events want reassurance. Food businesses in India generally need FSSAI registration or a licence, and many show the number on their premises, packaging and website. Add yours to the footer or the About page, and check the current display requirements for your type of business, especially if you sell packed tubs online.

  • Ice cream or frozen dessert: if any products are made with vegetable fat instead of milk fat, food rules generally require them to be described as frozen desserts rather than ice cream; check the current FSSAI rules and describe each product accurately
  • Claims: avoid "100% natural", "guilt-free" or "sugar-free" unless the recipe and labelling genuinely support it
  • Packed products: labelling rules for packaged food can apply to tubs sold online, so confirm what your labels need
  • Hygiene: a short note on how you store, handle and serve, describing only what you actually do

Franchise enquiries

Growing dessert brands often receive franchise questions mixed in with birthday orders. If you offer franchises, give them a separate page explaining the formats (kiosk, parlour or cart), the support you provide and the application process, without promising returns or payback periods; see websites for franchise brands. If you don't franchise, say so politely in an FAQ so the enquiries stop.

A worked example: page structure for a two-outlet parlour

Imagine a gelato brand with two outlets, a rotating counter, party orders and a few delivery apps. A practical structure would be:

  • Home: open now, today's flavours, party orders, order direct, WhatsApp
  • Flavours: the live flavour board, with dietary markers and allergens
  • Seasonal pages: mango season, festive specials, winter flavours, each kept at a permanent address
  • Party and event orders: tubs, cakes, counters and the quote form
  • Order online: pickup or delivery, with the delivery area and time slots
  • Outlets: one page per outlet with hours, map, parking and anything only that outlet serves
  • About: how you make the product, FSSAI details and hygiene
  • Franchise: only if you offer it
  • FAQs: storage, eggless options, delivery and advance orders

Separate outlet pages help each location be found for "ice cream parlour in {area}" and keep timings clear when outlets close at different hours. Add structured data using schema.org's IceCreamShop type to each outlet page (see schema markup explained), keep hours consistent across your website, Google and delivery apps, and share each new seasonal flavour as a Google Business Profile post.

Common mistakes on ice cream websites

  • The menu as a photo of the board or a PDF, which can't be updated quickly and is hard to read on a phone
  • Flavours listed that sold out months ago, so customers arrive disappointed
  • Delivery app buttons first and your own ordering hidden
  • Calling a frozen dessert "ice cream", or using claims such as "sugar-free" without support
  • No party order page, only a phone number that rings during the evening rush
  • A heavy Instagram feed widget or autoplay video that slows the menu down on mobile data
  • Late-night hours on the website that don't match Google

Photos matter too: use real shots of your own scoops and counter, taken in natural light before they melt, rather than a supplier's pictures, and compress them so the flavour board appears first.

FAQs

Should I put prices on the flavour menu?

Yes, at least for scoops, cups and tubs. People decide quickly on dessert, and a clear price list saves staff from answering the same question on WhatsApp all evening.

How often should I update the flavour list?

Whenever the counter changes. For most rotating parlours that means a quick update at opening, which takes a minute if the board is built with simple switches.

Can customers order tubs online for delivery?

Yes, with online payment and time slots, but keep the delivery area small enough to arrive frozen and use insulated packaging. Many parlours offer pickup for larger party orders.

Do I need to show my FSSAI number on my website?

Many food businesses do, and it builds trust. The exact requirements depend on your licence type and how you sell, so check the current FSSAI rules or ask your food safety consultant.

Is a delivery app listing enough?

It helps with single orders, but an app page can't hold your party order details, seasonal pages or outlet information, and the customer relationship stays with the app.

Should each outlet have its own page?

Yes, if outlets have different hours, menus or delivery areas. One website with an outlet page each is easier to manage than separate websites.
