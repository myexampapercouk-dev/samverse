Is Your Website Ready for Google Ads? A Pre-Launch Checklist for Tracking, Speed, Landing Pages, Forms and Policies

Is your website ready for Google Ads? Check tracking, landing pages, mobile speed, forms, call tracking and policy pages before you spend on clicks.

Key takeaways
  • Before launch, give every ad group a matching landing page that loads fast on a mid-range phone, and test it on mobile data.
  • Set up and test conversion tracking for forms, calls and WhatsApp clicks with real test enquiries, so no click is counted twice or missed.
  • Check that forms deliver, policy pages and business details are in place, and someone is ready to answer leads during the hours your ads run.

Your website is ready for Google Ads when every ad group sends people to a relevant page that loads fast on a phone, conversion tracking for forms, calls and WhatsApp clicks has been tested with real test enquiries, your forms deliver reliably, the site has clear business details and the policy pages Google expects, and someone is ready to answer leads quickly. If any of those is missing, fix it before you spend, because you pay for every click whether it becomes a lead or not.

This is the checklist I work through before a campaign page goes live as part of landing page design: the page, the form and WhatsApp, and conversions in GA4, Google Ads and the Meta Pixel, all confirmed with test enquiries before any ad money is spent.

This post is about readiness before launch. For what typically goes wrong on the page itself, see landing page mistakes that waste ad budget, and for a step-by-step tracking setup, see Google Ads conversion tracking on WordPress.

1. Conversion tracking you can trust

Without conversion tracking you can see clicks but not enquiries, and Google's automated bidding has nothing useful to learn from. Before launch:

  • Decide what counts. A short list: enquiry form submitted, phone tapped, WhatsApp clicked, and orders if you sell online. Make the valuable ones primary and keep softer actions, such as brochure downloads, secondary.
  • Count successful submissions, through a thank-you page or the form's success event, never clicks on the submit button; see why every form needs a thank-you page.
  • Choose one source per action. Import GA4 key events or use the Google Ads tag, but don't make both primary for the same action, or every enquiry is counted twice.
  • Link GA4 and Google Ads and keep auto-tagging on, so each ad click carries its click ID to your site.
  • Make sure nothing strips the click ID. Redirects, some caching setups and form plugins that send people to another domain can drop the parameters added to your landing page URL. Click a test ad link with a dummy parameter added and confirm it survives to the page that loads.
  • Consent. If you get visitors from the EU or UK, Google expects consent mode for its advertising features there at the time of writing; for Indian visitors, mention tracking in your privacy policy and check the current rules.

Then test it: submit a real test enquiry, tap the phone number and the WhatsApp button, and confirm each one appears in GA4's real-time report and in Google Ads' conversion diagnostics. New conversions can take hours to show in Google Ads, so test a day or two before launch, not an hour before.

2. Calls and WhatsApp, not just forms

For many Indian businesses, calls and WhatsApp chats bring more enquiries than forms, so leaving them out makes your ads look worse than they are.

  • Track taps on tap-to-call links on your landing pages as a key event. This measures intent, not a completed call.
  • Track clicks on the WhatsApp button the same way, and use a pre-filled message that names the campaign, such as "Hi, I saw your ad for AC servicing", so your team knows where the chat came from.
  • If you use call assets, calls made from the ad itself can be reported in Google Ads; which call details are available depends on your country and account.
  • Ask every caller how they found you and note it, because no tracking catches every call.

3. A landing page that matches each ad group

List your ad groups and the promise each one makes, then decide where each one lands. Ad groups with the same promise can share a page; a different service, offer or location needs its own version.

Ad group (example)Promise in the adWhere it should land
AC repair KothrudSame-day AC repair in KothrudAn AC repair page headed with that promise and area
AC annual maintenanceAnnual maintenance contractA separate page explaining the contract and what it covers
Brand searchesYour business nameThe homepage is fine here

Check every final URL: no 404s, HTTPS, no redirect to the homepage, and the headline in the first screen repeats the ad. Campaign versions that copy your service pages are usually best kept out of Google with a noindex setting, so they don't compete with the pages you want to rank; ads can still send traffic to them.

4. Speed on a real phone

Most ad clicks come from phones, and a visitor who leaves while the page loads has already cost you the click. Google Ads also scores landing page experience as part of Quality Score.

  • Run the page through PageSpeed Insights and look at the mobile result, especially Largest Contentful Paint.
  • Open it on a mid-range Android phone on mobile data, not only on office Wi-Fi.
  • Remove sliders and autoplay video from the first screen, compress images to the size phones need, and load chat widgets and extra tracking scripts after the page.
  • Check that a cookie or offer pop-up doesn't cover the headline and button.

5. Forms that work, every time

  • Test delivery. Submit from a phone and a laptop, and confirm every enquiry arrives in the inbox, CRM or sheet, not in spam. If WordPress sends email without SMTP, fix that first.
  • Keep it short. Name and phone are often enough for a first call; every extra field loses some people.
  • Spam protection that doesn't block real people. Test with a normal phone browser, and check whether your anti-spam tool silently discards genuine messages.
  • A clear confirmation saying what happens next and when.
  • A named person who receives the leads, with a backup when they are away.

6. Policy pages and business details

Google reviews ads and the pages they point to. Its policies at the time of writing expect a working destination, not a page under construction, and an honest picture of who you are and what you offer. Before launch, check:

  • Your business name, address or service area, phone number and email are easy to find, ideally in the footer and on a contact page.
  • A privacy policy explains what you collect through forms and tracking; see privacy policy and cookie consent basics.
  • Online stores show delivery, returns and refund terms, and the prices on the page match the ad.
  • Claims are accurate and specific. Avoid "guaranteed results", "number one" or before-and-after promises you can't back up.
  • Regulated categories, such as healthcare, financial services and some others, have extra restrictions or may need certification; check Google's current policy for your category before you write the ads.

7. Proof near the call to action

Ad visitors usually know nothing about you. Put real proof beside the form and buttons: genuine reviews or testimonials with permission, photos of real work, client logos you are allowed to show, and any registrations you genuinely hold. Avoid stock photos that look like stock.

8. People ready to respond

Leads from ads go cold fast, and a quick reply often wins the job before a competitor calls back. Agree who answers calls and WhatsApp during your ad schedule, what they say, and how fast they reply, and pause ads during hours when nobody can respond. 9. A budget and a review routine

Start with your most profitable services and locations, tightly targeted, rather than everything at once; setting a sensible Google Ads budget shows how to work out a starting figure. In the first weeks, check the search terms report for irrelevant searches to add as negatives, compare conversions with the enquiries your team actually received, and change one thing at a time on the landing page so you know what made the difference.

FAQs

How do I know if my website is ready for Google Ads?

Check that each ad group has a matching landing page, the page loads fast on a phone, conversions for forms, calls and WhatsApp are tracked and tested, forms deliver, and someone is ready to respond during ad hours.

Can I send Google Ads traffic to my homepage?

For searches on your business name, yes. For service or offer searches, a page that repeats the ad's promise usually converts better, because visitors don't have to hunt for what they clicked on.

Do I need a privacy policy to run Google Ads?

If your page collects personal details through forms or uses tracking tags, you should have one, and Google's policies expect honest information about your business. Check the current policies for your category.

How do I test conversion tracking before launching?

Submit a real test enquiry, tap the phone and WhatsApp buttons, and check that each appears once in GA4's real-time report and in Google Ads' conversion diagnostics. Allow a day for Google Ads to show new conversions.

Should my Google Ads landing page be indexed by Google?

Usually not, if it repeats what your service pages say. Keeping campaign versions out of search stops them competing with the pages you want to rank, and ads can still send visitors to them.
