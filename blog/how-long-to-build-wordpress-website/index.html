How Long Does It Take to Build a WordPress Website? Realistic Timelines by Type of Site

How long does it take to build a WordPress website? Timelines by site type, from landing pages to WooCommerce stores, each stage, and what causes delays.

Key takeaways
  • Counted from when content is ready, a landing page usually takes 3–5 working days, a small business site 1–2 weeks and a WooCommerce store 2–4 weeks.
  • Late content, scattered feedback and features added midway cause more delays than the build itself, so prepare text, photos and logins before day one.
  • To hit a launch date, work backwards from it, start third-party approvals in the first week, and launch the essential pages first if time is short.

Counted from the day your content is ready, a single landing page usually takes 3–5 working days, a small business website of 5–8 pages 1–2 weeks, a larger business or B2B site 2–4 weeks, and a WooCommerce store 2–4 weeks. Complex custom features such as bookings, memberships or a second language push a project to 4 weeks or more.

"When can it go live?" is usually the second question after "How much will it cost?". The build itself is rarely what takes longest: content and feedback are. Below are the timelines I work to by type of site, what happens at each stage, and how to keep a project on schedule. The number of unique page layouts matters more than the total number of pages. Ten service pages built from one template go much faster than ten pages that each look different. Two smaller jobs people often ask about: moving an existing WordPress site to a new host usually takes 1–3 working days including testing, and speeding up an existing site usually takes 2–4 working days.

The stages of a WordPress project

Every project, from a five-page site to a store, goes through the same five stages. These are rough durations for a typical business site; on small sites some stages overlap.

1. Discovery and planning (1–3 days)

Agreeing on goals, pages, features, reference websites and who the site is for. Before any design, I map out the pages and what each one has to answer. A clear brief saves days later; the website brief template covers what to send.

2. Design (2–5 days)

The homepage and other key pages are designed first and shared for your feedback, so the look is agreed before the full build. One round of clear, consolidated feedback keeps this stage short.

3. Development (3–15 days)

Building every page on a private staging link, with global colours and fonts so the site stays consistent and easy to edit, then adding forms, WhatsApp, maps, payment gateways and other features. This stage has the widest range, because a five-page site and a store with a hundred products are very different jobs.

4. Content entry (1–5 days)

Adding text, images, products, page titles and descriptions. It is quick when content arrives organised, and slow when it arrives page by page over WhatsApp.

5. Testing and launch (1–2 days)

Checking every page on real phones and desktops, testing forms to make sure the emails arrive, placing test orders on stores and checking speed. Then the site goes live, a backup is taken, and Search Console and Google Analytics are connected.

What to have ready before the project starts

  • Text for each page, or at least your services in order of importance, since they become the main pages. How to write content for your business website helps you draft it.
  • Logo and photos: real photos of your team, premises and work, in the best quality you have.
  • Two or three websites you like, and what you like about each one.
  • Domain and hosting logins, or a note that you need them set up in your name.
  • How enquiries should arrive: form, WhatsApp, phone or all three, and who answers them.
  • For stores: product names, prices, variations and photos in a spreadsheet, plus your choice of payment gateway and shipping rules.
  • Any fixed launch date, such as a trade exhibition or a campaign, shared at the very start.

Why website projects get delayed

The build is rarely the bottleneck. These are the usual causes, and how to avoid each one:

DelayWhy it happensHow to avoid it
Content arrives late or in piecesPages can't be finished without their text and photosPrepare content before the start date, or launch with the essential pages first
Scattered feedbackComments from several people over several days, sometimes contradicting each otherOne decision-maker and one consolidated list per round; see how to give clear website feedback
Scope creepNew pages or features are added midwayNote them for a second phase unless they are essential for launch
Third-party approvalsPayment gateway activation, booking engines or ad account access can take daysApply in the first week, not the last
Missing accessHosting, domain or DNS logins sit with a previous developer or an ex-employeeRecover the logins before the project starts
Disorganised product dataStore products arrive as photos of a price listA spreadsheet with one row per product and variation

Things outside the build that take time

  • Payment gateway approval: gateways usually ask for business documents and pages such as refund and privacy policies before they activate live payments, and approval can take days.
  • DNS changes: when your domain is pointed at the new site, the change usually spreads within a few hours.
  • Appearing on Google: going live is not the same as being found. Google has to discover and index the new pages, which can take from days to a few weeks; see how to get a new website indexed faster.
  • Google Business Profile edits, if you are changing your address, phone or website link at the same time, can go through Google's own review.

Can a WordPress website be built faster?

Yes, within reason. A landing page or a small site can go live in a few days if content is ready and decisions are quick. For urgent launches, the best approach is to launch the essential pages first, usually home, services, about and contact, and add the rest in a second phase. What I don't recommend is skipping testing: a form that silently fails or a broken mobile menu costs far more than the day it saved.

Ready-made themes and page builders like Elementor are a big part of why WordPress is quick to build with. If you're weighing WordPress against a fully custom build, WordPress vs a custom-coded website compares time to launch as well as cost and editing.

Plan your launch date backwards

If you need the site live for a campaign, an exhibition or the festival season, work backwards from that date:

  1. Take the build time for your type of site from the table above.
  2. Add time for content and feedback. For a business site, starting at least 3–4 weeks before the deadline is sensible, and longer for a store.
  3. Start third-party approvals, such as the payment gateway, in the first week.
  4. Share the deadline at the start, so the schedule is planned around it rather than squeezed at the end.

FAQs

How long does it take to build a WordPress website?

Counted from when content is ready, a landing page usually takes 3–5 working days, a small business site of 5–8 pages 1–2 weeks, a larger business or B2B site 2–4 weeks and a WooCommerce store 2–4 weeks. Custom features such as bookings, memberships or multiple languages take 4 weeks or more.

How long does it take to build a WooCommerce store?

Usually 2–4 weeks once product details and photos are ready. The number of products and variations, the shipping rules and the payment gateway approval decide where in that range a store lands.

Can a WordPress website be built in a day?

WordPress itself installs in minutes, and a basic site on a ready-made theme can be put together quickly. A business website that is planned, written, tested on phones and set up for enquiries and Google takes longer; even a single landing page usually takes 3–5 working days.

Why is my website project taking so long?

Usually because content or feedback is late, new pages or features were added midway, or the project is waiting on access or a third-party approval. Ask your developer what is blocking progress; often one decision or one missing login is all it takes to unblock it.

How long does a website redesign take?

Most redesigns take 1–3 weeks once content is ready. Large sites, a move from another platform, heavy content rewriting and slow feedback stretch it, and URLs that change need redirects planned before design starts.

How long after launch will my website show on Google?

Google needs to discover and index the new pages, which can take from days to a few weeks. Submitting the sitemap in Search Console helps. Ranking well for competitive searches takes much longer than being indexed; see how long SEO takes.
