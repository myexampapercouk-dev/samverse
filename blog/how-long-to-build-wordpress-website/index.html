How Long Does It Take to Build a WordPress Website? (Realistic Timelines)

By Sameer Gupta · 29 September 2026 · 3 min read

On this page
  1. Typical timelines by type of website
  2. The stages of a WordPress project
  3. What speeds a project up
  4. What slows a project down
  5. Can it be done faster?
  6. Plan your launch

"When can it go live?" is usually the second question after "How much will it cost?". The honest answer depends on the size of the site and, more than anything, on how quickly content and feedback arrive. Here are realistic timelines and the things that make the biggest difference.

Typical timelines by type of website

Type of websiteTypical build time*
Single landing page3–5 working days
Small business website (5–8 pages)1–2 weeks
Larger business / B2B site with catalogue2–4 weeks
WooCommerce online store2–4 weeks
Redesign of an existing site1–3 weeks
Complex custom features (bookings, memberships, multilingual)4 weeks or more

*From the moment content is ready. Waiting for content is the most common reason projects take longer.

The stages of a WordPress project

1. Discovery (1–3 days)

Agreeing on goals, pages, features, reference websites and the target audience. A clear brief here saves days later.

2. Design (2–5 days)

The look and layout of key pages, usually the homepage first. You review and give feedback before the rest of the site is built.

3. Development (3–15 days)

Building all pages in WordPress, setting up forms, WhatsApp, payment gateways or other features, and making everything responsive.

4. Content entry (1–5 days)

Adding text, images, products and SEO details. This is fast when content is ready and organised.

5. Testing and launch (1–2 days)

Checking every page on mobile and desktop, testing forms and payments, speed optimization, then going live and submitting the sitemap to Google.

What speeds a project up

  • Content ready before starting: text for each page, logo, and good photos.
  • Clear references: 2–3 websites you like and what you like about them.
  • One decision-maker: feedback from one person avoids back-and-forth.
  • Quick feedback: replying within a day keeps momentum.
  • Hosting and domain access available on day one.

What slows a project down

  • Content that arrives page by page over weeks
  • Adding new pages or features midway (scope creep)
  • Many rounds of design changes without clear direction
  • Waiting for payment gateway or third-party approvals (these can take days)
  • Product data for stores that isn't organised in a spreadsheet

Can it be done faster?

Yes, within reason. A landing page or small site can often go live in a few days if content is ready and decisions are quick. For urgent launches, a good approach is to launch the essential pages first and add the rest in a second phase.

Plan your launch

Work backwards from your launch date: if you need the site live for a campaign or event, start at least 3–4 weeks earlier for a business site, and longer for a store. Share your deadline at the start so your developer can plan the schedule around it.
