Websites for Wedding and Sangeet Choreographers

What a wedding and sangeet choreographer's website needs: fast-loading performance videos, clear packages, home and online sessions, availability and enquiries.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Families want to know you can teach beginners and older relatives, how many sessions they need and whether you come home, use a studio or teach online.
  • Host performance videos on a video platform with click-to-play previews, and get each family's permission before publishing wedding footage.
  • Clear packages, an enquiry form that asks for the sangeet date and group size, and honest reviews turn Instagram interest into bookings.

Families planning a sangeet don't just want a good dancer. They want someone who can get a nervous groom, two shy aunties and a grandfather who hasn't danced in decades on stage, in step and smiling, within a few weeks. Instagram shows your energy, but a website is where a family checks your packages, sees how sessions work and sends a proper enquiry with their date.

This guide is for choreographers who prepare couples and families for weddings. If you run regular weekly classes, see websites for music and dance academies instead, as the needs are quite different.

What families want to know before booking

Most enquiries come from the bride, the groom or a cousin who has been put in charge of the sangeet. Their questions are usually the same:

  • Can you teach non-dancers? Patience with beginners and older relatives matters more than your own skill
  • Which styles do you choreograph? Bollywood, Punjabi and bhangra, garba and dandiya, regional styles, couple dances, contemporary
  • How many sessions will we need, and how many weeks before the wedding should we start?
  • Where do sessions happen? At our home, at your studio or online
  • What else do you handle? Song edits and mixes, entries, stage blocking, props and coordinating with the DJ on the day
  • Are you free on our dates? Especially in peak wedding season

Answer these on your homepage and service pages, and you'll get fewer vague "rates?" messages and more enquiries from families ready to book.

Show performances without slowing the site

Video is your strongest proof, but it's also the quickest way to make a site painfully slow on mobile data. A few rules keep both:

  • Host videos on a platform such as YouTube or Vimeo, not as large files uploaded to WordPress
  • Use click-to-play previews (a thumbnail that loads the player only when tapped) rather than autoplaying embeds
  • Put one short highlight reel near the top of the homepage, then group the rest by type: couple dances, family performances, bridal entries, flash mobs
  • Embed only a few hand-picked Instagram reels; social media feed plugins load heavy scripts on every page
  • Add a line under each clip: the occasion, the city and who performed, such as "parents of the groom, first-time dancers"

Rehearsal clips next to the final performance show your teaching ability better than a polished video alone. Always get the family's permission before publishing wedding footage, and take extra care with clips of children. Videos set to commercial songs can sometimes be muted or blocked by platforms, so check each platform's current rules. For more on using video well, see video on your business website.

Packages that are easy to compare

Families compare two or three choreographers at once, so make your packages easy to understand at a glance.

PackageWhat to spell out
Couple danceNumber of sessions, length of each, one song or a medley
Family performancesNumber of acts, maximum dancers per act, age range you're comfortable with
Complete sangeetAll acts, running order, song edits, rehearsal at the venue and presence on the night
Online coachingLive video sessions, practice videos and how relatives in other cities join in
Destination weddingsTravel, stay, how many days you'll be there and on-site rehearsals

Also state what costs extra: additional sessions, travel beyond your usual area, props or costume coordination. Whether to publish prices is your decision; "starting from" figures filter out mismatched budgets without locking you in. See should you show prices on your website.

Home, studio and online sessions

Explain each format on its own page or in a clear section, because families plan around them very differently.

Home sessions

List the areas you cover, any travel charge, and what the family needs to arrange: a cleared room, a speaker and a time when everyone can be present. Say if you can send a male or female choreographer on request, as some families have a preference.

Studio sessions

Share the address with a map, parking details and photos of the space, so families know it's large enough for a group.

Online sessions

Many families have relatives in other cities or abroad. Explain how live video sessions work, how you share private practice videos and how the whole group comes together for final rehearsals.

Availability and enquiries

The wedding date is the first thing you need, so make the enquiry form ask for what you need to check availability and quote:

  • Sangeet date, city and venue
  • Number of performances and approximate number of dancers
  • Age range of the dancers
  • Preferred format: home, studio or online
  • When they'd like to start
  • Budget range (optional)

Add a WhatsApp button with a pre-filled message such as "Hi, we'd like choreography for a sangeet on...". If you show an availability calendar, keep it accurate; showing booked dates as free causes more problems than having no calendar at all. Explain how a booking is confirmed, the advance (by UPI or bank transfer) and what happens if the wedding is postponed.

Trust signals families look for

  • Google reviews from families, requested soon after the wedding while memories are fresh
  • Your training and experience, stated honestly, plus profiles of any assistant choreographers who'll take sessions
  • Photos from real practice sessions, not only stage shots
  • A note on how you adapt steps for older relatives or anyone with knee or back problems
  • Clear terms on rescheduling and missed sessions

Getting found

Families search with an occasion and a city: "sangeet choreographer in Delhi", "wedding choreographer near me", "couple dance choreographer in Mumbai", "bridal entry choreography". To appear for these:

  • Create separate pages for the services you're genuinely known for, with real clips and details
  • Set up a Google Business Profile, as a service-area business if you mostly visit homes
  • Link your Instagram bio to the most relevant page, not just the homepage
  • Build relationships with planners and venues who can refer you; see what they need in websites for event and wedding planners
  • Update your packages and dates before each wedding season
