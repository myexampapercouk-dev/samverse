Websites for Glass, Aluminium and uPVC Window Fabricators

What glass, aluminium and uPVC window and door fabricators need online: product pages, project galleries, site measurement booking and builder enquiries.

Key takeaways
  • Give homeowners and builders separate paths: product pages and site measurement booking for homes, project details and RFQ forms for builders.
  • Explain uPVC vs aluminium and the main glass types simply, and keep performance claims general or backed by manufacturer data.
  • Show real installations by product and project type, spell out warranty and after-sales service, and target product-plus-city searches.

Whether you make uPVC windows, aluminium sliding doors, toughened glass partitions or shower enclosures, your customers usually fall into two groups. Homeowners want better windows for a new flat or a renovation, and they compare a few local fabricators before inviting anyone home. Builders, architects and contractors need a reliable partner for a whole project, and they check whether you can handle the quantity and the specification. A good website serves both, and turns visits into site measurement bookings and project enquiries.

What customers look for

HomeownersBuilders, architects and contractors
Window and door styles that suit their homeThe profile systems, glass types and hardware you work with
Help choosing between uPVC, aluminium and different glass optionsFabrication capacity, and how many openings you can deliver on time
Whether you cover their area, and how soon you can measureSimilar projects you've completed, with their scale and scope
What affects the cost, and what the warranty coversYour installation team, site supervision and after-sales support
Real photos and reviews from nearby homesA company profile they can share with their team or client

Your homepage should point each group to the right place quickly, for example with two clear paths: "For homes" and "For builders and projects".

Give every product type its own page

People search for specific products, not "fenestration solutions". Separate pages help them find what they need and help you rank:

  • Windows: sliding, casement, tilt-and-turn, fixed, bay windows and ventilators
  • Doors: sliding, sliding-folding, French and balcony doors
  • Glass work: toughened glass partitions, shower enclosures, glass railings and canopies
  • Commercial work: shopfronts, office partitions, curtain walls and structural glazing, if you do them
  • Add-ons: mosquito mesh, grilles, blinds and security hardware

On each page, explain where the product works best, the colour and finish options, the glass choices, the hardware and locking, and show photos of your own installations. If you fabricate with a particular brand's profile system, name it only if you're genuinely authorised to, and be clear about which parts you make and which you buy in.

Help buyers choose the right material and glass

Most homeowners don't know the difference between uPVC and aluminium, or between toughened and laminated glass. Explaining it simply builds trust and brings in search traffic, because these are exactly the questions people type into Google.

Guide topicWhat to cover
uPVC vs aluminiumLook, frame size, colours and finishes, maintenance, where each suits best, and relative cost
Glass typesPlain, toughened, laminated and double-glazed units, and where safety glass matters
Noise, heat and dustWhat frames, seals and glazing can realistically improve, without overpromising
Monsoon performanceDrainage, sealing and installation practices that keep water out
Care and maintenanceCleaning tracks, lubricating hardware and checking seals

Be careful with performance claims. If you mention noise reduction or energy savings, keep it general, or base it on test data that the profile or glass manufacturer actually publishes.

Make site measurement the main call to action

Nobody orders windows straight from a web page. The real conversion is a site visit to measure and advise, so make booking one easy:

  • A clear "Book a site measurement" button on every product page, saying whether the visit is free or chargeable
  • A short form asking for area or pin code, property type, new construction or replacement, the products they're interested in, the approximate number of openings and a preferred date
  • A WhatsApp option so people can send photos of their existing windows or a floor plan; see adding WhatsApp to your website
  • A list of the areas you serve, so people outside them know before they enquire

Explain your process on its own page: measurement, quotation, order confirmation, fabrication, installation, finishing and handover. Mention what affects timing, such as whether plastering and flooring are complete, and only give typical lead times if you can keep to them.

Show projects, not just products

Real installations sell far better than catalogue images. Organise your gallery by product and by project type (apartments, villas, offices, showrooms), and for key jobs add a short write-up: the brief, the products and glass used, the number of openings and the result. Label any manufacturer or stock images clearly, and ask customers' permission before showing their homes. Before-and-after photos of window replacements work especially well.

A separate path for builders and architects

Project buyers need different information from homeowners. Give them a dedicated page with:

  • Your fabrication unit, machinery and installation team, with real photos
  • The size and type of projects you've handled, naming builders only where you have permission
  • An RFQ form that accepts drawings, window schedules or a BOQ, along with the site location and timeline
  • A downloadable company profile, plus any test reports or certificates you genuinely hold
  • How you handle samples, mock-up installations and site supervision

Much of this overlaps with how other trade businesses win project work; see websites for hardware and building material suppliers and websites for construction companies for what contractors expect.

Build trust with warranty and service details

  • Warranty: what's covered for profiles, hardware, glass and workmanship, for how long, and what isn't covered
  • After-sales service: how customers raise a request, such as a stiff slider or a worn seal
  • Safety: where you use toughened or laminated glass, and how railings and partitions are fixed
  • Reviews: genuine Google reviews and customer photos, shared with permission
  • Your business: years of experience, and the address of your workshop or showroom with a map

Local SEO and speed

Most searches are local: "upvc windows in {city}", "aluminium sliding windows near me", "toughened glass partition {city}", "shower glass enclosure {area}". To show up for them:

  • Keep your Google Business Profile complete, with installation photos and the right categories
  • Give each product its own page and title, rather than one long "services" page
  • If you serve several cities, build location pages around real projects there; see local landing pages done right
  • Publish guides that answer common questions, such as choosing between sliding and casement windows

Galleries full of large photos slow pages down on mobile data, so compress images, lazy-load long galleries and host videos on YouTube rather than on your own server.
