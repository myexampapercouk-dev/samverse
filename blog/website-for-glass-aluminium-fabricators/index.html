Websites for Glass, Aluminium and uPVC Window Fabricators

How to plan a website for glass, aluminium and uPVC window fabricators in India: product pages, glass guides, site measurement, projects and local SEO.

Key takeaways
  • Give each product type, such as sliding windows, partitions, shower enclosures and facades, its own page with real installations and plain explanations.
  • Help buyers choose between aluminium and uPVC and between glass types, then make a site measurement visit the main call to action.
  • Show completed projects by type and area, offer a separate path for builders and architects, and keep warranty and service details clear.

A website for aluminium and uPVC window fabricators should show each product with real installations, explain materials and glass options in plain language, and make booking a site measurement easy. Projects, a builder enquiry path and local SEO by area turn that into steady enquiries.

Customers usually contact a fabricator when building a new home, renovating, or dealing with leaking, noisy or rattling windows. Builders and architects need a reliable fabricator for whole projects. Both groups look at your work online before calling. This guide covers what your website should include.

What customers look for

  • Proof you have done similar work: photos of real windows, partitions or facades
  • Which products you make and install, and whether you fabricate in-house
  • Help choosing between aluminium and uPVC, and between glass types
  • Whether you come to measure, which areas you serve and typical timelines
  • Warranty, after-sales service and who to call when something needs adjusting
  • For builders: capacity, project experience and how you work with site teams

Give every product type its own page

People search for specific products, so a single "Our products" page misses most searches. Typical product pages include:

  • Aluminium sliding windows and doors
  • uPVC windows and doors (casement, sliding, tilt and turn)
  • Toughened glass partitions for offices and homes
  • Shower enclosures and bathroom glass
  • Glass railings for balconies and staircases
  • Structural glazing and ACP facades for commercial buildings
  • Mosquito mesh and grill options

On each page, explain where the product suits, the options available (profiles, colours, glass, hardware), photos of your own installations and the next step. Write in plain language; buyers rarely know trade terms.

Help buyers choose the right material and glass

Most homeowners are unsure whether to choose aluminium or uPVC, and which glass to use. A clear, honest guide positions you as the expert:

QuestionWhat to explain
Aluminium or uPVC?Look, profile sizes, sound and heat insulation, maintenance and where each suits in your climate
Single or double glazing?When double glazing helps with noise or heat, and when it is not needed
Toughened or laminated glass?Safety differences, typical uses such as railings, partitions and large panes
Hardware and sealsLocks, rollers, handles, gaskets and the brands you use
FinishesPowder coating, anodising, wood-look and colour options

Stay general on performance. Avoid specific insulation or noise-reduction claims unless they come from the profile or glass manufacturer's published data, and say so when they do. Note that glass thickness and type for railings or large panes should follow the applicable standards and your structural advice.

Make site measurement the main call to action

Almost every job starts with a site visit. Put a "Book a site measurement" button on every product page and make the form short: name, phone, area or pin code, property type, products needed, approximate number of windows or openings and preferred visit time. Let people upload or WhatsApp photos of the openings; see adding WhatsApp to your website.

Explain your process on a separate page: site visit and measurement, quote, order confirmation, fabrication, installation and final check. Give typical timelines only if you can meet them, and mention what affects them, such as pending civil work or glass lead times.

Explain what affects the price

Customers want an idea of cost before the visit. Instead of a price list, explain the main factors: material and profile series, glass type and thickness, size and number of openings, hardware, finish, mesh, and the height and access of the site. If you quote per square foot for some products, explain what that includes and what is extra, such as GST, scaffolding or removal of old frames. Make it clear that the final price follows measurement.

Show projects, not just products

Projects build trust faster than any product description. For each project, show photos, the products used, the type of building and the area. Organise projects so visitors can filter by type: homes, apartments, offices, showrooms, hospitals or schools. Before-and-after photos of replaced windows are especially convincing.

Ask permission before naming a client or a building, and avoid showing house numbers or anything that identifies a private home's location.

A separate path for builders and architects

Builders, contractors and architects buy differently from homeowners. Give them their own page explaining your fabrication capacity, the project sizes you handle, how you coordinate with site teams, your approach to shop drawings and samples, and payment terms in general. Add a project enquiry form asking for project type, location, number of units or floors, drawings and timelines.

For context on what these buyers look for, see websites for construction companies and websites for hardware and building materials businesses. If you also manufacture profiles or supply other fabricators, the advice in websites for manufacturers applies too.

Build trust with warranty and service details

  • What the warranty covers (profiles, hardware, glass, workmanship) and for how long, written plainly
  • What is not covered, such as breakage after installation or damage from civil work
  • How to raise a service request and typical response times
  • Maintenance tips, such as cleaning tracks and checking seals
  • Your workshop or factory, with photos of machines and fabrication, if you make in-house
  • Genuine Google reviews and customer photos, shared with permission

Mention authorised dealer or fabricator status for a profile brand only where you genuinely hold it.

Guides that answer common window problems

Many people start their search with a problem, not a product: windows leaking during the monsoon, traffic noise in a bedroom, dust coming through old sliding windows, or a balcony that needs a safe railing. Short, practical guides on these problems bring in buyers before they have chosen a supplier, and give your team useful links to send on WhatsApp.

Good topics for fabricators include:

  • Why sliding windows leak in heavy rain, and what can be done
  • Replacing old windows in a flat without major civil work
  • Choosing windows for a home near a busy road
  • Glass railings versus steel railings for balconies
  • Glass partitions for small offices and clinics
  • How to maintain uPVC and aluminium windows

Keep each guide honest and practical. Explain the likely causes and options, mention when a site visit is needed to judge properly, and end with a link to the matching product page and the site measurement form. Add a photo or simple diagram where it helps. One well-written guide a month, based on questions your team actually hears, builds a useful library over a year.

Local SEO and speed

Most enquiries come from local searches such as "uPVC windows in Pune", "aluminium fabricator near me" or "glass partition Wakad". Keep a complete Google Business Profile with the right categories, project photos and reviews; see the Google Business Profile checklist. If you serve several cities or areas, build pages around real projects and genuine local details rather than copies with the city name changed; see local landing pages without doorway pages.

Project galleries can get heavy, so compress photos, use modern image formats and lazy-load long galleries. Keep project pages light enough to open quickly on a site visit, and avoid autoplay videos on the home page. Test the site on a phone over mobile data, because many enquiries come from people standing at a site.

FAQs

What should a website for aluminium and uPVC window fabricators include?

Separate product pages, a materials and glass guide, real project photos, a site measurement booking form, a page for builders and architects, and clear warranty and service details.

Should I put prices on my fabrication website?

Explain what drives the price instead of a fixed list, and say the final quote follows measurement. If you quote per square foot, state what it includes.

How do I get more enquiries from builders?

Create a dedicated page showing project experience, capacity and process, with a project enquiry form that asks for drawings and timelines.

Can I claim noise or heat reduction on my website?

Only using the manufacturer's published data for the specific profile or glass, and say that results depend on installation and site conditions.

Do I need a separate page for each product?

Yes, usually. People search for specific products such as glass partitions or uPVC sliding windows, so separate pages help them find you.
