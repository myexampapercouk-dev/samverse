How to Show Google Reviews on Your Website: WordPress and Shopify Options for Indian Businesses

How to show Google reviews on your website in WordPress or Shopify: widgets, plugins, manual quotes, speed impact, review schema rules and what to avoid.

Key takeaways
  • You can show Google reviews on your website with a plugin or widget that pulls them automatically, or by quoting a few manually with a link to your profile.
  • Choose a lightweight option, place reviews near the decision points, and always link to your Google Business Profile so visitors can verify them.
  • Do not add review star schema for your own business reviews pulled from Google, and never edit, invent or selectively misrepresent reviews.

To show Google reviews on your website, you have three practical options: a WordPress plugin or Shopify app that pulls reviews from your Google Business Profile automatically, a third-party widget you embed with a short code snippet, or a few hand-picked reviews quoted on the page with a link to your profile. For most Indian small businesses, a lightweight plugin or a short manual selection works best, placed near your contact or buy buttons.

This guide compares the options, explains where to place reviews for the most effect, and covers the speed, schema and honesty points that are easy to get wrong.

Why show Google reviews on your website?

Many customers in India check Google reviews before calling a business, booking an appointment or placing an order. If they find the same reviews on your website, near the button they are about to press, they do not need to leave the site to check. Reviews also answer questions your own copy cannot, such as whether staff are polite, whether deliveries arrive on time or whether a clinic explains things clearly.

Google reviews carry extra weight because visitors know you cannot easily write them yourself. That is also why they must be shown honestly and linked back to the source.

Showing Google reviews on WordPress

On WordPress, several plugins can display Google reviews. Most need you to connect your Google Business Profile, either through a Google account login, a Place ID or an API key. When comparing them, check:

  • How reviews are fetched: some show only a limited number of recent reviews because of how Google's data is accessed, while others need a paid plan for more.
  • Caching: reviews should be stored on your site and refreshed periodically, not loaded live from Google on every page view.
  • Layout options: a simple grid or a short slider is usually enough. Avoid autoplay carousels that move before people can read.
  • Script weight: test page speed before and after installing. Some review plugins add noticeable JavaScript and CSS.
  • Updates and support: choose a plugin that is actively maintained.

If the plugin adds files to every page, you can often limit it to the pages that actually show reviews. The guide on how to reduce unused CSS and JavaScript in WordPress explains how.

Showing Google reviews on Shopify

Shopify stores usually have two kinds of reviews: product reviews from buyers, collected through a reviews app, and Google reviews of the business as a whole. Google reviews work well on the home page, the about page and near the checkout reassurance area, while product reviews belong on product pages.

Several Shopify apps can show Google reviews in a theme section. Check whether the app adds scripts to every page, whether it fits your theme without custom code and what it costs over a year. For a smaller store, a few manually added quotes in a custom section can do the same job without another app. The Shopify store slow fix guide explains why fewer apps usually means a faster store.

Where to place reviews for the most effect

Reviews work best where a visitor is deciding something. Good spots include:

  • Just below the main headline on the home page, as a short rating summary with a link.
  • Next to the enquiry form or booking button on service pages.
  • On the contact page, beside the map and phone number.
  • Near the add-to-cart area or in the cart, for online stores.
  • On location pages, using reviews that mention that branch.

Choose reviews that mention specific things, such as punctuality, quality or after-sales support, rather than generic one-liners. Always include a "See all reviews on Google" link so visitors can check the full picture.

Businesses with several branches

If you run more than one clinic, store or centre, each branch usually has its own Google Business Profile and its own reviews. Mixing them all into one slider can confuse visitors, especially if one branch is rated higher than another. A cleaner approach is:

  • Create a separate page for each branch with its address, timings and map.
  • Show that branch's reviews on its page, with a link to the matching Google profile.
  • On the home page, show a short selection from different branches and link to each location page.

Most review plugins can connect to more than one profile, though some charge extra for it. Check this before buying if you have, or plan to open, several locations. It also makes the reviews more useful to the reader, because someone in Kothrud cares more about the Kothrud branch than one across the city.

For a single-location business, keep it simple: one review section linked to one profile, with recent reviews and a clear link to see all of them on Google.

Review schema: what not to do

Many review plugins offer to add star rating schema so that stars appear in Google search results. Be careful. Google's guidelines say that self-serving reviews, meaning reviews about your own business displayed on your own site, are not eligible for review rich results for local businesses and organisations. Adding such markup will not get you stars and can cause problems in Search Console.

Product reviews collected on your own store for specific products are a different case and can be marked up properly. The schema markup explained guide covers the basics.

If you do add review markup, check it with a rich snippet testing tool so errors are caught before Google sees them.

Honesty and permission

  • Never edit a review's wording, even to fix spelling, without making it clear.
  • Do not invent reviews or copy them from other businesses.
  • Show the reviewer's name as it appears on Google and keep the date.
  • Do not imply that the reviews you selected represent everyone if your overall rating says otherwise.
  • Reply to negative reviews on Google calmly. The guide on how to handle negative reviews helps.

Getting more reviews to show

A review section only helps if there are recent reviews to show. Ask happy customers at the right moment, such as after a successful delivery or appointment, and make it easy with a direct review link sent on WhatsApp. The guide on how to get more Google reviews covers the details. Avoid offering discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews, which breaks Google's policies.

Keeping the review section fresh

A review slider showing the same five reviews from three years ago can do more harm than good, because visitors notice the dates. If you use a plugin, check every few months that it is still fetching new reviews, since changes to Google's systems or an expired connection can quietly stop updates. If you quote reviews manually, set a reminder to swap in a few recent ones each quarter.

It also helps to look at what customers keep praising or complaining about. If several reviews mention quick delivery in your city, that is a point worth repeating in your page copy. If several mention slow replies, fix the process before featuring more reviews.

FAQs

How do I show Google reviews on my website?

Use a WordPress plugin or Shopify app that connects to your Google Business Profile, embed a third-party review widget, or quote a few reviews manually with a link to your Google profile.

Will a Google reviews plugin slow down my website?

Some do, because they load extra scripts and styles. Choose one that caches reviews, test speed before and after, and load it only on the pages that need it.

Can I get star ratings in Google search from my Google reviews?

Not for your own business. Google does not show review rich results for self-serving reviews of local businesses, so adding that markup will not produce stars.

Can I show only my best Google reviews?

You can choose which reviews to quote, but always link to the full list on Google and do not present a selection as your overall rating.

Is it allowed to copy Google reviews onto my website?

Quoting genuine reviews of your own business with the reviewer's name and a link back to Google is common practice. Do not alter the wording or present them as written on your site.
