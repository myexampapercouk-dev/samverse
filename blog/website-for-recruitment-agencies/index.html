Websites for Recruitment and Placement Agencies: Job Listings, CV Uploads, Employer Enquiries and Trust

Plan a recruitment or placement agency website: job pages with JobPosting schema, safe CV uploads, employer hiring forms, ATS links and fraud warnings.

Key takeaways
  • Give employers and candidates separate journeys from the homepage, each with its own pages, forms and calls to action, so hiring enquiries never get lost among CVs.
  • Publish every live job as its own page with JobPosting structured data and a closing date, and take filled roles down promptly so candidates and Google only see real openings.
  • Treat CVs as sensitive personal data: collect them through a secure form with a clear consent line, limit who can open them, delete them on a schedule and warn candidates about fake offers.

A recruitment or placement agency website has to do two jobs at once: convince employers that you can fill their roles, and give job seekers a trustworthy place to find openings and send a CV. The best agency sites split these into two clear journeys from the homepage, publish each live job as its own page that Google can read, handle CVs as the sensitive personal data they are, and make the employer enquiry form ask the questions your consultants need before the first call.

This guide covers the pages, forms and integrations that matter for staffing firms, executive search consultants and placement agencies in India. Two audiences, two journeys

Most agency websites fail in the same way: one "Contact us" form that receives a hiring manager's urgent requirement and a fresher's CV in the same inbox, and a homepage that talks to neither. Start by writing down what each audience needs:

Employers and HR teamsCandidates
What they want to knowWhich roles and industries you hire for, how you work, how fast you shortlist and what it costsWhich jobs are open, whether they qualify, and whether you are genuine
Main actionShare a requirement or book a callApply for a job or register a CV
Key pagesServices, industries, process, client proof, hiring enquiryJob listings, job pages, CV registration, fraud warning, career tips
What puts them offVague claims and no named specialismsExpired jobs, requests for money and no physical address

Put two clear buttons near the top of the homepage, such as "Hire talent" and "Find a job", and keep that split in the main menu. Separate forms send each enquiry to the right person. Studio Agama Interiors, an interior design studio I built a site for, uses the same principle in a different field: design partners apply through their own page and form, so partner enquiries never get mixed up with homeowners asking for quotes.

Pages for the employer side

  • Services: permanent hiring, contract and temporary staffing, bulk or volume hiring, executive search, campus hiring, payroll or compliance support. Give each one a page only if you genuinely offer it, and explain how it works and who it suits.
  • Industries and functions: pages such as IT and software, manufacturing and engineering, BFSI, healthcare, retail or warehousing. Name the roles you fill in each, in the terms hiring managers use.
  • How you work: from requirement to shortlist, interviews, offer and joining, with what the employer receives at each step. If you offer a replacement period, state the conditions plainly.
  • Proof: client logos and testimonials only with written permission, and short descriptions of the kind of mandates you handle. Never invent placement numbers or turnaround claims.
  • Hiring enquiry: a form built for employers, covered below.

An employer enquiry form that qualifies the lead

Ask what a consultant needs before calling: company name, role title, number of positions, location, experience range, employment type (permanent, contract or bulk), expected joining date and an optional job description upload. A dropdown for the hiring service tells you whether the enquiry is a single senior search or a volume drive. A budget or salary band field can be optional; many employers prefer to discuss it on the call.

Job listings that candidates and Google can read

One page per job

Each live opening should have its own page with a specific title ("Accounts Executive, Wakad, Pune" rather than "Urgent opening"), location, experience required, key responsibilities, qualifications, employment type, a salary range if the client allows it, a job reference number, the closing date and a clear Apply button. A listings page with filters for function, location and experience helps once you have more than a handful of roles, and a job alert sign-up gives candidates a reason to return.

JobPosting structured data

Google can show job openings in its own job search experience, where available, using JobPosting structured data on each job page. The markup includes the title, description, date posted, the hiring organisation, the location, the employment type and, where you can share it, the salary. Google's guidelines expect the page to describe one real, current opening that people can apply for, and filled or expired jobs to be removed or given an end date with validThrough. Confidential mandates need care: check Google's current guidance on how to describe the hiring organisation when you cannot name the client. Schema markup explained covers how structured data works, and none of it guarantees that a job will appear.

Keep the job board honest

  • Remove or close jobs the day they are filled, and redirect the page to a related listing
  • Don't post the same role many times with small changes to fill the page
  • Don't publish "evergreen" adverts for jobs that don't exist just to collect CVs; if you want a talent pool, call it that

CV uploads and candidate data

A CV contains a name, phone number, address, employment history and sometimes much more, so treat every upload as sensitive personal data. India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act expects businesses to collect personal data for a clear purpose, with notice and consent, and to protect it; India's DPDP Act and your website explains the basics, and your lawyer can confirm what applies to your agency as the rules take effect.

  • Collect only what you need: name, phone, email, current role, experience, location and the CV. Leave Aadhaar, PAN, bank details and certificates until a later stage, and never on a public form.
  • Consent line: say why you are collecting the CV, that you may share it with prospective employers for suitable roles, how long you keep it and how to ask for deletion.
  • File handling: accept PDF and Word files only, set a size limit, and store uploads where they cannot be opened by a direct public link.
  • Access: limit who can see CVs, give each consultant their own login, and avoid forwarding CVs around a shared email inbox.
  • Retention: decide how long you keep unsuccessful applications and delete them on a schedule.

Connecting the site to an ATS or CRM

Most agencies track candidates in an applicant tracking system, a CRM or a spreadsheet. The website should feed that system, not become a second database someone has to copy from.

ApproachGood forWatch out for
ATS careers widget or job feed embedded in the siteAgencies already using an ATS that offers oneJobs shown inside a widget may not be readable by Google; check whether each job gets its own crawlable page
Job pages managed in WordPress, applications sent to the ATS by email or APIAgencies wanting job pages that rank, with their ATS kept as the master recordTwo places to update unless jobs are synced
WordPress job pages with forms sent to a CRM or Google SheetSmaller agencies without an ATSAccess control and deletion become your responsibility

Whichever you choose, test that every application arrives with its CV attached or linked, tagged with the job reference. Connecting WordPress forms to a CRM or Google Sheets walks through the options.

Fraud warnings and fee transparency

Fake job offers that use a real agency's name, then ask candidates for "registration" or "training" fees, are common. They damage your reputation even though you had nothing to do with them. Protect candidates, and yourself, with a short page linked from the footer and every job page:

  • State plainly whether you ever charge candidates, and if so, for what
  • List your official website, email domain, phone numbers and WhatsApp number, and say you never recruit through personal email addresses
  • Explain how to verify an offer, and how to report a suspicious one

If you recruit for jobs abroad, Indian law generally requires recruiting agents for overseas employment to hold a registration from the Ministry of External Affairs, and some states regulate placement agencies, especially for domestic workers. Show any registration number only if you hold it, and check the current rules. Agencies that place nannies, cooks and caretakers face their own questions; see websites for domestic help and caretaker placement agencies.

Local SEO and content for recruitment agencies

Employers search for "recruitment agency in Pune" or "staffing company for warehouse staff in Chakan", and candidates search for "jobs in Hinjewadi" or "placement consultants near me". To be found for both:

  • A complete Google Business Profile for each office candidates can visit, with the same name, address and phone as the website
  • Industry pages, and city pages only where you genuinely recruit, each with real local roles and clients rather than copies with the city name swapped; see local landing pages without doorway pages
  • Guides for hiring managers, such as how to write a job description or plan a bulk hiring drive
  • Interview and CV advice for candidates, written by your consultants
  • Salary guides only if they come from your own placement data, with the date and how you gathered it

For campus drives or a large hiring campaign, a dedicated landing page with its own form keeps those applications separate and measurable.

Common mistakes on agency websites

  • One contact form for everyone, so employer enquiries sit unanswered among CVs
  • Jobs that closed months ago still listed, or "urgent" roles that never change
  • CVs sent as email attachments to a shared inbox anyone can open
  • Big placement numbers or shortlisting promises that you cannot back up
  • Client logos used without permission
  • No fraud warning, no fee policy and no physical address

FAQs

Should a recruitment agency website have a job portal?

Yes, if you have a steady flow of live roles you are allowed to advertise. A simple job board with one page per opening, filters and an apply form is enough for most agencies. If most of your work is confidential executive search, a CV registration form and specialism pages may matter more than listings.

Can my jobs appear in Google's job search results?

They can, where Google offers that feature, if each job has its own page with valid JobPosting structured data and follows Google's guidelines. Expired jobs must be removed or marked with an end date. Nobody can guarantee that a listing will be shown.

Is it safe to accept CVs through the website?

It can be, if uploads are stored privately, access is limited, the form explains how the data is used and you delete old CVs on a schedule. Ask your lawyer to review the consent wording against the current DPDP rules.

Can the website connect to our applicant tracking system?

Often, yes. Many ATS products offer a job feed, an embeddable widget or an email or API route for applications. The right choice depends on whether you want job pages that rank in Google or are happy for the ATS to host them.

Should we show salaries on job pages?

Where the client allows it, a salary range helps candidates decide quickly and can be included in the job's structured data. If the client prefers not to disclose it, say that salary is discussed at shortlisting.
