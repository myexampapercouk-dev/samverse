Websites for Cloud Kitchens: Direct Ordering, WhatsApp Orders, Multiple Brands, Delivery Areas and FSSAI Details

Plan a cloud kitchen website: your own ordering versus delivery apps, a WhatsApp order flow, menus that rank, delivery areas, multiple brands and FSSAI details.

Key takeaways
  • Keep delivery apps for discovery, and use your own website and WhatsApp to turn repeat customers into direct orders you keep more of, with a clear reason to order direct.
  • Choose the ordering flow that matches your team: WhatsApp orders for small menus, WooCommerce ordering with payment, delivery areas and kitchen hours for higher volumes.
  • Put every brand menu on real web pages with prices, veg markers and allergens, show your FSSAI details, and check Google rules before creating profiles for virtual brands.

A cloud kitchen website is not there to replace the delivery apps. It is there to win the orders you should not be paying commission on: repeat customers, office and party orders, and people who find your brand on Google or Instagram. The sites that work show each brand's menu on proper web pages, make ordering direct easy through WhatsApp or online checkout, are clear about delivery areas and timings, display FSSAI details, and give customers a real reason to order direct next time.

My websites for restaurants, cafes and food brands cover exactly these pieces: a mobile-friendly menu you update yourself, WhatsApp ordering for simple setups or full WooCommerce ordering with online payments and delivery areas, ordering that pauses outside kitchen hours, and your FSSAI number on ordering pages. This guide is about the delivery-only model, where there is no dining room to fill and every order arrives by phone.

Delivery apps or your own ordering site?

For most cloud kitchens the honest answer is both, with different jobs:

Delivery appsYour own website and WhatsApp
Who finds youPeople browsing the app, next to every competing kitchen nearbyPeople searching your brand, dish or area on Google, and your existing customers
Cost per orderCommission and fees set by the platformPayment gateway charges on online payments, plus your own delivery cost
Customer detailsMostly stay with the platformCome to you, with permission to contact them
DeliveryThe platform's ridersYour riders, a delivery partner you book, or pickup
Menu and pricing controlWithin the app's formats and rulesFull control: combos, party packs, subscriptions

Read your platform agreements before you plan direct-order offers, since some terms cover pricing and in-bag promotion. Restaurant online ordering options compared walks through the main routes in brief.

One brand or several virtual brands?

Many cloud kitchens run several brands from one kitchen: a biryani brand, a pizza brand and a healthy bowls brand, for example. There are three sensible structures:

  • A separate website per brand: the strongest identity and search presence for each, but more domains, hosting and updates to manage
  • One website with a section per brand: simpler to run, and customers can order from more than one brand in a single order if your kitchen and packing can handle it
  • A parent brand site with brand pages: useful if you also want franchise or investor enquiries, with each brand still getting its own menu pages

Whichever you choose, each brand needs its own menu, photos and story, not one combined list. Be careful with Google Business Profiles: several virtual brands at one kitchen address can run into Google's guidelines, and profiles that break them can be suspended. Check the current rules before creating a profile for each brand.

Direct ordering flows that suit your team

WhatsApp ordering

The simplest start: the menu lives on your website, and an "Order on WhatsApp" button opens a chat with a pre-filled message. A small cart that sends the chosen dishes, quantities and delivery address to WhatsApp saves typing for both sides. You confirm the order and delivery time, send a UPI payment link or QR code, and dispatch. It costs nothing extra to run, but every order is handled by hand, so it suits smaller menus and lower volumes. Adding WhatsApp to your business website covers buttons, pre-filled messages and tracking.

Online ordering with payment

Once WhatsApp orders become hard to keep up with, a proper ordering system pays for itself in staff time. A WooCommerce menu can handle add-ons and portion sizes, combos, UPI and card payments, delivery areas, minimum order values, delivery fees and time slots, and it can stop taking orders outside kitchen hours or when you switch it off on a busy evening. New orders can reach the kitchen by email, WhatsApp or SMS notification. For Southern Flavours, an Indian restaurant in the UK, I built a WooCommerce shop listing more than 100 dishes in 13 categories, each with a price, a quantity control and an Add to Cart button, alongside a newsletter sign-up that lets the restaurant reach regulars directly.

Pickup and takeaway

If customers can collect from your kitchen, offer it at checkout with clear pickup instructions, especially if the kitchen is hard to find in a commercial building. Pickup orders cost you nothing in delivery.

Delivery areas, timings and logistics

  • Define the area: a list of PIN codes or localities, or a radius, shown on the site so customers know before they choose dishes; a pin code delivery checker stops orders you cannot fulfil
  • Fees and minimums: delivery fees by distance or zone, and a minimum order value, shown before checkout
  • Who delivers: your own riders, a hyperlocal delivery partner booked per order, or pickup; plan what happens when riders are busy
  • Honest times: estimated delivery windows you can actually meet at peak hours
  • Packaging: containers that keep food hot and leak-free over the distance you cover

Menu pages that can be found on Google

A menu uploaded as an image or PDF can't be read well by Google and is awkward on a phone. Build each brand's menu as a web page, with sections, dish names, short descriptions, prices, veg or non-veg markers, spice level, portion size or "serves", and allergen notes supplied by your kitchen. Putting your menu online explains why a web page beats a PDF.

People search for dishes and areas, such as "biryani delivery in Kharadi", "healthy lunch box near me" or "late night food delivery Baner". Use those words naturally in page titles and headings, give your signature dishes a section or page of their own, and add Restaurant structured data with a link to the menu page where it fits.

Giving customers a reason to order direct

Customers will not leave an app they are used to without a reason. Options that work, as long as they fit your platform agreements:

  • A free add-on, dessert or drink on direct orders
  • Combos, family packs or dishes only available direct
  • Loyalty rewards for repeat orders, kept simple
  • Faster help on WhatsApp if something goes wrong
  • Party and office orders, which apps handle poorly
  • A QR code on your own packaging or menu cards linking to the direct ordering page, where your agreements allow it; see QR codes for your business website

Ask direct customers for permission to send offers on WhatsApp or email, and keep messages occasional. Subscription meal plans are a natural extension if you cook daily meals; see websites for tiffin and meal subscription services.

FSSAI, hygiene and kitchen trust

Delivery-only kitchens have no dining room for customers to judge, so the website has to show the kitchen instead.

  • FSSAI details: food businesses in India need FSSAI registration or a licence, and FSSAI expects the number to be shown to customers, including when food is sold online; put it in the footer and on ordering pages, and check the current rules for your licence type with your FSSAI consultant
  • Kitchen photos or a short video of the real kitchen, staff in hygiene gear and your packing area
  • Hygiene practices you actually follow, described plainly
  • Veg and non-veg handling: if you prepare both, say how you separate them, only if it is true
  • Clear totals with taxes, packing and delivery charges shown before payment, and invoices in the format your CA advises

Measuring whether direct ordering is working

Track direct orders per week, repeat customers, average order value and what each direct order costs you compared with an app order. Tag the links on your packaging, Instagram bio and WhatsApp broadcasts so you can see which brings orders. A direct channel that grows slowly but steadily is doing its job.

Common mistakes on cloud kitchen websites

  • Menus as photos of a printed card, or prices that differ from the app with no explanation
  • No delivery area shown, so customers order and then get cancelled
  • Ordering left open when the kitchen is closed
  • All brands mashed into one list with no identity for any of them
  • No FSSAI number, kitchen photos or allergen information
  • Direct-order offers that break the terms of a platform agreement

Running direct orders day to day

A direct ordering site only works if the kitchen can handle its orders as smoothly as aggregator orders. Plan the daily operation before launch, not after the first busy Friday.

One place to see every order

Kitchen staff should not have to watch email, WhatsApp and a dashboard at once. Choose a setup where direct orders appear on one screen or tablet with a sound alert, and where staff can mark an order as accepted, being prepared and dispatched.

Turning items and the store off quickly

When an item runs out or the kitchen is overloaded, someone must be able to hide that dish or pause ordering in a few taps from a phone. Ordering that stays open while the kitchen cannot cope does more harm than having no website.

Delivery handover

Decide whether you use your own riders, a hyperlocal delivery service or both, and how the delivery partner gets the address and the customer's number. Test the full flow, from order to doorstep, with real addresses in each delivery zone.

Payments and refunds

Accept UPI and cards through a payment gateway, and decide in advance whether you allow cash on delivery. Write down how refunds work for a cancelled or wrong order, who approves them and how quickly they are processed, and put the customer-facing version on the site.

Customer data, handled with care

Direct orders give you customer phone numbers and addresses. Use them only for orders and for offers the customer agreed to receive, keep them secure, and publish a short privacy policy explaining this.

FAQs

Should a cloud kitchen stay on delivery apps after launching its own website?

Usually yes. The apps bring new customers who have never heard of you; your website and WhatsApp suit repeat customers, bulk orders and people searching your brand.

Is WhatsApp ordering enough to start with?

For a small menu and modest volumes, yes. Move to online ordering with payment when manual replies start to delay orders or cause mistakes.

Can one website handle several virtual brands?

Yes. Each brand can have its own section, menu and identity, and a shared cart is possible if your kitchen can pack mixed orders reliably.

Do I need to show my FSSAI number on my website?

If you take orders online, it is expected and it reassures customers. Put it in the footer and on ordering pages, and confirm the current requirements for your licence type.

Can the website stop orders when the kitchen is closed?

Yes. Online ordering can follow your kitchen hours, pause on holidays, and be switched off for a busy evening from the dashboard.

Can a cloud kitchen take orders through WhatsApp from its website?

Yes. A simple menu page can send the chosen items to WhatsApp as a pre-filled message. It is quick to set up, but for higher volumes a proper cart with online payment is easier for the kitchen to manage.
