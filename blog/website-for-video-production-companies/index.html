Websites for Video Production Companies: Showreels, Services and Enquiries

What a website for video production companies needs: a fast showreel, service pages, case studies, a project brief form and local search visibility.

Key takeaways
  • A video production website should lead with a short, fast-loading showreel and make it obvious what kinds of videos you make and for whom.
  • Case studies that explain the brief, your approach and the deliverables sell better than a wall of embedded videos with no context.
  • A project brief form that asks about video type, timeline and budget range brings better enquiries than a plain contact form.

A website for video production companies has one main job: show your work quickly and turn the right viewers into project enquiries. That means a short showreel that loads fast on a phone, clear pages for the types of video you make, case studies with context, and an enquiry form that collects enough about the project for you to reply properly.

Here is how to structure the site, present your work without slowing it down, and attract the clients you actually want.

What a website for video production companies needs

  • A clear positioning line: corporate films, brand ads, product videos, weddings, events, documentaries, animation, or a specific mix.
  • A short showreel near the top of the homepage.
  • Service pages for each main type of video.
  • Selected work organised by industry or video type, with case studies for the best projects.
  • Process: how a project runs from brief to delivery.
  • Team and equipment, briefly, without turning into a gear list.
  • A project brief form, plus WhatsApp and phone.

The showreel: short, fast and focused

Most visitors decide within the first seconds whether your style fits. A good showreel helps; a slow or overly long one loses them.

  • Keep the homepage reel short and show your best and most relevant work first.
  • Host videos on YouTube or Vimeo and embed them with a lightweight click-to-play preview (a poster image that loads the player only when tapped). Embedding several full players directly can make pages very slow.
  • If you use a silent background video, keep it short, compressed and muted, with a static image on mobile.
  • Make separate reels for different audiences if your work varies a lot, such as corporate and wedding.

Service pages for each type of video

A single "Services" page listing everything is hard for buyers and search engines. Separate pages work better, each with examples, process and FAQs specific to that type:

Service pageWhat buyers want to see
Corporate filmsCompany profiles, culture videos, interviews, approval process
Brand and ad filmsConcept development, scripts, casting, formats for TV and social
Product and e-commerce videosStudio setup, demos, short formats for listings and reels
Event coverageMulti-camera, same-day edits, live streaming if offered
Explainer and animationScript, storyboard, voice-over, revisions
Social media contentVertical formats, batch shoots, monthly retainers

Each service page should end with a link to the brief form, pre-selecting that video type where possible, so a buyer who has just read about product videos does not have to start again. Only list services you genuinely deliver well. A focused site with three strong service pages beats a long list of everything.

Case studies that sell

A wall of embedded videos shows style but not how you work. Short case studies for your best projects answer the questions buyers have:

  • The client and the brief: what they needed and why (with permission to name them).
  • Your approach: concept, locations, crew, challenges solved.
  • Deliverables: main film, cut-downs, vertical versions, stills.
  • The final video, embedded with a lightweight player.
  • What the client said, only if they gave a genuine quote you can use.

Avoid inventing view counts or results. If a client shares real outcomes and agrees to publish them, include them accurately; otherwise describe the work itself. Portfolio websites for creatives has more on presenting work, and video testimonials covers client interviews if you can get them.

Explain your process and what affects cost

Buyers new to video production often do not know what to expect. A clear process page reduces nervousness and time spent explaining:

  1. Brief and discovery call.
  2. Proposal, concept and quote.
  3. Pre-production: script, storyboard, locations, casting, schedule.
  4. Shoot days.
  5. Editing, colour, sound, graphics.
  6. Review rounds and final delivery in agreed formats.

Rather than quoting fixed prices, explain what drives cost: number of shoot days, crew size, locations, talent, equipment, animation, number of versions and revision rounds. That helps buyers prepare a realistic budget range for your form.

A project brief form that brings better enquiries

"Hi, need a video, what's the cost?" is hard to answer. A structured form gives you what you need to reply properly:

  • Name, company, phone and email.
  • Type of video (dropdown).
  • Where it will be used: website, social, TV, events, internal.
  • Rough length and number of versions.
  • Timeline or deadline.
  • Budget range (optional, but helps a lot).
  • Reference videos they like.

A multi-step form keeps this from feeling long. Send enquiries to email and, if you use one, a CRM, and reply quickly.

Getting found in search

Clients search for "corporate video production company in Pune", "product video shoot Mumbai" or "explainer video company India". To appear:

  • Use clear titles and headings that name the video type and city on each service page.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile complete, with photos from shoots and the website link.
  • Write helpful articles answering buyer questions, such as how to prepare for a corporate shoot.
  • Give each video a proper title and description on YouTube that link back to the site. YouTube SEO for small businesses explains how.

Team, crew and equipment pages

Buyers want to know who will turn up on the day and who will edit their film. A short team page with real photos, roles and relevant experience builds confidence, especially for corporate clients who need to brief your team about their brand. Keep it honest: list people who actually work on your projects, and say clearly if you bring in freelance crew for larger shoots.

An equipment list can help when buyers have specific needs, such as multi-camera events, drones or studio lighting. Keep it brief and focused on what it means for the client, such as "two-camera interviews with professional audio", rather than a long list of model numbers. If drone shoots are part of your work, mention that you follow current drone rules and permissions without claiming approvals you do not hold.

Blog and resources that attract buyers

A few helpful articles can bring in clients who are early in their planning. Ideas that tend to match what buyers search for:

  • How to plan a corporate video shoot, and what to prepare before the crew arrives.
  • What to include in a video brief.
  • Choosing between live action and animation for an explainer.
  • How many versions and formats you need for website, Instagram and YouTube.
  • What affects the cost of a brand film.

Link each article to the relevant service page and the brief form. Keep them practical; buyers can tell the difference between real advice and filler written only for search engines.

Keep the portfolio current

Your best work from three years ago may not reflect your current style or quality. Review the showreel and portfolio every few months, remove weaker or outdated projects, and add recent ones with short case studies. A smaller, current portfolio is more convincing than a large archive.

Common mistakes on video production websites

  • A heavy autoplay video that takes many seconds to load on mobile data.
  • Dozens of embedded players on one page.
  • No clear statement of what kinds of videos you make.
  • No context on projects, just thumbnails.
  • Only an email address, no form or WhatsApp.
  • An old showreel that no longer reflects your best work.

Agencies and partners

Many production houses work with marketing agencies as their production partner. If that is part of your business, add a page for agencies explaining white-label options, how you handle briefs and approvals, and confidentiality. It can bring steady repeat work.

FAQs

What should a website for video production companies include?

A short showreel, service pages for each video type, case studies with context, a process page, a brief form with project details, and clear contact options including WhatsApp.

How do I add videos without slowing down my site?

Host on YouTube or Vimeo and use a click-to-play preview image so the player loads only when tapped. Avoid many full embeds on one page and heavy autoplay backgrounds.

Should a video production company show prices?

Fixed prices rarely fit, but explaining what affects cost and asking for a budget range in the form helps both sides.

Can I show client videos on my website?

Check your agreement with the client and get permission, especially for unreleased or internal videos.

Is WordPress good for a video portfolio site?

Yes. It handles portfolios, case studies and forms well, as long as videos are embedded efficiently.
