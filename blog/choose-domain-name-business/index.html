How to Choose a Domain Name for Your Business: Brand vs Keyword, .in vs .com, Trademark Checks, Buying, Renewing and Mistakes to Avoid

How to choose a domain name for your business: brand vs keyword, length, .in vs .com, trademark and history checks, buying, renewing and common mistakes.

Key takeaways
  • Pick a short name that matches your business, passes the phone test and works as an email address, on .in or .com depending on where your customers are.
  • Before buying, check trademarks, existing businesses, social handles and the domain's history, and avoid hyphens, numbers and spellings you would need to explain.
  • Register it in your business's own registrar account with a business email, turn on auto-renew and two-factor login, and compare renewal prices, not first-year offers.

A good business domain name is short, easy to say and spell, matches your business name and still makes sense as an email address, such as hello@yourbrand.in. Choose .in or .com based on where your customers are, check the name against trademarks, existing businesses and social handles before buying, and register it in your own business's account with auto-renew switched on. Keywords in the domain help far less than most people expect, so a clean brand name usually beats a long keyword string.

Brand name or keyword domain?

There are three common patterns. The examples are fictional:

TypeExampleStrengthsWeaknesses
Brand namekavericlinic.inMemorable, matches your signboard and visiting card, room to growSays nothing about what you do until people know you
Brand plus a wordkaveriphysio.inAdds context while staying short; useful when the plain brand is takenCan box you in if you add other services later
Keyword or locationbestphysiotherapypune.inDescribes the service at a glanceLong, generic, hard to trust, and tied to one city and one service

Years ago, keyword-stuffed domains could give a ranking boost. Google has long since reduced that effect, and today the words in your domain carry little weight compared with your content, your Google Business Profile and links from other sites. A keyword domain can also look like a generic lead-generation site rather than a real business. If customers already know your business name, use it.

10 checks for a good domain name

  1. Short. Fewer characters mean fewer typing mistakes and a cleaner look on signboards, vehicles and visiting cards.
  2. The phone test. Say it to someone over the phone. If you have to spell it out or explain it, keep looking.
  3. No hyphens or numbers. "Is that the number 4 or the word?" and "with or without the dash?" both lose visitors.
  4. No awkward letter runs. Read it as one word. Joined-up words can accidentally spell something else, and double letters, such as where one word ends and the next begins with the same letter, get mistyped.
  5. Matches your business name, so people can guess it and find you after seeing your shop, van or WhatsApp message.
  6. Works as an email address. Write out name@domain and read it aloud. Customers will type it more often than the website address.
  7. Room to grow. Avoid tying it to one product or one area if you plan to expand.
  8. Easy in your customers' languages. Transliterated words should have one obvious English spelling, or you'll need the variants too.
  9. The right extension for your audience: usually .in or .com for Indian businesses.
  10. Available everywhere that matters, including the main social platforms, so your name is consistent.

.in, .com or something else?

For a business whose customers are mostly in India, .in is a natural choice and often still available when the .com isn't. For exporters, software and businesses selling abroad, .com is the safer default because it carries no country signal. If both are free, many businesses register both, use one and redirect the other.

Newer extensions such as .co, .shop or .online can work for a strong brand, but people instinctively type .com or .in, and some will send email to the wrong address. Avoid twisting your name into a hyphenated or misspelt .com just to get the extension; a clean .in is better than an awkward .com.

Check trademarks and existing businesses

A name that's free as a domain can still belong to someone else as a brand. Before you print anything:

  • Search the trademark registry. At the time of writing, India's trademark registry offers a free public search on the IP India website. Look for the same or similar names in your line of business.
  • Search Google and Google Maps for the name, especially in your city and industry.
  • Check company and LLP names if you plan to register one, so the domain and the legal name can match.
  • Check social handles on the platforms your customers use.

If an established business in your field already uses the name, choose another. Trademark disputes can cost you the domain and the brand you've built on it. For anything unclear, or if you want to register your own trademark, ask a trademark lawyer; this guide can't tell you what's legally safe.

Check the domain's history

A domain someone else has used before can carry baggage. Look up the name on the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine to see what was on it, and search Google for the domain in quotes. A past life as a spam site, a casino or a hacked pharmacy shop can mean bad links pointing at it and a shaky start with Google. This matters most when buying a name that's for sale, or one that expired and was released.

Buying the domain

  • Use an accredited registrar, or a well-known host that registers domains, and buy in an account that belongs to your business, with an email address you control.
  • Register it in the business's name, not your developer's, a marketing agency's or an employee's. The website ownership checklist covers the other accounts you should control.
  • Compare renewal prices. A standard .in or .com usually costs roughly ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year, but first-year discounts and "free domain with hosting" offers often renew higher. Check the second-year price and whether GST is included.
  • Premium names listed by the registry or a seller can cost far more, and some renew at premium prices every year.
  • Privacy: many registrars hide personal contact details from public lookups, but the rules differ between extensions, so ask yours what's shown.
  • Security: turn on two-factor login and keep the registrar lock on, which blocks unauthorised transfers.

If the name you want is registered but unused, you can approach the owner or use a marketplace with escrow, but only pay what the name is genuinely worth to you. A slightly different available name is often the better deal.

Renewing and protecting it

A lapsed domain takes your website and email down at the same time, and in the worst case someone else registers it. To avoid that:

  • Switch on auto-renew, and keep the saved card or payment method current.
  • Consider registering for several years at once.
  • Make sure renewal reminders go to an inbox someone reads, not a former employee's address.
  • Keep a note of the registrar, the login owner and the renewal date alongside your hosting and email renewals.

If a renewal is ever missed, act quickly; what to do when a domain expires explains the grace periods. Expect official-looking letters and emails offering renewals, "SEO registration" or rival versions of your name in other countries; most are sales tactics or scams, as domain and SEO scam emails explains.

Should you buy extra domains?

A few extra registrations can protect your brand and catch typing mistakes: the other main extension, a common misspelling, or the version with and without a word such as "the". Point them all to your main site with permanent redirects. Don't build separate copies of your site on them, and don't buy dozens of variations you'll forget to renew.

Common domain name mistakes

  • Registering the domain in a developer's or agency's account
  • Choosing a long keyword string instead of the business name
  • Hyphens, numbers or unusual spellings that need explaining
  • Skipping the trademark and existing-business checks
  • Picking a name tied to one city or service when you plan to expand
  • Looking only at the first-year price
  • Letting the renewal email go to an address nobody checks

FAQs

How long should a business domain name be?

As short as your business name allows while staying clear. There's no fixed limit that matters in practice, but a name you can say once over the phone without spelling it out is a good test.

Does a keyword in the domain help SEO?

Very little today. Google relies on your content, links and business details far more than the words in your domain. A keyword can help people understand what you do, but it isn't worth a long or awkward name.

Should I buy both .in and .com?

If both are available at a normal price, it's sensible for many businesses: use one as your main address and redirect the other. It also stops someone else using the version people may type by mistake.

Can I change my domain name later?

Yes, but it takes care: every old URL needs a permanent redirect to its new equivalent, and you'll update email, print and profiles. Changing your domain without losing SEO explains the steps. It's far easier to choose well at the start.

What if the domain I want is already taken?

Try the other main extension, add a short word that fits your business, or ask whether the owner will sell. Avoid hyphens and misspellings, and avoid names close to an active business in your field.
