Yoast SEO vs Rank Math vs All in One SEO: Which WordPress SEO Plugin Should You Use?

Key takeaways
  • Yoast SEO, Rank Math and All in One SEO all handle the basics well: titles, meta descriptions, XML sitemaps, canonical tags and basic schema.
  • Pick one plugin, configure it properly and keep it; running two SEO plugins together causes duplicate tags and confusing settings.
  • The plugin is a tool, not a ranking factor: content, internal links, speed and real reviews matter far more than which plugin you install.

Short answer: for most Indian small business and WooCommerce sites, Yoast SEO, Rank Math and All in One SEO (AIOSEO) will all do the job. Rank Math gives the most features in its free version, Yoast is the most familiar and conservative, and AIOSEO has a friendly setup wizard. The choice matters far less than configuring one plugin properly and never running two at once.

This guide compares the three in plain terms, explains which settings actually matter, and shows how to switch from one to another without losing your titles and descriptions.

What a WordPress SEO plugin actually does

WordPress on its own does not give you a clean way to set an SEO title and meta description for each page, generate a proper XML sitemap, or control which pages Google should index. An SEO plugin fills those gaps. The core jobs are:

  • Titles and meta descriptions: a field on every page and post, plus templates for categories, tags and products.
  • XML sitemaps: an automatically updated list of URLs you can submit in Search Console.
  • Indexing controls: noindex for thin pages such as tag archives, author pages or thank-you pages.
  • Canonical tags: telling Google which version of a URL is the main one.
  • Schema markup: structured data for your organisation, articles, products, FAQs and local business details.
  • Social previews: the image and text shown when a page is shared on WhatsApp, Facebook or LinkedIn.

Anything beyond that, such as keyword scores, content analysis and redirect managers, is helpful but optional. None of it replaces writing pages people actually want to read.

When Yoast SEO makes sense

Yoast has been around the longest and many developers and content writers already know its interface. It is a sensible choice when:

  • Your team is used to it and you do not want to retrain anyone.
  • You prefer fewer settings and sensible defaults over lots of toggles.
  • The site is mainly a services site with pages and a blog rather than a large store.

The free version covers titles, descriptions, sitemaps and a solid schema graph. If you need a redirect manager, you can use a separate free redirection plugin instead of upgrading.

When Rank Math makes sense

Rank Math packs a lot into its free version: redirects, more schema types, a 404 monitor and basic local business settings. That makes it popular with small businesses that want one plugin to handle most things. It suits:

  • Local businesses that want local schema without paying for an add-on.
  • WooCommerce stores that want product schema options without extra plugins.
  • Owners comfortable switching off modules they do not need.

The risk with Rank Math is turning on every module. Each one adds settings, and some, such as the 404 monitor, can store a lot of data over time. Enable only what you will actually use.

When All in One SEO makes sense

AIOSEO has a clean wizard and a tidy dashboard, which suits owners who want to set things up once and leave it. It is a reasonable choice when someone non-technical will manage the site and you want clear guidance on each screen. Several of its more advanced features sit in paid plans, so compare what you need against the free version before committing.

A practical way to decide between the three: list the five or six things your site genuinely needs, such as titles, sitemaps, local schema, product schema and redirects, then check which plugin covers them in its free version. If two plugins tie, choose the one your developer or content writer already knows. Familiarity means fewer mistakes, and it is easier to hand the site over later if someone else takes on the maintenance. For an agency handing sites to clients, using the same plugin across every project also keeps training and support simple.

Which is the best SEO plugin for WooCommerce stores in India?

For an Indian WooCommerce store, the plugin matters less than getting product and category pages right. Whichever you choose, make sure it handles:

  • Product schema with price, availability and reviews, so Google can show rich results where eligible.
  • Noindex for filtered and sorted URLs that create near-duplicate pages.
  • Clean category titles and descriptions, written for how Indian buyers search, such as fabric, size or city delivery.
  • Sitemaps that include products and categories but skip cart, checkout and account pages.

The WooCommerce SEO guide covers product pages in detail. If you are on Shopify instead, the platform handles sitemaps and canonicals itself, and the Shopify SEO checklist is the better starting point.

Settings that matter more than the plugin choice

Most SEO plugin problems I see on Indian business sites come from setup, not from the plugin itself:

  1. Empty or duplicate meta titles: every important page needs a unique title with the service and city, for example a service name plus Pune or Ahmedabad.
  2. Thin archive pages indexed: tag, author and date archives often add nothing and should usually be set to noindex.
  3. Sitemap not submitted: submit the sitemap URL in Search Console and check it is read without errors. See XML sitemaps explained.
  4. Wrong organisation details: business name, logo and contact details in the schema settings should match your Google Business Profile.
  5. Search engine visibility box ticked: the WordPress setting that discourages search engines is sometimes left on after launch. The guide on a WordPress site not showing on Google covers this.

How to switch SEO plugins without losing data

All three plugins include import tools that copy titles, descriptions and some settings from the others. A safe switch looks like this:

  1. Take a full backup of files and database.
  2. Note your current titles and descriptions for the most important pages, or export them.
  3. Install the new plugin and run its import tool from the old one.
  4. Check a sample of pages: titles, descriptions, canonical tags, noindex settings and schema.
  5. Deactivate the old plugin only after confirming the import worked, then delete it.
  6. Resubmit the new sitemap URL in Search Console if it changed.

Never leave two SEO plugins active together. They both output titles, canonicals and schema, which produces duplicate tags and makes it unclear which settings are live.

Common mistakes with WordPress SEO plugins

  • Chasing a green score instead of writing for the reader; the score is a checklist, not a ranking signal.
  • Stuffing the focus keyword into every heading to satisfy the analysis.
  • Adding FAQ or review schema for content that is not visible on the page.
  • Installing extra SEO add-ons that duplicate what the main plugin already does, which can slow the site.
  • Never opening Search Console to see what Google actually reports.

FAQs

Yoast vs Rank Math vs AIOSEO: which is best?

There is no single winner. Rank Math offers the most in its free version, Yoast is simple and widely known, and AIOSEO has a friendly setup. For most small business sites, any of them works well when configured properly.

Does an SEO plugin improve my rankings by itself?

No. It gives you the controls for titles, sitemaps, indexing and schema. Rankings come from useful content, sensible site structure, internal links, speed and trust signals such as reviews.

Can I use two SEO plugins together?

No. Two SEO plugins output duplicate titles, canonical tags and schema. Choose one, import your data into it, and remove the other.

Will switching from Yoast to Rank Math hurt my SEO?

Not if you use the import tool, check important pages afterwards and keep your titles, descriptions and noindex settings the same. Take a backup before you start.

Do I need the paid version of an SEO plugin?

Usually not for a small business site. The free versions cover the essentials. Paid versions add conveniences such as redirect managers, extra schema types or local features, which you can often handle in other ways.
