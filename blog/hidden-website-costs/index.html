Hidden Website Costs: Domain and Hosting Renewals, Plugin Licences, Email, SSL, Maintenance, Content, Ads and Payment Gateway Fees You Pay After the Build

The website costs that arrive after launch: renewal prices, premium licences, business email, SSL, maintenance, content, ads and payment gateway fees.

Key takeaways
  • Compare running costs over two or three years, not the first year, because introductory hosting prices and bundled free domains usually end after the first term.
  • Ask for a written list of every paid licence, subscription and account, with renewal dates and whose name each one is in.
  • Budget for upkeep and for getting visitors, since a site that is never maintained or promoted tends to cost more later than it saved at the start.

The build quote is the cost you see. The costs you do not see are the ones that renew: the domain, hosting at its full rather than introductory price, premium theme and plugin licences, business email per mailbox, maintenance, content updates, the ads or SEO that bring visitors, and, if you sell online, payment gateway fees on every order. None of these is unusual. They only become hidden when nobody lists them before the site goes live.

This guide lists them so you can budget properly and compare quotes fairly. Where I give rupee figures, they are the same rough ranges I use across this site; prices change, so treat them as a guide and check current rates with each provider. If you would rather not track renewals and updates yourself, that is part of what a WordPress maintenance plan covers.

Why the first-year bill is misleading

Two things make year one look cheaper than every year after it.

  • Introductory hosting prices. Many hosts advertise a low price that applies only to the first term. The renewal is charged at the regular rate, which can be much higher. Why hosting renewals cost more explains how this works and what to do about it.
  • Bundled freebies. A domain thrown in with a hosting plan normally costs nothing in year one only. Free mailboxes may come with small storage limits. Theme licences bought by a developer may cover one year of updates.

The fix is simple: whenever you compare offers, add up the cost over two or three years, using the renewal prices.

Licences you may not know you have

WordPress itself is free, and plenty of sites run well on free plugins. But a typical business build might quietly include a premium theme, a page builder's Pro version, a forms plugin, a backup tool, a caching or image plugin, and on a store, one or two WooCommerce extensions. Each may have its own yearly renewal.

When a licence lapses, the software usually keeps working but stops getting updates and support. That is not an immediate emergency, but over months it becomes a security risk and, eventually, a compatibility problem after a WordPress update. Ask your developer for a list like this at handover:

And never accept "nulled" copies of premium plugins. They are pirated, they cannot be updated, and some carry malware.

Email: the cost that grows with your team

An address at your own domain is not always included in a website quote. Some hosting plans include basic mailboxes; dedicated services charge per user, so the bill rises every time you add a person. Storage limits, shared inboxes and whether you need calendars and document tools all affect which option makes sense. Business email options compares the common choices.

SSL: usually not a cost any more

The padlock and https come from an SSL certificate. Most good hosts now include a free one that renews automatically, which is enough for nearly every business site. Be cautious about paid SSL add-ons offered at checkout unless you have a specific reason. The real hidden cost here is an expired certificate that nobody noticed, which puts a full-page browser warning in front of every visitor.

Keeping the site running

Updates, off-site backups, security checks, uptime monitoring and checking that forms still deliver are ongoing work. You can do it yourself, pay for individual tools, or use a plan. What is never free is skipping it: cleaning up a hacked site or rebuilding from scratch after data loss usually costs much more than steady care. Website maintenance costs in India breaks down what plans typically include.

Content edits are a separate line to check. A price change, a new staff photo or a Diwali offer banner seems trivial, but if each one is billed separately they add up. Ask whether small edits are included in any maintenance arrangement.

Content you will keep paying for

  • Photography: new products, a new office, new staff
  • Stock images and fonts: some are free; others need a paid licence, priced by use
  • Writing: new service pages, product descriptions and articles, whether you pay a writer or spend your own hours
  • Translations: if you run Hindi or regional language pages, each new page needs translating and checking

Getting visitors

A website does not bring visitors on its own. Budget for at least one way to reach people:

  • Google or Meta ads: the ad budget itself, plus management fees if someone runs them for you
  • SEO: ongoing work on content, technical health and your Google Business Profile
  • Tools: email marketing, booking, chat or CRM subscriptions

Ad spend and the fee for managing ads are different things. When comparing marketing quotes, check which one a number refers to.

If you take payments online

Selling or taking bookings adds costs that grow with your sales:

  • Transaction fees: payment gateways usually charge a percentage of each payment, with rates that can differ for UPI, cards, international cards and wallets. Check the current pricing page of each gateway; payment gateways in India compared covers the differences.
  • Tax on fees: GST is generally charged on the gateway's fee, so the deduction is a little more than the headline rate. Your CA can confirm how to account for it.
  • Refunds: on many gateways, the original fee is not returned when you refund a customer, so check your gateway's terms
  • Store extras: shipping integrations, invoice plugins and paid extensions

Costs that only appear when something goes wrong

These are the truly hidden ones, because you only meet them in a crisis:

  • Recovering a domain that expired, which can cost more than a normal renewal and is not always possible
  • Cleaning up after a hack, plus lost enquiries while the site was down
  • Rebuilding access when the previous developer held the hosting, domain or licence accounts; see regaining access from an old developer
  • Emergency fixes after an untested update broke the site

Most of these come down to the same causes: renewals nobody tracked, accounts in the wrong name, and no backups. The domain and hosting renewal checklist prevents the first.

Questions to ask before you accept a website quote

  • Which costs are one-time and which renew, and at what renewal price?
  • Which premium themes and plugins will the site use, and who buys the licences?
  • Are domain, hosting and email included, and for how long?
  • What does support cost after the first month, and are small edits included?
  • Will every account be in my business's name, with logins handed over at launch?
  • If I sell online, which gateway and extensions will be used, and what do they charge?

Selling online adds its own costs, such as payment gateway fees, shipping and paid extensions; see e-commerce website cost in India for what drives the price of a store.

FAQs

What is the yearly cost of running a small business website in India?

For a typical WordPress site, the fixed part is hosting at around ₹2,500 to ₹15,000 a year and the domain at around ₹500 to ₹1,200 a year. Premium licences, if the site uses any, are added on top. Maintenance, email, content and marketing come on top and depend on your choices.

Why did my hosting bill jump at renewal?

Most likely because the first term was billed at an introductory price and the renewal is at the regular rate. Check the renewal price before buying, and compare plans over several years.

Do I need to keep paying for premium plugins?

If the site depends on them, yes, to keep receiving updates and security fixes. Sometimes a paid plugin can be replaced with a well-maintained free one, which is worth reviewing once a year.

Is a paid SSL certificate necessary?

For most business websites, no. The free certificate most hosts include gives the same padlock. Just make sure it renews automatically.

How can I avoid surprise website costs?

Ask for a written list of every recurring cost and account at handover, keep it with renewal dates in a calendar, and make sure every account is in your business's name.
