Hidden Website Costs: What You'll Pay After Your Website Is Built

The website costs people forget: domain and hosting renewals, plugin and theme licences, business email, fonts and images, backups, maintenance, ads and SEO.

Key takeaways
  • A website keeps costing money after launch: domain and hosting renewals, premium licences, business email and maintenance all recur.
  • Check renewal prices, keep licences in your business's name, and remember that SSL is often included free by good hosts.
  • List every running cost with how often it is billed, and budget for at least one way of bringing visitors, such as SEO or ads.

The quote for building your website is only part of what it costs. Once it's live, a website has running costs, and some are easy to miss until a renewal email or an expired licence catches you out. Here are the costs business owners most often forget, what drives each one and how to plan for them. Every site is different, so treat this as a checklist rather than a price list.

Domain and hosting renewals

Your domain name and hosting renew every year, or every few years if you pay for a longer term. Two things regularly surprise people:

  • Renewal prices: many hosts advertise a low introductory price that applies only to the first term, with renewals at the regular rate
  • "Free" domains: a domain bundled with hosting is usually free for the first year only

Check renewal prices before you buy and compare the total over several years, not just the first. See why hosting renewals cost more. As your site and traffic grow, you may also need a bigger hosting plan.

Premium plugin and theme licences

WordPress itself is free, and many sites run well on free plugins. But premium themes, page builder Pro versions, form and backup plugins and WooCommerce extensions are often sold as yearly licences. When a licence lapses, the software usually keeps working but stops receiving updates and support, which becomes a security risk over time.

  • Ask your developer for a list of every paid licence, its renewal date and who it's registered to
  • Make sure licences are bought in your business's name, not the developer's
  • Avoid "nulled" copies of premium plugins and themes; they can contain hidden malware

Business email

An address like info@yourbusiness.in looks far more professional than a free Gmail address, but it isn't always part of the website quote. Some hosting plans include basic mailboxes, while dedicated services such as Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 or Zoho Mail usually charge per mailbox, so the cost grows with your team. Check current plans and storage limits before you choose; see business email options.

Fonts, images and other media

  • Fonts: Google Fonts are free to use, but some premium fonts need a web licence, priced according to how they're used
  • Stock photos and illustrations: images found through a Google search aren't free to use; licensed stock is usually paid per image or by subscription
  • Photography and video: professional photos of your team, premises or products cost money but usually build more trust than stock
  • Icons and graphics: some libraries are free, while others need a paid licence for commercial use

Keep a record of what was licensed and under whose account, so you can show you're allowed to use it.

SSL certificates: often free now

SSL gives your site the padlock and "https" in the browser. Most good hosts now include a free certificate that renews automatically, which is enough for most business websites. Think twice before paying for an SSL upgrade at checkout unless you have a specific reason, and check that the free certificate really does renew, because an expired one triggers browser security warnings.

Backups, security and maintenance

A WordPress site needs regular updates, off-site backups, security monitoring and checks that forms still deliver enquiries. You can do some of this yourself, pay for individual tools, or use a maintenance plan. Skipping it is the most expensive option in the long run, because cleaning up a hacked site or rebuilding after data loss usually costs far more than prevention.

Content updates add up too: new services, price changes, staff photos, festival offers. Check whether your plan includes small edits or charges for each one. See website maintenance costs in India for what plans usually cover.

Getting visitors: ads, SEO and content

A website doesn't bring customers on its own. Most businesses spend something on getting found:

  • Google or Meta ads: the ad budget itself, plus management time if someone runs the campaigns for you
  • SEO: ongoing work on content, technical health and your Google Business Profile
  • Content: service pages, articles and product descriptions, whether you pay a writer or spend your own time
  • Tools: email marketing, booking, live chat or CRM subscriptions connected to your site

None of these is compulsory, but if your website exists to bring in enquiries, budget for at least one way of driving visitors to it.

How to budget for the full cost

Make a simple list of every running cost, how often it's billed and who pays it:

CostUsually billedWatch for
DomainYearly or multi-yearRenewal price and auto-renew settings
HostingYearly or multi-yearIntroductory vs renewal price
Business emailMonthly or yearly, per mailboxCost growing with your team
Plugin and theme licencesUsually yearlyLicences in someone else's name
MaintenanceMonthly or yearlyWhat's included, especially content edits
MarketingMonthlyAd spend vs management fees
