Using AI Tools to Write Website Content Responsibly: What Google Says, Fact-Checking, Real Experience and a Safe Workflow

You can use AI tools to help write your business website, as long as a person who knows the business checks every fact, adds real experience and takes responsibility for what is published. Google's guidance, at the time of writing, is that it rewards helpful content however it is produced, while using automation to churn out pages mainly to rank breaks its spam policies. Unedited AI text tends to be generic and sometimes wrong, and customers notice both.

What Google says about AI-generated content

Google's position has been fairly consistent since it published guidance on AI content in early 2023. At the time of writing, the main points are:

  • Method doesn't matter, quality does. Content is judged on whether it is helpful, reliable and written for people, not on whether a person or a tool typed it.
  • Scaled content abuse is spam. Producing many pages mainly to manipulate rankings, with little value for readers, breaks Google's spam policies whether it is done with AI, templates or cheap writers.
  • Low effort is noticed. Google's guidelines for its search quality raters describe pages made mostly of copied, paraphrased or automatically generated text with little originality or added value as the lowest quality.
  • Who, how and why. Google suggests being clear about who created content and, where readers would reasonably expect it, how automation was used, and making sure the reason for the page is to help people rather than to rank.

Google changes its guidance from time to time, so check its Search Central documentation for the current wording. The practical takeaway hasn't changed: AI is a tool, and the page still has to deserve to rank.

Where AI goes wrong on business websites

  • Confident errors. AI tools can state wrong prices, GST rates, regulations, dosages, distances or dates in perfectly fluent sentences.
  • Invented statistics and sources. "Studies show" with no real study behind it, or a quote from a report that doesn't exist.
  • Sameness. Text that could sit on any competitor's site, full of phrases like "in today's fast-paced digital world" that tell the reader nothing.
  • Wrong local details. Areas you don't serve, landmarks in the wrong place, or rules from another country presented as Indian ones.
  • Made-up experience. Case studies, client results and testimonials that never happened. These are never acceptable, however they are produced.
  • Regulated topics. Health, legal, financial and tax content written without a qualified person checking it, in fields where professional bodies have their own rules on what can be said.

Add what only you know

The difference between a page that ranks and one that doesn't is often the part an AI tool can't write: first-hand experience. Before publishing, add at least some of these:

  • Real examples from your work, described accurately, with the client's permission where they could be identified
  • Your own photos of your premises, team, products and finished work
  • The questions customers actually ask and the mistakes you see them make before they call you
  • Your process, step by step, including what can go wrong and what you do about it
  • Local detail: the areas you serve, seasonal patterns in your city, how things work in your trade in India
  • Your opinion, clearly marked as such, on what you would and wouldn't recommend
  • A real author, with a name, role and relevant experience

Case studies are one of the strongest ways to show experience; how to write case studies for your website shows how to structure them honestly, and how to write website content covers homepage, about and service pages.

A fact-checking routine

  1. Highlight every fact in the draft: numbers, prices, names, dates, laws, technical claims and comparisons.
  2. Check each one against a primary source: an official website, the regulation itself, a manufacturer's documentation or your own records.
  3. Delete anything you can't verify. A shorter, accurate page beats a longer one with a guess in it.
  4. Replace vague claims ("the best in Pune", "trusted by thousands") with specifics you can stand behind, or remove them.
  5. Hedge facts that change, such as rules, platform policies and fees, with "at the time of writing" and a date you review the page.
  6. Have a qualified person review anything medical, legal, financial or tax-related before it goes live.

AI images and visuals

AI-generated images are fine for abstract illustrations, such as a blog header showing the idea of website speed. They are not fine for anything a customer would take as real: your team, your premises, your products, finished projects, before-and-after results or food you serve. A generated photo of a "happy client" or a kitchen that isn't yours misleads people, and they tend to notice. Some platforms also now expect AI-generated images to be labelled, so check the rules anywhere you list products. Real photos, even simple phone photos taken in good light, build more trust; stock photos vs real photos explains why.

Should you disclose AI use?

There is no rule that every page must carry a label, but honesty with readers matters. A sensible approach for a small business:

  • Put a real person's name on articles, someone who reviewed and stands behind the content, never an invented author.
  • Where readers would reasonably expect to know, such as a guide presented as personal advice, a short line like "Drafted with the help of AI tools and reviewed by [name]" is honest and costs you nothing.
  • Never use AI to write reviews, testimonials or star ratings. Fake reviews break Google's review policies, and consumer protection rules in India and elsewhere increasingly treat them as misleading.

Keep customer data out of AI tools

Don't paste customer names, phone numbers, health details, contracts or anything confidential into an AI tool unless you know how that tool stores and uses what you enter. Many tools have settings about whether your inputs are used for training; check them, and use business accounts where your team relies on these tools. If your website collects personal data, India's data protection law also applies to how you handle it; see the DPDP Act and your website.

Responsible AI use on a business website: a workflow

  1. Start with a real question from customers or Search Console, and one page per question.
  2. Write or record your own notes: what you'd tell a customer face to face.
  3. Use AI for an outline or a draft built from those notes, not from a one-line prompt.
  4. Add experience: examples, photos, process and local detail.
  5. Fact-check using the routine above.
  6. Edit for your voice: cut filler, clichés and anything you wouldn't say out loud; common copywriting mistakes lists what to look for.
  7. Get a qualified review for regulated topics.
  8. Publish under a real name, with a sensible title, description and internal links.
  9. Review after a few weeks in Search Console and improve the page; updating old blog posts shows how.

What not to do: generate a page for every locality or keyword variation, rewrite competitors' articles, or set up anything that publishes AI text automatically without a person reading it. These are exactly the patterns spam policies are written for.

FAQs

Does Google penalise AI-generated content?

Not for being AI-generated, according to Google's guidance at the time of writing. It acts against unhelpful content and against pages produced at scale mainly to rank, whatever tool made them. Edited, accurate, genuinely useful pages are fine.

Can I use AI to write my service pages?

You can use it for a first draft, but service pages need your real process, areas, pricing approach, examples and photos. Those details are what persuade customers to enquire, and a generic draft won't contain them.

Do I have to say that I used AI?

There is no blanket requirement, but be honest where readers would expect to know, and always have a real, named person review and stand behind the content.

Can AI translate my website into Hindi or Marathi?

It can produce a starting draft quickly, but have a fluent speaker review it before publishing, especially headings, calls to action and technical terms.

Will AI-written content help me appear in AI search answers?

Not because it is AI-written. AI assistants tend to draw on clear, specific, trustworthy pages, which is the same standard people use; see AI search and your website.
