Websites for Lift and Elevator Companies: Sales, AMC and Breakdown Support

What lift and elevator companies need on their website: product range, home vs commercial lifts, AMC plans, safety and compliance details and breakdown support.

Sameer Gupta 1 October 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways
  • Organise products by use and give homeowners and commercial buyers separate paths with the details each needs.
  • Explain comprehensive and non-comprehensive AMCs side by side, and describe safety and compliance only in terms you can back up.
  • Keep a breakdown number or button visible on every page, separate from sales, with honest support hours.

A lift company's website has two very different jobs. It has to win new installations from builders, architects, hospitals and families adding a home lift, and it has to serve existing customers, including the society secretary calling because a lift has stopped between floors. Many lift websites do neither well: a brochure-style homepage, a generic contact form and a service number buried in the footer. Here's how to structure a site that brings in installation and AMC enquiries while keeping support easy to reach.

Who visits, and what each visitor needs

VisitorWhat they wantWhere to send them

Product pages organised by use

Group your range the way buyers think about it, not by internal model codes. Typical categories include passenger lifts, home lifts, hospital and stretcher lifts, goods lifts, car lifts, dumbwaiters, and escalators or travelators if you supply them. Explain drive types in plain language too: traction, machine-room-less, hydraulic and, for homes, compact or vacuum lifts if you offer them.

Each product page should cover:

  • Capacity (persons or kg), speed range and maximum number of stops
  • Space requirements such as shaft size, pit depth and headroom, noting that final figures follow a site survey
  • Door types, cabin finishes and control options, with photos of real installations
  • Power supply needs and safety features such as automatic rescue devices, overload sensing, door sensors and emergency alarms, listing only what the product actually has
  • Warranty, and what happens when it ends

If you're a dealer or authorised partner for a manufacturer, say so accurately and use their logos only with permission.

Separate paths for homes and commercial buildings

A family adding a lift to a duplex and a builder specifying lifts for a tower need very different conversations, so give them separate sections.

Home lifts

Homeowners worry about whether a lift will fit in an existing house, how much civil work is needed, what happens during a power cut and whether it's safe for elderly parents and children. Answer these in plain English, show retrofit examples and make booking a site survey the main call to action. Many of these buyers are older themselves, so readable text and large buttons matter; see making your website easy for older visitors.

Commercial and project sales

Builders and consultants want specifications, layout drawings and evidence that you deliver on schedule. Offer downloadable brochures and layouts, a project list by building type, and an enquiry form asking for the number of lifts, floors, building type, project stage and timeline. Add a modernisation page too, since replacing controllers, doors or whole lifts in occupied buildings is a distinct service with its own buyers.

Explain your AMC plans clearly

Annual maintenance contracts bring recurring revenue, and many buyers choose an installer partly on service. Lift AMCs are usually offered as comprehensive or non-comprehensive. Generally, a comprehensive AMC includes most spare parts, while a non-comprehensive one covers servicing with parts charged separately, but terms vary, so spell out yours:

QuestionWhat your AMC page should answer
Which parts are covered?Under each plan, which parts are included and which are charged extra
How often are visits?Preventive maintenance frequency and the checks done on each visit
How fast is breakdown response?Response times you can genuinely meet, and whether support runs round the clock
Do you service other brands?Whether you take over lifts installed by other companies
What's excluded?For example, damage from misuse, water entering the pit or electrical faults outside the lift

Finish with a short AMC quote form asking for the number of lifts, make, age, floors served and location.

Safety and compliance, stated carefully

Lifts are safety-critical, and buyers, especially societies and hospitals, want to know you work properly. In India, many states have their own lift rules covering installation permission, registration and periodic inspection, and the details vary from state to state. On your website:

  • Explain which statutory steps you handle for clients in the states you serve, such as licence applications or inspection support, using wording you can back up
  • Name the standards your products are designed to only if you're confident they apply
  • List company licences, registrations and ISO certification only if current, with the correct scope
  • Describe technician training, site safety procedures and the testing you do before handover
  • Avoid absolute claims like "100% safe" or "zero breakdowns"

A short guide for building owners on routine checks and what to do in a breakdown also builds trust. Keep the advice simple: use the alarm or intercom, never try to force the doors open, and call your helpline or emergency services.

Make breakdown support impossible to miss

When a lift stops, nobody wants to hunt through menus. Put a "Report a breakdown" button or service number in the header and keep it visible on mobile. Also:

  • Use separate numbers for sales and service, so breakdown calls don't queue behind sales enquiries
  • State support hours honestly, and claim round-the-clock support only if someone really answers at night
  • Offer a short breakdown form: building name, lift number, the problem, whether anyone is trapped and a contact number
  • Add a WhatsApp option for non-urgent service requests; see WhatsApp on your business website
  • Make sure the service number inside every lift car matches the one on your website

For larger AMC customers, a simple login area with service history and visit reports can be added later.

Local SEO and speed

  • Target searches like "home lift in {city}", "lift AMC in {city}", "elevator company near me" and "lift modernisation in {city}"
  • Keep a Google Business Profile for each branch, with the most accurate category, real installation photos and reviews from societies, builders and homeowners
  • If you serve several cities from one office, build city pages around real local projects rather than copies; see SEO for service-area businesses
  • Lazy-load installation videos and compress photos, so product pages load quickly on mobile
