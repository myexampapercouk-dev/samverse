WordPress Site Not Showing on Google at All? What to Check and How to Fix It

WordPress site not showing on Google? Check search visibility, noindex, robots.txt, Search Console, sitemaps, hacks and new-site timing, in fix order.

Key takeaways
  • Start with the quick checks: the WordPress "Discourage search engines" setting, noindex tags, robots.txt and a site: search on Google.
  • Verify the site in Google Search Console, submit the sitemap and use URL Inspection to see exactly why a page is or is not indexed.
  • A new site can take time to appear, but if established pages vanish, check for hacks, manual actions, migrations and redirects.

If your WordPress site is not showing on Google at all, the cause is usually one of a few settings: the "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" box left ticked after launch, a noindex tag from an SEO plugin, a robots.txt rule that blocks Google, or a site that is simply too new and has not been discovered yet. Check those first, then use Google Search Console to see exactly what Google reports.

This guide walks through the checks in the order I would do them on an Indian business site, from the two-minute fixes to the less common causes such as hacks and migrations.

Step 1: Check whether Google has indexed anything

Search Google for site:yourdomain.com (with your real domain). This shows a rough sample of pages Google has indexed.

  • No results: Google has not indexed the site, or something is blocking it. Continue with the steps below.
  • Some results, but not the pages you expect: specific pages are blocked, thin or not yet discovered.
  • Strange results, such as Japanese or pharmacy spam: the site may be hacked. Jump to step 7.

Also search for your exact business name. If a directory or social profile appears but not your website, Google may not have found or trusted your site yet. Step 6 covers how to help Google connect your name to your site.

Step 2: Check the WordPress search engine visibility setting

In the WordPress dashboard, go to Settings, then Reading. If "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" is ticked, untick it and save. This box is often ticked during development and forgotten at launch. It adds a noindex instruction across the whole site, so nothing else you do will help until it is off.

Step 3: Look for noindex tags

SEO plugins such as Yoast, Rank Math and All in One SEO let you set pages, posts, categories or the whole site to noindex. Check:

  • The plugin's global settings for each content type.
  • The advanced settings on individual important pages.
  • The page source: search for "noindex" in the robots meta tag.

Some page builders, maintenance-mode plugins and coming-soon plugins also add noindex while they are active. Switch off coming-soon mode once the site is live. The WordPress SEO plugins comparison shows where each plugin keeps these settings.

Step 4: Check robots.txt

Open yourdomain.com/robots.txt in a browser. If you see "Disallow: /" under "User-agent: *", the whole site is blocked from crawling. Remove that rule. Also check that important folders such as the uploads folder are not blocked. Remember that robots.txt controls crawling, not indexing; if you want a page out of Google, use noindex instead. The guide to indexed, though blocked by robots.txt explains the difference.

Step 5: Set up Google Search Console

Search Console is the only reliable way to see what Google thinks of your site. If you have not set it up:

  1. Add your site as a Domain property, verified through DNS at your domain registrar, or a URL-prefix property.
  2. Submit your XML sitemap, usually at a sitemap index URL generated by your SEO plugin.
  3. Use URL Inspection on your homepage and key service pages to see whether they are indexed, and if not, why.
  4. Click Request Indexing for important pages once any blocking issue is fixed.
  5. Check the Pages report for the main reasons pages are not indexed.

See setting up Google Analytics and Search Console and Search Console page indexing errors for the details.

Step 6: If the site is new, help Google find it

A brand-new website on a new domain can take time to be discovered and indexed. You can help:

  • Submit the sitemap and request indexing for the homepage and main pages.
  • Link to your website from your Google Business Profile, social profiles and business directories.
  • Get a few genuine links from suppliers, partners or associations.
  • Make sure every important page is linked from the menu or other pages, so none are orphaned.
  • Publish useful, original content rather than a few thin pages.

The guide on getting a website indexed faster covers this in more depth. Indexing is not the same as ranking: once pages are indexed, they still need to earn positions for the searches you care about.

Step 7: Rule out hacks and manual actions

If an established site suddenly disappears, or spammy pages appear in a site: search, check for a hack. Look at the Security Issues and Manual Actions reports in Search Console. Hacked WordPress sites often have thousands of spam pages, cloaked redirects or injected links. Clean the site fully, update everything, change passwords and then request a review. See "This site may be hacked" on Google for the cleanup steps.

Step 8: Check migrations, domain changes and HTTPS

Pages often vanish after a move to new hosting, a switch from HTTP to HTTPS, a change from www to non-www, or a new domain. Check that:

  • Old URLs redirect permanently, with a 301, to the matching new URLs.
  • The Search Console property matches the version of the site you now use.
  • The sitemap lists the new URLs, not the old ones.
  • The staging site is not the one being indexed instead of the live site.

Step 9: If pages are crawled but not indexed

When Search Console shows pages as crawled or discovered but not indexed, Google has seen them but chosen not to index them yet. Common reasons are thin content, near-duplicate pages, weak internal linking or a new site with little trust. Improve the most important pages first: add genuine detail, link to them from related pages, and avoid publishing many similar, thin pages. Slow servers can also limit crawling, so check site speed on cheap shared hosting.

Hosting, firewalls and server errors

Sometimes the WordPress settings are fine but Google still cannot reach the site. Budget shared hosting can return server errors or time out when Googlebot crawls several pages at once. Some security plugins, hosting firewalls and CDN settings block unfamiliar bots too aggressively, and Googlebot gets caught in the net. In Search Console, look at Settings, then Crawl stats, for spikes in server errors or failed requests, and use URL Inspection to run a live test on a key page.

If the live test fails while the page loads fine in your browser, ask your host whether any firewall rules or rate limits are blocking crawlers, and check the security plugin's blocked requests log. Moving to reliable hosting and enabling proper caching often fixes both speed and crawling problems at the same time. Also confirm the domain has not expired and that DNS points to the right server after any hosting change.

Shopify stores not showing on Google

Shopify stores have their own common causes: a store still password-protected after launch, a new store on a plan that keeps the storefront locked, or a theme or app adding noindex to certain pages. Remove the password, check the theme's robots settings and submit the sitemap in Search Console. The Shopify SEO checklist covers the rest.

FAQs

Why is my WordPress site not showing on Google?

The usual causes are the "Discourage search engines" setting, noindex tags from an SEO or coming-soon plugin, a robots.txt block, a site too new to be discovered, a hack, or a migration that broke URLs.

How long does it take for a new WordPress site to show on Google?

It varies. Some sites appear within days of submitting a sitemap and requesting indexing; others take weeks. Links from your Google Business Profile and directories can help Google find you sooner.

Does submitting a sitemap guarantee my site will be indexed?

No. A sitemap helps Google discover pages, but it still decides whether to index each one based on quality, duplication and technical signals.

My site shows on Google but not for my business name. Why?

Google may not yet be confident your site is the official one. Make sure your name appears clearly on the site, link to it from your Google Business Profile and profiles, and keep details consistent.

Can a hack remove my site from Google?

Yes. Google may show warnings, demote affected pages or apply a manual action. Cleaning the hack and requesting a review is the way back.
