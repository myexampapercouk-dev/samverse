How to Make Sure Your Business Ranks for Its Own Name

Website not showing when you search your business name? Fix indexing, titles, Google Business Profile, consistent details and schema to rank for your brand.

Key takeaways
  • If your site does not appear for its own name, check indexing first: Search Console, a noindex tag or a blocked robots.txt are the usual culprits.
  • Put the exact business name in the homepage title, H1, About page and structured data, and use the same name, address and phone everywhere.
  • A verified Google Business Profile, consistent directory listings and genuine mentions from other sites help Google connect your name to your website.

If your website is not showing when you search your business name, first check that Google has indexed it: search site:yourdomain.in, open Google Search Console and look for a noindex tag or blocked robots.txt. Then make the name unmistakable: put it in the homepage title, main heading, About page and Organization structured data, keep the same name, address and phone everywhere, verify your Google Business Profile, and get genuine mentions on other sites that link to you.

This matters more than it seems. People who hear about you from a friend, an ad, a hoarding or a WhatsApp forward will search your name. If they find a directory listing, a competitor or a similarly named company instead, you lose the easiest enquiry there is.

Step 1: check that Google can index your site

  1. Search site:yourdomain.in. If nothing appears, Google has not indexed your pages.
  2. In WordPress, go to Settings, Reading and make sure "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" is unticked. Developers often tick it during a build and forget.
  3. Check your SEO plugin for pages set to noindex, especially the homepage.
  4. Open yourdomain.in/robots.txt and make sure it does not say Disallow: / for everything.
  5. Add the site to Google Search Console, submit your sitemap and use URL Inspection on the homepage to request indexing.

Step 2: make your name clear on your own site

Google needs to be confident that your website is the official home of the business name being searched.

  • Homepage title: start with the exact business name, then what you do and where, such as "Shree Ganesh Interiors: Interior Designers in Nashik".
  • Main heading: include the business name or a clear line with it near the top.
  • About page: the full legal or trading name, when you started, who runs it and where you are. A clear, honest About page also builds trust with visitors.
  • Contact page: full address, phone and email in plain text, not only inside an image.
  • Footer: name, address and phone on every page.
  • Logo alt text: the business name.
  • Site name setting: WordPress Settings, General, Site Title should be your business name, which Google may use as the site name in results.

Step 3: add Organization or LocalBusiness structured data

Structured data tells search engines your official name, logo, address, phone and social profiles in a machine-readable way. Most SEO plugins can add Organization or LocalBusiness markup. Make sure it includes:

  • Name exactly as you use it elsewhere
  • Logo URL
  • Address and phone for local businesses
  • sameAs links to your official profiles: Google Business Profile, LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, IndiaMART or JustDial if you use them

Test it with Google's Rich Results Test. Schema markup explained covers the basics.

Step 4: claim and verify your Google Business Profile

For any business with a location or service area, a verified Google Business Profile is often what appears first for a name search, especially on mobile. Use the same name you use on signboards and invoices; adding extra keywords to the name breaks Google's guidelines and can get the profile suspended. Add your website link, correct category, hours and real photos. The Google Business Profile checklist covers every field.

Step 5: make your details consistent across the web

Name, address and phone (often called NAP) should match exactly across your website, Google Business Profile, directories, social profiles and marketplaces. Small differences confuse search engines and customers.

  • Choose one spelling: "Pvt Ltd" or "Private Limited", "Rd" or "Road", and stick to it.
  • Update old listings after a move or number change.
  • Claim listings on JustDial, IndiaMART, Sulekha or sector directories that matter in your industry.
  • Remove or merge duplicate listings where you can.

Step 6: get genuine mentions and links

When other sites mention your business name and link to your website, Google becomes more confident the two belong together. Practical sources for Indian small businesses:

  • Suppliers, distributors and brands you work with, many of which have dealer or partner pages
  • Local business associations, chambers of commerce and industry bodies
  • Event sponsorships and local news coverage
  • Your own social profiles, with the website in the bio
  • Clients who are happy to mention you, such as an "our architect" or "website by" note

Avoid buying links or mass directory submissions. They do not help and can harm.

Generic, shared or common names

If your business is called something like "Sai Enterprises", "Royal Interiors" or "Star Solutions", hundreds of businesses share it. Ranking nationally for that name alone is unrealistic, but people usually add a city or service anyway. Help them:

  • Use name plus city and service in your homepage title and Google Business Profile category.
  • Make your logo, domain and social handles consistent, so the brand is recognisable.
  • Tell customers to search a distinct phrase, such as "Royal Interiors Kothrud".
  • If you are naming a new business, check how crowded the name is first. How to choose a domain name for your business covers this.

Shopify stores and online brands

D2C brands on Shopify often find that marketplace listings or Instagram outrank their own store for the brand name. The same principles apply: set the store name and homepage title in Shopify's Preferences, add Organization structured data (many themes do this), link all social profiles to the store, and get your brand mentioned by press, bloggers and retailers that link to the store. If you sell on marketplaces as well, make sure your brand page there links back where allowed. Shopify development can cover the set-up.

How long does it take?

For a correctly set up site with no indexing problems and a distinct name, the homepage often appears for its own name within days or a few weeks of indexing. Generic names, brand-new domains and businesses with confusing histories take longer. If nothing changes after a month, recheck Step 1.

Use Search Console to find the reason

Put the question to Search Console rather than guessing. The Performance report shows whether your homepage appears for brand searches at all and at what position, and URL Inspection shows whether the page is indexed and which version Google chose. That evidence tells you whether you have an indexing problem, a naming problem or simply a young site that needs more time.

FAQs

Why is my website not showing when I search my business name?

The most common reasons are that the site is not indexed, the "Discourage search engines" box is still ticked in WordPress, the domain is very new, or the name is shared with other businesses.

How do I get Google to show my website for my business name?

Get indexed through Search Console, put the exact name in your homepage title and About page, add Organization schema, verify Google Business Profile and keep details consistent everywhere.

Should I add keywords to my Google Business Profile name?

No. Use your real business name. Adding keywords breaks the guidelines and risks suspension.

Does a .in or .com domain matter for brand search?

Not much. Either can rank for your name. A .in can signal an India focus; choose based on availability and audience.

Will social media profiles outrank my website?

They can at first. As your site builds signals, it usually takes the top spot, with your profiles appearing below it.
