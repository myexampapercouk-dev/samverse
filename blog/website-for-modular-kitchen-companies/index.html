Websites for Modular Kitchen and Wardrobe Companies: Designs, Materials and Consultations

What a modular kitchen website should include in India: design galleries, materials, honest pricing, consultation forms, WhatsApp and local SEO by city.

Key takeaways

A modular kitchen website should help a buyer do three things: see designs that match their home, understand materials and what drives cost, and book a design consultation without friction. Everything else, from the gallery to local SEO, supports those three jobs.

People buying a modular kitchen or wardrobes are usually fitting out a new flat or renovating a home they plan to live in for years. They compare several companies, browse a lot of photos and want to understand materials before they visit a showroom. This guide covers what to include, page by page, for Indian kitchen and wardrobe companies.

What a modular kitchen website should include

Before deciding on design or colours, list the questions buyers ask on the phone and in the showroom. Your website should answer most of them before the first call. In practice that means:

  • Kitchens and wardrobes that look like what they want, in homes like theirs
  • Clear information on carcass materials, shutter finishes and hardware, not just attractive renders
  • A rough idea of cost, or at least what drives it
  • How the process works, from measurement to installation, and how long it typically takes
  • Warranty and after-sales service, written plainly
  • Proof that you are a real, established business: a showroom, a factory or workshop, and genuine reviews

Organise designs the way buyers think

Most people start with a layout or a style, so give them galleries they can browse that way:

  • Kitchens by layout: straight, L-shaped, U-shaped, parallel, island and peninsula
  • Kitchens by style: contemporary, handleless, classic, and compact kitchens for smaller flats
  • Wardrobes: sliding, hinged, walk-in, with lofts, and storage for children's rooms
  • Other units you make, such as TV units, crockery units, study tables and vanities

Mark clearly which images are real installations and which are 3D renders. Buyers notice, and honest labelling builds trust. For each installed project, a short note on the layout, materials, finish and city helps far more than a photo alone. Explain materials, finishes and hardware

This is where many modular kitchen websites fall short, and where you can stand out. Buyers hear confusing terms in every showroom. A clear materials page answers their questions and positions you as the expert.

TopicWhat to explain
Carcass materialThe board options you use (for example BWP plywood, HDHMR or MDF), where each suits, and what you use in wet areas like the sink unit
Shutter finishesLaminate, acrylic, PU paint, membrane, veneer or glass: look, durability, maintenance and relative cost
CountertopsGranite, quartz and any other options you supply or recommend
HardwareHinges, drawer systems, baskets and corner solutions, and the hardware brands you use
AppliancesWhether you supply and fit a hob, chimney or built-in appliances

Only list materials and brands you actually use, and update the page when your range changes. Comparisons like "acrylic vs laminate shutters" or "plywood vs HDHMR for kitchens" also make good blog topics, because buyers search for exactly these questions while planning. Each guide can end with a link to your consultation page.

Make the design consultation the main call to action

For most modular kitchen companies the goal is not an online sale; it is a consultation that leads to a 3D design and a quote. Make that step obvious:

  • A prominent "Book a design consultation" button on every design page (only call it free if it is)
  • A choice of how to meet: showroom visit, home visit for measurement, or a video call
  • A short form asking for city or area, property type (new flat or renovation), what they need (kitchen, wardrobes or both), expected possession or start date, and a preferred time
  • A WhatsApp button so people can send a floor plan or photos of their space; see adding WhatsApp to your website

Explain your process on its own page: consultation, site measurement, 3D design and revisions, quote, order confirmation, production, installation and handover. Give typical timelines only if you can honestly meet them, and say what can cause delays, such as pending civil or electrical work at the site.

Handle the pricing question honestly

Everyone wants to know what a kitchen will cost. You don't have to publish a full price list, but saying nothing makes people assume the worst. Options that work well:

  • Explain what drives the price: size, layout, carcass material, finish, hardware and accessories, countertop and appliances
  • Offer a few clearly defined packages or "starting from" guides, if your pricing allows it, and state exactly what is included and whether GST is extra
  • Add a simple estimate tool where visitors choose layout, size and finish level to see an indicative range, followed by the consultation form

Whatever you show, make it clear that it is indicative and that the final quote follows measurement and design. See should you show prices on your website for the trade-offs.

Build trust beyond the photos

  • Showroom page: address, map, timings, parking, and photos of the displays so people know the visit is worthwhile
  • Factory or workshop: if you manufacture in-house, show your machinery and production process
  • Warranty: what is covered, for how long, and what isn't
  • After-sales service: how customers can request an adjustment or repair after installation
  • Reviews: real Google reviews and customer photos, shared with permission
  • Trade partners: if you work with builders, architects or interior designers, a separate page and enquiry form for them

Small details matter. A named contact person, a landline or mobile that is actually answered, and a GST number in the footer all signal a business that will still be around when a hinge needs adjusting two years later.

Local SEO for kitchen and wardrobe companies

Buyers search locally: "modular kitchen in Pune", "modular kitchen showroom near me", "sliding wardrobe manufacturer Hyderabad". To show up for these searches:

  1. Keep your Google Business Profile complete, with showroom and installation photos, the right categories and regular updates; see the Google Business Profile checklist
  2. Create separate service pages for modular kitchens, wardrobes and other units, each with its own title and content
  3. If you serve several cities, build location pages around genuine local projects rather than copies with the city name swapped
  4. Publish helpful guides: kitchen layouts for small flats, choosing a shutter finish, planning wardrobe storage
  5. Ask happy customers for reviews that mention the product and area in their own words

Keep a photo-heavy site fast

Galleries, 3D renders and walkthrough videos can make pages slow, especially on mobile data. Resize and compress images before uploading, use modern image formats, lazy-load long galleries, and embed videos from YouTube rather than uploading large files to your own hosting. A fast gallery keeps people browsing long enough to book a consultation. If your current site already feels slow, WordPress speed optimization can usually be done without a full rebuild.

WordPress suits most kitchen and wardrobe companies, because galleries, project pages, forms and blog guides are easy for your own team to update. If you also sell ready-made accessories or hardware online, a small WooCommerce or Shopify shop can sit alongside the main site.

FAQs

What should a modular kitchen website include?

Design galleries by layout and style, real project photos with short notes, a materials and finishes page, your process, warranty details, showroom information, and a simple consultation form with a WhatsApp option.

Should a modular kitchen company show prices online?

Showing what drives cost, or clearly defined "starting from" packages, usually helps. State that figures are indicative and that the final quote follows measurement and design.

Is it fine to use 3D renders in the gallery?

Yes, as long as they are labelled. Mix them with real installation photos so buyers can see that the finished work matches the designs.

How do I rank for "modular kitchen near me"?

Start with a complete Google Business Profile, regular photos and genuine reviews, then add service pages for kitchens and wardrobes and location pages built around real local projects.

WordPress or Shopify for a modular kitchen company?

WordPress usually fits better because the main goal is consultations, not checkout. Shopify or WooCommerce makes sense only if you also sell standard products online.
