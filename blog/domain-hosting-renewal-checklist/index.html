Domain, Hosting, SSL, Email and Licence Renewals: A Checklist That Keeps Your Website Online

A renewal checklist for your domain, hosting, SSL, email and premium licences: auto-renew, card expiry, renewal price rises, a reminder calendar and who pays.

Key takeaways
  • List every renewal the website depends on, from the domain and hosting to email, licences and smaller services, with its date, cost and login.
  • Switch on auto-renew with a payment method that will not expire, send renewal emails to an address the business checks, and set reminders well ahead.
  • Keep every account and bill in the business name, so renewals never depend on a developer or a former employee remembering to pay.

Five kinds of renewal can take a website offline or quietly stop it being maintained: the domain name, the hosting, the SSL certificate, business email, and premium theme and plugin licences. Keep them safe by listing each one with its renewal date, switching on auto-renew with a payment method that won't expire, sending renewal emails to an address the business monitors, and setting reminders a month or two ahead so price rises and card problems surface in time.

When I take on a site for WordPress maintenance, the first health check includes SSL renewal and whether premium licences are active and in your name, because an expired licence is one of the most common reasons updates quietly stop arriving. The renewals themselves are paid to the providers, ideally in your name, and this checklist helps you keep track of them.

Renewals are only part of what a site costs to run; hidden website costs covers the rest, from email to content and marketing.

Why renewals fail

Most lapses aren't a decision to stop paying. They usually come from one of these:

  • An expired or replaced card. The card on file expired, was reissued after a fraud alert, or belonged to someone who has left.
  • Recurring payment rules. Indian banks apply extra authentication to recurring card payments, so an automatic renewal can be declined unless the card or mandate is set up for it. Check with your bank and the provider, and act on any "payment failed" email.
  • Auto-renew switched off, sometimes by default on promotional plans.
  • Renewal emails going to the wrong person: a former employee, a developer, or a personal inbox nobody checks.
  • Accounts in someone else's name. If a developer or reseller holds the domain or hosting, your renewal depends on them remembering and paying; the website ownership checklist shows how to check.
  • Different dates and providers, so nobody has the full picture.

Renewal prices: why the second year costs more

Hosting and domains are often sold at an introductory price for the first term, then renew at the standard rate, which can be much higher. A "free" domain with hosting is usually free for the first year only, and Indian prices are often shown before GST. None of this is hidden, but it's easy to miss when you buy.

A month or two before each renewal, check the price for the same plan, whether a longer term costs less per year, and whether you still need every add-on. If a hosting invoice has already surprised you, hosting renewal price shock sets out your options, including moving before the renewal date.

The renewal checklist

  1. List every paid service the website depends on, with the provider, what it covers, the login email, renewal date, term and amount.
  2. Confirm each account is in the business name, and that you, not only a developer, can log in.
  3. Switch on auto-renew for the domain, hosting and email, and for licences you still use.
  4. Check every payment method's expiry date, and replace any card belonging to someone who has left.
  5. Set billing contacts to an address the business monitors, such as accounts@ on your domain, plus a backup address.
  6. Keep the domain's contact details accurate and its transfer lock on. Registrars can suspend domains whose contact details fail verification.
  7. Consider renewing the domain for several years at once, so one failed payment can't take it offline.
  8. Remove add-ons you don't use, such as spare mailboxes, old domains or licences for sites that no longer exist.
  9. Put every date in a shared calendar with reminders, as below.
  10. Keep the invoices together for your accountant, particularly if you claim GST input credit.

A renewal calendar you can copy

Some developers and agencies bundle hosting, domains and licences into one bill. It can be convenient, but agree in writing that the accounts are in your name, or that they'll be moved to you whenever you ask. Licences bought on a developer's account usually can't be transferred, and when that licence lapses, updates stop for your site too; updating a premium theme safely explains what that means for bundled plugins.

Renewals when you move host or change developer

  • Moving host: don't cancel the old hosting until the site has been live and checked on the new host for a week or two, so you have a fallback. Do check its renewal date, so you aren't charged for another year while you wait, and switch its auto-renew off once the move is confirmed.
  • Changing developer: update the billing contact and payment method on every account they managed, and check that no renewal still goes to their card or inbox.
  • A staff member leaving: move renewal emails, cards and calendar reminders from their name to a role address before their last day.

Watch out for fake renewal notices

Scam emails and letters copy the look of real domain renewal notices, expiry warnings and "SEO for your domain" invoices. Never pay from a link in an email. Log in to your registrar or host directly and check the date there. Domain renewal and SEO scam emails shows how to spot them.

If something has already lapsed

  • Domain: log in to the registrar now. Most offer a grace period after expiry when you can renew at the normal price, then a redemption period that costs more; the timings vary by registrar and extension. Domain name expired? walks through each stage.
  • Hosting: pay the invoice and ask support to restore the account. If files were deleted, restore from your own off-site backup.
  • SSL: a free certificate that failed to renew can usually be reissued from the hosting panel once the cause, often a DNS change, is fixed; SSL certificate errors explained helps with the warnings.
  • Email: renew, then check mail flows both ways; messages sent during the gap may have bounced.
  • Licences: renew, then run the updates you missed, after a backup.

FAQs

Should I turn on auto-renew for my domain?

Yes. It's the simplest protection against losing the domain. Pair it with a payment method that won't expire soon and a renewal email you check, because auto-renew still fails if the payment is declined.

Do SSL certificates need to be renewed manually?

Not usually. Free certificates from most hosts renew automatically every few months, as long as the domain still points to the host. Certificate lifetimes are being shortened across the industry, which makes automatic renewal more important. Paid certificates are renewed with the provider and then installed.

What happens if a premium plugin licence expires?

The plugin usually keeps working, but updates and support stop, which becomes a security risk over time, and some plugins restrict features. Renew the licences you depend on and remove plugins you no longer use.

Is it cheaper to renew hosting for several years at once?

The price per year is often lower on a longer term, but you're committed for longer. Compare the total for the term with the cost of moving to another host, and read the refund policy.

Can my developer pay the renewals for me?

Yes, if they invoice you and the accounts stay in your name. The risk is accounts held only by them: if they stop paying or stop working with you, the site goes with them.
