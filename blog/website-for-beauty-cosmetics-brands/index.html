Websites for Beauty, Skincare and Cosmetics Brands in India

How Indian beauty, skincare and cosmetics brands can build a D2C website: product pages, ingredient info, careful claims, honest reviews and repeat orders.

Key takeaways
  • Beauty shoppers want full ingredient lists, suitability by skin type or shade, and honest swatches before buying from a new brand.
  • Keep claims careful: avoid medical or guaranteed-result claims, and only say "dermatologically tested" or "cruelty-free" when you have the evidence.
  • Bundles, trial sizes, refill reminders and a clear returns policy for opened products help turn first orders into repeat customers.

Beauty shoppers are careful. Before they buy a serum, lipstick or sunscreen from a brand they haven't tried, they want to know what's in it, whether it suits their skin type and tone, and whether people like them were happy with it. A good website answers those questions honestly, and gives you a store where you own the customer relationship instead of renting it from a marketplace.

What beauty shoppers look for

  • Ingredients they understand: the full list, plus the key actives explained in plain language
  • Suitability: skin type, concern, hair type, or a shade match for Indian skin tones
  • Real experiences: reviews, customer photos and honest swatches
  • Safety basics: patch test advice, expiry dates and how to store the product
  • Easy buying: UPI, cards, COD where it makes sense, clear delivery times and a fair return policy

Own store, marketplaces or both?

Most Indian beauty brands also sell on marketplaces, and that's fine. Marketplaces bring volume and discovery; your own site brings better margins, customer relationships (with consent) and room to tell your story and educate. Many brands use their website for launches, bundles, subscriptions and loyal repeat buyers. See own website vs marketplaces for the trade-offs.

On platform: WooCommerce gives you full control over content, SEO and integrations, while Shopify is quicker to start with monthly fees and paid apps. Either can run a good beauty store; the right choice depends on your team and plans. WooCommerce vs Shopify in India compares them in detail.

Essential pages

  1. Home: hero products, what the brand stands for, and shop-by-concern shortcuts
  2. Shop by concern and category: acne, pigmentation, dryness, sun care, hair fall; plus cleansers, serums, lip and eye
  3. Product pages that answer every question (more below)
  4. Ingredient glossary: a short page per key ingredient, what it does and which products contain it
  5. Our story: who founded the brand, where products are made, and your quality checks
  6. Routine guides: morning and night routines, and the order to apply products in
  7. Policies: shipping, returns and refunds, privacy, terms and full contact details
  8. FAQs and contact: including WhatsApp for pre-purchase questions

Product pages that answer every question

  • The full ingredient list, with key ingredients highlighted and explained
  • Who it's for: skin type and concern, and who should avoid it or check with a doctor first
  • How to use it: amount, frequency, where it sits in a routine, and patch test advice
  • Texture, fragrance and finish, shown in close-up photos and a short video
  • For makeup: shade swatches photographed on several skin tones, in natural light
  • Pack size, price, shelf life, and storage advice
  • Reviews that show the reviewer's skin type, where they choose to share it

Write every description yourself instead of copying the carton text; see how to write product descriptions that sell.

Be careful with claims

This is where beauty websites most often go wrong. In India, cosmetics are regulated under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act and the Cosmetics Rules, advertising is covered by consumer protection law, and the Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) publishes guidelines on claims and influencer disclosures. Rules change, so check the current position with a regulatory consultant or lawyer, but some sensible habits apply to every brand:

  • Avoid medical claims: a cosmetic that "cures acne" or "treats hair loss" is making a drug-like claim
  • Don't promise guaranteed, permanent or overnight results
  • Only say "clinically tested", "dermatologically tested", "vegan" or "cruelty-free" if you have the evidence or certification, and can show it if asked
  • Be wary of "chemical-free" and "100% natural"; vague claims like these invite complaints
  • Never retouch before-and-after photos, and make clear that results vary
  • Ask influencers and affiliates to disclose paid partnerships clearly

The details printed on your packs, such as manufacturer, net quantity, price and dates, should generally be visible on the product page too. Your adviser can confirm exactly what your products need.

Reviews and social proof, done honestly

  • Ask for a review a couple of weeks after delivery, once customers have actually used the product
  • Publish critical reviews too, and reply politely; a wall of perfect ratings looks suspicious
  • Let customers add photos, and ask permission before featuring them elsewhere
  • Never buy reviews or write them yourself
  • Show creator content only with permission, and label paid collaborations

Store features that grow repeat orders

  • Bundles and regimens: a cleanser, serum and sunscreen sold together as a routine
  • Minis and trial sizes that lower the risk of trying something new
  • Subscriptions or refill reminders for products people use up regularly
  • Gift sets for festivals, weddings and corporate gifting
  • Payments: UPI and cards through an Indian payment gateway, and COD with sensible limits if your customers expect it
  • A clear returns policy: many brands don't accept opened products for hygiene reasons, so spell out what happens with damaged, leaking or wrong items
  • WhatsApp and email updates for orders, restocks and launches, sent only with consent

SEO and speed

Beauty searches are specific: "niacinamide serum for oily skin", "sunscreen for Indian skin", "matte lipstick for dusky skin". Build concern and category pages that match those needs, and publish helpful guides on ingredients and routines, reviewed by a qualified person where the topic touches on skin conditions. Add product schema so price, availability and ratings can appear in Google results.

Keep pages fast. Beauty stores are image-heavy and most shoppers browse on their phones, so compress product photos and swatches, lazy-load reviews and videos, and resist adding a new plugin or app for every small feature.
