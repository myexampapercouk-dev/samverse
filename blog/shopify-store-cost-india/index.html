What a Shopify Store Costs in India: Plan, Apps, Payment Fees, Theme, Setup and Running Costs Explained

What a Shopify store in India really costs: plan, apps, gateway and Shopify fees, theme, setup and running costs, compared honestly with WooCommerce.

Key takeaways
  • A Shopify budget is made of a plan, apps, payment charges, a possible Shopify fee on outside gateways, and one-off theme, setup and content costs.
  • Apps and per-order fees grow as the store sells more, so work out the total from expected orders rather than headline prices.
  • Shopify bundles hosting and security into the plan, while WooCommerce has no platform fee but needs hosting and upkeep, so neither is cheaper for every store.

A Shopify store in India costs a monthly plan fee, whatever paid apps you add, a payment provider charge on every online order, possibly a Shopify fee on top when you use an outside gateway, and one-off spending on a theme, setup work and content. There is no single price, because your plan, your apps and your order volume move the total much more than the design does. The useful question is which of these costs apply to your store, and which of them grow as you sell more.

This guide goes through each cost driver so you can build a budget before you sign up. I don't quote Shopify's prices because they change and vary by country, so keep Shopify's current pricing page for India open alongside this. Shopify costs at a glance

Every Shopify budget is made of the same handful of parts. Some are paid once, some every month, and some on every order.

CostHow it is chargedWhat pushes it up
Shopify planMonthly or yearlyNeeding more staff accounts, deeper reports or lower fees from a higher tier
AppsMostly monthly; some per orderEvery feature the theme and Shopify do not already cover
Payment provider chargesPer prepaid orderOrder volume, the mix of UPI, cards and international cards
Shopify fee on outside gatewaysPer order, at the time of writingYour plan tier and order value; check the current rules
ThemeFree, or a one-off purchaseChoosing a paid theme for features you may not use
Domain and business emailYearly or monthlyNumber of mailboxes and the email provider you pick
Setup and developmentOne-off, then occasionalCatalogue size, custom sections, migrations, integrations
Photos and product contentOne-off, then with each new productNumber of products and whether you shoot them yourself

The plan, apps and payment charges recur for as long as the store is open. The theme, setup and first round of content are paid once. The last row is not a Shopify cost at all, but for a store sending out many parcels it can outweigh the platform bill, so it belongs in the same budget.

The monthly bill: plan, apps and payment charges

The plan

Shopify sells its plans in tiers. At the time of writing, even the entry-level plans run a complete online store, and higher tiers add staff accounts, more detailed reports and, in some cases, lower fees. Check whether yearly billing is offered at a lower rate than monthly, and whether the plan names and features shown to you match what you read elsewhere, since Shopify adjusts both from time to time.

Most new stores do not need a higher tier on day one. Upgrade when a feature you actually need sits on that tier, or when the fee arithmetic shows the bigger plan has become cheaper at your order volume.

Apps

Apps are where Shopify budgets most often drift. A typical Indian store looks at apps for GST invoices, cash on delivery controls, reviews, extra product filters, WhatsApp order updates, courier bookings and back-in-stock alerts. Pricing comes in three shapes: free with limits, a flat monthly charge, or a charge that rises with the number of orders. The third kind gets dearer exactly as the store succeeds, so read the pricing tiers before installing.

Apps also cost speed, because many load scripts on every page. To audit what each one loads, see why a Shopify store is slow and how to fix it.

Payment provider charges and the Shopify fee

Shopify Payments is, at the time of writing, unavailable to businesses based in India, so stores here connect an outside payment provider. That provider charges a fee per transaction, which usually differs between UPI, domestic cards, net banking, wallets and international cards. Its fees typically attract GST as well; your CA can tell you how to account for that.

Depending on your plan, Shopify may also charge its own transaction fee on orders paid through an outside provider. This is the cost most often missed when comparing plans, because it scales with sales. Check Shopify's current terms for India, and read payment gateways on Shopify in India for how the two fees stack up.

One-off costs: theme, setup and content

The theme

Shopify's own free themes are maintained, quick and perfectly good for a small catalogue. A paid theme from the Theme Store is a one-off purchase, and theme licences are normally for a single store, so check the seller's terms. The hidden cost of a feature-heavy paid theme is not the price; it is the code for features you never switch on.

Setup and development work

Developer time depends on the job rather than on Shopify. The smallest setup is a free theme, a clean product spreadsheet and standard payments and shipping. Work grows with:

  • a large catalogue, many variants, or product data that needs cleaning before import;
  • custom sections or a design that the theme editor cannot produce, covered in Shopify theme customisation;
  • moving from another platform, with customers, orders and redirects, as in moving from WooCommerce to Shopify;
  • connecting accounting software, an ERP, marketplaces or a warehouse;
  • configuring and testing COD controls, invoice apps and courier apps.

Photos and product content

Consistent photos, your own product descriptions, collection introductions and plain-language policy pages take time or money, and they are usually underestimated. They also do more for sales and search visibility than most apps do.

Domain, email and other running costs

  • Domain. A yearly renewal. You can buy one through Shopify, but keeping an existing domain with its current registrar works fine.
  • Business email. At the time of writing, Shopify offers email forwarding rather than full mailboxes, so proper inboxes come from a separate email provider with its own charge.
  • Marketing email. Shopify's own email marketing tool has had a free allowance with charges above it; dedicated email apps price by contacts or sends.
  • Ongoing changes. New sections for a campaign, a seasonal banner, a new app to configure, or a theme update that needs custom code carried across.
  • Shipping and returns. Courier charges, packaging, and the two-way cost of refused cash on delivery parcels.

How the cost structure differs from WooCommerce

Neither platform is cheaper for every store. They spend your money in different places.

Cost areaShopifyWooCommerce
Hosting and securityIncluded in the planPaid separately, with updates and security your job or a maintenance plan's
Platform feeA monthly or yearly planNone for the core plugin; hosting takes its place
Per-order feesGateway charges, plus a possible Shopify fee on outside gatewaysGateway charges only
Extra featuresApps, often billed monthlyPlugins; many free, premium ones usually on yearly licences
UpkeepShopify updates the platform; you look after apps and themeWordPress, plugin and theme updates, backups and monitoring
Custom workLimited in places, such as checkout, by what your plan allowsAlmost anything, at development cost

In short, Shopify's costs are predictable and rise with sales and app count, while WooCommerce has lower platform costs but asks you to pay for hosting and upkeep in time or money. A checklist for budgeting your Shopify store

  1. Estimate monthly orders and average order value for the first year, and the share you expect to be prepaid rather than COD.
  2. Pick a plan for the features you need now, and note the fee on outside gateways at that tier from Shopify's current pricing.
  3. Get the payment provider's current rates for UPI, cards and international cards, and ask about settlement time and GST on fees.
  4. List only the apps you know you need, with each one's pricing model: free tier, flat fee or per order.
  5. Decide on a free or paid theme from a written list of features, not from the demo.
  6. Get a fixed quote for setup with a written scope, so extras are visible before they are billed.
  7. Budget for photos, descriptions and policy pages.
  8. Add the domain, mailboxes and any marketing email tool.
  9. Add courier charges, packaging and an allowance for refused COD parcels.
  10. Put a reminder in the calendar to review apps and the plan every quarter.

FAQs

Is Shopify cheaper than WooCommerce in India?

Not automatically. Shopify bundles hosting and security into the plan but adds apps and possibly its own fee on outside gateways. WooCommerce has no platform fee but needs hosting, upkeep and sometimes paid plugins. Work out both from your expected orders rather than from headline prices.

Do I pay Shopify a fee if I use an Indian payment gateway?

At the time of writing, Shopify may charge a transaction fee on orders paid through a third-party provider, at a rate that depends on your plan. It sits on top of the gateway's own fee. Check Shopify's current pricing for India, as this has changed before.

Can I start on a lower plan and upgrade later?

Yes. Products, orders and customers stay where they are when you change plan. Start with what you need and upgrade when a feature or the fee arithmetic justifies it.

Do I need paid apps to launch?

Usually only a few. A GST invoice app is common for registered sellers, and a courier app saves manual work. Reviews, upsells and loyalty apps can wait until you have orders to judge them by.
