Heatmaps and Session Recordings: See How Visitors Really Use Your Website

How heatmaps and session recordings show where visitors click, scroll and get stuck, which tools to try, privacy basics and how to act on findings.

Key takeaways
  • Heatmaps show patterns across many visitors, while session recordings show individual journeys; use both to find where people get stuck.
  • Start with your highest-value pages, such as the home page, a key service or product page and the enquiry or checkout page, and watch a reasonable sample before changing anything.
  • Mask form fields, mention the tool in your privacy policy and avoid recording sensitive pages, then turn findings into small, tested changes.

Heatmaps and session recordings show how real visitors use your website: where they click, how far they scroll, and where they hesitate or leave. Analytics tells you that people left a page; heatmaps and recordings help you see why. For a business website in India, where most visitors arrive on mobile, they often reveal problems you never notice on a desktop.

This guide explains what each tool shows, which ones to try, how to set them up on WordPress or Shopify, privacy basics, and how to turn what you see into improvements.

What heatmaps and session recordings show

ToolWhat it showsGood for
Click heatmapWhere visitors tap or click, coloured by frequencyFinding ignored buttons and clicks on non-clickable items
Scroll heatmapHow far down the page people scrollSeeing whether key content or CTAs are reached
Move or attention mapWhere the mouse moves on desktopRough sense of what people read
Session recordingA replay of one visitor's journeySpotting confusion, form problems and bugs
Rage and dead click reportsRepeated taps or clicks that do nothingFinding broken or misleading elements

Heatmaps summarise many visits; recordings show individual behaviour. Use heatmaps to spot where a problem might be, then watch recordings of that page to understand it.

Which tools to use

Several tools offer heatmaps and recordings. Microsoft Clarity is free and widely used, and Hotjar is a well-known paid option with a free tier. Other analytics and conversion tools include similar features. Check current plans and limits on each tool's own site, because they change.

For most small and mid-sized business websites, a free tool is enough to start. What matters more is choosing the right pages and actually reviewing the data.

Setting up heatmaps on WordPress or Shopify

Most tools work by adding a small tracking script to every page.

  • WordPress: add the script through a header code plugin, Google Tag Manager, or an official plugin if the tool offers one. Avoid pasting it directly into theme files that are overwritten on updates.
  • Shopify: add the script via the theme code or a supported app, and check whether the tool can track checkout pages under current Shopify rules.
  • Tag Manager: if you already use Google Tag Manager, adding the tool there keeps all tracking in one place.

After installing, check that recordings are being captured and that your own visits are excluded where possible, so they do not muddy the data. Watch for any effect on speed; tracking scripts add some weight, so load them sensibly.

Which pages to watch first

Do not try to analyse the whole site at once. Start with pages that matter most to enquiries or sales:

  1. Home page: do people find the main services or products?
  2. Top service or product pages: do they reach the price, details and enquiry button?
  3. Landing pages for ads: are paid visitors doing what you expect?
  4. Contact, quote or booking form: where do people abandon it?
  5. Cart and checkout for stores: where do buyers drop off?

Let enough visits accumulate before drawing conclusions. A handful of recordings can be misleading.

Common problems heatmaps reveal

  • Clicks on things that are not links, such as images, icons or underlined headings. Visitors expect them to open something.
  • Key buttons below where most people stop scrolling, so the call to action is never seen.
  • Menu items nobody uses, and others that get far more attention than expected.
  • Rage taps on mobile from buttons too small or too close together.
  • Form fields that cause hesitation or drop-off, such as an unclear phone format or a required field people do not want to fill.
  • Pop-ups that get closed immediately or block the content people came for.
  • Layout bugs on certain phones, such as overlapping text or a hidden button.

Reading session recordings without wasting hours

Recordings can eat a lot of time. Filter them to make review useful:

  • Filter by page, such as recordings that visited the contact page.
  • Filter by device, especially mobile.
  • Filter for rage clicks, dead clicks or quick backs.
  • Filter by traffic source, such as Google Ads visitors.
  • Watch at higher speed and skip idle time.

Write down what you see in a simple list: page, problem, how often it appeared, possible fix. Patterns across several visitors matter more than one odd session.

Privacy and consent in India

Recordings capture what visitors do on your site, so handle them responsibly:

  • Mask form fields and personal data. Most tools mask inputs by default; confirm this is on.
  • Do not record pages showing account details, medical information or payment data.
  • Mention the tool in your privacy policy and cookie notice.
  • India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act and its rules affect how businesses handle personal data. Check the current requirements, and ask a lawyer if you handle sensitive data. If you have visitors from Europe, other consent rules may apply.

Turning findings into improvements

Seeing problems is only useful if you act on them. A simple process:

  1. List problems with the page and how often you saw them.
  2. Pick the ones closest to enquiries or sales.
  3. Make one clear change at a time, such as moving the enquiry button higher or shortening a form.
  4. Track the result in Google Analytics, using events for form submissions, calls and WhatsApp clicks.
  5. Check heatmaps again after a few weeks.

The guide to conversion rate optimisation basics explains how to test changes, and GA4 events explained covers tracking the actions that matter.

How often to review

A monthly review is enough for most business websites. Check heatmaps for your key pages, watch a filtered set of recordings, and note any new problems. Also review after any redesign, new landing page or ad campaign, because changes often introduce issues you did not expect. Keep notes in one shared document so the next review starts where the last one ended.

Heatmaps vs analytics: use both

Google Analytics answers how many people visited, from where, and what they did in numbers. Heatmaps and recordings answer how they behaved on a page. Together they give a fuller picture. For example, analytics might show that a service page has many visits but few enquiries; a scroll map might show most visitors never reach the enquiry button; and recordings might show a pop-up covering it on mobile.

Heatmaps for WooCommerce and Shopify stores

For online stores, the most useful pages to watch are collection or category pages, product pages, the cart and checkout. Look for shoppers who open many products but never add to cart, filters that get tapped but do not respond on mobile, size charts or delivery details that are hard to find, and drop-off at shipping or payment steps.

In India, payment and delivery questions often decide a purchase. If recordings show people hunting for COD availability, delivery time or return policy, make those details visible on the product page, near the add-to-cart button. If the checkout is slow on mobile, that affects every order; the WooCommerce developer page covers store work.

Examples of small fixes

  • Adding a sticky call or WhatsApp button on mobile after seeing people scroll up and down looking for contact details.
  • Turning an image that gets many clicks into a link to the related product or service.
  • Splitting a long form into steps after recordings show drop-off at a certain field. See multi-step forms.
  • Moving pricing or key details higher on a page where scroll depth is low.
  • Fixing a menu that does not open properly on some phones.

These fixes usually need small design or development changes. If your site is slow as well, fix speed first, because a slow page affects every visit.

FAQs

What are heatmaps and session recordings for business websites?

Heatmaps show where visitors click and how far they scroll, summarised across many visits. Session recordings replay individual visits so you can see where people get confused, hesitate or leave.

Are heatmap tools free?

Some are. Microsoft Clarity is free, and others offer free tiers with limits. Check each tool's current plans before choosing.

Do heatmap tools slow down my website?

They add a script, which adds some load. The effect is usually small, but test your speed after installing and avoid running several similar tools at once.

Is it legal to record visitor sessions?

Generally it is allowed with proper notice and data protection, but rules differ by country. Mask personal data, mention the tool in your privacy policy and check current requirements under India's data protection law.

How many recordings should I watch?

Enough to see patterns rather than one-off behaviour. Use filters to focus on key pages, mobile visitors and rage clicks instead of watching random sessions.
