Heatmaps and Session Recordings: See How Visitors Really Use Your Website

How heatmaps and session recordings from tools like Microsoft Clarity show where visitors click, scroll and get stuck, and which privacy settings to use.

Key takeaways
  • Heatmaps and session recordings show why visitors leave a page that analytics shows is underperforming.
  • Review recordings with one clear question and filters, and act on patterns seen across many sessions rather than single visits.
  • Keep form inputs masked, skip sensitive pages, mention the tool in your privacy policy and handle consent where required.

Analytics can tell you that hundreds of people visited your services page last month and only a few enquired. It can't show you why the rest left. Heatmaps and session recordings fill that gap by showing where visitors click, how far they scroll and where they get stuck. Free tools such as Microsoft Clarity make this easy to try, but recording visitors also comes with privacy responsibilities. Here's how to use these tools well.

What heatmaps and session recordings show

A small script records how visitors interact with each page and shows the data in a few ways:

  • Click (tap) heatmaps: which parts of a page get clicked or tapped most, including things that aren't links.
  • Scroll heatmaps: what share of visitors reach each part of the page. Useful for checking whether people ever see your call to action, prices or testimonials.
  • Session recordings: a replay of one visit's taps, scrolling and page changes, reconstructed from the page rather than a camera recording of the person.
  • Frustration signals: some tools flag patterns automatically. Clarity, for example, highlights "rage clicks" (repeated clicks in one spot), "dead clicks" (clicks that do nothing) and "quick backs" (visitors who leave a page almost immediately and return to the previous one).

Why analytics alone isn't enough

Google Analytics 4 is good at the numbers: how many people came, from where, and how many converted. It's much weaker at explaining behaviour on a single page. Once you know which pages matter, using the metrics that actually matter, heatmaps and recordings help you find the reason a busy page doesn't convert.

Common problems they reveal:

  • Visitors tapping a phone number that isn't a clickable link
  • People repeatedly tapping gallery photos that don't open
  • Most mobile visitors leaving before they reach the enquiry form at the bottom
  • A floating WhatsApp button covering the "Submit" button on small screens

Setting up Microsoft Clarity on WordPress

Clarity is a free tool from Microsoft and a sensible first choice for most small businesses. Other tools, such as Hotjar, offer similar features on free and paid plans; check current plans and limits before choosing.

  1. Create a project in Clarity with your website's address.
  2. Add the tracking code using Clarity's official WordPress plugin, Google Tag Manager or your theme's header settings. Use one method only, so the script doesn't load twice.
  3. Check the masking settings before real visitors are recorded (more on this below).
  4. Connect Google Analytics if you use GA4; Clarity offers an integration (check its current documentation).
  5. Wait a week or two for data to build up before drawing conclusions.

Every third-party script adds some weight, so run a speed test before and after installing it. If you only need it for a round of research, remove it afterwards.

What to look for, and what to try

What you seeWhat it may meanWhat to try
Rage clicks on photosVisitors expect them to enlargeAdd a lightbox or link to a full gallery
Dead clicks on text or iconsIt looks clickable but isn'tMake it a real link, or restyle it so it doesn't look like a button
Few visitors scroll to the formThe call to action is too far downAdd a button, call link or WhatsApp link near the top
Visitors stop at one form fieldThe field is confusing or feels intrusiveRemove it, make it optional or explain why you need it
Quick backs from ad trafficThe page doesn't match what the ad promisedMatch the headline and offer to the ad
Pinch-zooming on mobileText or buttons are too smallIncrease font size and spacing between buttons

Treat each observation as a clue, not proof: act on patterns seen across many sessions, not one impatient visitor.

How to review recordings without wasting hours

A busy site can collect thousands of recordings, and watching them at random tells you little. Instead:

  • Start with one question, such as "why don't mobile visitors on the AC repair page enquire?"
  • Filter by page, device, traffic source or country, and by sessions with rage or dead clicks.
  • Watch sessions that nearly converted, such as visitors who reached the contact page but didn't submit.
  • Note each problem in a simple list and count how often it appears across, say, 10 to 20 recordings.
  • Check heatmaps have enough data. Heatmaps from a few dozen visits can mislead, and sliders or pop-ups make them harder to read.

Privacy: record responsibly

Session recordings capture what visitors do on your site, so handle them with care, especially if you run a clinic, law practice or financial business, or collect sensitive details in forms. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

  • Mask what visitors type. Clarity masks sensitive content by default and offers a stricter masking mode. Keep form inputs masked, and never switch masking off to "see what people typed".
  • Exclude sensitive pages. Don't record account areas, checkout pages, patient or medical forms, or anything that shows personal details. Most tools let you mask specific elements or keep the script off certain pages.
  • Disclose it. Mention the tool in your privacy policy. Clarity's terms ask sites to tell visitors they use it, so check the current terms.
  • Handle consent where required. Visitors from the EU and UK are covered by stricter consent rules, and Clarity has introduced consent requirements for visitors from some regions. In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its rules are being phased in. See privacy policy and cookie consent basics, and ask a lawyer about your situation.
  • Limit access. Only people who need the data should see the dashboard, and recordings shouldn't be shared in WhatsApp groups.
  • Collect only what you'll use. If nobody is reviewing the data, remove the tool.

Turning what you see into improvements

Recordings are only useful if they lead to changes. Sort what you find into three groups:

  1. Obvious fixes: broken buttons, phone numbers that can't be tapped, overlapping elements on mobile. Fix these straight away.
  2. Likely improvements: moving a call to action higher, shortening a form, answering a common question near the price. Make the change and compare conversions over the following weeks.
  3. Bigger questions: a new offer or a different page structure. These are worth testing properly; the CRO basics guide explains how to form and test a hypothesis.

After changes go live, check the heatmaps again to confirm visitors now reach and use the form.
