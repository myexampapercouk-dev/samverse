Adding a Wishlist to Your WooCommerce Store: When It Helps and How to Do It Right

When a wishlist helps a WooCommerce store, guest vs logged-in lists, shareable gift lists, consent-based stock and price alerts, and keeping pages fast.

Key takeaways
  • A wishlist helps most on gifting, wedding, baby and high-consideration stores where buyers shortlist before buying.
  • Let guests save items without logging in, then offer an account or OTP login to keep the list across devices.
  • Send back-in-stock and price-drop reminders only with clear consent, and keep the plugin from breaking page caching.

A wishlist lets shoppers save products they like but aren't ready to buy yet. On some stores it quietly becomes one of the most-used features; on others it's a heart icon nobody clicks. Here's how to tell which kind of store you run, and how to add a wishlist to WooCommerce without cluttering the shop or slowing it down.

When a wishlist actually helps

A wishlist earns its place when people naturally take time over a purchase, or buy on behalf of someone else:

  • Gifting: stores selling gifts, toys, books or hampers, where shoppers shortlist ideas before a birthday, Diwali or Rakhi
  • High-consideration products: jewellery, furniture, electronics, mattresses and designer clothing, where buyers compare options over days or weeks and often check with family first
  • Weddings and new babies: registry-style lists that couples or expecting parents share with relatives, so people don't buy the same thing twice
  • Large catalogues: fashion and home decor stores where shoppers browse many items and want to come back to a shortlist
  • Trade buyers: interior designers or retailers saving products for a client before placing an order

It helps far less on stores with a handful of low-cost products bought on impulse or on repeat. There, a faster checkout or a subscription option is a better use of your effort.

Wishlist vs cart: different jobs

Many shoppers already use the cart as a parking spot for "maybe later" items. That muddies your abandoned cart numbers and makes it harder to tell who was genuinely about to buy. A wishlist gives browsing intent a proper home, so the cart stays closer to buying intent.

It doesn't replace cart recovery, though; the two work together. See WooCommerce abandoned cart recovery for the other half of the picture.

Guest wishlists vs logged-in wishlists

This is the main design decision, and each option has trade-offs.

QuestionGuest wishlistLogged-in wishlist
Where it's savedIn the browser, or on the server tied to a cookieIn the customer's account on your site
FrictionNone; one tap and it's savedNeeds an account or login
Works across devicesNo; a list made on a phone isn't on the laptopYes
How long it lastsUntil cookies are cleared or the browser changesAs long as the account exists
Reminders possibleOnly if the shopper gives an email addressYes, with consent

A sensible approach for most Indian stores: let anyone save items without logging in, then offer to keep the list safe by creating an account or logging in with a one-time password. Forcing sign-up before someone can tap the heart icon loses most people, especially on mobile.

Sharing lists for gifting, weddings and baby showers

Sharing is where a wishlist turns into a sales channel. Useful options include:

  • A shareable link that opens a read-only copy of the list
  • A WhatsApp share button, since that's where most families coordinate gifts
  • A list name and short note, such as "Wedding gifts, December"
  • Showing which items have already been bought, so relatives don't double up (this usually needs a registry-style plugin or custom work)
  • Delivery to the list owner's address, with gift wrap and a message

Keep privacy in mind. Lists should be private by default, with the owner choosing to share, and a shared link shouldn't reveal the owner's address or phone number to whoever opens it.

Back-in-stock and price-drop reminders, with consent

A wishlist tells you exactly what someone wants. Reminders turn that into sales, but only if the shopper has agreed to hear from you:

  • Back in stock: when a saved item that was sold out returns
  • Price drop or sale: when a saved item goes on offer
  • Low stock: a gentle note that a saved item is nearly gone, sent only when it's true

Ask for permission clearly, with an unticked checkbox or a separate "Notify me" button. Say what you'll send and how often, and make unsubscribing easy. Email is the natural channel; WhatsApp reminders need the customer's opt-in and approved message templates through the WhatsApp Business Platform.

India's data protection law also sets expectations around consent and purpose; see the DPDP Act and your website, and check current rules with your adviser.

Choosing a wishlist plugin

WooCommerce doesn't include a wishlist out of the box, so you'll use a plugin or custom code. Several established wishlist plugins exist, often with a free version and a paid upgrade. When comparing them, check:

  • Guest wishlists, and whether they merge into the account after login
  • Sharing options, including WhatsApp and a clean shareable link
  • Multiple named lists, if you sell for weddings or registries
  • Back-in-stock or price-drop emails built in, or easy integration with your email tool
  • Support for variable products, so the chosen size or colour is saved, not just the parent product
  • Compatibility with your theme, page builder and caching setup
  • Recent updates, good reviews and responsive support

Sometimes a simple heart icon and a "save for later" link in the cart are all you need. If a plugin does five times more than that, you pay for the extras in speed and maintenance.

Keeping the wishlist from slowing your store

Wishlist plugins can quietly hurt performance, mainly because they need to know who the visitor is on every page:

  • Caching: a wishlist count in the header can stop pages being cached. Make sure the count loads separately with a small request, so the rest of the page stays cached
  • Scripts and styles: load the wishlist's files only where they're used, not on blog posts and policy pages
  • Icon fonts: some plugins load a whole icon library just for one heart; a small inline SVG does the same job
  • Database growth: guest lists stored on the server can pile up, so set them to expire
  • Real-world testing: check category pages on a mid-range Android phone after installing

See WooCommerce speed optimisation for the wider checklist.

Measuring whether it's worth keeping

Track wishlist adds, shares and how many saved items are eventually bought. Then put the data to work: restock popular saved items first, feature them in festive campaigns, or combine them into bundles and gift sets. If, after a few months, hardly anyone uses the wishlist, remove it and keep the store lighter.
