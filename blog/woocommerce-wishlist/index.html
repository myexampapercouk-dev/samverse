Adding a Wishlist to Your WooCommerce Store: When It Helps and How to Do It Right

How to add a WooCommerce wishlist that helps sales: guest vs logged-in lists, gift sharing, back-in-stock alerts with consent, Shopify options and speed.

Key takeaways
  • A wishlist helps stores with considered or gift purchases, such as jewellery, sarees, furniture and baby products, more than low-cost repeat buys.
  • Allow guest wishlists, make sharing on WhatsApp easy, and send back-in-stock or price-drop reminders only to shoppers who opted in.
  • Choose a lightweight plugin or Shopify app, check it works with caching, and track wishlist adds and resulting orders before keeping it.

A WooCommerce wishlist lets shoppers save products to buy later or share with family, which helps stores selling considered or gift purchases. To add one well, use a lightweight plugin, allow guests to save items, make sharing easy, send reminders only with consent, and measure whether it actually leads to orders.

Many Indian shoppers browse on their phone, shortlist a few items, and discuss them with family before buying. A wishlist supports that habit. But a badly chosen plugin can slow the store or clutter product pages for little benefit. This guide covers when a wishlist is worth adding and how to set it up properly on WooCommerce, with notes for Shopify stores.

When a wishlist actually helps

Wishlists work best where buyers take time to decide or buy for others:

Store typeWishlist valueWhy
Jewellery, sarees, ethnic wearHighHigher prices, family input, wedding and festival shopping
Furniture and home decorHighPlanning a room over weeks, comparing options
Baby products and giftingHighRegistries and lists shared with relatives
Electronics and appliancesMediumComparing models, waiting for offers
Groceries, daily needs, low-price itemsLowQuick repeat purchases; a reorder button helps more

If most of your orders are small and fast, a wishlist may add clutter without much benefit. In that case, focus on a smooth checkout and abandoned cart recovery instead.

Wishlist vs cart: different jobs

Shoppers often use the cart as a temporary wishlist, which makes cart data confusing and can trigger cart reminders when they were not ready to buy. A wishlist gives them a separate place to save "maybe later" items, so the cart reflects real buying intent. Keep the two clearly separate on the page: a heart or "Save" icon for the wishlist, and a distinct "Add to cart" button.

On the wishlist page itself, let shoppers move items to the cart in one tap, show current price and stock status, and remove items easily.

Guest wishlists vs logged-in wishlists

Forcing people to create an account just to save a product loses many of them. A better approach:

  • Allow guests to save items, stored in their browser or a session
  • Offer to save the list permanently if they log in or create an account, without making it compulsory
  • Merge a guest list into the account when they log in later

Guest wishlists are tied to one browser and device, so tell shoppers that logging in keeps their list across devices. Many Indian shoppers browse on the phone and buy on a laptop, or the other way round.

How to add a WooCommerce wishlist step by step

  1. Decide what you need: simple saving, sharing, multiple lists, gift registries or reminders.
  2. Choose one plugin that covers those needs without many extras you will not use.
  3. Place the button on product pages and, if it suits, on category pages, without crowding the add-to-cart button.
  4. Add a wishlist link in the header or account menu, with a count of saved items.
  5. Configure guest saving and what happens when a guest logs in.
  6. Test with caching to make sure the heart icon and counts update correctly for each visitor.
  7. Set up tracking for wishlist adds and orders that include wishlisted items.

Design details that make a wishlist usable

Small design choices decide whether shoppers notice and use the wishlist. On mobile, which is where most Indian shoppers browse, the save icon should be large enough to tap easily and placed consistently, usually at the top corner of product images on category pages and next to the product title or add-to-cart button on product pages.

When someone saves an item, give quick feedback: the heart fills in, a short message confirms it was saved, and the wishlist count in the header updates. Avoid popups that interrupt browsing. On the wishlist page, show a clear image, the selected size or colour, the current price, stock status and a one-tap button to move the item to the cart.

If an item goes out of stock or its price changes, show that clearly rather than silently. A note such as "Back in stock soon, notify me" turns a disappointment into a reason to return, if the shopper chooses to be notified.

Think about empty states too. A wishlist page with nothing saved should explain how to save items and link to popular categories or bestsellers, so it does not feel like a dead end. Linking related products or bundles from the wishlist can also help; see WooCommerce product bundles and upsells.

Sharing lists for gifting, weddings and baby showers

Sharing is where wishlists become especially useful in India. A bride shortlisting jewellery, parents preparing for a baby, or someone planning a housewarming often wants family to see the options. Add simple sharing:

  • A WhatsApp share button, which most Indian shoppers will use first
  • A copyable link for email or other apps
  • An option to make a list private or shareable

For gift registries, some plugins let others buy items from a shared list and mark them as purchased, so two relatives don't buy the same gift. Test this carefully, including how delivery addresses and privacy are handled.

Back-in-stock and price-drop reminders, with consent

A wishlist can tell you which products people want, and reminders can bring them back:

  • "Back in stock" alerts for saved items that were unavailable
  • Price-drop or sale alerts for saved items
  • Gentle reminders before a festival sale ends

Only send these to shoppers who have agreed to receive them, with a clear opt-in and an easy way to stop. Keep messages relevant and occasional. India's data protection rules require a clear purpose and consent for personal data, so check the current requirements; see DPDP Act basics for websites. Wishlist data also helps with buying decisions: frequently saved items that are out of stock may be worth restocking.

Choosing a wishlist plugin

Several well-known WooCommerce wishlist plugins exist, from free versions to premium ones with sharing and reminders. When comparing them, check:

  • Guest wishlists and merging on login
  • Sharing options, including WhatsApp
  • Compatibility with your theme, page builder and caching plugin
  • Support for variable products, so the saved size or colour is remembered
  • Email or notification features, and whether they respect consent
  • How much extra code it loads on every page
  • Recent updates and active support

Avoid installing a plugin with dozens of features you will not use. Each one adds settings, scripts and potential conflicts.

Wishlists on Shopify

Shopify does not include a full wishlist feature by default, so stores usually add one through an app from the Shopify App Store or with custom theme code. The same principles apply: allow guests to save, make WhatsApp sharing easy, ask for consent before sending reminders and check how much the app adds to page load. Some Shopify themes include a basic "save for later" or favourites feature, so check your theme first. If your store already feels slow, see fixing a slow Shopify store before adding another app.

Keeping the wishlist from slowing your store

Wishlists need to show different information to each visitor, which can clash with page caching. A poorly built plugin may load scripts on every page or make extra requests that slow down mobile visitors. To avoid this:

  • Test page speed before and after installing
  • Check that the wishlist icon state updates correctly with caching enabled
  • Load wishlist scripts only where needed, if the plugin allows it
  • Remove the plugin completely if you stop using it

See WooCommerce speed optimization for broader fixes.

Measuring whether it's worth keeping

After a few months, check whether the wishlist earns its place. Track wishlist adds, shares and how many saved items are later bought. Compare products with high wishlist counts against their sales. If people save but rarely buy, look at price, stock or product page quality; see WooCommerce product page optimization. If almost nobody uses it, remove it and simplify the page.

FAQs

How do I add a wishlist to WooCommerce?

Install a wishlist plugin, place the save button on product pages, add a wishlist link to the header, enable guest saving, and test it with your caching setup.

Does a wishlist increase sales?

It can help stores with considered or gift purchases by bringing shoppers back. Measure wishlist adds and resulting orders on your own store before deciding.

Can guests use a WooCommerce wishlist without logging in?

Yes, with most plugins. Guest lists are stored in the browser, so offer login to keep the list across devices.

How do I add a wishlist on Shopify?

Check whether your theme includes one; otherwise add a wishlist app or custom theme code, and test its effect on speed.

Can I send reminders about wishlisted products?

Yes, if shoppers opt in. Back-in-stock and price-drop alerts work best when they are relevant, occasional and easy to stop.
