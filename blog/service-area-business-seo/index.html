SEO for Service-Area Businesses: How to Rank Without a Shopfront

How plumbers, cleaners, movers and other businesses that visit customers can set up Google Business Profile, service pages and area pages to rank locally.

Key takeaways
  • If customers never visit you, hide your address on your Google Business Profile and set realistic service areas instead of borrowing an address.
  • A hidden address still counts for distance, so strong service pages are what help you rank in areas beyond your base.
  • Build separate area pages only where you have real jobs, reviews and local detail; otherwise list your areas on one strong page.

Plumbers, electricians, cleaners, pest control teams, packers and movers, home tutors and mobile mechanics have one thing in common: customers never visit them. They go to the customer. Google calls these service-area businesses, and they play by slightly different rules in local search. Get the set-up right and you can compete with businesses that have a shop on the main road. Get it wrong and your Business Profile can be suspended, or your website fills up with pages Google ignores.

How local search treats a business without a shopfront

Google says local results depend mainly on three things: relevance (how well you match the search), distance (how far you are from the searcher or the place they typed) and prominence (how well known and well reviewed you are).

Hiding your address on your Business Profile doesn't take it out of this; the profile is still tied to the location you verified. In practice:

  • You'll usually show most strongly in the map pack near your base
  • Listing lots of localities won't make you appear in the map pack across the whole city
  • For areas further away, your website ranking in the normal results matters more

So you need two things working together: a correctly set-up profile for nearby searches, and a website that can rank for "{service} in {area}" searches everywhere else you work.

Set up your Business Profile correctly

  1. Hide your address if customers don't come to you. Google's guidelines say a business that only serves customers at their location should remove its address and set service areas instead. Showing your home address as if it were a shop risks suspension.
  2. Set realistic service areas. Add the cities, localities or PIN codes you actually cover. Google limits how many you can add, and its help pages advise keeping the overall area within roughly two hours' drive of your base; check the current guidance.
  3. Don't borrow an address to appear in another city. Google's guidelines generally treat virtual offices, PO boxes and unstaffed addresses as ineligible, and competitors do report them.
  4. One profile per real business. Creating a separate profile for each area you serve, without staffed premises there, breaks the guidelines.
  5. Hybrid businesses, such as a repair counter that also does home visits, can show the address and add service areas as well.

Google may ask you to verify with a short video showing your vehicle, tools and proof of business, so keep these handy. The rest of the profile (categories, services, photos, reviews) works the same as for any business; see the Google Business Profile checklist.

Make your coverage obvious on your website

Your website doesn't need your home address, but visitors should know within seconds whether you come to their area.

  • Say "Based in {city}, serving {areas}" near the top of the homepage and on the contact page
  • Use the same business name and phone number as your Business Profile, everywhere
  • Add an "Areas we serve" section or page listing localities, grouped by zone
  • Make call and WhatsApp buttons easy to tap on a phone, because most of these searches are urgent and on mobile

Area pages: when to build them and what goes on them

This is where most service-area websites go wrong. Twenty pages saying "Best plumber in {area}" with only the area name swapped are doorway pages, and they can drag down the whole site. Our guide to local landing pages without doorway pages explains the rules; here's how they apply when you don't have a branch in each area.

Build a separate area page only when you have genuine material for it, such as:

  • Jobs you've actually done there, with photos (never showing a customer's house number or face without permission)
  • Reviews from customers in that area
  • Local conditions that change the work: hard water, older buildings with outdated wiring, monsoon seepage, or society rules on lift bookings and working hours for movers
  • Practical details: response time, visiting charge and which team covers it

Packers and movers often want route pages such as "Pune to Bengaluru". The same test applies: build one only if it says something genuinely useful about that move, such as the transit time you can commit to, vehicle options and what the customer should prepare.

Everywhere else, one strong service page plus a clear list of areas beats a thin page per locality. Start with your two or three busiest areas, do them properly, and add more as real jobs and reviews build up.

Let service pages do the heavy lifting

For searches beyond your immediate area, Google usually ranks service pages, not your homepage. Give each main service its own page: bathroom leakage repair, deep cleaning, home shifting, termite treatment. On each one:

  • Mention your city and main areas naturally, in the title and the text
  • Explain the problems you solve, what a visit involves and what affects the cost
  • Answer the questions you get on every call: timings, guarantees, what the customer needs to arrange
  • Show photos of real work and your team

Reviews, photos and mentions without an address

Prominence is the factor you can build steadily, and for a service-area business that mostly means reviews.

  • Ask after every job, while the technician is still there or on WhatsApp the same evening
  • A gentle prompt such as "it helps others if you mention which service we did" is reasonable; never write reviews for customers or offer discounts in return
  • Reply to every review, mentioning the service where it fits naturally
  • Add job photos to your profile regularly

See how to get more Google reviews ethically for message templates. Directory listings help too if your name and phone number are consistent and your address is hidden where possible; see business directories and local citations in India covers which ones are worth it.

Track which areas bring enquiries

Decide where to add area pages based on data, not guesses.

  • Add a locality or PIN code field to your enquiry form
  • Ask callers where they found you and note their area
  • Check Business Profile performance for calls, website clicks and the searches that found you
  • Use Search Console to see which service and area searches your pages already appear for

If one area keeps producing jobs, it has earned a proper page. If another never does, it may be too far away to rank, or not worth the travel.
