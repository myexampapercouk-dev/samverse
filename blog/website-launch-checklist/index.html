Website Launch Checklist for Small Businesses: What to Check Before, On and After Launch Day

A website launch checklist for small businesses: content, mobile, forms, SEO settings, analytics, security, legal pages, launch-day steps and the first week.

Key takeaways
  • Test the things that cost you enquiries first: every form, call and WhatsApp link on a real phone, with the email arriving where someone will answer it.
  • Most launch disasters are settings, not design: a ticked "discourage search engines" box, leftover noindex tags, staging URLs, missing redirects or no backup.
  • Launch early in the week, test forms again on the live address, submit the sitemap, then watch enquiries, Search Console and 404 errors closely for the first week.

Before a website goes live, check that every page is proofread and accurate, the site works on real phones, every form and button actually reaches you, search engines are allowed to index it, analytics and Search Console are connected, it runs on HTTPS with a fresh backup, and the privacy policy and other legal pages are in place. On launch day, switch off any "discourage search engines" or coming-soon setting, test the forms again on the live address and submit your sitemap to Google.

This is the business owner's version of the checklist, so you can check any developer's work before you sign it off, mine included. Content and trust

  • Every page proofread, ideally by someone who didn't write it
  • No placeholder text, demo testimonials, sample "Hello world" post or theme demo pages left behind
  • Phone number, address, email and opening hours identical on every page and matching your Google Business Profile
  • Prices, services, service areas and team members up to date
  • Photos that are real, sharp and properly licensed
  • One clear next step on every page: call, WhatsApp, enquire or book
  • Favicon, a social sharing image and the current year in the footer

Mobile and browsers

  • Tested on your own phone, plus at least one Android phone and one iPhone
  • Checked in Chrome, Safari and Edge on a laptop
  • No sideways scrolling, overlapping text or images cut off at the edges
  • Buttons large enough to tap, and menus that open and close cleanly
  • Pop-ups and chat widgets that can be closed and don't cover the main button
  • Pages tried on mobile data, not just the office Wi-Fi

Forms, calls and WhatsApp

This is the section that decides whether the website earns its keep, so test it properly:

  • Submit every form with real details and check the email arrives in the inbox of the person who will answer it, not in spam
  • Make sure replying to a form email goes to the customer, not back to your own website address
  • Confirm visitors see a clear thank-you message or page after sending
  • Tap every phone link on a phone; the number should include the country code, such as +91
  • Check WhatsApp links open a chat with the right number; wa.me links need the full number with the country code and no plus sign or spaces, in the form https://wa.me/91XXXXXXXXXX
  • Check spam protection is switched on, and that it doesn't block your own test from a phone

If anything here fails, why a contact form stops getting enquiries walks through the usual causes.

SEO settings that break launches

Most launch disasters are settings, not design. A site can look perfect and still be invisible to Google:

  • Settings > Reading: "Discourage search engines from indexing this site" must be unticked on the live site. Developers tick it on staging, and it's easy to forget.
  • No leftover noindex: check your SEO plugin hasn't set pages, posts or whole content types to noindex.
  • robots.txt doesn't block the whole site, and any staging password protection is removed.
  • Titles and meta descriptions written for every important page, with one main heading per page.
  • An XML sitemap loads, usually at /sitemap_index.xml with Yoast or Rank Math, or /wp-sitemap.xml with WordPress's built-in sitemap.
  • No staging addresses left in links, images or canonical tags.
  • Redirects from old URLs if this replaces an existing site, so visitors and Google land on the new pages. This is the step that protects your rankings; see redesigning without losing rankings.
  • Image alt text on meaningful images, and structured data where it fits, such as business details and FAQs.

Speed

  • Home page and one key service or product page tested on PageSpeed Insights in mobile mode; speed test tools explained shows how to read the results
  • Images resized and compressed, with no multi-megabyte photos in sliders
  • Caching switched on and tested once the site is live
  • Unused plugins and themes removed

Analytics, Search Console and tracking

  • Google Analytics 4 installed once, with your own visit showing in the real-time report
  • Key events set up for form submissions, phone taps and WhatsApp clicks
  • Search Console verified for the live domain, ideally as a domain property, which covers every version of the address
  • Google Ads or Meta conversion tracking tested, if you run ads
  • Every account owned by your business's Google account, not a developer's

Setting up Google Analytics and Search Console covers each step.

Security, backups and ownership

  • Domain, hosting and the main WordPress administrator account in your name; the website ownership checklist lists what you should hold
  • Separate logins for everyone, strong passwords and two-factor authentication for administrators, and test accounts deleted
  • HTTPS on every page, with http:// and the non-preferred www or non-www version redirecting to one address
  • A full backup taken at launch, and automatic off-site backups scheduled
  • Uptime monitoring, so you hear about downtime before customers do
  • Plugins and themes updated, with anything unused deleted

Legal pages and policies

  • A privacy policy that matches what your site actually collects and which tools it uses; see privacy policy and cookie consent basics
  • Terms, and refund, cancellation and shipping policies if you take bookings or payments
  • A cookie consent banner if you use tracking tools and serve visitors from regions that require one
  • Business details and registrations shown where your profession or industry expects them

These pages carry legal weight, so have a lawyer or CA check them; this checklist isn't legal advice.

Launch day, step by step

  1. Pick a sensible time. Launch early in the week, when you and your developer are around for a few days afterwards. Avoid a Friday evening or the day before a campaign, festival sale or trade show.
  2. Prepare DNS. If the domain is moving to new hosting, lower the DNS TTL a day or two before so the switch spreads faster. If your email uses the same domain, make sure the mail records are kept, or email stops arriving.
  3. Back up the old site, if there is one, and the new one.
  4. Move the site live and replace every staging address with the live one.
  5. Check HTTPS: the certificate is active, http:// redirects to https:// and no browser warnings appear.
  6. Open the site to search engines: untick "Discourage search engines", and switch off maintenance or coming-soon mode and any password protection.
  7. Clear every cache: the caching plugin, the server and any CDN.
  8. Test again on the live address: every form, phone link and WhatsApp button, plus every menu and footer link.
  9. Tell Google: submit the sitemap in Search Console and request indexing for the home page and key pages with the URL Inspection tool.
  10. Update your links: Google Business Profile, social media profiles, email signatures and directory listings.

The first week after launch

  • Check enquiries are arriving every day, and ask a friend to send one from their phone
  • Look at Search Console for indexing problems, 404 errors and security messages
  • Watch for old addresses that now show "page not found", and add redirects for them
  • Confirm Analytics is recording visits and key events
  • Fix the small issues real visitors find; there are always a few

FAQs

How long before launch should I start testing my website?

Start checking content a couple of weeks before, and leave at least several days for testing on the staging site. Larger sites, booking systems and online stores need longer, because there are more paths to test.

What is the best day to launch a website?

Early in the week, at a time when you and your developer can fix problems over the following days. Avoid launching just before a weekend, a holiday or a campaign that will send lots of visitors.

Do I need to tell Google my new website is live?

Google can find it on its own, but submitting your sitemap in Search Console and requesting indexing for key pages speeds things up. Linking the site from your Google Business Profile helps too.

Should I take my old website down before launching the new one?

No. Replace it on the same domain at launch, with redirects from old page addresses to their new equivalents. Taking the old site down early leaves customers and Google with nothing to find.

Why isn't my new website showing on Google?

New pages can take days or weeks to appear. If nothing shows after that, check the "Discourage search engines" setting, noindex tags and robots.txt first; see a WordPress site not showing on Google for the full list.
