A Shopify developer sets up and changes the parts of a store that the admin and theme editor cannot: custom sections and templates in Liquid, theme fixes, app clean-ups, speed and SEO work, migrations and integrations. You need one when the job goes beyond settings and content, or when getting it wrong would cost sales. Hire on how they work on your live store, who owns the accounts and what is written in the quote, not on a portfolio alone.

I am a freelance developer in Pune working on Shopify, WordPress and WooCommerce, so this guide is written from the developer's side of the table, but the questions in it are the ones I would want a client to ask anyone. You can see how I run Shopify projects on the Shopify developer service page; the last section here summarises it.

What a Shopify developer actually does

Shopify looks after hosting, security and the core platform, so a Shopify developer's work sits on top of that. Typical jobs include:

  • Store setup: theme, products and collections, payments, cash on delivery, shipping, taxes, domain and launch checks.
  • Theme work: custom sections and blocks, product templates, metafields shown on the storefront, and turning a design file into a working theme. See Shopify theme customisation for what that involves.
  • Fixes: a variant picker showing the wrong price, a cart drawer that misbehaves, layout bugs on phones.
  • Speed and SEO: removing app leftovers, trimming scripts, fixing product links, titles and structured data.
  • Migrations: moving products, customers and redirects from WooCommerce, WordPress or Wix, or from Shopify to WooCommerce.
  • Integrations: courier, invoice, accounting or marketplace connections, usually through apps and their settings.

Day-to-day running, such as adding products, writing descriptions, running sales and handling orders, is normally the store owner's job once the developer has set things up and shown the team how.

Developer, listed Shopify partner or agency

Shopify runs a directory of service partners that store owners can browse, which has gone by names such as the Experts Marketplace and the Partner Directory; the name and the criteria for listing change from time to time. Being listed is one signal among several. Plenty of capable developers and agencies work outside it, and a listing does not tell you who will actually do the work on your store.

When you need a developer, and when you don't

You can probably manage without one if you are using a free theme with its existing sections, have a small catalogue, need only standard payments and shipping, and are comfortable following the step-by-step setup guide. Shopify's admin is built for owners to run themselves.

A developer earns their fee when:

  • the design needs sections or layouts the theme editor cannot produce;
  • you are moving from another platform and need products, customers and redirects handled without losing search traffic;
  • the store is slow, and nobody knows which app or script is responsible;
  • payments, COD controls, GST invoices and courier apps need to work together and be tested;
  • you have a launch date and cannot afford to learn by trial and error on a live store.

The general version of this decision is covered in hiring a developer vs doing it yourself.

Questions to ask before you hire a Shopify developer in India

  1. Which Shopify stores have you worked on, and what did you do on each? Ask for specifics: setup, theme code, migration. Some work is under confidentiality, so a developer may describe a project without naming it; that is reasonable, but the description should be concrete.
  2. Will you work on a duplicate theme? The answer should be yes, with a preview link for you to check before anything is published.
  3. How will you get access? A collaborator request or staff account, never your owner login.
  4. What exactly is in the quote? A written scope listing pages, sections, apps, integrations, testing and handover.
  5. Which apps will I need, and what will they cost me each month? A good developer reduces the app list rather than growing it.
  6. How will checkout be tested? Prepaid, failed and COD orders on a phone, then a real low-value order and refund.
  7. What happens after launch? Bug fixes within an agreed period, how later changes are charged, and how quickly they respond.
  8. What will I be able to do myself afterwards? Sections built with settings let your team edit them without code.

Put the answers in the agreement. The website contract checklist lists the clauses worth having.

Access and ownership

Your store should be yours from the first day. That means:

  • the Shopify account is opened in your business name, with the plan billed to you;
  • the domain, payment provider account and every app subscription are in your name and paid by you;
  • the developer works through a collaborator account, which you approve, or a staff account you create, with only the permissions the job needs;
  • you can remove that access at any time from the users and permissions settings.

At the time of writing, collaborator accounts do not use one of your plan's staff seats, and you can require a collaborator request code so that nobody can send a request without it. When the project ends, ask for any custom code to be documented and remove access you no longer need.

If part of the store or a companion site runs on WordPress instead, the same questions apply when you outsource WordPress development to India: who does the work, who owns the access and how changes are handled.

Red flags

  • Asking for your owner password instead of sending a collaborator request.
  • Offering to open the Shopify account, domain or payment account in their own name.
  • Editing the live theme directly, with no duplicate and no preview.
  • No written scope, or a quote that is suspiciously low with extras to be agreed later.
  • Recommending a long list of paid apps without explaining what each one does.
  • Claims of guaranteed rankings or sales figures.
  • Reluctance to say who will do the work, or to describe past projects in any detail.
  • No plan for redirects when URLs change in a migration or redesign.

How a Shopify project runs with Samverse

The process is the same whether the store is new or already selling, and it is the one I followed on a recent Shopify build for a fashion brand.

  1. Free review and fixed quote. You send the store address or a note on what you sell, how many products and how you want to be paid. I reply with a fixed price, a timeline and the scope in writing.
  2. Access. You approve a collaborator request or add a staff account, choosing what I can open.
  3. Duplicate theme. Layout and code changes happen on an unpublished copy, shared through a preview link.
  4. Build. Products, collections and metafields, then sections and templates, then payments, shipping, tax and only the apps the store needs.
  5. Checkout testing. Prepaid, failed and cash on delivery orders on a phone; order emails, invoices and stock checked; then one real low-value order placed and refunded.
  6. Launch. Domain connected, password removed and the new theme published, with the previous one kept as a fallback.
  7. Handover. A walkthrough of orders, refunds, products and the theme editor, and a note on which sections and apps control what.

Everything stays in your name, and you pay Shopify and the app makers directly.

FAQs

Where can I hire a Shopify developer in India?

You can hire a Shopify developer in India through Shopify's partner directory, freelance platforms, referrals from other store owners or directly from a developer's own website. Whichever route you take, ask the questions above and check how they handle access and ownership. If you want to talk about your store, see my Shopify developer services.

Do I need a Shopify developer to start a store?

Not always. A small store on a free theme can be set up by the owner. A developer helps when you need custom design, a migration, integrations or a tested launch by a fixed date.

Should I give a developer my Shopify password?

No. Approve a collaborator request or create a staff account with limited permissions, and remove it when the work is finished.

How much does it cost to hire a Shopify developer in India?

It depends on the scope: catalogue size, theme work, migrations and integrations. I don't publish prices for Shopify work; send your requirements for a free review and a fixed quote.

Is a freelancer or an agency better for Shopify?

A freelancer suits setup, theme work, fixes and migrations where you want one point of contact. An agency suits larger projects that bundle design, development, content and marketing.

Who owns the theme code a developer writes?

Agree it in writing. Custom sections built for your store should be yours to keep and edit, and they stay in your theme after the project ends.

If you are weighing up who should work on your store, send the address or a short note on what you plan to sell. Through my Shopify development service you get a free review, a written scope and a fixed quote, with every account in your own name from the start.
