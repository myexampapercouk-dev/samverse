How a WordPress maintenance plan starts: the first health check

I do not start clicking "update" on day one. Whether I built your site or someone else did, the first job is to understand it, because most maintenance problems come from things nobody knew were there.

  1. Access and a full backup, before anything is touched.
  2. An inventory. Every plugin and theme, its version, whether its developer still maintains it, and whether premium licences are active and in your name. An expired licence is one of the most common reasons updates quietly stop arriving.
  3. Health checks. PHP version, Site Health warnings, failing scheduled tasks, whether site emails actually arrive, SSL renewal, and who has administrator access. Old PHP versions are covered in updating your PHP version safely, and access in auditing your WordPress user accounts.
  4. Urgent fixes, agreed with you first, such as an abandoned plugin with a known vulnerability or contact forms that have stopped sending. If forms are silent, the cause is often email delivery; see fixing WordPress emails with SMTP.
  5. Monitoring and a baseline. Uptime monitoring, the backup schedule, and starting speed figures for key pages, so later changes can be compared.

The health check usually takes a few working days. It depends on how many plugins the site runs, how many problems turn up and how quickly access arrives. A neglected site with years of skipped updates is brought up to date in careful stages rather than all at once.

What gets tested after every update

Updating is the easy part. Checking that nothing broke is what you are really paying for, and it goes beyond glancing at the home page:

  • Key page templates on a phone and a desktop: home, a service or product page, a blog post, the contact page.
  • Every form submits, and the email actually arrives. On a contact form with a service-type dropdown, like the one I built for Vansh Group, the enquiry has to arrive with the right service attached.
  • Interactive features, such as the free anxiety self-assessment on the Dr. Sudhir Arora site I built, because these break more quietly than a layout does.
  • For WooCommerce stores: a test order through the payment flow (Razorpay or UPI in test mode, or cash on delivery), the order emails and, if you use one, the GST invoice.
  • Logins and sign-ups, on sites where customers or members have accounts.

When an update does break something

Restoring the whole site from last night's backup sounds simple, but on a store it would also wipe out every order placed since. So the first step is narrower: roll back the one plugin or theme that caused the problem, or fix the conflict directly, and hold that update until its developer releases a fix. A full restore is the last resort, not the first. Finding and fixing plugin conflicts shows how the culprit is tracked down.

What website maintenance costs in India, and what drives the price

I do not publish one price, because two sites that look similar can need very different amounts of care. You get a fixed monthly quote after a free review of your site. What decides it:

  • Brochure site or online store. Stores need test orders after updates and usually run more plugins.
  • Plugin count, and how many are premium or custom-built.
  • Backup frequency and how long backups are kept.
  • Content work you expect each month.
  • Number of sites, for businesses or agencies with several.
  • Hosting, and whether it is part of a package or paid separately.

What is included, and what is not

The plan contents are listed near the top of this page. Work that is quoted separately:

  • New pages, new features and redesigns.
  • Larger content projects, such as adding a product catalogue or rewriting service pages.
  • A full speed overhaul, if the site is slow to begin with. That is a one-time speed optimization job; the plan then keeps it fast.
  • Hosting, domain and premium licence renewals, which are paid to the providers, ideally in your name.

Signs your site needs a maintenance plan

  • The dashboard shows a long list of pending updates and nobody is sure it is safe to click.
  • You do not know when the last backup was taken, or where it is stored.
  • The developer who built the site has moved on, and nobody else has the logins.
  • Forms or order emails stopped arriving, and you heard about it from a customer.
  • A premium theme or plugin licence expired, so updates stopped.
  • Your host keeps emailing warnings about PHP versions, disk space or resource limits.

How to prepare, especially if you are switching developers

  • WordPress admin and hosting logins, plus the domain registrar login if you have it.
  • Licence keys, and the account emails used to buy premium themes and plugins.
  • A list of who else has access: past developers, agencies and staff.
  • Any custom code or theme changes, and known issues you have been living with.
  • Your most important pages and forms, and for stores, the payment methods you offer.

FAQ

Common questions

Do you maintain websites you didn't build?

Yes. I start with a quick health check of your site, fix any urgent issues, and then maintain it monthly.

What if my site breaks after an update?

Updates are tested and a backup is taken first, so anything that breaks can be fixed or rolled back quickly.

Are content changes included?

Yes, small changes like text, images and prices are included each month. Bigger work is quoted separately.

Can I cancel anytime?

Yes. Plans are month to month.

How much does WordPress maintenance cost in India?

It depends on whether the site is a store, how many plugins it runs, how often it needs backups and how much content work you expect each month. Rather than a one-size price, you get a fixed monthly quote after a free review of your site.

Do you maintain WooCommerce stores?

Yes. Store maintenance adds a test order through the payment flow after updates, checks on order emails and invoices, and a closer eye on scheduled tasks and the database as orders build up.

Can I just switch on auto-updates instead?

Auto-updates are fine for minor and security releases on simple sites. What they do not do is take a backup first, check forms and checkout afterwards, or roll back a bad update, which is what a maintenance plan adds.

Where are my backups kept, and can I have a copy?

Backups are stored off-site, separate from your hosting, so a server problem cannot take out both. You can ask for a copy at any time, and you take the latest one with you if you ever leave.
